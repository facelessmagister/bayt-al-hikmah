## WHILE-READING (Read the Text)

### Part 1: What Is a Rainbow?

Have you ever looked up at the sky after rain and seen a rainbow? A rainbow is a big, beautiful arc of colours. It has red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet. Sometimes you can see it very clearly. Sometimes it is faint and hard to see.

A rainbow is not a real thing that you can touch. You cannot walk up to it and find a pot of gold. It is just light. But how does light make all those colours? The answer is in the rain.

### Check Your Understanding 1
- True or False? You can touch a rainbow. __________
- Name three colours you can see in a rainbow. _______________________
- What makes the colours of a rainbow? (a) paint / (b) light / (c) birds

---

### Part 2: Sunlight and Rain

To make a rainbow, you need two things. You need sunlight and rain. The sun must be behind you. The rain must be in front of you. When these two things meet, a rainbow can appear.

Sunlight looks white, but it is not really white. White sunlight is made of many colours. You just cannot see them because they are all mixed together. The colours are red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet. Together, they are called the spectrum.

When sunlight goes into a raindrop, something special happens. The light bends. This is called refraction. The light bends and then it splits. The colours separate. Each colour bends a little bit differently. Red bends the least. Violet bends the most.

### Check Your Understanding 2
- What two things do you need to make a rainbow? _______________________
- Where must the sun be? (a) behind you / (b) in front of you / (c) above you
- What happens when sunlight goes into a raindrop? _______________________

---

### Part 3: How We See the Rainbow

After the light splits inside the raindrop, it hits the back of the drop. Then it bounces back. It bounces back toward your eyes. This is called reflection. The light reflects off the back of the raindrop.

Millions of raindrops do this at the same time. Each droplet sends one colour back to your eyes. Some droplets send red. Some send orange. Some send yellow. Together, they make a big arc of colours.

That is why you see a rainbow. It is not magic. It is science!

Did you know? A rainbow is actually a full circle. But you can only see half of it because the ground blocks the bottom half. If you are in an aeroplane, sometimes you can see a full circular rainbow!

### Check Your Understanding 3
- What happens after the light splits inside the raindrop? _______________________
- Why do we only see half a circle? _______________________
- True or False? You can see a full rainbow from an aeroplane. __________
