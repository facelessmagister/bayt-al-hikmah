## WHILE-READING (Read the Text)

### Part 1: What Is a Drone?

When you look up at the sky, you might see something buzzing. It has four spinning blades, a small body, and maybe a camera underneath. It is not a bird. It is not a plane. It is a drone.

A drone is an aircraft that flies without a pilot inside it. The technical name is UAV, which stands for Unmanned Aerial Vehicle. Drones can be as small as your hand or as large as a car. Some are controlled by a person on the ground using a remote control. Others are autonomous — they fly on their own using a computer program.

The most common type of drone is the quadcopter. "Quad" means four, and "copter" comes from helicopter. A quadcopter has four arms, each with a propeller on the end. A propeller is a spinning blade that pushes air downward. When air is pushed down, the drone is pushed up. This upward force is called thrust.

### Part 2: How Drones Fly

Flying is not easy. A drone has to fight against gravity to stay in the air. It does this with four forces: lift, weight, thrust, and drag. You already know about thrust — the force from the propellers. Lift is the upward force that keeps the drone up. Weight is the force of gravity pulling the drone down. Drag is the air resistance that slows the drone as it moves.

For a drone to take off, the thrust and lift must be greater than the weight. For a drone to hover — staying in one place in the air — the thrust must exactly match the weight. For a drone to go forward, the front propellers tilt slightly, and the thrust pushes it ahead.

The science behind all of this is called aerodynamics. Aerodynamics is the study of how air moves around objects. Engineers use aerodynamics to design drones that fly smoothly and efficiently. The shape of the drone's body, the size of the propellers, and even the material it is made from all affect how well it flies.

A drone's battery is also important. The battery stores the electrical energy that powers the motors. Most small drones can fly for fifteen to thirty minutes before the battery runs out. When the battery is low, the drone must land — or it will fall out of the sky!

### Part 3: Sensors and GPS — The Drone's Brain

A drone is more than just propellers and a battery. Modern drones are packed with technology that helps them fly safely and intelligently.

GPS is one of the most important tools on a drone. GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It uses signals from satellites in space to find the drone's exact location. With GPS, a drone can hold its position in the air even when the wind pushes it. It can fly to a specific point and come back on its own. If the remote control loses connection, the drone can use GPS to return to where it started.

Drones also have many sensors. An accelerometer measures how fast the drone is moving. A gyroscope helps the drone stay level and balanced. A barometer measures altitude — how high the drone is above the ground. Some drones even have cameras that can see obstacles and avoid crashing into them.

All of this information goes to the drone's flight controller — a small computer that makes decisions in a fraction of a second. If the drone tilts too far to the left, the controller adjusts the motors to level it out. If the drone is flying too high, the controller slows the motors down. This happens automatically, hundreds of times per second.

### Part 4: Drones in the Real World

Drones are not just toys. They are changing the way people work and live.

In farming, drones fly over fields and take pictures that help farmers check the health of their crops. In rescue operations, drones with thermal cameras can find missing people in forests or in the snow. In filmmaking, drones capture stunning aerial shots that used to require expensive helicopters. In medicine, drones deliver blood samples between hospitals in places where roads are poor.

Some companies are testing delivery drones that bring packages right to your door. In a few cities around the world, you can already order food and watch a drone bring it to you in minutes.

But drones also come with challenges. They can be dangerous if they fly in the wrong place — near airports, for example. That is why most countries have rules for drone pilots. In many places, drones cannot fly above 120 metres (400 feet). They cannot fly near airports or over crowds of people. And because drones have cameras, they raise questions about privacy. Who is allowed to film what, and from where?

The future of drones is exciting. Engineers are working on drones that are quieter, can fly longer, and can work together in swarms — like bees. Some future drones will take off vertically and then fly forward like a plane, combining the best of both worlds. Autonomous drones will become smarter, using artificial intelligence to make their own decisions in the air.

If you want to fly a drone, start small. A small, safe drone costs less than you might think. Learn the rules in your country. Practice in an open field. And always respect other people's privacy and safety.
