## WHILE-READING (Read the Text)

### Part 1: What Is a Robot?

When you hear the word "robot," you might think of a metal human from a movie. But real robots do not always look like people. Some look like arms. Some look like cars. Some are so small you cannot see them with your eyes.

A robot is a machine that can move and do tasks. Most robots are controlled by code — the same kind of code that runs computer programs. The code tells the robot what to do, step by step.

Every robot has three main parts. The first is the body, or frame. This is the structure that holds everything together. The second is the electronics — the circuits, motors, and actuators that make the robot move. The third is the brain, which is a small computer that runs the program.

Think about a robot arm in a car factory. The frame holds the arm in place. The motors move the arm left, right, up, and down. The actuators open and close the hand at the end of the arm. And the computer tells the arm exactly where to go and what to grab.

### Part 2: Sensors — How Robots See and Feel

A robot cannot do much if it does not know what is around it. This is why robots have sensors. A sensor is a part that lets a robot feel or see its environment, a bit like our eyes, ears, and skin.

There are many types of sensors. A distance sensor measures how far away an object is. This helps a robot avoid crashing into walls. A light sensor detects how bright or dark it is. Some robots use light sensors to follow a line on the floor. A touch sensor tells the robot if it has bumped into something. Some robots even have temperature sensors to feel heat and cold.

Think about a robot vacuum cleaner. It moves around your house by itself. How does it know not to fall down the stairs? It uses a distance sensor to detect the edge. How does it know when to turn? It uses a touch sensor to feel the wall. Without sensors, the robot would be blind.

Sensors send information to the robot's brain. The program reads this information and decides what to do. If the distance sensor says "wall ahead, 10 centimetres," the program might say "turn left." The robot turns left. This happens in a fraction of a second.

### Part 3: Motors and Actuators — How Robots Move

Sensors help a robot understand the world. But a robot also needs to act. This is where motors and actuators come in.

A motor makes a robot move. Motors turn wheels, spin propellers, and move joints. When you see a robot roll across the floor, motors are turning its wheels. When you see a robot arm reach out, motors are moving its joints.

An actuator is a part that makes a robot do a physical action. Think of an actuator as a robot's muscle. If a robot needs to grab a ball, an actuator closes its fingers around the ball. If a robot needs to push a button, an actuator moves its arm forward.

All of these parts are connected by circuits. A circuit is a path that electricity flows through. The battery sends power through the circuits to the motors and actuators. The computer controls when and how much power to send. This is how a robot moves smoothly and precisely.

Engineering is the science behind all of this. Engineers design the frame, choose the right motors, connect the circuits, and write the program. Building a robot is an engineering challenge. Every part must work together perfectly.

### Part 4: Building Your Own Robot

You do not need to work in a big factory to build a robot. Many 12-year-olds build robots at school or at home.

There are many robot kits designed for beginners. LEGO makes robotics kits where you build a robot from blocks and then program it using a visual language. You drag and drop code blocks to make the robot move, turn, and respond to sensors. The Makeblock mBot is another popular kit. It comes already built — you just program it and watch it go.

For students who want to go further, there are kits that let you design, build, and modify your own robot. You can build a robot that navigates a maze, picks up objects, or even competes in a robot competition.

The process is simple but exciting. First, you build a prototype — the first version of your robot. Then you test it. Maybe it does not work the first time. Maybe the sensor is in the wrong place, or the motor is too slow. You make changes and test again. This is how all engineers work.

Some robots today are autonomous. This means they can work on their own without a human controlling them. A self-driving car is an autonomous robot. A robot vacuum is autonomous. In the future, autonomous robots will do even more — from helping in hospitals to exploring other planets.

Robotics teaches problem-solving, teamwork, and creativity. Whether you are building a LEGO robot or dreaming of a Mars rover, robotics is one of the most exciting fields in the world today.
