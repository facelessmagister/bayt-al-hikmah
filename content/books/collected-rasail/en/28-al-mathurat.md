# Epistle 6 (continued): Al-Maʾthūrāt — The Small Daily Litany and the Qur'anic Litany

## The Small Daily Litany (*al-Waẓīfah al-Ṣughrā*)

If a brother finds himself pressed for time, or weary in spirit, or if his brothers with whom he recites the litany are likewise constrained, let him shorten it as follows:

Let him recite the *istighnā*, *al-Fātiḥah*, *Āyat al-Kursī*, the last verses of *al-Baqarah*, and *Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ* — each of them three times — then the *taḥmīd* (praising Allah), the *tasbīḥ* (glorifying Allah), and the concluding supplication.

---

> Glory be to your Lord, the Lord of Might, above what they describe, and peace be upon the messengers, and praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.

Imām al-Nawawī reports in *al-Adhkār*, on the authority of ʿAlī, may Allah ennoble his countenance, in *Ṣilat al-Awliyāʾ*: "Whoever wishes to be weighed with the fullest measure, let him say at the end of his night, when he rises: *Glory be to your Lord, the Lord of Might, above what they describe, and peace be upon the messengers, and praise be to Allah, Lord of the Worlds.*"

Then after that, *Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ* and the two *muʿawwidhatayn* — each three times.

Then the supplication leading to the final *istighfār*:

> I seek forgiveness from Allah, there is no deity except He...

Then recite the *istighfār* immediately, followed by *Subḥānaka Allāhumma wa bi-ḥamdika...* to the end of the litany.

---

## The Qur'anic Litany (*al-Wird al-Qurʾānī*)

### The Excellence of the Qur'an

The Noble Qur'an is the comprehensive constitution of the rulings of Islam and the spring from which goodness and wisdom pour forth upon the believing hearts. It is the best means by which the worshippers draw near to Allah, Blessed and Exalted, through its recitation.

On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, who said:

> "This Qur'an is the banquet of Allah; so take as much of it as you can. For this Qur'an is the clear light, the healing remedy, and a protection and reward for the one who clings to it, and a humiliation and perdition for the one who abandons it." Narrated by Ibn Mājah.

> "Indeed, this Qur'an is the banquet of Allah" — that is, His invitation; the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, likened the Qur'an to a table laid by Allah, containing every good and benefit.

> "Indeed, this Qur'an is the rope of Allah" — that is, His firm guidance.

> "It is the clear light" — meaning, it illuminates the paths of guidance and dispels the darkness of error.

> "It is the healing remedy" — meaning, it heals the diseases of the hearts and the doubts of the minds.

> "And it will not wear out" — al-Nawawī said: with *kasr* on the *bāʾ* or *fatḥ* is permissible, and the *hāʾ* is unvowelled. It is also read with *kasr* on the *lām*: *lā yubli* and *yublā*, meaning "it does not lose its majesty and sweetness from frequent recitation and reading."

> "The angels of al-Safrah" — meaning, the noble recording angels. "And those who recite it with difficulty, even if it is hard upon them, shall have a double reward." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

---

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, enjoined upon the people the whole Qur'an as an obligation, and he urged the one who finds it heavy to complete a minimum portion. And no Muslim who is unable to read it should be without some connection with the Book of Allah, Blessed and Exalted.

On the authority of ʿAbdullah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Take hold of the Qur'an; for by it comes an illuminated light and a shining lamp." Narrated by al-Bukhārī.

On the authority of Abū Hurayra, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, sent an expedition and appointed a man from the Ansār as their commander. He used to lead them in prayer and would sometimes conclude the recitation with *Sūrat al-Baqarah*. One of the men said to him: "Why do you always end with *al-Baqarah*?" He replied: "Because of the verse, *There is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence* (2:255)." He (the man) said: "Do you always recite *al-Baqarah* with them?" He said: "Yes." He said: "Go, for you have been appointed over them because of it." Narrated by Muslim.

---

Our righteous predecessors, may Allah be pleased with them, knew the worth of the Qur'an. They made it the source of their guidance, the constitution of their rulings, the foundation of their principles, the litany of their worship. They opened their hearts to it, turned their attention toward it, and its sublime meanings permeated their souls, so Allah established for them glory in this world.

> "So recite what is easy of it" — that is, He commands them to recite what is within their ability.

The complete *ḥadīth* is: A man from the nobles said: "O Messenger of Allah, what prevents me from learning *al-Baqarah* is the fear that I will not be able to stand with it (in prayer)." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Learn the Qur'an and recite it. The likeness of the Qur'an for the one who learns it, recites it, and stands with it is like a bag of musk whose fragrance fills every place; and the likeness of the one who learns it and then sleeps, while it is in his bag, is like a bag of musk that has no fragrance."

His saying, peace and blessings be upon him, "*wa kīʿ mīk*" means tied with a cord or a strap; it is the rope with which a water-skin is bound.

---

On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The rewards of my community were displayed before me: even the removal of filth by a man from the roads. And the sins of my community were displayed before me, and I did not see a sin greater than a chapter of the Qur'an or a verse that was given to one of them and then forgotten." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī.

Therefore, the Muslim Brothers should make the Book of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, the first of their litanies, and it is part of their practice that every one of them should arrange upon himself a *ḥizb* of the Noble Qur'an each day.

---

### On the Quantity of the Litany

The circumstances of the brothers and their conditions differ; therefore we do not fix a set amount for the litany, but leave that to the situation of each person and his capacity. The preferred view, as al-Nawawī stated in *al-Tibyān*, is that this varies according to individuals. Whoever is able to reflect upon subtle meanings and knowledge should limit himself to what he can absorb from what he reads; likewise, whoever is occupied with spreading knowledge or other important religious and general Muslim concerns should limit himself to an amount that does not cause him to neglect what is required of him. And whoever is not one of these two categories should do what he is able, without reaching the point of boredom and aversion.

> Let no day pass upon him in which he does not read anything from the Book of Allah, the Exalted.

We shall now mention the ways in which our righteous predecessors divided the Qur'anic litany, may Allah be pleased with them.

---

### Divisions of the Qur'anic Litany

**First division:** The shortest *khatmah* is in three days. They disliked a person completing it in less than three days, and also in more than forty days. They said that completing it in less than three days is hastiness that does not permit proper understanding and reflection, and completing it in more than forty days is wasteful neglect of recitation.

On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever reads the Qur'an in less than three days does not understand it." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, and Ibn Mājah. Al-Tirmidhī said: *ḥadīth ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*.

> "Whoever reads the Qur'an in less than three days..." — that is, he does not grasp its apparent meanings. As for its subtle meanings, they are not grasped by the minds of created beings. The intent is negation of understanding, not negation of reward.

**Second division:** The middle limit is to complete a *khatmah* every week, if one is able to do that. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, instructed ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr to complete it every week. Likewise was the practice of some of the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them: ʿUthmān, Zayd ibn Thābit, Ibn Masʿūd, and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. ʿUthmān, may Allah be pleased with him, used to begin his Friday night with *al-Baqarah* up to *al-Māʾidah*; Saturday night with *al-Anʿām* up to *Yūsuf*; Sunday night with *Yūsuf* up to *Maryam*; Monday night with *Ṭā Hā* up to the story of Mūsā and Pharaoh, that is, *al-Qaṣaṣ*; Tuesday night with *al-ʿAnkabūt* up to *Ṣād*; Wednesday night with *al-Tawr* up to *al-Raḥmān*; and Thursday night to complete the *khatmah*. Ibn Masʿūd, may Allah be pleased with him, used to divide it differently.

The complete *ḥadīth*: "I came to him and he said to me: 'Did I not tell you that you fast perpetually and recite the Qur'an every night?' I said: 'Yes, O Prophet of Allah, and I only intended thereby to seek reward.' He said: 'Then fast three days out of every month.' I said: 'O Prophet of Allah, I am able to do more than that.' He said: 'Then fast one day and break one day — the fast of Dāwūd, peace be upon him, for he was the most devoted of people.' I said: 'O Prophet of Allah, what is the fast of Dāwūd?' He said: 'He used to fast one day and break one day, and recite the Qur'an every month.' I said: 'O Prophet of Allah, I can do better than that.' He said: 'Then recite it every twenty nights.' I said: 'O Prophet of Allah, I can do better than that.' He said: 'Then recite it every ten nights.' I said: 'O Prophet of Allah, I can do better than that.' He said: 'Then recite it every seven nights, and do not exceed that, for your wife has a right upon you, your guest has a right upon you, and your body has a right upon you.' I persisted, and he persisted with me, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'You do not know; perhaps you will live long.' So I returned to what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said. When I grew old, I wished I had accepted the concession of the Prophet of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

His saying, peace and blessings be upon him, "your guest has a right upon you" means your visitor. His saying, "every seven nights and do not exceed that" — al-Nawawī said: This is guidance toward moderation in worship and toward gradual recitation of the Qur'an.

**Third division:** Completion according to the number of *sūrahs* — to complete it every week. Many reports have come concerning division over the week.

> This division is not fixed; it is only a means of following their recitation. If one is a person of recitation, let him rely upon listening or upon memorising some *sūrahs* by reciting them whenever opportunity permits.

---

### *Sūrahs* It Is Recommended to Recite Frequently

Among the Qur'anic litanies of the brothers is regular recitation of these *sūrahs* every day: *Yāsīn*, *al-Dukhān*, *al-Wāqiʿah*, and *Sūrat al-Mulk*. This is especially emphasised on Friday and Friday night. To these may be added *Sūrat al-Kahf* and *Āl ʿImrān*. Reports have come concerning them.

> *Sūrat al-Mulk* is the preventer and the saver from the punishment of the grave. Narrated by al-Ṭabarānī and al-Ḥākim, who authenticated it.

> Whoever recites *Sūrat al-Mulk* every night has done much; and whoever recites it in the day has done much. Narrated by al-Nasāʾī.

On the authority of Abū Hurayra, may Allah be pleased with him: "Whoever recites it by night will be prevented from the punishment of the grave." Narrated by al-Tirmidhī and al-Aṣbahānī.

On the authority of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever recites *Sūrat al-Kahf* on Friday night will have light radiating from his feet to the heaven on the Day of Gathering." Narrated by al-Nasāʾī and al-Bayhaqī, *muʿallaq*.

On the authority of Ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever recites the *sūrah* in which the family of ʿImrān is mentioned — meaning *Āl ʿImrān* — Allah and His angels send blessings upon him until the sun sets." Narrated by al-Ṭabarānī in *al-Awsaṭ* and *al-Kabīr*.

Many reports, both *marfūʿ* and *mawqūf*, have also come on the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd, may Allah be pleased with him, concerning *Sūrat al-Wāqiʿah* and *Yāsīn*. Therefore it is recommended for the Muslim not to deprive himself of the merit of reciting this *sūrah* once every day, and at night is even better. And on Friday there is no harm in reciting it once at night and once in the day. The best time for *Āl ʿImrān* is from ʿAṣr to Maghrib, for that is the hour of response; so let him be occupied with the best of remembrance, which is recitation of the Qur'an.

---

### Etiquettes of Recitation

We mentioned in the introduction something of the etiquettes of remembrance. We add here that among the etiquettes of recitation is to strive with all one's effort in reflection and contemplation, for that is its primary purpose. Allah, Blessed and Exalted, said:

> [This is] a blessed Book which We have revealed to you, [O Muḥammad], that they might reflect upon its verses and that those of understanding would be reminded. [Qur'an, Sād 38:29]

And how should he not reflect, when it is the speech of the Lord of the Worlds, the Mighty?

## Recitation Etiquettes

Among the etiquettes of recitation is to observe the rules of *tajwīd*: articulating the letters from their correct points of pronunciation (*makhārij*), applying the necessary characteristics, prolonging the lengthened letters, observing *ghunnah* where it is due, giving *tafkhīm* to the emphatic letters and *tarqīq* to the refined ones.

On the authority of Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Indeed, this Qur'an has come down gloomy (*kaʿāmid*), so when you recite it, weep; if you do not weep, then strive to weep. And the one who is not concerned with the Qur'an is not one of us." Narrated by Ibn Mājah.

What is meant by weeping here is sorrow and the manifestation of humility along with beautifying the recitation. This is supported by the *ḥadīth* of Jābir, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Indeed, among the people with the best voices in reciting the Qur'an is the one whom, when you hear him recite, you perceive that he fears Allah." Narrated by Ibn Mājah.

Ibn Kathīr said: The purpose sought by the Law is to beautify the outward recitation of the Qur'an, to understand it, to humble oneself, to submit, and to be led to obedience. As for the melodies and modern tunes based on *awrāq* and *arḍāʿ* and musical rhythms, the Qur'an is far exalted above these; it is monstrous to perform it in the manner of a musical school, and the Sunnah has rebuked such things.

## The Gathering of Listening

Among the litanies of the Muslim Brothers is the gathering to listen to the Qur'an, in honour of the Book of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, and in appreciation of beautiful recitation. The reciter in this gathering should recite in a manner that observes the preceding etiquettes. The listeners should be silent, reflect upon the meanings, and be in the utmost humility, veneration, and awe for the Book of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, recalling the noble verse:

> And when the Qur'an is recited, then listen to it and pay attention that you may receive mercy. [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:204]

The Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to listen to the Qur'an until birds would alight upon their heads. The righteous elders of Makkah, when they wished to be reminded, would gather around al-Shāfiʿī, may Allah be pleased with him, because of his beautiful recitation; he would recite to them, and they would listen until the onlookers thought they were weeping from the intensity of the listening:

> And when they hear what has been revealed to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflowing with tears because of what they have recognized of the truth. [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:83]

It is recommended, for benefit, that those present in this gathering summarise for themselves the main objectives of the verses recited.

## Memorisation

It is also recommended for the Muslim Brother — and it is one of the Qur'anic litanies — to exert himself in memorising whatever he can of the Noble Qur'an. Let him assign himself every day an *āyah* or *āyāt* according to his capacity, firmly memorising them. By this gradual method he can memorise much of the Book of Allah, Blessed and Exalted.

On the authority of ʿUthmān, may Allah be pleased with him: "The best of you is the one who learns the Qur'an and teaches it." Narrated by al-Bukhārī.

On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "It is more beloved to me that I return with two camel-ropes (*qīrān*) from an expedition than that I come back with nothing but the spoils." Then he said: "Is there not one of you who goes in the morning to the mosque or recites two *āyāt* from the Book of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, which are better for him than two she-camels? And three *āyāt* are better for him than three; and four are better for him than four, and their equal in number from camels." Narrated by Muslim and Abū Dāwūd.

So strive, O my brother, to win this merit, and ask Allah to make us and you among the people of the Qur'an, for by that we become among the people of Allah and His chosen ones. Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best Disposer of affairs.

## Supplications of Day and Night

### First: Supplication upon Waking

1. On the authority of Ḥudhayfa and another from the Ansār, may Allah be pleased with them, who said: When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, woke up, he would say: "All praise is due to Allah who gave us life after causing us to die, and to Him is the resurrection."

2. On the authority of ʿĀʾisha, may Allah be pleased with her, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "When one of you wakes up, let him say: 'Praise be to Allah who has restored my soul to me, given me health in my body, and permitted me to remember Him.'" Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

3. On the authority of ʿĀʾisha, may Allah be pleased with her, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "No servant says, when his soul is returned to him, 'There is no deity except Allah alone, with no partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise, and He is over all things competent,' except that his sins are forgiven, even if they are like the foam of the sea." Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

4. On the authority of Abū Hurayra, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "When a person wakes at night and says: 'There is no god but Allah, the One, Who has no partner; to Him belongs sovereignty and to Him belongs praise, and He is over all things competent,' then says: 'Praise be to Allah; glory be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest; there is no power nor strength except with Allah,' then says: 'O Allah, forgive me,' or supplicates, his prayer is answered. If he performs *wuḍūʾ* and prays, his prayer is accepted." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

5. On the authority of ʿUbadah ibn al-Ṣāmit, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Whoever wakes in the morning and says: 'O Allah, I ask You for health in this world and in the Hereafter,' Allah, the Exalted, will grant him health in this world and in the Hereafter." Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

6. On the authority of ʿAlī, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to say when he woke: "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from leprosy, insanity, and all ugly diseases." Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

### Second: Supplication for Putting on and Taking off Clothes

1. On the authority of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to say when he put on a new garment — a shirt, a cloak, or a turban: "O Allah, I ask You for its good and the good of what it was made for, and I seek refuge in You from its evil and the evil of what it was made for." Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

2. On the authority of Muʿādh ibn Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever puts on a new garment and says: 'O Allah, praise be to You, You have clothed me; I ask You for its good and the good for which it was made, and I seek refuge in You from its evil and the evil for which it was made,' Allah will protect him so long as he remains in it." Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

3. On the authority of ʿAtāʾ ibn Yasār, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The nakedness of the jinn lies between their eyes, and the nakedness of the sons of Adam lies between the legs. When one of you enters a place of concealment, let him say: 'In the name of Allah; there is no deity except Allah.'" Narrated by Ibn al-Sunnī.

### Third: Supplication for Leaving and Entering the House

1. On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever says, upon leaving his house: 'In the name of Allah, I put my trust in Allah; there is no power nor strength except with Allah,' it will be said to him: 'You have been sufficed, protected, and guided,' and the devils will move away from him." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, and others. Al-Tirmidhī said: *ḥadīth* *ḥasan* *ṣaḥīḥ*.

2. On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: "When a man enters his house, let him say: 'O Allah, I ask You for the best entrance and the best exit. In the name of Allah we enter, and in the name of Allah we leave, and upon our Lord we rely.'" Narrated by Abū Dāwūd.

### Fourth: Supplication for Walking to the Mosque, Entering it, and Leaving it

1. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to say when he went to the mosque: "O Allah, place light in my heart, light in my tongue, light in my hearing, light in my sight, light behind me, light before me, light above me, light beneath me, light on my right, light on my left, and light greater than that." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

2. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with them, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that when he entered the mosque he said: "I seek refuge in Allah the Mighty, in His Noble Face, and in His Ancient Dominion, from the accursed Satan." When he said that, Satan said: "He is protected from me for the whole day." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd and al-Nasāʾī.

3. On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, entered the mosque, he said: "In the name of Allah; prayers and peace be upon the Messenger of Allah. O Allah, open for me the doors of Your mercy." And when he left, he said: "In the name of Allah; prayers and peace be upon the Messenger of Allah. O Allah, I ask You of Your bounty." Narrated by Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, and al-Nasāʾī.

### Fifth: Supplication for Relieving Oneself and Intimacy

1. On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to say when entering the place of answering the call of nature: "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from filth and the devils of the privy." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

2. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with them, who said: When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, left the privy, he said: "I seek Your forgiveness." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd.

3. On the authority of ʿĀʾisha, may Allah be pleased with her, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when he exited the privy, said: "Your forgiveness." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd.

4. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "When one of you goes to his wife, let him say: 'In the name of Allah, O Allah, keep Satan away from us and from what You bestow upon us,' then if a child is decreed for them, Satan will never harm him." Narrated by al-Bukhārī.

### Sixth: Supplication for Ablution, Bathing, and the Call to Prayer

1. On the authority of Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, while he was performing *wuḍūʾ*. When he finished, I took the water he had used and poured it over myself. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Who does that?" I said: "I do." He said: "May Allah have mercy on you." Then he said: "O Allah, forgive my sins and widen my abode, and bless me in my sustenance." Narrated by al-Tirmidhī, who said it is *ḥasan gharīb*.

2. On the authority of ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever performs *wuḍūʾ* as it is prescribed for him and then says: 'I bear witness that there is no deity except Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muḥammad is His servant and His Messenger,' the eight gates of Paradise are opened for him; he may enter through whichever he wishes." Narrated by Muslim and al-Nasāʾī.

3. On the authority of Jābir ibn ʿAbdullah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Whoever says, when he hears the call to prayer: 'O Allah, Lord of this perfect call and established prayer, grant Muḥammad the *wasīla* and the excellence, and raise him to the *maqām maḥmūd* that You have promised him,' his intercession becomes incumbent for him on the Day of Resurrection." Narrated by al-Bukhārī.

### Seventh: Supplication for Eating and Drinking

1. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with them, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that he used to say when food was brought near him: "O Allah, bless it for us and feed us better than it." Narrated by Ibn Mājah.

2. On the authority of ʿĀʾisha, may Allah be pleased with her, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "When one of you eats, let him mention the name of Allah; if he forgets at the beginning, let him say: 'In the name of Allah at the beginning and at the end.'" Narrated by Abū Dāwūd and al-Tirmidhī.

3. On the authority of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that when he finished eating he said: "Praise be to Allah who fed us, gave us drink, and made us Muslims." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, and al-Nasāʾī.

4. On the authority of Muʿādh ibn Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever eats food and says: 'O Allah, bless us in it and give us better than it,' Allah will be pleased with him and forgive him." Narrated by al-Tirmidhī, who said: *ḥadīth ḥasan*.

5. On the authority of Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came to Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ when he was eating *tharīd* and said: "O Saʿd, eat it, for it quickens the recovery of the sick and is the best food for the bereaved." Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Eat together and mention the name of Allah over your food; it will be blessed for you." Narrated by Abū Dāwūd.

### Eighth: Supplication for Night Waking, Insomnia, and Dreams

1. On the authority of ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them, who said: When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, stood at night, he said: "O Allah, to You belongs praise. You are the Light of the heavens and the earth and whatever is in them. To You belongs praise; You are the Keeper of the heavens and the earth and whatever is in them. To You belongs praise; You are the True, and Your promise is true, and Your meeting is true, and Your word is true, and Paradise is true, and the Fire is true, and the Hour is true. O Allah, I submit to You, and in You I believe, and upon You I rely, and to You I repent, and with You I contend, and to You I judge. Forgive me what I have sent forward and what I have kept back, what I have concealed and what I have done openly, what I have exceeded in, and what You know better than I. You are the First and the Last; there is no deity except You, and there is no power nor strength except with You." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

2. On the authority of Abū Hurayra, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, concerning dreams: "When one of you sees a good dream, let him praise Allah for it, and let him inform whomever he loves of it. But if he sees something he dislikes, let him spit on his left side three times and seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan and from the evil of what he saw, and it will not harm him." Narrated by Muslim.

3. On the authority of Abū Qatādah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Good dreams are from Allah, and bad dreams are from Satan. When one of you sees something he dislikes, let him spit on his left side three times and seek refuge in Allah from its evil, and it will not harm him." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

4. On the authority of ʿUthmān ibn Abī al-ʿĀṣ, may Allah be pleased with him, that he complained to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, about a pain he felt in his body since he had become Muslim. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "Place your hand on the place of the pain and say: 'In the name of Allah,' three times, and say seven times: 'I seek refuge in Allah and in His Power from the evil of what I find and of what I fear.'" Narrated by Muslim.

5. On the authority of Khālid ibn al-Walīd, may Allah be pleased with him, that he complained to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, about insomnia. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "Shall I not teach you words that, if you say them, Allah will drive away what grieves you and what comes from the people?" He said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." He said: "Say: 'O Allah, Lord of the heavens and what they cover, Lord of the earths and what they carry, Lord of the devils and what they mislead, be for me a protection against the evil of Your creatures, against the evil that approaches by night and by day, against every visitor of the night except one that brings good, O Merciful.'" Narrated by Abū Dāwūd and al-Ṭabarānī in *al-Muʿjam al-Awsaṭ*.
