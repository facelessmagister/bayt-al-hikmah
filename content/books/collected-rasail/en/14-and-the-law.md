# The Muslim Brothers and the Law

I have already said that the constitution is one thing and the law is another, and I have explained the position of the Muslim Brothers toward the Constitution. I now explain to you their position toward the law.

Islam did not come empty of laws; rather, it clarified many of the principles of legislation and details of rulings, whether civil, criminal, commercial, or international. The Qur'an and *ḥadīth* are full of these meanings, and the books of the jurists are abundantly rich in all these aspects. Foreigners themselves have acknowledged this reality and affirmed it before the International Court at the League of Nations, before jurists from the whole world.

It is neither reasonable nor conceivable that the law in a Muslim Ummah should contradict the teachings of its religion, the rulings of its Qur'an and Sunnah, and collide with everything that has come from Allah and His Messenger. Allah warned His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, of that, saying:

> "And judge, [O Muḥammad], between them by what Allah has revealed and do not follow their inclinations and beware of them, lest they tempt you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you. And if they turn away — then know that Allah only intends to afflict them because of some of their sins. And indeed, many among the people are defiantly disobedient. Then is it the judgement of [the time of] ignorance they desire? And who is better than Allah in judgement for a people who are certain [in faith]?" [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:49-50]

That is after His saying:

> "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed — then it is those who are the disbelievers, the wrongdoers, the defiantly disobedient." [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:44-47]

What, then, is the position of a Muslim who believes in Allah and His words when he reads these verses and others of *ḥadīth* and rulings, and then sees himself ruled by laws that collide with them? If he demands reform, he is told that foreigners will not accept or agree to this — and then, after this restriction and distress, it is said that the Egyptians are independent and free after they have enjoyed freedom of religion, which is the most sacred of freedoms!

These man-made laws, as they collide with the religion and its texts, also collide with the man-made constitution itself, which states that the religion of the state is Islam. How can these two be reconciled, O people of understanding?

If Allah and His Messenger have forbidden fornication, forbidden interest, forbidden [unjust] bloodshed, and fought wine; and if the law comes to protect fornication and wine, obliges interest, permits wine, and regulates [matters] — how can the Muslim's position be? Does he obey Allah and His Messenger and disobey the government and its law, and that is better and more lasting? Or does he disobey Allah and His Messenger and obey the government, and so is wretched in the Hereafter and the present? I want the answer to this from His Excellency the Prime Minister and His Excellency the Minister of Justice, and from our virtuous, learned scholars.

As for the Muslim Brothers, they will never agree to this law, nor will they ever accept it. They will work in every way to replace it with the just, virtuous Islamic *sharīʿah* in the fields of law. I am not here to reply to the objections that may be raised in this regard or to the obstacles that may be encountered in its path. We are here to declare our position, which we have announced and shall work for, overcoming every obstacle in its path, clarifying every doubt, so that there may be no *fitnah* and so that the religion may be entirely for Allah.

The Muslim Brothers have already presented to His Excellency the Minister of Justice a detailed memorandum on this subject, and they have warned the government in its newspaper against putting people in this predicament. For creed is the reality of existence; it will undoubtedly return and overcome, and that will not be their last effort:

> "But Allah will perfect His light, although the disbelievers dislike it." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:32]
