# Epistle 7: Establishing the Islamic Creedal Pillars (*Iqāmat al-Maqāʾid al-Islāmiyyah*)

## Preliminaries

### 1. Defining the Station

The articles of creed (*al-ʿaqāʾid*) are the matters that must be firmly rooted in your heart, that your soul may be reassured by them, and that become certainty in you—certainty that no doubt disturbs and no suspicion mingles with.

### 2. The Levels of Conviction

People are of many categories in the strength and weakness of their creed, according to the clarity of the proofs and how deeply they are embedded in the souls of every people. To clarify this station for you, consider the following parable:

If a man is informed of the existence of a country—such as China, for example—by another man who is not known for lying, he will believe in the existence of that country. If, in addition to this, he is informed by a number of men, his confidence increases; though this does not prevent him from doubting his belief if doubts arise. If he sees a photograph of it, his conviction increases, and the proof becomes overwhelming. If he travels and sees its landmarks and signs, his certainty increases. If he descends into it and sees it with his own eyes, no room for doubt remains; the creed becomes firmly rooted in his soul to the point that it would be impossible for him to turn away from it even if all the people called him to the contrary. If he then walks its roads and streets and studies its conditions and circumstances, his experience and knowledge increase, becoming an additional support for his belief.

If you have understood this, know that in Islam people are likewise divided regarding religious convictions. Among them are those who received them merely by tradition and believed them as custom; this does not protect them from wavering if doubts present themselves. Among them are those who reflected and so their faith was strengthened and their certainty confirmed. Among them are those who persevered in contemplation, deepened their thought, sought help through obedience to Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—fulfilled His command, and perfected their worship; then the lamps of gnosis shone in their hearts, and they saw the traces of divine help in everything that strengthened their certainty. Allah the Exalted says:

> "And as for those who are guided, He increases them in guidance and gives them their righteousness." [Qur'an, Muḥammad 47:17]

So seek help through the obedience of your Lord in knowing the roots of your religion, that you may reach the ranks of the people of certainty and ascend the degrees of knowledge.

### 3. The Intellect as the Basis of Creed

The basis of the Islamic creeds, as of all Sharīʿah rulings, is the Book of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—and the Sunnah of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him.

And you should know, along with this, that every one of these convictions has its sound intellect and its sound witness. For this reason Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—honored the intellect by addressing it, made it the basis of moral responsibility, and called it to research, observation, and reflection. Allah—the Exalted—says:

> "Say, 'Observe what is in the heavens and earth.'" [Qur'an, Yūnus 10:101]

And Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—says:

> "Have they not looked at the heaven above them—how We structured it and adorned it and [how] it has no rifts? And the earth—We spread it out and cast therein firmly set mountains and made grow therein [something] of every beautiful kind, as insight and remembrance for every servant who turns [to Allah]. And We have sent down blessed rain from the sky and made grow thereby gardens and grain of the harvest, and lofty palm trees having fruit arranged in layers." [Qur'an, Qāf 50:6-10]

And Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—says:

> "And how many a sign within the heavens and earth do they pass over while they, from it, are turning away." [Qur'an, Yūsuf 12:105]

This is a call to evidence and proof until falsehood becomes clearly false, respecting the proper limits of discourse and showing the honor of the true method. It is reported that Bilāl came to call the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to the dawn prayer and found him weeping. He asked him the reason for his weeping. The Prophet said: "O Bilāl! What makes you weep while you call to prayer, when Allah has revealed this night: *Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the night and the day are signs for those of understanding…*" Then he said: "Woe to him who recites it and does not reflect upon it!" [Ibn Abī al-Dunyā, *al-Tafakkur*]

From this you learn that Islam has not placed a barrier before ideas nor imprisoned minds; rather, it has guided them to set proper bounds, acquainted them with the limits of their causes, and called them to increase in knowledge. Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—says:

> "And say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'" [Qur'an, Ṭāhā 20:114]

And He says:

> "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the night and the day are signs for those of understanding." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:190]

Al-Qurṭubī said: Allah concluded this Sūrah with a call to look and infer from His signs, for they indicate a Living, Self-Sustaining, All-Powerful, Holy, Peaceful, Independent Lord; so they must lead the one who reflects to tawḥīd, not to blind imitation. [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:190-191]

## Establishing the Islamic Creedal Pillars

The Islamic creeds are divided into four main categories, under each of which are many branches.

**The First Category: Divinity (*al-Ilāhiyyāt*)**. It investigates everything related to Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—in terms of His attributes, names, and acts. Included in it is whatever the believer must hold regarding servitude to his Lord.

**The Second Category: Prophethood (*al-Nubuwwāt*)**. It investigates everything related to the Prophets, peace and blessings be upon them, in terms of their qualities, their inerrancy, their messages, and the necessity of believing in them. Included in it is whatever relates to the righteous allies (*awliyāʾ*) of Allah, to miracles (*muʿjizāt*) and saintly wonders (*karāmāt*), and to the revealed books.

**The Third Category: Spiritual Beings (*al-Rūḥāniyyāt*)**. It investigates what relates to the unseen realm apart from Allah—such as the angels, peace be upon them, the jinn, and the spirit.

**The Fourth Category: Revealed Eschatological Matters (*al-Samʿiyyāt*)**. It investigates what relates to the Hereafter: the conditions of the grave, the signs of the Hour, resurrection, the Standing, the Reckoning, and recompense.

### Part One: Divinity

#### His Essence, Exalted is He

Know, O my brother—may Allah guide us and you to right guidance—that the essence of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—is greater than human words can encompass or human thoughts can perceive; for however far the intellect may reach in knowledge and comprehension, it remains a created power of limited capacity. We shall devote to you, Allah willing, a special discussion from which you will learn the extent of the human heart's inability. I remind you now that our intellects, from the greatest to the smallest, benefit from many things without fully comprehending their realities. Electricity, magnetism, and the like are powers we use and benefit from, yet we know nothing of their true essence; and the greatest scholar of our time cannot tell you anything decisive about them. To comprehend the essences of things and their realities is one thing, while it suffices us to know of their properties what brings us benefit.

If this is our condition with things we deal with, what then of the essence of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified? Certain people who spoke about it only caused darkness, confusion, and disorder for themselves, because they spoke of what cannot be delimited or truly known. For this reason the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade reflection upon the essence of Allah and commanded reflection upon His creation.

ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, reported that some people reflected upon Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> "Reflect upon the creation of Allah, and do not reflect upon Allah, for you will never be able to comprehend Him." [al-Ṭabarānī, *al-Muʿjam al-Awsaṭ*]

This is not a restriction upon the freedom of thought, nor rigidity in research, nor constriction of the intellect; rather, it is a protection for it from falling into the depths of misguidance and a keeping it away from subjects for which the means of investigation are not available and which would occupy its strength with matters too great for it to remedy. This is the method of the righteous servants of Allah who know the greatness of His essence and the majesty of His power.

It was said about Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—that He said: "He is Allah, the One, the Known before limitation and before description." And Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh was asked: "Tell us about Allah, the Mighty and Majestic." He said: "The One." They asked: "How is He?" He said: "A King, All-Powerful." They asked: "But who is He?" He said: "He is in the watchtower." The questioner said: "I am not asking you about that." He replied: "Anything other than that is a description of the created; as for His attribute, I have not informed you of it."

So confine yourself, in realizing the greatness of your Lord, to reflecting on His creation and beholding the evidences of His attributes.

#### The Names of Allah, Exalted is He

The Lord—the Exalted and Majestic—has made Himself known to His creation by names and attributes befitting His majesty. It is incumbent upon the believer to preserve them with reverence, to recite them in remembrance, and to act in accordance with their greatness. Here is the authentic hadith that has come down from Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, who is the tongue of revelation and the lamp of prophethood:

> "Allah has ninety-nine names: whoever preserves them will enter Paradise, and He is Odd and loves the odd." [al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

In a narration of al-Bukhārī: "whoever counts them." And al-Tirmidhī narrated it and added: "He is Allah, than whom there is no deity, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful."

They are: Allah, al-Raḥmān, al-Raḥīm, al-Malik, al-Quddūs, al-Salām, al-Muʾmin, al-Muhaymin, al-ʿAzīz, al-Jabbār, al-Mutakabbir, al-Khāliq, al-Bāriʾ, al-Muṣawwir, al-Ghaffār, al-Qahhār, al-Wahhāb, al-Razzāq, al-Fattāḥ, al-ʿAlīm, al-Qābiḍ, al-Bāsiṭ, al-Khāfiḍ, al-Rāfiʿ, al-Muʿizz, al-Mudhill, al-Samīʿ, al-Baṣīr, al-Ḥakam, al-ʿAdl, al-Laṭīf, al-Khabīr, al-Ḥalīm, al-ʿAẓīm, al-Ghafūr, al-Shakūr, al-ʿAliyy, al-Kabīr, al-Ḥafīẓ, al-Muqīt, al-Ḥasīb, al-Jalīl, al-Karīm, al-Raqīb, al-Mujīb, al-Wāsiʿ, al-Ḥakīm, al-Wadūd, al-Majīd, al-Bāʿith, al-Shahīd, al-Ḥaqq, al-Wakīl, al-Qawiyy, al-Matīn, al-Waliyy, al-Ḥamīd, al-Muḥṣī, al-Mubdiʾ, al-Muʿīd, al-Ḥayy, al-Mumīt, al-Muḥyī, al-Qayyūm, al-Wājid, al-Mājid, al-Wāḥid, al-Ṣamad, al-Qādir, al-Muqtadir, al-Muqaddim, al-Muʾakhkhir, al-Awwal, al-Ākhir, al-Ẓāhir, al-Bāṭin, al-Wālī, al-Mutaʿālī, al-Barr, al-Tawwāb, al-Muntaqim, al-ʿAfuww, al-Raʾūf, Mālik al-Mulk, Dhū al-Jalāl wa al-Ikrām, al-Muqsiṭ, al-Jāmiʿ, al-Ghaniyy, al-Mughnī, al-Māniʿ, al-Ḍārr, al-Nāfiʿ, al-Nūr, al-Hādī, al-Badīʿ, al-Bāqī, al-Wārith, al-Rashīd, and al-Ṣabūr.

Some of their meanings: *al-Quddūs* is the One purified of defects. *al-Muʾmin* is the One who gives security, or the One free of defects. *al-Muhaymin* is the One who is truthful and whose promise and threat are true. *al-Muʿizz* is the One who gives honor. *al-Mutakabbir* is the One who is above the attributes of created things, the One alone in the attributes of His greatness. *al-Bāriʾ* is the Creator, more manifest in creating beings with spirits. It is said: *Bāriʾ al-samawāt wa al-khāliq al-arḍ*—the Maker of the heavens and the Creator of the earth. *al-ʿAlīm* is the All-Knowing. *al-Ḥasīb* is the Sufficient. *al-Wakīl* is the One who protects everything entrusted to Him, so nothing entrusted to Him is lost. *al-Raʾūf* is the One who is gentle with His servants through His mercy and kindness. *al-ʿAdl* is the Just in His judgment. *al-Rashīd* is the One who guides creation to their interests. *al-Ṣabūr* is the One who does not hasten to punish the disobedient.

##### Matters Related to the Most Beautiful Names of Allah

**First: Names beyond the ninety-nine.**

These ninety-nine names are not all that has been reported of the names of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified. Other names have also come in ḥadīths. In other narrations of this ḥadīth there appear *al-Ḥayy* (already counted), *al-Ḥakam*, *al-Badīʿ*; and among His names are also *al-Mubayyin*, *al-Kafīl*, *Dhū al-Ṭawl*, *Dhū al-Maʿālī*, *Dhū al-Faḍl*, and *al-Ḥallāq*.

Abū Bakr ibn al-ʿArabī said in his commentary on al-Tirmidhī, relating from a group of scholars, that one thousand names can be gathered from the Book and the Sunnah. And the author of *al-Qaṣd al-Mujarrad* also indicates this, and al-Shawkānī pointed to it in *Tuhfat al-Dhākirīn*. He then said: But the best that has been reported concerning counting them is the ḥadīth mentioned, and it suffices.

**Second: Permissible Metaphorical Expressions.**

You should know that some ḥadīths contain words that appear to be names of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—but the context of the words and the origin of their usage indicate otherwise. In such cases they are taken as metaphorical usage, not as literal names; that is, the thing is named by something else because of a connection between them, or because of an ellipsis. An example is the ḥadīth narrated by Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said:

> "Do not curse time, for Allah is time." [Muslim]

And the ḥadīth of ʿĀʾishah, may Allah be pleased with her: "Leave him, for groaning is one of the traces of Allah…" [al-Suyūṭī, *al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr*, from al-Rāfiʿī; graded ḥasan]. And other examples include applying the name "Ramaḍān" to Allah in some reports.

All of this does not require us to adopt their literal meanings as actual names; rather, the intended meaning in the first example is that Allah is the cause of what occurs in time, so it is not correct to attribute anything to time or to curse it. In the second, groaning is a trace from Allah by which the sick find relief; and likewise other expressions are interpreted according to their contexts.

**Third: Our Stance on the Names and Attributes of Allah.**

The majority of Muslims agree that it is not permissible to apply to Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—a name or description that is not confirmed by the Sharīʿah with the intention of adopting it as a name for Him, even if it is close in meaning. So it is not correct to say "the Engineer of the Great Universe" or "the Universal Beneficent Force" as names or attributes of Allah that may be conventionally used and agreed upon. But if such expressions occur incidentally in speech to explain His disposing of things, as an approximation for minds, there is no harm; yet it is better to avoid that with reverence for Allah—the Exalted and Glorified.

**Fourth: Intellect and Convention in Names and Attributes.**

Among these names, one is the original name of the divine essence: *Allāh*; and all the rest are considered with respect to the meaning of attributes. For this reason it is correct that they be reports of an essence that is not described by a derivative?—and this is a disputed question that does not entail… [The text is damaged here, but the meaning is that the names may be derivative descriptions of the essence.]

##### The Excellence of Remembrance by the Names

Some people, when they mention the names of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—claim that every name has particular properties and secrets through which it produces an outpouring or a summation. Some exaggerate this to the point of claiming that every name has a spiritual servant, and that whoever persists in remembering it is served by that servant, and the like.

What I believe in this matter—and beyond every person of knowledge is the All-Knowing—is that the names of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—are the highest of all speech, great in dignity, mighty in effect, and that remembrance of them has tremendous benefit. When a person persists in remembering Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—with a present heart and understanding of the meaning, his soul is purified and his spirit is refined. But as for what goes beyond that, it has no basis in the Book or the Sunnah. We have been sternly forbidden from inventing things in the religion of Allah and adding to it; so we must confine ourselves to what has been reported.

The Greatest Name (*ism Allāh al-aʿẓam*) is mentioned in many ḥadīths. Among them:

1. From Buraydah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, heard a man supplicating while saying: "O Allah, I ask You by the fact that I testify that You are Allah, there is no deity worthy of worship except You, the One, the Self-Sufficient Master, who has not begotten and was not begotten, and to whom no one is equal." The Prophet said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, he has asked Allah by His Greatest Name, by which if He is asked He gives, and by which if He is supplicated He answers." [Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, and Ibn Mājah]

2. From Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, entered the mosque and found a man who had finished praying and was supplicating, saying in his supplication: "O Allah, there is no deity except You, You are the Self-Sufficient Master, the Originator of the heavens and the earth, Possessor of Majesty and Honor…" The Prophet said: "Do you know what he has supplicated Allah with? He has supplicated Allah by His Greatest Name, by which if He is asked He gives, and by which if He is supplicated He answers." [Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, and Ibn Mājah]

3. From Asmāʾ bint Yazīd, may Allah be pleased with her, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The Greatest Name of Allah is in these two Sūrahs: *Allah, there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence*—and in the opening of Āl ʿImrān: *Allah, there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence.*" [Aḥmad, Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī, and Ibn Mājah]

4. From Saʿd ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, say: "Shall I not guide you to the Greatest Name of Allah, by which if He is asked He gives and by which if He is supplicated He answers?" It is the supplication with which Dāwūd called out in the darkness: "There is no deity except You, You are the One, there is no partner with You!" The man said: "O Messenger of Allah, was it for Dāwūd specifically or for the believers in general?" The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: Allah—the Mighty and Majestic—says in His Book: "So We responded to him and saved him from distress." [al-Ḥākim]

From these and other ḥadīths, it is clear that the Greatest Name is one composed of several of Allah's names, and that the scholars differ in identifying it because they differ in preferring some ḥadīths over others. What we take from these noble ḥadīths and from the statements of the people of knowledge among the men of Allah is that the Greatest Name is a combination of several of Allah's names; when a person supplicates with them, fulfilling the conditions required by the Sharīʿah, Allah responds to him. This is explicitly stated by the noble ḥadīths in several places.

If this is established, then the claim of some people that they possess secret names by which they perform marvels for certain individuals, extract answers, violate the ordinary course of events, and attain extraordinary powers beyond other people—these are additions beyond what has come from Allah and His Messenger. When such people confront the noble verse in which Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—says:

> "If you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then produce a Sūrah the like thereof and call upon your witnesses other than Allah, if you should be truthful." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:23]

or when they claim that there is a Greatest Name hidden from the common people, we say to them: The exegetes have explicitly stated that that [claim]—if it means anything—is either "the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence" or another expression; and the claim that it is a Syriac formula is without proof and unsupported by the authentic ḥadīths.

The upshot of the discussion is that some people have become entangled with whisperings, claims of hidden knowledge, and additions in invocations, saying things that have no basis in the Book or the Sunnah. We have been sternly forbidden from that, so let us beware of going beyond the light.

### Part Two: The Attributes of Allah, Exalted is He

#### The Attributes of Allah in the Light of the Human Intellect

If you look at this universe and what it contains of wonders of creation, marvels of the cosmos, subtle precision, and grand design—if you observe the interconnection, harmony, proportion, renewal, and invention; if you behold this clear heaven with its planets, stars, suns, moons, and orbits; and this earth with its vegetation, minerals, treasures, elements, and materials; and the animal kingdom with its strange instincts and behaviors; indeed, if you consider the composition of the human being and the many organs it contains, each performing its function and fulfilling its task; and if you see the seas and what they contain of waves and wonders, and you know the cosmic powers and what they contain of wisdom and secrets—electricity, magnetism, heat, radium, and other forces—then your reflection will pass from the realities of things and their qualities to the bonds and relationships between them, and how each of them is connected to the other in a firm bond so that from their totality arises a single cosmic unity, every part of which serves the other parts as an organ in a single body serves the rest of the organs. All of this comes to you without any proof or evidence you have brought, but rather it is a witnessing of the senses and a judgment of the intellect before which there is no escape. From this you will necessarily arrive at the conviction that this universe has a Creator, a Manager, a Director, whose power encompasses everything and whose knowledge embraces all things.


# Epistle 7: Establishing the Islamic Creedal Pillars (*Iqāmat al-Maqāʾid al-Islāmiyyah*) — Part 1

## The Logical Premise

This is a Qurʾanic chapter opened by that sound natural premise: that this universe has a creator, a maker, a bringer into existence; that this maker must be greater beyond what the weak human mind imagines of greatness; powerful beyond what man understands of the meanings of power; all-encompassing in knowledge and awareness; independent of all these created things, because He existed before they existed; knowing in the widest limits of knowledge, because He is above the secrets of this universe since He originated them; prior to these existents because He created them; and master of them because He will govern them with His wisdom. In short, you will find your soul naturally compelled by this premise that the Maker of this universe and its Manager is described with all the attributes of divinity beyond what the small human mind conceives, and is exalted above every attribute of deficiency. You will find this premise rooted in the depths of your heart and soul — the *fiṭrah* (فطرة) upon which Allah created people: “There is no altering the creation of Allah.” [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:30]

And after this introduction we shall present to you some of the wonders of events in this universe, and you will see that their consideration — in relation to the order of the universe and the precision and perfection within it — is enough to plant certainty firmly in your soul.

## Observations from the Universe

### First Observation: Breathing

The air we breathe is composed of several elements, two of which are especially important: a beneficial part for human respiration, which chemists call oxygen; and a harmful part called carbon dioxide. Look at the connection between the units of this wondrous existence: this part harmful to man is inhaled by plants and is beneficial to them. At the very time man inhales oxygen and expels carbon dioxide, the plant does the reverse: it inhales carbon dioxide and expels oxygen. Consider the mutual relationship between man and plants in something so essential to life as breathing, and then tell me: does this happen in the universe without a capable, all-wise, meticulous power?

### Second Observation: Digestion

You eat food composed of several vegetable or animal elements. Allah divides it into nitrogenous, starchy, or fatty substances, for example. You see the saliva digest some starchy materials, dissolve the sugary substances, and transform them before swallowing; the stomach with its gastric juice digests the nitrogenous substances such as meat and the like; the secretions of the liver complete the digestion of fats and break them into fine particles suitable for absorption; then the pancreas comes after that and secretes four juices, each of which undertakes the digestion of one of the three elements — starchy, nitrogenous, or fatty — and the fourth transforms starch into sugar. Reflect on this amazing connection between the elements of the human body, and the elements of plants, animals, and the foods by which man is nourished!

### Third Observation: Flowers

Look at the flower of a plant and you will see it as beautiful, attractive leaves adorned with joyful colors. If you ask a botanist about the wisdom in this, he will answer that this is an attraction for insects and creatures that suck the sweetness of flowers and alight upon the flower. When they stand upon its stems, pollen grains adhere to their limbs and are transferred by them from the male flower to the female flower, so fertilization takes place. See how Allah has adorned these leaves within the flower as a link between plant and animal, by which the plant uses the animal for the fertilization necessary for fruit and reproduction!

## The Conclusion from Creation

All that is in the universe points to the existence of a sublime wisdom, a transcendent will, an overwhelming power, and laws of the utmost precision and perfection upon which this existence runs. The Lord of this wisdom, the owner of this order, and the establisher of these laws is Allah.

The Qurʾan has expanded on this and on directing attention to these subtle wonders and lofty secrets. There is scarcely a passage in it or a surah that does not point to His power, His wisdom, His mercy, and urge people to contemplate these things and persist in reflection.

Allah the Exalted says:

> "And of His signs is that He created you from dust; then, suddenly you were human beings dispersing [throughout the earth]." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:20]

> "And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquillity in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy. Indeed in that are signs for a people who give thought." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:21]

> "And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth and the diversity of your languages and your colors. Indeed in that are signs for those of knowledge." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:22]

> "And of His signs is your sleep by night and day and your seeking of His bounty. Indeed in that are signs for a people who listen." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:23]

> "And of His signs is [that] He shows you the lightning [causing] fear and aspiration, and He sends down rain from the sky by which He brings to life the earth after its lifelessness. Indeed in that are signs for a people who use reason." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:24]

> "And of His signs is that the heaven and earth remain by His command. Then when He calls you with a [single] call from the earth, immediately you will come forth." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:25]

And He says:

> "It is Allah who sends the winds, and they stir the clouds and spread them in the sky however He wills, and He makes them fragments so you see the rain emerge from within them. And when He causes it to fall upon whom He wills of His servants, immediately they rejoice. Although they were, before it was sent down upon them — before that, in despair. So observe the effects of the mercy of Allah — how He gives life to the earth after its lifelessness. Indeed, that [same one] will give life to the dead, and He is over all things competent." [Qurʾan, Ar-Rūm 30:48-50]

And much more in Sūrat al-Mulk, al-Qaṣaṣ, al-Anbiyāʾ, al-Naḥl, Sād, and other surahs of the Noble Qurʾan.

## 2. The Attributes of Allah in the Qurʾan

The verses of the Noble Qurʾan point to some of the necessary and eternal attributes of Allah that are entailed by divinity. Here are some of those noble verses.

### 1. His Existence

Allah the Exalted says:

> "Say, "Who is Lord of the heavens and earth?" Say, "Allah." Say, "Have you then taken besides Him allies not possessing [even] for themselves any benefit or any harm?" Say, "Is the blind equivalent to the seeing? Or is darkness equivalent to light? Or have they attributed to Allah partners who created like His creation so that the creation [of each] seemed similar to them?" Say, "Allah is the Creator of all things, and He is the One, the Prevailing."" [Qurʾan, Ar-Raʿd 13:16]

And He says:

> "And Allah created you from dust, then from a sperm-drop; then He made you mates. And no female conceives nor does she give birth except with His knowledge. In these noble verses there is an indication of the attributes of eternity and everlastingness of Allah the Blessed and Exalted.

In this is an indication of His attribute of being unlike created things, far from a mate, a child, a like, or an equal.

In this is an indication of His self-sufficiency by Himself and His independence of His creation, even as they need Him.

To these and the like of them belong the many verses establishing the power of Allah the Blessed and Exalted, the magnificence of His greatness, and His all-encompassing knowledge.

To these and the like belong the noble verses that point to the affirmation of the will of Allah the Exalted, and that it is above every will and desire.

## The Distinctiveness of Divine Attributes

The one who wants to understand must grasp that the meaning intended by the words used for the attributes of Allah the Blessed and Exalted differs from the meaning intended by the very same words when used for created beings. If you say, “Allah is knowing and knowledge is His attribute,” and you say, “So-and-so is knowing and knowledge is his attribute,” is the word in both constructions one and the same? Far be it! Rather, the knowledge of Allah the Blessed and Exalted is limitless, infinite, and no created being can approach it in any way. Likewise life, likewise hearing, likewise sight, likewise speech, likewise power and will. All of these are verbal usages, but their usages in relation to the Creator differ in meaning and manner from their usages in relation to created beings, because Allah the Blessed and Exalted does not resemble any of His creatures. So understand this subtle point. I do not ask you to comprehend its reality; I only ask you to believe in what exists in the universe and its necessary implications in His right, and may He grant us protection from error and good success.

## The Proofs of the Theologians for the Divine Attributes

The scholars of the principles of religion hold that proving the attributes of Allah the Blessed and Exalted by rational proofs is good, because the Qurʾan is the source of knowledge and the basis of religious obligation, and so that no remnant of doubts and false notions remains in anyone's soul. But the matter is clearer than that, and the existence of the Creator the Blessed is more evident.

Indeed, proving these divine attributes has become among the self-evident truths that require no proof or evidence, and no one demands a proof for them except one whose heart is diseased and who finds no proof and does not understand. Still, there is benefit in mentioning some concise rational and analogical proofs.

**First Proof:** This existence, by its order and precision, points to the existence of its Creator, His greatness, and His wisdom.

**Second Proof:** The giver of something cannot give what he does not possess. If the controller of this universe is not described with the attributes of divinity, how can those attributes appear in His creatures?

**Third Proof — specific to the Oneness of this Deity, that He is One without multiplicity:** Multiplicity is a cause of corruption, disagreement, deficiency, and quarrelling, which are unworthy of divinity, majesty, and purity. If each of the multiple ones acted independently, the attributes of the others would be suspended; if they shared, some of the attributes of each would be suspended. The suspension of divine attributes contradicts majesty and greatness; therefore this Deity must be One, with no partner beside Him.

These are some of the logical proofs for the existence of the Deity and the affirmation of His attributes. Whoever wants to elaborate may do so at length.

But the matter is settled in the *fiṭrah* (فطرة) of pure souls and established in the depths of sound hearts: “And whomsoever Allah wills to guide, He expands his breast to Islam.” [Qurʾan, Al-Anʿām 6:125]

## How to Dispel Satanic Whispers

It is reported in a ḥadīth from Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “People will continue to ask until someone says, ‘This is the creation of Allah, so who created Allah?’ Whoever encounters anything of that, let him say: ‘I believe in Allah.’” [Muslim]

This question, though it is false in its foundation — because we have declared that we do not investigate the essence of Allah the Blessed and Exalted, for our limited minds are incapable of grasping the reality of their own selves, let alone of grasping the reality of the essence of Allah the Blessed and Exalted — still, it may occur to some people. We want to heal them with an answer that removes the confusion, Allah the Exalted willing. So say:

If you placed a book on your desk, then left the room and returned after a while to find the book you had left on the desk placed in a drawer, you would certainly believe that someone must have put it in the drawer, because you know from the attributes of this book that it cannot move by itself. Keep this point in mind and move to a second point: if there were a person sitting in a chair in your room, and you left and returned to find him sitting on a rug, for example, you would not ask about the reason for his moving, nor would you believe that someone had carried him from his place, because you know from the attributes of this person that he moves by himself and does not need anyone to move him. Keep this second point in mind along with what I say: created things, by their nature and attributes, cannot bring themselves into existence; there must necessarily be a bringer into existence. So we know that the one who brought them into existence is Allah the Blessed and Exalted. He is not like created things, whose existence entails need for other than themselves; rather, among His attributes is His self-existence, so we know that Allah the Blessed and Exalted exists by Himself and has no need of anyone to bring Him into existence. When you put the two previous points beside this statement, the matter will become clear to you — and the human mind is too short to plunge deeper than this. And what can we say of Allah? He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.

Here are the words of some Western scholars affirming the existence of Allah the Exalted and acknowledging the wisdom of His attributes — and to Him belongs our success and yours:

1. **Descartes**, the French philosopher, said with awareness of the deficiency of his own soul: “At the same time that I feel this, I bear witness to the necessity of the existence of a Perfect Being, and I am compelled to believe that this awareness was planted in me by that Perfect Being, Who is adorned with all the attributes of divinity.” Thus in his words he affirmed the existence of his own soul and its deficiency, and the existence of Allah and His wisdom. He recognized that his awareness of Allah is a gift from Allah to him and a *fiṭrah* upon which Allah created people.

2. **Isaac Newton**, the famous Englishman and discoverer of the law of gravity, said: “We cannot doubt God, for it is impossible that chance and coincidence should be the origin of the order in existence.”

3. **Herschel**, the English astronomer, said: “Every time the sphere of knowledge expands, the decisive and powerful proofs of the existence of an Eternal Creator, limitless in power and dominion, increase. Geologists, mathematicians, philosophers, and naturalists have all built upon the structure of faith, which is the structure of the greatness of Allah alone.”

4. **Laplace**, as reported by Camille Flammarion the Frenchman in his book *God in Nature*, said: “Allah is the Eternal, the Eternal of all eternity, Witness over all things. He has manifested Himself to me through the wonders of His creation until I have become bewildered in awe before Him: His power, His wisdom, His creative inventiveness in His handiworks, whether in the smallest or the greatest of things. The harmonies we perceive in these beings draw us by the order of Allah's mercy to His worship, just as they are preserved from dissolution and renewed in existence by His greatness and His power of preservation.”

5. **Herbert Spencer**, the Englishman, said in this meaning in his essay on education: “The educated man does not contradict the facts, but he does not contradict faith either. There is much in common natural knowledge that has a spirit of atheism, but the sound knowledge rooted in the depths of the truthful is free from this evil. The truly learned mind acknowledges religion, and acknowledgment of the secrets of things is a worship of their Maker and a recognition of His power. This orientation is not a verbal glorification; it is a reverence that arises from study, from reflection, and from work.”

And the Noble Qurʾan alludes to that in His words:

> "The seven heavens and the earth and whatever is in them exalt Him. And there is not a thing except that it exalts [Allah] by His praise, but you do not understand their [way of] exalting. Indeed, He is Forbearing and Forgiving." [Qurʾan, Al-Isrāʾ 17:44]

The mind of man, compared to what surpasses its capacity, is like the tip of a finger. Then he gives examples of what he says, saying: “If the water of the sea were a single drop, it is composed in a particular proportion of oxygen and hydrogen, so that if this proportion differed it would be something other than water — a testimony to the greatness, power, wisdom, and vast knowledge of the Creator that is more profound and stronger than any natural science, which has nothing to do with it except as a raindrop falling from the sky.” And similarly he mentions the snowflake that falls in the cold, beneath which the microscope reveals the intricacy, the precision of its structure, the beauty of its form, and the subtlety of its design — far more than the eye can grasp, except that it is frozen rain from the intensity of cold.

The sayings of the scholars of the universe in this are beyond enumeration, and what we have mentioned is sufficient. We have only cited them so that our youth may know that whoever seeks from Allah the Blessed and Exalted will find in Him strength, firmness, and confirmation, in accordance with His words:

> "We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?" [Qurʾan, Fuṣṣilat 41:53]

We have sufficed with the verses in mentioning them. There are also many other words of this kind in numerous hadiths attributed to the essence of Allah—the Blessed and Exalted, and we shall mention some of them:

1. From Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Allah created Ādam in His image; his length was sixty cubits." Then when he created him, He said: "Go and greet them." So they greeted him, and he said: "Peace be upon you." They said: "Peace be upon you and the mercy of Allah." Then he added: "The mercy of Allah." So whoever enters Paradise will be in the form of Ādam: sixty cubits. The people have continued to diminish until now." [al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

2. From Anas ibn Mālik, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: "Paradise and Hellfire continue to dispute, and Hellfire says: 'The arrogant and the tyrants have been created for me.' Until the Lord of Honor places His foot in it, and it says: 'Enough, enough, by Your honor and Your nobility,' and it continues to contract until there is nothing left of its capacity. Then Allah will complete for it what He has placed within it." [al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

3. From Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah is more joyful at the repentance of one of you than one of you is at finding his lost camel." [al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

## The Sects Concerning This Matter

The people concerning this matter are divided into four groups:

1. A group that takes the apparent meanings of these texts as they are, and so attributes to Allah a face like faces, a hand like hands, laughter like laughter, and so on, until they imagine Allah as a body. Some of them even imagine Him as a youth. These are the *mujassima* (المجسمة) and the *shāhija* (الشاحجة), and they have nothing to do with Islam, nor do they have any share of correctness. Allah—the Exalted—says to them:

> "Say, 'He is Allah, [who is] One, Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent.'" [Qurʾan, Al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1-4]

2. A group that negates the meanings of these texts altogether, claiming that Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—has no direction, no place, no sitting, no hand, and no eye, since that cannot be except with a limb, and limbs are impossible for the One transcendent beyond them. By this they nullify the attributes while pretending to declare Him transcendent. These are the *muʿaṭṭila* (المعطلة). Some of the exponents of Islamic creeds call them *jahmiyya* (الجهمية). I do not think that anyone at Mecca or Mina, possessed of intellect, would adopt this bankrupt opinion. Speech and hearing are not established for them except through organs, yet how could the speech of Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—be dependent upon organs? Allah is exalted above that with a great exaltation.

These two opinions are false in a manifest way, and the view of the people of truth is between them. It is the view of the *salaf* (السلف) and the view of the *ahl al-ḥaqq* (أهل الحق).

## The Position of the Salaf Concerning the Verses of the Attributes and Their Hadiths

The position of the *salaf*, may Allah be pleased with them, in these verses and hadiths is between negation and affirmation. They believe in the verses of the attributes and their hadiths as they came, and they leave the explanation of their intended meanings to Allah—the Blessed and Exalted, while affirming the transcendence of Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—beyond resemblance to His creation. So they affirm the Hand, the Eye, the Eyes, the Establishment, the Laughter, the Joy, and all of that as attributes whose reality we do not know, and they leave comprehension of them to Allah—the Blessed and Exalted. There is no harm in that, for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> "Reflect upon the creation of Allah, and do not reflect upon Allah, for you will never be able to comprehend Him." [Aḥmad]

al-ʿIrāqī said: "It was narrated by Aḥmad with a weak chain." And al-Bayhaqī narrated it in *al-ʿItiqād* with a sound chain. And Abū al-Shaykh narrated likewise, along with their affirmation—may Allah be pleased with them—of the negation of resemblance between the Creator and the created. Among their words in that are:

1. It is reported from ʿUthmān ibn Saʿīd al-Dārimī, the companion of Abū Ḥanīfah, in *Uṣūl al-Sunnah*: "The people of the *qiblah* from the east to the west, the trustworthy narrators from among the followers of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, in the description of the Lord—mighty and majestic—narrated without limitation, without description, and without comparison; so whoever explains anything of that today has departed from what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was upon and has opposed the community, for they neither describe nor liken but say what is in the Book and the Sunnah, then remain silent."

2. Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal said in his treatise, and in another place in his book *al-Sunnah*: "I asked Abū ʿAbd Allāh about the hadiths that are narrated: 'Indeed, Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—descends to the heaven of this world,' and 'Indeed, Allah laughs,' and 'Indeed, Allah places His foot,' and what resembles these hadiths. Abū ʿAbd Allāh said: 'We believe in them and affirm them, without how and without meaning, and we do not reject anything of them. We believe that what the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, brought is true, even if the mind does not comprehend it, and we do not argue about Allah, and we do not describe Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—as more than He has described Himself, without limit and without end, by any means He wills.'"

3. It is reported from Ḥarum ibn Ḥayyān, from ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Abī Rawwād, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAwn al-Juḥanī, who said for a long time and in many words: "The tongue of the Messenger of Allah named Him as He named Himself, and we are not tasked with knowing how He is described."

Know—may Allah have mercy on you—that the protective bond in religion is that you stop where you have been stopped and do not transgress what has been permitted to you. For among the foundations of religion are knowledge of good and denial of evil. If a matter is spread out before you, and hearts are silent regarding it, and its origin is mentioned in the Book and the Sunnah, and the community has transmitted it, then do not be afraid to mention His attributes as your Lord has described Himself. Do not deny any attribute He has described Himself with, nor limit Him in any of the descriptions that He has limited. Do not be silent about what you deny, and do not find mention of it in the Book of your Lord or in the hadith from your Prophet of a description of your Lord, then be an opponent to it with your tongue, nor describe Him with your tongue. Be silent as the learned are silent about it, for your denial of an attribute He has not described Himself with is like your denial of what He has described Himself with. You have magnified what the deniers have denied of His description; likewise, it is a great error for the describers to describe Him with what He does not describe Himself with. They have erred. They are the safe ones: those who know the known and deny the denied, and by their denial they deny, and they hear what Allah has described Himself with in His Book and what has reached them like it from His Prophet. They affirm what the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, mentioned of the attributes of his Lord, and they do not deny His power, nor compare His attributes to those of any other believer. Whatever the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, kept silent about of the attributes of his Lord, that is more deserving than what the describers have described.

The saved ones are those who follow the path of the safe: they know Him by what He has described Himself with, they deny for Him what He has denied for Himself, and they do not deny a description of Him by what He has kept silent about. They do not deny His description, nor do they liken Him by it. And whoever follows a way other than the way of the believers, Allah will turn him toward what he has turned and burn him in Hell. And that is easy for Allah.

> "Say, 'He is Allah, [who is] One.'" [Qurʾan, Al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1]

## The Position of the Khalaf Concerning the Verses of the Attributes and Their Hadiths

As for the *khalaf* (الخلف), they said: "We assert that the meanings of the words of these verses and hadiths are not their apparent meanings. On that basis, it is permissible for us to interpret them." So they began interpreting the Face as the Essence, the Hand as Power, and so on, fleeing from the suspicion of resemblance. Here are some of their words:

1. **Abū al-Faraj ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn al-Jawzī al-Ḥanbalī**, in his book *Dafʿ Shubah al-Tashbīh*: Concerning the verse of Allah—the Exalted and Glorified—"And there will remain the Face of your Lord," he said: "It means: your Lord will remain." And likewise they said about His words—the Exalted and Glorified—"Under My eye": "It means: under My watchful care." And al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿUbaydah said: "Everything will perish except His Face," meaning: except Him.

He composed at the beginning of the book a chapter broadly refuting those who said that taking the apparent meanings of these verses and hadiths is the madhhab of the *salaf*. The summary of what he said is that taking the apparent meaning is anthropomorphism and comparison, because the apparent meaning of a word is what it was coined for, so there is no meaning for "hand" in reality except the limb, and so on. As for the madhhab of the *salaf*, it is not taking them according to their apparent meanings but remaining silent about inquiry into them. He also went so far as to say that the naming of these as "verses of attributes" and "hadiths of attributes" is an innovation that does not occur in the Book or the Sunnah, and that they are not real but merely additions. He supported his statement in that with lengthy proofs that there is no need to mention here.

2. **Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī**, in his book *Asās al-Taqdīs*, said: "Know that the texts of the Qurʾan cannot be taken according to their apparent meanings for several reasons. The first is that the apparent meaning of His words—the Exalted and Glorified—'And I bestowed upon you love from Me that you would be brought up under My eye' would require that Mūsā, peace be upon him, be settled upon that eye, attached to it and elevated above it, which is not the statement of a rational person. The second is that His words—the Exalted and Glorified—'And construct the ship under Our eyes' would require that that craft be that eye. The third is that affirming eyes in a single face is repugnant, so there is no escape from interpretation, which is that these words be understood as intense care and watchfulness."

3. **Imām al-Ghazālī**, in the first volume of his book *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn*, in his discussion of every attribution of the outward to the inward and the categories in which manifestation, inwardness, interpretation, and the like occur, said: "The third category is that the hand may be a true hand and a real limb, but it be concealed from the listener by means of indication and allusion, so that its awe in the heart of the hearer is greater. Among that is the saying of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him:

> 'The mosque shrinks from spittle as a garment shrinks from fire.'

The meaning is that the spirit of the mosque and its holiness reject spittle, since spittle has a place in it that contradicts the meaning of the mosque in the same way that fire contradicts a garment by consuming its parts. You see that the open courtyard of the mosque is not the neighbor's head, yet the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'It is better for one of you that his head be rubbed with iron than that he should pray in the courtyard of the mosque of the neighbor,' and that is not with respect to form or being, but with respect to the attribute; for the head of the neighbor is not his reality, his being, or his form, but his characteristics, which are foolishness and ignorance. So when the head is rubbed before the imam, it becomes the head of the neighbor in terms of foolishness and ignorance, and that is the intended meaning, not the form. This hidden meaning is known either by rational proof or by Sharʿī proof.

As for the apparent meaning being impossible, that is like his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> 'The heart of the believer is between two of the fingers of the Merciful.'

For if you were to open the breasts of the believers, you would not find hearts between fingers in them. So it is a metaphor for power, which is the source of the fingers and their spirit, and the fingers are mentioned in place of power because that is more effective in conveying the meaning of ability."

We shall mention something more of this discussion in another place, and what we have mentioned is sufficient.

## Between the Salaf and the Khalaf

Up to this point, the two paths of the *salaf* and the *khalaf* have been clarified before you. These two paths were the cause of intense dispute among many of the scholars of the Muslims, and each group supported its madhhab with proofs and evidence. I would almost say that the distance between the two paths is nothing if the people of each of them would abandon extremism and stubbornness, and that debate in a matter such as this, in which so much has been said, ultimately leads to only one result: *al-tafwīḍ* (التفويض) to Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—which we shall explain to you in detail, Allah—the Exalted—willing.

The explanation of this is from several angles:

First: both groups agree on declaring Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—transcendent above resemblance to His creation.

Second: each of them asserts that the intended meaning of the words of these texts in relation to Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—is not their apparent meanings as coined for created beings, and that returns to their agreement on [His] transcendence above resemblance.

Third: each of them asserts that words are coined to convey meanings in the souls, or that there is nothing under the phrases that pertains to the conventions of language and its coiners, and that language, however broad it may be, is incapable of providing people with the realities of what pertains to the essence of Allah—the Blessed and Exalted. Therefore language is unable to provide words that convey these realities without divergence in defining their meanings.

When this is established, the *salaf* and the *khalaf* agree on the principle of interpretation, and the dispute between them is only that the *khalaf* specified the intended meanings when they deemed transcendence required it because of a suspicion of resemblance, while the *salaf* left the matter in general terms. This is a disagreement that does not deserve blame or concern.

We believe that the path of the *salaf*—silence and *tafwīḍ* of these meanings to Allah—the Blessed and Exalted—is safer and more worthy of being followed, because it cuts off the ways of interpretation and nullification. If you are one of those whose heart Allah has expanded with the tranquility of faith and whose breast He has relieved with the coolness of certainty, then do not substitute anything for it. We also believe, alongside this, that the interpretations of the *khalaf* do not entail disbelief or wickedness, and do not leave this long-standing dispute between them and others in ancient and modern times, while the bosom of Islam is wider than all of that. The strongest of people in adhering to the view of the *salaf*, may Allah be pleased with them, while resorting to interpretation in some places, was Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, may Allah be pleased with him. Among that is his interpretation of the hadith: "The Black Stone is the right hand of Allah on earth," and his saying, peace and blessings be upon him: "The heart of the believer is between two of the fingers of the Merciful," and his saying, peace and blessings be upon him: "I find the coolness of my Lord from the direction of al-Yaman."

I have seen from Imām al-Nawawī, may Allah be pleased with him, what indicates the closeness of the distance between the two groups, leaving no room for strife and argument, especially since the *khalaf* divided interpretation into categories that are permissible rationally and Sharʿan, so long as they do not conflict with a fundamental of the religion.

al-Rāzī said in his book *Asās al-Taqdīs*: "If we permit interpretation, we engage in mentioning those interpretations in detail as a voluntary act; and if we do not permit interpretation, we delegate them to Allah—the Exalted. This is the universal principle to which one returns in all matters of resemblance, and Allah grants success."

The summary of this discussion is that the *salaf* and the *khalaf* agree that the intended meaning is not the commonly understood apparent meaning between the two groups, which is interpretation. We believe that the path of the *salaf*—*tafwīḍ* to Allah—is safer, broader, and more deserving of being followed, and Allah knows best.
