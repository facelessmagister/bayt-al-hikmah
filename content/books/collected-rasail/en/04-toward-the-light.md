## Third: In the Economic Sphere

1. **Organising *zakāh* (alms)** as revenue and expenditure according to the teachings of the gentle *sharīʿah* (Islamic law), and using it for charitable projects in which there is no doubt — such as shelters for the elderly and poor, orphanages, and strengthening the army.

2. **Abolishing usury (*ribā*)** and organising the banks in a manner that leads to this goal, with the government being an example in that by renouncing "interest" in its own projects, such as the Bank of Loans, industrial credit, and the like.

3. **Encouraging economic projects and multiplying them**, employing the unemployed of the nation therein, and reclaiming what is in the hands of foreigners for purely national benefit.

4. **Protecting the masses from the tyranny of companies**, compelling them to remain within their limits, and securing every possible benefit for the masses.

5. **Improving the condition of lower-ranking employees** by raising their salaries, preserving their allowances and bonuses, and reducing the salaries of high-ranking employees.

6. **Limiting positions, especially the numerous ones**, restricting them to what is necessary, and distributing work among employees with just distribution, carefully supervising this.

7. **Encouraging agricultural and industrial guidance**, and caring for the advancement of the peasant and the craftsman from the productive aspect.

8. **Caring for technical and social crafts**, and raising their level in the various necessary fields.

9. **Exploiting natural resources** such as barren land, neglected mines, and the like.

10. **Giving priority to necessary projects over luxuries** in construction and expenditure.

## 1. The Call of the Trustworthy Prophet and the Method of the Noble Qur’an

One thousand three hundred and seventy years ago, Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh stood in the valley of Makkah and on the top of Ṣafā and said: "O people, I am the Messenger of Allah to you, believing in Him to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth; there is no deity worthy of worship except He. So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered prophet who believes in Allah and His words, and follow him that you may be guided." Thus that comprehensive call was a decisive dividing line in the entire universe: between a dark past and a brilliant, shining future; and a present rich with happiness; and a clear, manifest proclamation of a new system whose legislator is Allah the All-Knowing, the All-Forgiving, conveyed by His Messenger, the bringer of glad tidings and the warner, and his book the Qur’an, clear and illuminating, and his foremost vanguard of the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, and the religion followed by them with excellence.

## 2. The Fundamental Principles of Complete Social Reform Brought by the Noble Qur’an

The Qur’an is the comprehensive gatherer of the principles of this complete social reform. It continued to be revealed upon the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and to announce to the believers their rights and duties according to events, circumstances, and occasions:

> "Thus it is, that We may strengthen your heart thereby, and We have revealed it in a measured recitation. And they do not come to you with an argument except that We bring you the truth and the best explanation." [Qur’an, Al-Furqān 25:32–33]

Until the revelation was completed by it, preserved in hearts and written upon pages over a period of twenty-two years and some months. Allah placed in it an explanation of all things for this nation, and the principles of complete social reform brought by it can almost be summarised in these fundamentals:

(a) Lordship (*rubūbiyyah*).

(b) Elevation of the human soul.

(c) Establishment of the doctrine of recompense.

(d) Proclamation of brotherhood among people.

(e) The uplift of both man and woman together, and the proclamation of mutual responsibility and equality between them, with a precise determination of the scope of each.

(f) Securing society by establishing life, ownership, work, health, freedom, knowledge, and security for every individual, and defining the sources of earning.

(g) Regulating the two instincts: the instinct of self-preservation and the instinct of preservation of the species, by organising the demands of the mouth and the private part.

(h) Severity in combating the original germs [of corruption].

(i) Affirmation of the unity of the nation and the elimination of every manifestation of division and its causes.

(j) Obligating the nation to expend its wealth in the path of the true principles brought by this system.

(k) Considering the state as the exemplar of the idea and charged with protecting it, and responsible for realising its objectives in the specific society and conveying them to the people through it.

## 3. The Practical Forms of This System

This Qur’anic system distinguished itself from other man-made systems and theoretical philosophies by not leaving its principles and teachings as theories in souls, opinions in books, or words on tongues. Rather, it placed them as firm beliefs and attachments, and derived benefit from their effects and fruits. It made practical manifestations and binding duties incumbent upon the believing nation that is devoted to it and charged with being diligent in these works, not allowing leniency in neglecting them. Rather, it rewards the workers and punishes the negligent with a punishment that may remove the individual from the bounds of this Islamic society and cast him into a remote place. Among these duties that this system made ways to consolidate its principles are:

(a) Prayer, remembrance, repentance, seeking forgiveness, and so on.

(b) Fasting, sacrifice, and warning against extravagance.

(c) *Zakāh* and charity.

(d) Pilgrimage, swimming, travel, scouting, and observing the dominion of Allah.

(e) Earning and work, with begging forbidden.

(f) *Jihād* and fighting, equipping fighters, and caring for their families and dependants after them.

(g) Commanding good and offering sincere counsel in the various forms of *ḥisbah* befitting each.

(h) Good dealing and adornment with noble morals.

(i) Social solidarity between the people and the government through care and obedience together.

Whoever neglects the demands of these duties and fails to rise to them has fallen short of the Qur’anic system, and upon him is a deficiency in some part of it. They have all been mentioned in the Noble Qur’an, and the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, explained them with clear explanation to those who followed him with excellence, in simplicity and clarity. Every work in what has been prescribed strengthens and consolidates one or several principles of the above-mentioned theories that this system came to realise and to benefit people through their fruits and effects.

## 4. The First Islamic State

Upon the foundations of this noble Qur’anic social system the first Islamic state was established, spreading its radiance and proclaiming it to the worlds, until the first caliph, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "If a mere goat’s rope were withheld from me, I would find it in the Book of Allah." And indeed he fought those who prevented *zakāh*, considering them apostates by their demolition of this pillar from the pillars of this system, and he said: "If they withhold from me even a rope that they used to give to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, I would fight them for it so long as I hold fast to my religion." And unity, with all its meanings and manifestations, encompassed this rising nation: social unity inclusive through the general application of the Qur’anic system and the language of the Qur’an; political unity inclusive under the shade of the Commander of the Believers and the banner of the caliphate in the capital; and wherever the Islamic idea was, it was a decentralised idea in armies, in the homes of Allah, and in the administrations of the governors. For the people worked with one creed and one united faith. These Qur’anic principles pursued idolatry in the Arabian Peninsula and the lands of Persia until they eradicated them; they pursued crafty Judaism until they confined it to a narrow scope and put an end to its religious and political authority; and they fought Christianity until its shade was restricted to the continents of Asia and Africa and it retreated to Europe under the Eastern Roman state in Constantinople. Thus spiritual and political authority became concentrated in the Islamic state in the two great continents, and it pressed its assault upon the third continent against Constantinople from the east and besieged it until the siege tightened; then it came upon it from the west and conquered al-Andalus, and its victorious soldiers reached the heart of France and the north and south of Italy, and established in the heart of Europe a towering state, radiant with knowledge and awareness — this limited part from the heart of Europe. And the Islamic fleets ploughed the waves of the White and Red Seas until each became an Islamic lake. These Islamic nations, unlike other nations, inherited many civilisations, but they overcame all of them by the strength of their cohesion and the firmness of their system, and they almost Arabised them and were able to impress them and carry them upon their language and religion, with all their splendour, vitality, and beauty. (a) **Political and tribal disputes**, rivalry for leadership and command, together with the severe warning that Islam gave in this regard, its disdain for rule, and its drawing attention to this aspect which is the worm of nations and the destroyer of peoples and states:

> "And do not dispute and [thereby] lose courage and your strength would depart; and be patient. Indeed, Allah is with the patient." [Qur’an, Al-Anfāl 8:46]

And with the great counsel to devote sincerity to Allah alone in word and deed, and to purify oneself from love of fame and praise.

(b) **Religious and sectarian disputes**, and turning away from the religion as creeds and acts of worship to dead words and sterile terms that have neither spirit nor life; and preferring the books of men and the words of men over the Book of Allah and the *Sunnah* of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him; and haughtiness and fanaticism for opinions and sayings; and obsession with disputation, debates, and argumentation — all of which Islam warned against and forbade most sternly, until the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "No people go astray after being guided except that they are given to disputation."

European civilisation and culture rest upon the principle of excluding religion from the manifestations of life, especially the state, the judiciary, and the school. The materialist outlook and the deification of appetites prevailed in everything, so that the manifestations of this civilisation became purely materialistic, swiftly destroying what the heavenly religions had built and utterly contradicting what Islam, the comprehensive, had established as the foundation of its civilisation — a civilisation that stood between spirituality and matter together. After that they were free to alter the foundations of life, judgement, education, and politics, legislation, and culture with their own colour in the strongest of the Islamic lands. They brought into these lands their unveiled prostitutes, their wine, their theatres, their dance-halls, their taverns, their newspapers, their novels, their cinemas, their tales, their games, and their amusements, and they spread in them crimes that are not committed even in their own countries. They adorned this clamorous life, overflowing with sin and brimful of indecency, in the eyes of the simple, innocent Muslims, the rich, the men of opinion, and the people of status and power. Nor did they stop there; they established schools and cultural institutes in the very heart of the Islamic lands, casting doubt and atheism into the souls of their children, teaching them to despise themselves, their religion, their homeland, and their nation, to strip themselves of their customs and beliefs, and to sanctify everything Western, believing that whatever comes from the Europeans alone is the highest ideal in this life. These schools contained only the children of the upper class, and became an endowment for them; and the sons of this class — the doctors and rulers, and those who will soon hold the reins of affairs in these nations and peoples — whoever does not complete his ripening in these harmful institutes, the successive missions guarantee for him its completion.

This organised, violent cultural invasion succeeded with the greatest success. It is an invasion directed at souls, clinging to hearts, long-lived, and powerful in effect; therefore it is more dangerous than political and military invasion by many times over. Some Islamic nations were carried away with admiration for this European civilisation and discontent with their Islamic colour, until Turkey declared itself a non-Islamic state and followed Europe with harsh severity in everything it did. The amān Allāh Khān, king of Afghanistan, attempted the same, but that adventure destroyed him and increased. In Egypt the manifestations of this imitation spread and grew severe, until any man of opinion would say that there is no way to progress except by taking this civilisation wholesale — its good and its evil, its sweet and its bitter, its commendable and its blameworthy. It passed quickly and powerfully from Egypt to the neighbouring lands until it reached the farthest Maghrib and encircled the Sacred Sanctuaries in the regions of the Ḥijāz. We can divide the Islamic lands, according to the extent of their being affected by this material civilisation and the tyranny of its matter over them, into three categories:

1. **Lands in which this effect was extremely great**, reaching hearts and feelings and not merely outward forms and appearances. Of these are Turkey and Egypt, for the shadow of the Islamic idea in these lands retreated from every social aspect, and the Islamic idea was driven to confine itself in mosques, *zāwiyahs*, *ribāṭs*, and *takāyahs*.

2. **Lands affected by this civilisation in their official conditions and appearances**, but which it did not overcome in the feelings of the heart, such as Iran and the lands of North Africa.

3. **Lands not affected by this civilisation except in a special class of intellectuals and rulers**, not among the common people and masses, such as Syria, Iraq, the Ḥijāz, and many parts of the Arabian Peninsula and the rest of the Islamic world.

Yet this flood continues with the speed of lightning to reach whatever souls, classes, and conditions it reaches. The enemies of Islam were able to deceive the intelligent among the Muslims and to place many veils before their eyes, portraying Islam itself as a narrow conception in certain doctrines, acts of worship, and customs, alongside a mass of rituals, superstitions, and empty appearances. Ignorance of the reality of their religion helped them in this deception, until many Muslims settled upon this conception, were reassured by it, and were content with it; and they remained for a long time in this condition, until it became possible to say that Islam in their view is merely a complete social system embracing every affair of life. After that we can say that Western materialistic civilisation has triumphed in this social struggle over the Islamic civilisation in the Muslim lands themselves, and it has triumphed in the field of souls, spirits, beliefs, and minds of the Muslims just as it triumphed in the political and military field. There is no wonder in this, for the manifestations of life are indivisible, and strength is strength in all of them, and weakness is weakness in all of them likewise:

> "And these days [of varying conditions] We alternate among the people…" [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:140]

Yet if the principles and teachings of Islam remain strong in themselves, fertile with goodness and life, attractive and captivating by their splendour and beauty, they will remain so until the end of time, and human life cannot be complete and excellent without them, for they are the revelation of Allah and in His protection:

> "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an, and indeed, We will be its guardian." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥijr 15:9]

> "But Allah will perfect His light, although the disbelievers dislike it." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:32]

## A Glimpse of Hope

Just as that political aggression had its effect in provoking national feeling, so this social tyranny had its effect in reviving the Islamic idea. Voices rose from every direction calling for a return to Islam, understanding its rulings, and applying its system. The day must soon come when the edifices of this material civilisation will collapse upon the heads of its people; then they will feel the spiritual hunger blazing in their hearts and souls, and will find no food, healing, or remedy except in the words of this Noble Book:

> "O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers. Say, 'In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy — in that let them rejoice; it is better than what they accumulate.'" [Qur'an, Yūnus 10:57–58]

## Our Call Is a Call of Revival and Salvation

### A Heavy Inheritance

Thus, O Brothers, Allah willed that we inherit this heavy legacy with its burdens, and that the light of our call should shine in the midst of this darkness, and that Allah should use us to exalt His word, manifest His law, and establish His state anew:

> "Indeed, We will support Our messengers and those who believe during the life of this world and on the Day when the witnesses will stand." [Qur'an, Ghāfir 40:51]

### Our General Goals

What do we want, O Brothers? Do we want wealth, which is a fleeting shadow? Or do we want fame, which is a passing show? Or do we want domination upon earth?

> "And the earth belongs to Allah; He causes to inherit it whom He wills of His servants." [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:128]

We recite the saying of Allah, Blessed and Exalted:

> "That home of the Hereafter We assign to those who do not desire exaltedness upon the earth or corruption. And the [best] outcome is for the righteous." [Qur'an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:83]

Allah knows that we do not want any of this, nor is this our call. But remember, O caller, that we have two essential goals:

1. **That the Islamic homeland be liberated from every foreign dominion**, so that every human being may practise his full rights — which no tyrant or oppressive wrongdoer can deny.

2. **That in this free homeland there arise a free Islamic state**, implementing the rulings of Islam, applying its social system, proclaiming its just principles, and conveying its wise call to people. Until this state is established, all Muslims are responsible before Allah, the Exalted and Mighty, for their negligence in establishing it and their remaining inactive in bringing it about. It is among the greatest crimes against humanity in these critical circumstances that a state should stand upon oppressive principles and call to harsh doctrines, while among the people there is none who works for the establishment of a state of truth, justice, and peace. We want these two goals to be realised in the Nile Valley, in every land illuminated by the creed of Islam — religion, relationship, and doctrine uniting the body of Muslims.

### Our Specific Goals

Beyond these two goals we have specific goals without which society cannot become fully Islamic. Remember, O Brothers, that more than eighty per cent of Egyptians live in a condition lower than that of animals, scarcely obtaining their sustenance except by the hardest toil; that Egypt is threatened by deadly famine and exposed to many economic problems whose solution is known only to Allah; that Egypt has more than thirty foreign companies monopolising all public utilities and all important benefits in every part of the country; that the wheel of commerce, industry, and economic establishments is entirely in the hands of foreign usurers; that real estate wealth passes with the speed of lightning from the hands of the sons of the homeland to the hands of these foreigners; that Egypt is the most diseased and plagued of the lands of the East; that more than half of the Egyptian people are afflicted by anaemia, loss of senses, and various illnesses and diseases; that Egypt still has, out of every hundred thousand persons, no more than the number whose education exceeds the programmes of primary schools; that crime multiplies in Egypt and increases to an enormous degree, so that prisons produce more than schools; and that Egypt has not yet been able to pass a single fully equipped military battalion. These meanings and pictures appear in every country of the Islamic world. Therefore, among our goals is that you work to reform education, eliminate poverty, sickness, disease, ignorance, and crime, and form a society worthy of being attributed to the law of Islam.

### Our General Means

How do we reach these goals? Sermons, speeches, correspondence, lessons, and lectures, diagnosing the disease and prescribing the remedy — all of this by itself is of no avail and does not achieve any goal, nor does it bring the caller to any of the objectives. Rather, the call has general means that must be adopted and worked upon. These general means do not change, vary, or exceed these three:

1. Deep faith.
2. Precise formation.
3. Continuous work.

These are your means, O Brothers. So believe in your idea, gather around it, and work in it according to what you have been charged.

### Additional Means

Beside these general means there may be additional means that must be adopted according to the occasion: some of them negative, some positive; some in agreement with people's custom, some departing from it and contradicting it; some gentle, some severe. We must train ourselves to bear all of this and prepare for all of it, so that success is assured. It may be demanded of us that we oppose customs and familiar ways and depart from systems and conditions to which people are accustomed. In reality, the call is nothing but a departure from familiar ways and a changing of habits and conditions. So are you prepared for that, O Brothers?

### Doubt

Many people will say: What do these letters mean? What good can they do in building a nation and repairing a society amid these chronic problems and this settled corruption? How can you manage the economy without interest as a foundation? What will you do in the question of woman? How will you obtain your right without force? Know, O Brothers, that Satan's whisperings do not cease to assail every reformer; but Allah nullifies what Satan casts, then confirms His verses. And remember, all of you, that history relates to us from the conditions of past and present nations what contains lesson and warning. The nation that resolves upon life cannot be powerless.

### Obstacles in Our Path

I wish to be frank with you: the call is still unknown to many people. The day they know it and understand its aims and goals, they will oppose it with severe hostility and harsh enmity, and you will find before you many hardships and many obstacles. At that time alone will you have begun to walk the path of the people of the call. As for now, you are still unknown, still introducing the call and preparing what it requires of struggle and *jihād*. The people's ignorance of the reality of Islam is an obstacle in your path. You will find among the religious and among the common people those who will be astonished at your call to Islam and will deny your *jihād* in its cause. The leaders, grandees, men of status and power will look upon you with contempt, every government will stand in your way and will try to curb your activity and place obstacles in your path. The envious will seize every opportunity to resist you and extinguish the light of your call; in this they will seek help from weak governments, weak morals, and hands stretched out to them with begging and to you with harm and aggression. They will stir up dust and the shadow of accusations around your call, and will try to attach to you every defect and present it to the people in an ugly picture, relying on their power and authority:

> "They want to extinguish the light of Allah with their mouths, but Allah refuses except to perfect His light, although the disbelievers dislike it." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:32]

And you will undoubtedly enter the circle of trial and examination: you will be imprisoned, detained, exiled, expelled, your interests confiscated, your works suspended, and your call fought. This examination may be prolonged:

> "Do the people think that they will be left to say, 'We believe,' and they will not be tried?" [Qur'an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:2]

But Allah has promised, after all of this, victory to the strivers and reward to the doers of good:

> "O you who have believed, shall I guide you to a transaction that will save you from a painful punishment?" [Qur'an, As-Ṣaf 61:10]

> "And Allah made firm those who believed, with the firm word, in worldly life and in the Hereafter. And Allah sends astray the wrongdoers. And Allah does what He wills." [Qur'an, Ibrāhīm 14:27]

So are you resolved to be Allah's helpers?

### Factors of Success

It is fitting, O Brothers, that we mention, in the face of these obstacles, that we call to the call of Allah — the highest of calls — and proclaim the idea of Islam — the strongest of ideas — and present to people the law of the Qur'an — the most just of laws:

> "The colour of Allah — and who is better than Allah in colour?" [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:138]

All people are in need of this call, and everything apart from it is a path to ruin. Praise be to Allah, we are free from personal motives and do not shrink from bitter struggle; we intend only the face of Allah and the good of people, and we work only seeking His pleasure. We await the help of Allah and His victory, and whoever Allah helps, none can overcome:

> "That is because Allah is the protector of those who have believed and because the disbelievers have no protector." [Qur'an, Muḥammad 47:11]

The strength of our call, the need of the world for it, the purity of our intention, and the help of Allah for us are the factors of success before which no obstacle stands and no impediment blocks the path:

> "But Allah is predominant over His affair, but most of the people do not know." [Qur'an, Yūsuf 12:21]

## Words to the Muslim Brothers

O Muslim Brothers, listen:

I wanted these words to place the idea before your eyes, for difficult hours may lie ahead, separating me from you for a time, when I will not be able to speak with you or write to you. So I instruct you to ponder these words, preserve them if you can, and gather around them. Beneath every word there are many meanings.

O Brothers: you are not a charitable association, nor a political party, nor an organisation for limited aims. You are a new spirit revived in the heart of this nation by the Qur'an; a new light rising to dispel the darkness of matter by knowledge of Allah; a voice of David calling aloud, echoing the call of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him. It is fitting that you feel that you are bearing this burden after people have abandoned it. If it is said to you, "To what do you call?" say: "We call to the Islam brought by Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him; and government is part of it, and freedom is one of its obligations." If it is said, "Then this is politics!" say: "This is Islam, and we do not recognise these divisions." If it is said, "You are callers to revolution!" say: "We are callers to truth and peace, which we believe in and hold dear." If you are fought and stood in the way of your call, then you have permission to defend yourselves against the wrongdoing aggressors. If it is said to you that you rely upon persons or institutions, say:

> "We have believed in Allah alone and disbelieved in whatever we used to associate with Him." [Qur'an, Ghāfir 40:66]

Then if they persist in their aggression, say:

> "Peace be upon you. We seek not the ignorant." [Qur'an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:55]

## Duties

O Brothers:

Fear none but Him, submit to none but Him, perform His obligations, and avoid His forbidden things. Adorn yourselves with virtues and train yourselves through trials. Be strong by your morals, honoured with the honour Allah has given to believers and the dignity of the righteous.

Cling to the Qur'an, studying it together, and to the pure Sunnah, remembering it together. Be people of action, not people of disputation; for when Allah guides a people, He guides them to action, and no people go astray after guidance except that they are given to disputation.

Love one another among yourselves, and be most careful to preserve your bond, for it is the secret of your strength and the basis of your success. Persevere until Allah opens for you and your people with right, and He is the best of openers.

Listen and obey your leadership in hardship and ease, willingly and unwillingly, for it is the symbol of your idea and the link between you in your call.

After that, await the victory and support of Allah; the opportunity is at hand, no doubt:

> "And that day the believers will rejoice in the victory of Allah. He gives victory to whom He wills, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Merciful." [Qur'an, Ar-Rūm 30:4–5]

And guide us and you upon the paths of peace. May Allah grant us and you success in what He loves and is pleased with. Forgive us our living and our dead, our present and our absent, our young and our old, our males and our females. O Allah, to whomsoever of us You give life, make his life a life of faith and righteousness, and whomsoever of us You take in death, make his death a death of martyrdom. You are our Protector, and You are the best of helpers.
