# Epistle 5: The Economic System

## 1. Sufficiency and the Economic System

> "And [mention] when your Lord proclaimed: 'If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.'" [Qur'an, Ibrāhīm 14:7]

These are among the principles upon which the economic system of Islam rests. It is the system that makes sufficiency a goal, not luxury; that regards need, not caprice, as the measure; that makes public benefit, not personal profit, the standard of economic transactions; and that links the wealth of the Ummah to the wealth of the individual.

Allah the Exalted says:

> "And do not give the weak-minded your property, which Allah has made a means of support for you." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:5]

Among the basic principles that arise from this, and that Islam has made a foundation of its economic order, is that wealth is the life-blood of the community and the basis of its security and strength. Therefore, the preservation of this wealth — in its totality and in its details — is a duty upon the Muslim state and the Muslim individual, and the wasting, dissipation, or destruction of it is forbidden.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "No one has ever eaten better food than that which is the fruit of his own handiwork." And he said: "The son of Ādam has no right except to these things: a house in which to live, a garment with which to cover his nakedness, a piece of bread and water." In another narration: "Sufficiency for the son of Ādam is a garment to cover his nakedness, and food for his day."

These texts indicate that the economic system of Islam is built upon sufficiency and the fulfillment of genuine need; and that the acquisition of wealth is not an end in itself, but a means to sustain life, to strengthen the community, and to enable the worship of Allah.

## 2. The Spirit of the Islamic Economic System

That is the spirit of the economic system of Islam, and a summary of its rules in the briefest form. Each of these rules has an elaboration that would fill large volumes. If we were guided by that guidance and walked in its light, we would find in it abundant good.

### Monetary Independence

We have already mentioned some of the principles upon which the Islamic economic system rests, and the spirit that governs those principles. From the correct application of these principles there necessarily follows a sound economic situation, the first requirement of which is the independence of our currency and its reliance upon our own reserves and our own gold, not upon the debts of the British Treasury, the British Mint, and the British National Bank — even though its seat may be Egypt.

Consider the noble verse:

> "And do not give the weak-minded your property, which Allah has made a means of support for you." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:5]

Among the gravest forms of violating this divine command is that a people should surrender its efforts and its products in return for pieces of paper that have no value except through British authority. If Egypt resolves its affairs and reviews its transactions, it will undoubtedly reach this independence. We have already broken away from the sterling bloc, and we have begun to nationalize the National Bank, and we have demanded the many debts owed us by the English. All of these and similar measures secure the Egyptian currency; so if Allah brings them about — and we have prepared for them with every preparation — He will save them.

Perhaps it is fitting that I write these words at a time when negotiations between Egypt and England concerning the sterling balances are reported, or are about to be reported, as having failed due to English intransigence and their insistence that they will not pay more than twelve million for the year 1948, while Egypt requests a modest sum of eighteen million.

The weakness of control over the currency and reliance upon others has produced the inflation, the high cost of living, the difficulty of importing and exporting, by whose fire the people now suffer.

This is no novelty in the history of civilized states. We know that "Baring" exploited a decision of the Ministry of Finance — that infamous exploitation — as the National Bank did when it issued paper money without anyone's signature in the year 1919, by which it put into circulation as much paper as it wished.

### Nationalizing Companies

These principles also require complete concern with nationalizing companies and replacing foreign capital with national capital whenever that is possible; and with liberating public utilities — which belong to the people — from the hand of foreigners. It is not permissible that the land, construction, transport, water, light, internal communications, external transport, even salt and soda — should be in the hands of foreign companies whose capital and profits run into millions, from which the national treasury and the national worker receive nothing but misery, hardship, and deprivation.

### Exploiting Natural Wealth

The rapid and productive exploitation of natural resources is something that Islam requires. The Book of Allah has drawn our attention to the traces of the mercy of the Creator of existence and the good things He has placed in the earth and in the heavens, and has expanded in its rulings on mines (*rikāz*), and has urged the seeking of good wherever it may be.

Among us there are resources in the water, resources in the deserts, and resources everywhere, which are diminished only by directed thought, driving determination, and working hands. Then after that read whatever you will of the verse:

> "Do you not see that Allah has sent down rain from the sky and We brought forth thereby fruits of varying colors? And in the mountains are tracts white and red of varying shades and [others] very black. And among people and moving creatures and grazing livestock are various colors similarly. Indeed, in that are signs for those who remember." [Qur'an, Faṭir 35:27-28]

Those here who know the created things and the good that is in them for people, and who behold in the subtlety of their making the power of Allah, are the scholars, both the specialists and the ordinary believers. Allah created the earth and the heavens.

### Large National Projects

Concern for the great national projects whose time has grown long, and which have been delayed by slackness and laziness, or buried by party spirit, or concealed by personal rivalries, or destroyed by foreign intrigues and forbidden bribery — all of these must be directed anew to their purposes. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah loves that when one of you does a thing, he does it well." [Muslim]

We would have profited if the Aswan Dam project had actually been realized from the year 1927 — as the late Ṭalʿat Ḥarb, may Allah be pleased with him, sought to advance projects there. Many projects have been studied and investigated, then placed on the shelf and left for a long time before the war. There is no excuse for this negligence when the need is severe and urgent and the matter does not admit delay.

Shake the dust from the files of these projects and re-examine them carefully and diligently:

> "And Allah will make evident those who believe, and He will make evident the hypocrites." [Qur'an, Al-'Ankabūt 29:11]

### Industry

The turn toward industry is part of the spirit of Islam. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah loves the believer who is skilled in a craft." [Aḥmad] And the Book of Allah praised those who work with their hands: "The one who works with his hands will be forgiven." Dāwūd and Sulaymān are set before us as examples of this industrial progress, and we are told of the refinements of civilization in their times that amazed people. They made use of the strength of the jinn and the devils.

It is not permissible for the Ummah that reads in its Book the praise of Dāwūd, peace be upon him — that "We taught him the making of coats of mail to protect you from your enemy" — not to have an arms factory. And it reads of Sulaymān:

> "And to Solomon [We subjected] the wind, blowing a month's journey in the morning and a month's journey in the evening, and We made flow for him a spring of [liquid] copper. And among the jinn were those who worked for him by permission of his Lord. And whoever deviated among them from Our command — We will make him taste of the punishment of the Blaze. They made for him what he willed of enclosures and statues and bowls like reservoirs and stationary kettles. [We said], 'Work, O family of David, in gratitude.'" [Qur'an, Sabaʾ 34:12-13]

And so it is not fitting that we should not have a great foundry or a complete factory for metal tools. Then Allah says:

> "And We sent down iron, in which is mighty power and benefits for the people." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥadīd 57:25]

Yet we neglect what we have of this iron — one of the finest kinds, which experts estimate will suffice the world for two hundred years.

All of this is forbidden negligence!

## 3. The Practical Economic Reform

In the previous chapter I presented some aspects of the theoretical principles of the Islamic economic system. In this chapter I present some practical applications of the economic reform we call for.

### The System of Mortgages in Egypt

The spirit of upright Islam and its fundamental economic rules require us to reconsider the system of mortgages in Egypt. Large mortgage banks should be abolished, their owners compensated for their rights in a way that is better for them and for society, and small, cooperative banks established so that the poor and destitute may find in this homeland what concerns them and matters to them, and so that the government may extend its hand to these small owners until they thrive.

### Reforming Taxation

The spirit of Islam in its economic legislation requires first and foremost the Zakāh tax.

There is in the world no legislation more just than a tax on capital itself, not merely on profit. For this is one of the means of preventing the accumulation of wealth and keeping it in circulation. Wealth was only made a means of this circulation, from which every person in whose hands this circulating wealth rests derives benefit.

Islam made the expenditure of Zakāh purely social, so that it may be a healing for the deficiency and shortcoming that human feeling and sound patriotism would remedy; for through it society is purified, souls are refined, and people are ennobled:

> "Take, [O Muḥammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:103]

Therefore, it is necessary to care for social taxes on a progressive basis — according to capital, not profit — from which the poor are naturally exempt, and which are taken from the rich and well-to-do and spent in raising the standard of living by every possible means. It is one of the elegant rulings of ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, that he imposed heavy taxes on grapes because they are the fruit of the rich, and a negligible tax on dates because they are the food of the poor. Thus he was the first among rulers and princes — may Allah be pleased with him — to observe this social principle.

### Combating Usury

The spirit of Islam requires us to combat usury (*ribā*) decisively, to prohibit it, and to abolish every transaction based upon it. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in his Farewell Sermon: "Indeed, usury is forbidden; and the first usury I abolish is that of al-ʿĀṣib ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib." And the Messenger of Allah spoke the truth.

In the past, reformers used to avoid saying these words, lest they be told that it was impossible and that the entire economic wheel would stop. But today this argument has collapsed and has no value, after Russia has prohibited usury and made it one of the gravest enormities in its house. It is shameful that Russia should precede us to this Islamic virtue. Usury is forbidden, forbidden, forbidden, and the people most entitled to forbid it are the Muslim Ummah and the Islamic states.

### Encouraging Cottage Industries

The spirit of Islam also requires encouraging cottage industries in the villages. This is the door of swift relief for those stricken families, and the door to the industrial spirit and industrial condition. The first thing these idle hands should do is spinning and weaving on small looms, soap-making, perfume-making, the making of jams, and many other kinds in which women, girls, and boys can spend their time to good profit, sparing them the misery of need and the humiliation of begging.

We have seen this with our own eyes long since in the western districts of Fūwah, Banī ʿAdī, Manfālūṭ, and other regions of Egypt. We have seen in these lands wealth and riches and the secret of livelihood. The Ministry of Social Affairs had begun this beloved project and brought in varieties of spinning, but we do not know what Allah has done with it — or what the government has done, as people say. But the matter is no longer tolerable for waiting.

### Reducing Luxuries and Contenting Ourselves with Necessities

Guiding the people to reduce luxuries and content themselves with necessities, and that the elders should set an example for the young in this, will put an end to these dissolute celebrations, forbid this corrupt extravagance, and restore the body to its dignity, modesty, composure, and reverence in homes, palaces, clubs, and salons. This is required by upright Islam, and all of it requires preparation.

All of these are duties we must carry out with the utmost determination. So, to work!

---

And now: You have seen, from what has preceded, how we are proceeding without an economic system, either in theory or in practice, and how this improvisation and haphazardness have led us into a distress that has seized the people entirely.

It is not enough that we should improvise remedies and confront circumstances with sedatives and palliatives whose reaction threatens the gravest consequences. The important thing is that we should look at matters with a comprehensive, penetrating vision, and restore them to a stable root on which we rely and upon which we build. That is nothing but the Islamic system, complete and detailed; it is the best means of deliverance.

Allah has granted us causes of economic ease and material success that He has not granted to other nations and peoples: this close bond of language, creed, interest, and history between us and the nations of Arabism and Islam. Egypt is the noblest of Allah's lands in His earth, the most favored in soil, the most temperate in climate, the richest in blessings, the most abundant in raw materials and resources of every kind.

This bond, if we use it well, provides us with the path of self-sufficiency and economic independence, and saves us from this Western domination of exports and imports and all that follows from it.

The matter requires no more than resolve, initiative, strengthening of ties, consolidation of bonds, supporting missions and studies, and striving by every means to establish a commercial fleet, and spreading the spirit of unity and cooperation between us and the nations of Arabism and the peoples of Islam.

The Egyptian people have endured long patience in the face of this harsh, severe trial, and this grinding attrition that no one can see through except by a miracle of faith. Whoever looks at the Egyptian worker, the Egyptian peasant, and the general mass of the Egyptian people is seized with wonder at what he sees of poverty and hardship.

One of the brothers who had recently come from England told me, after a short tour in Cairo: "We used to think that what the newspapers in England publish about the bad condition of the Egyptian people and the low standard of their living was merely propaganda to humiliate them. But I spent this short period in Cairo and visited some of its popular quarters, and I was saddened by what I saw." I was embarrassed by his words, but I replied on behalf of myself and the people, saying to him: "Ask these newspapers that publish this — is not this misery from the injustices of the occupation?"

And I was addressed a second time by the director of a foreign company, who said: "Are you content with the condition of these poor people?" But I replied to him also: "Do you not know that the cause of this misery is the exploitation of these companies and their extraction from these people of what equals the necessities of life?"

The matter is serious, not a jest; it has reached its limit and its utmost extent, and there must be a decisive and swift remedy. We shall not find it, as I have said, except in the medicine of upright Islam and its treatment.

So, O head of government; O heads of ministries and departments; O all those concerned with the tranquility and peace of this homeland:

Take the affairs of the matter in hand with resolution, and return to the system of Islam.

I have conveyed the message... Allah, be my witness.

— Ḥasan al-Bannā
