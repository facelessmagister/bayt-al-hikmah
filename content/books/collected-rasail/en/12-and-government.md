# The Muslim Brothers and Government

Another group of people asks: Is it in the program of the Muslim Brothers to become a government and to demand rule? What are their means to that? I do not leave these questioners in perplexity either, nor do I withhold from them the answer. The Muslim Brothers will proceed in all their steps and along all their paths according to the guidance of the upright Islam, as we stated at the beginning of this speech. This Islam that the Muslim Brothers carry with them considers government one of its pillars. It relies upon execution as it relies upon guidance. The third caliph, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "Allah restrains by the authority what He does not restrain by the Qur'an." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, made obedience one of the pillars of Islam. Government is counted in our *fiqh* books among the creeds and foundations, not among the subsidiary branches and details. Islam is government and limitation as it is legislation and instruction, as it is law and judiciary; one of these is not separated from another.

The Islamic reformer, if he is content to be a mere jurisconsult or guide who pronounces rulings, recites teachings, enumerates branches and foundations, while leaving the executors to legislate for the Ummah what Allah has not permitted and to protect by executive power transgression against His commands — the natural result will be that this reformer's voice is a cry in a valley and a puff upon ashes, as they say.

It may be imagined that Islamic reformers are limited to the rank of admonition and guidance if they find among the executors listening to Allah's commands, executing His rulings, and conveying His verses and *ḥadīth*. But as the situation now is: Islamic legislation is in one valley, and actual executive legislation is in another. The sitting back of Islamic reformers from demanding an Islamic government cannot be atoned for except by rising and wresting executive power from the hands of those who do not adhere to the rulings of upright Islam. This is clear speech that we do not bring from ourselves; rather, we declare by it the rulings of upright Islam.

Therefore the Muslim Brothers do not demand rule for themselves. If they find in the Ummah one who is prepared to undertake this matter, discharge this trust, and assume rule with an Islamic Qur'anic method, they will be his soldiers, supporters, and helpers. If they do not find him, that is part of their program, and they will work to wrest it from the hands of every government that does not execute Allah's commands.

On this basis, the Muslim Brothers are too wise and too cautious to advance to take the rule of the Ummah in its present condition. There must be a period in which their principles spread, dominate, and in which the people learn to prefer the public welfare over private interest.

A second thing that must be said in this position is that the Muslim Brothers have found in no government they have lived under — neither the present government nor the previous government nor any other party government — one willing to bear this burden, or showing the right readiness to support the Islamic idea. Let the Ummah know that, and let it demand from its rulers their Islamic rights and the works of the Muslim Brothers.

A third thing is that there is nothing more mistaken than the opinion of some people that the Muslim Brothers, at any stage of their call, were a tool for any government, executors of a goal other than their own, or workers upon a program other than their program. Let those who did not know this, among the Muslim Brothers and others, know it.
