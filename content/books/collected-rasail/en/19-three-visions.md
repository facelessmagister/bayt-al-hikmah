# Epistle 3: Three Visions and Objections

> “Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned…” [Qur’an, Ar-Rūm 30:41]

To the Prime Minister, as the first person responsible…

To the members of the previous Parliaments, in all their variety, as the official callers to the system of Islam…

To the heads of popular political, national, and social associations, as leaders of thought and movers of masses…

And to everyone who loves the public good and the sovereignty of the children of Adam…

I direct these words, fulfilling the trust and answering the call.

I have conveyed [the message]; so witness, O Allah.

## The First Vision

As for the first vision, it concerns what the situation in our beloved homeland, the Nile Valley, has reached: corruption pervading every facility, encompassing every aspect of life:

… Our national demands have not reached anything …

The morale of the people is utterly shattered, more broken than this stagnation.

Disunity and disagreement dominate the souls of leaders and rulers, the governors and the governed alike…

The administrative apparatus has been corrupted by personal ambition, party aims, bad training, weak morals, narrow centralisation, slow procedures, and flight from bearing responsibility.

The law has weakened in authority over souls and conditions because of the many tricks and exceptions that have been forced upon it.

… Severe high prices, the great number of unemployed because of works, and the lowering of the standard of living — to a degree scarcely conceivable — among the great majority of the population, with the drying up of the springs of mercy from hearts, and the hardening of cruelty, the spirit of tyranny, and domination over souls. This has begun to turn into a state of discontent, manifesting in the frequency of strikes, and appearing in many demonstrations and slogans…

Morals have lost their foundations — or nearly — and the mountain of moral [decline] has swept through every place…

Thoughts are confused, and souls are restless, scarcely settling on anything.

All of this grows day by day and doubles hour by hour, warning of a grave affliction and spreading evil if the people of reason do not attend to it before it is too late.

## The Second Vision

As for the second vision, it concerns what the situation has reached in our dear, precious Arab-Islamic lands:

*Palestine*: extended by this struggle to which this international manoeuvre — of Americans, Russians, and English alike — has led, through the agency of international Zionism that has employed Western governments and peoples, with its previous preparation, pouring terror upon the Arabs and the Muslims wherever they are…

*And newborn Pakistan*: oppressed by this armed pagan aggression, aided by the intrigues of imperialism and the weapons of imperialism in its various states, even Russia — which pretends to respect nations and peoples — also joins in attacking the newborn state, if what has reached us today by telegram and news is true.

*And Indonesia*: whose seventy million or more Muslims are pressed by Holland, which would not have broken the power of Japanese occupation except by the hands of others from the “Allied soldiers,” and which wishes to stand between the brave Muslim people and what it has the right to of freedom and independence…

*And Tripolitania and Barqa*: entangled in the snares of the plebiscite, and none knows the consequences of this political game except Allah; and indeed tomorrow is near for those who watch…

*And Tunisia*: its people ask and seek, and its struggler does what he can to break the chains and to gain its right to free, honourable life and complete independence…

And say the same of every Arab-Islamic country; you will not find a single one of them safe from the manoeuvres of aggression and the intrigues of imperialism in its political situation; and likewise, its social situation is no better than what we have described in the Nile Valley… All of us are in one trench.

## The Third Vision

As for the third vision, it concerns the degradation to which thought has descended among the leaders of states and politicians of peoples. Religion has allowed them the positions through which they could become leaders of public welfare.

The highest ideals have disappeared completely and vanished from sight. The kingdom that these people had once called the people to at the hour of difficulty, and for which they had mobilised the strength of nations against oppression and tyranny…

Social justice, the four freedoms, the principles of the United Nations Charter, etc. — this long, broad list of lofty principles and alluring goals has become a thing of the past, and is no longer in the minds of politicians and leaders. Philosophy has descended to the philosophy of material interests, imperial ambitions, spheres of influence, seizure of raw materials, all of it in a form of greed and voracity that the world has not known even after the First World War. These ideas alone have become the subject of competition between the victorious powers: Russia on one side, and America and England on the other. Each of them tries to cover its greed and ambitions with a veil of claims of sound social principles and excellent human systems, whether Communist or democratic; but behind these two words lie nothing but imperial ambitions and material interests in every place.

The result of this deviation — which in reality is a disfiguring of humanity by humans — is nothing but a third world war, armed with atomic bombs, suffocating gases, destructive weapons, and what we know and have not yet heard of from the equipment of ruin and destruction that nearly matches what the heavenly books have described of the striking calamity, the overwhelming disaster, and the day when people shall be like scattered moths and the mountains like carded wool.

This is the picture of the situation in our particular homeland, in our Arab and Islamic homeland, and in our general human homeland. If a community of the new call does not arise in the world to carry the message of truth and peace, then woe to the world and woe to humanity.

And if the remedy lies in the hand of the physician and the bottle of medicine, then it is that we reform ourselves and call others; if we succeed in that, [well and good]; and if not, we will have conveyed the message and fulfilled the trust and returned [it] to the people. It is not right at all that we despise ourselves, for one of the factors of success for those who carry messages and perform calls is that they be believers in them, sincere, and struggling in their paths, and that time await them and money anticipate them. Who will answer the call?

> “Say, ‘I advise you only to one thing, that you stand for Allah, [seeking truth] in pairs and individually, and then give thought.’ There is not in your companion any madness. He is only a warner to you before a severe punishment.” [Qur’an, Sabaʾ 34:46]

> “[And say, ‘Ours is] the religion of Allah. And who is better than Allah in [ordaining] religion? And we are worshippers of Him.’” [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:138]

## Bewilderment and the New Message

Our society today is dominated by bewilderment, and if bewilderment lasts, there is nothing behind it but revolution — a blind revolution without goal, without restraint, without system, without limits, producing nothing but ruin, destruction, and grievous loss, especially in this merciless age, which drives the people of caprice as a dog drives its mangy skin, and in a homeland that attracts every glance and is tossed about by greed from within and without.

This is a view agreed upon by all who care for the condition of this homeland; you hear it from leaders and thinkers as you hear it from ordinary people in their councils, from the gathered in their clubs, from workers in their places, from the people of the camel when you ride with them, and from the seller of vegetables when you speak with him. If we deny that, or neglect its effect, or belittle its spread, we are like the ostrich that buries its head in the sand and thinks that by this it deceives the hunter.

In the midst of this condition, patchwork administrative repair and governmental routine will not avail; nor will deep studies in overlapping committees help the despairing. Indeed, this partial, material treatment only increases their despair and pain. And the government’s attempts at fairness, reform, promises, or speeches to silence the shouting mouths, or the hungry bellies, or the naked bodies, will not be able to do so and will not reach that goal. The evidence is clear and the proof is manifest, for confusion, anxiety, and disturbance have touched souls, hearts, and minds before touching appearances and conditions. Therefore, these souls will not be convinced and will not rest except through a new message and a new form of life, in which they see a symbol of their aspirations and a way to realise their demands. Once the souls are secure with the new message as an idea and a system, they will be at peace and will try to apply it to the conditions of life. All the history of renaissances and comprehensive reforms bears witness to the truth of this:

> “Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves.” [Qur’an, Ar-Raʿd 13:11]

And from this gap, and through the application of this unchanging social law, the new principles and new calls look to penetrate into Egypt, and struggle in every possible and impossible way to seize the Egyptian souls and Egyptian hearts. Hence, many of these voices are repeated in the circulating newspapers, in councils and clubs.

Communism is serious in imposing its teachings upon the sons of this society; and flimsy imperialist democracy tries from its side to resist this flood; and some stand in the middle calling to socialism. Between all of these and our Islamic Ummah, firm and settled in these lands for fourteen centuries, dominant in them, influencing them through its wealth, majesty, awe, and sublimity — it is impossible that it should descend from its rank or abandon these lands that believed in it and strove with the noblest *jihād* to proclaim it, preserve it, and elevate it, repelling from it the raids of the crusaders, the invasions of the Tatars, and the intrigues of Zionism:

> “…but Allah is predominant over His affair, but most of the people do not know.” [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:21]

But how long will this struggle and conflict between these opinions and conditions last, which have filled hearts and minds to such a degree that if today it is small, tomorrow it will not remain so?

And how long will the people of opinion in Egypt look upon this conflict with heedlessness and distraction, as if the matter does not concern them and as if it relates to another country and other people? There is no escape for us but to choose.

If we choose today while we are content, tomorrow — nay, the very near tomorrow — we shall meet it content. And indeed I see the gleam through the ashes, and it is about to flare into a blaze.

We must choose the new life we love, for the social conditions in Egypt, with all their corruption, are no longer adequate before the new evolution in morals, thoughts, and human needs. The wise one is he who sees the matter before it happens and prepares for it.

Before us lies Communism and Socialism, both strong in the logic of today’s international alliance and in the arena of democracy, and the democrats cannot present anything other than this. And before us also lies the system of Islam, its orientation, its teachings, and its judgments.

In reality we are not innovators, nor are we free in the choice, for we have all believed in this upright Islam as religion and state, and we have considered Egypt an Islamic state, indeed the leader of the Islamic states. Its constitution has stated explicitly in Article 149: “The official religion of the state is Islam, and its language is Arabic.”

And this people — the people of the whole Nile Valley, north and south — live by this upright religion, and the non-Muslim minority of the sons of this homeland knows full well how it finds tranquillity, security, justice, and complete equality in all its teachings and judgments, for its Book says:

> “Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes — that you be righteous to them and deal justly with them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly.” [Qur’an, Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8]

The matter in this meaning is settled, and the long, broad history of noble social relations between the sons of this homeland — Muslims and non-Muslims alike — relieves us of elaboration and excess. We must record for these noble citizens that they appreciate these meanings on every occasion, and consider Islam one of the meanings of their nationality even if its judgments and teachings are not part of their creed.

Therefore, there is no escape for the Egyptian government, the Egyptian institutions, and the Egyptian parties but to fulfil their religious and national covenant to Allah and His Messenger on the day they pronounced the testimony of faith and committed themselves to Islam — and their civil, national covenant to this people on the day they issued the constitution and stipulated in it that the official religion is Islam. Otherwise, they will have betrayed their homeland and violated the trust of Allah and the people; and they must be frank with the people to define their position toward it and its position toward them. There is no solution today through evasion and deception.

This loyalty will protect the homeland from lasting social dangers, and bring tranquillity and serenity to souls and hearts. But it requires a complete reversal of trends and conditions, and the open declaration that the Nile Valley is the bearer, implementer, and conveyor of Islam’s message, without evasion, weakness, or mere words.

So why are hearts closed to this warning? Why do they not return to the lap of Islam in word, deed, and orientation?

> “But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muḥammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission.” [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:65]

O Prime Minister.

O men of noble al-Azhar…

O heads of associations, groups, and parties…

O all who care for the good of this precious homeland…

O sons of this homeland, all of you…

To you I direct the call. So [return] to the teachings of Islam:

> “[And say, ‘Ours is] the religion of Allah. And who is better than Allah in [ordaining] religion? And we are worshippers of Him.’” [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:138]

I have conveyed; so witness, O Allah.

## Objections

> “Those who believe say, ‘Why has a sūrah not been sent down?’ But when a precise sūrah is revealed and fighting is mentioned therein, you see those in whose hearts is hypocrisy looking at you with a look of one overcome by death. So woe to them. The obedient ones would be more fitting for them, and more sound in speech. And if they become resolved upon [going to] battle, it would be better for them and more suitable. But if they turn away — then indeed, Allah is Knowing of the corrupters.” [Qur’an, Muḥammad 47:20–22]

My call is strong: choose — or, more correctly and clearly, return society in all its manifestations to the foundations of upright Islam. By that our society will be cured of this deficiency, disturbance, and confusion that have encompassed everything, hindered all progress, and placed us in a state such that we cannot find the straight path to treating any of our many issues, inward and outward, except by this orientation as creed and action, with all the firmness and speed we can muster.

It may be said: How can that be, when modern civilisation in the whole world is not based on religion in any of its aspects, and the nations that today hold the reins of affairs and direct the destinies of nations and peoples have agreed to separate social life from religious creeds, to exclude religion from every sphere of life, and to confine it between conscience and mosque, and they are the only ones to have benefited from the faith that connects them to Allah — so those who say this must first study Islam, examine its teachings and judgments, and understand its true nature and peaceful character: that it is religion and state, mosque and government, this world and the Hereafter, and that it deals with the practical worldly affairs of life more than it deals with acts of worship, though it has established both upon the pillars of soundness of heart, protection of feeling, mindfulness of Allah, and purity of soul. Religion is the foundation, and we as Muslims are required that our religion and our worldly life rest upon the foundations of Islamic principles:

> “And who is better than Allah in judgment for a people who are certain [in faith]?” [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:50]

Hence the jurists distinguished, in their legislative perspective, between matters of fixed rules of transactions and worldly social affairs: they allowed scope for opinion and adaptation in the latter that they did not allow in the former, so that there should be no hardship or difficulty for people therein, for Allah intends ease for you and does not intend hardship for you, and [He has made rulings for people] in accordance with what they have introduced of corruption.

It may be said: This is a regression that takes the world back a thousand years or more. How can we adopt today a system that came to a nation that lived fourteen generations before us, in a land other than our land, and in a form of life different from our forms of life?! What about the law of evolution and the laws of progress and ascent? We say to these people also: You too do not understand the nature of upright Islam, which came to all people as a noble idea that defines the highest goals, lays down the basic principles, addresses the general issues without entangling itself in details, and leaves the social events and developments of life to act and expand as they will without colliding with anything in it. The history of Islamic legislation tells us that Ibn ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, used to give an opinion on a case one year, then when the same case was presented the following year he would give a different opinion. When asked about that, he would say: That was according to what we knew then, and this is according to what we know now — or words to that effect. And it tells us that al-Shāfiʿī, may Allah be pleased with him, established his old school in Iraq, then established his new school in Egypt in accordance with the nature of the environment and to suit the phenomena of new life, without that impairing the sound application of the general Islamic principles. So we say “al-Shāfiʿī said in the old [school]” and “al-Shāfiʿī said in the new [school],” and we see a single man in a single issue varying according to time, as Ibn ʿUmar did, or according to place, as al-Shāfiʿī did, or according to both together. We are told that ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, refrained from cutting the hand for theft in the year of famine, and a man came complaining of the theft of his servant; he brought the servants, who confessed and explained that they were unable to support themselves from one year, and [their clothes were worn]… and so on. So ʿUmar left them and warned the man, saying: If your servants steal a second time, I shall cut your hand, and consider them like a group that has been driven [by necessity]. Observe circumstances and calamities…
