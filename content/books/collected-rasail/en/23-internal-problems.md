# Epistle 7: Our Internal Problems in the Light of the Islamic System

*(Hasan al-Bannā Collection, no. 7)*

## 1. The Economic System

I have previously written a number of words under the title "Our Internal Problems in the Light of the Islamic System," in which I explained that the Islamic system of government rests on three principles:

(a) the responsibility of the ruler;

(b) respect for the will of the Ummah;

(c) and preservation of its unity.

It is indeed a fortunate coincidence that these are also the pillars of the modern parliamentary system that we have chosen for ourselves. I also explained that we do not apply either system soundly, and that as a result all affairs have become disturbed. For this matter is the root, and everything else branches from it. "Verily, in the body there is a piece of flesh: if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Verily, it is the heart." The government is, without doubt, the heart of all social reform. If its conditions are corrupt, the whole affair is corrupt; if they are sound, the whole affair is sound. I called upon those in authority to hasten to reform and to return to upright Islam so that they might take guidance from its guidance, walk in its light, and follow its path; only then can we attain the true reform we desire.

Here I take up "our economic situation" through this gate, hoping that these sincere words may find ears that heed and hearts that understand, feel the danger, and work to repel it before the disease spreads, the remedy becomes difficult, and the cauterization becomes painful. Nothing moves souls, stirs thoughts, and awakens feelings like the financial crisis that seizes the throats of the masses and prevents them from obtaining the necessities of life, let alone its luxuries. There is no crisis more violent than the crisis of the loaf of bread, no bite stronger than the bite of hunger and famine, and no need more pressing than the need for sustenance. The seeker of sustenance is relentless. A slave-girl once came to ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Ḥasan al-Shaybānī, the companion of Abū Ḥanīfah, and said, "O master, I have menstruated." He replied, "May Allah kill you! You have menstruated from my opinion forty questions."

There are truths that no one can deny or ignore:

### (a) Natural Wealth

This country is not poor by nature; indeed, it is perhaps the richest of Allah's lands in natural resources and varied wealth — agricultural, water, animal, and mineral — and in its wonderful Nile, its fertile valley, and whatever you wish of the bounty of Allah, the Exalted, upon Egypt and the people of Egypt from ancient times: "Go down to Egypt; there you will have what you asked for."

### (b) Foreign Exploitation

Among them is that the foreigners who occupied this homeland through the negligence of its people and the complaisance of its rulers, and who remain among its usurpers, are in a happier state than its own people and their children. They have placed their hands upon the best of its sources of wealth, whether as companies or individuals: industry, trade, public utilities, and principal means of transport are all in the hands of these foreigners in reality, or of foreigners who have taken Egyptian nationality as a cover while they remain attached to their own homelands and send back their gains to them. Many of these foreigners still look upon the Egyptian citizen, the Egyptian government, and the Egyptian administration with an eye that values them not and does not seek their welfare.

### (c) Opulent Wealth and Destitute Poverty

Among them is that the disparity is enormous, the gap immense, and the difference great between the various classes of this people: opulent wealth and destitute poverty, and the middle class is almost non-existent. What we call the middle class is nothing but relatively less poor people, whom we call middle-class on the principle that some evil is lighter than other evil. May Allah have mercy on our jurists who took such pains in distinguishing between the poor (*al-fuqarāʾ*) and the needy (*al-masākīn*), although both are wretched needy persons.

### (d) Economic Confusion

Among them — and this is the most serious — is that we are in the midst of this confusing, fierce, harmful struggle between economic principles, whether capitalism, socialism, or communism. Our economic affairs at a time that demands determination have become so complicated that half-measures are of no use; nothing avails now except complete clarity, precise definition of objectives, and resolute, forceful progress. Although these conditions are mixed with political considerations, in most of their forms, motives, and consequences they are economic pains and disasters. Therefore we must choose a color from among these colors, or from others if we can, so that we may live within a defined system that has its own characteristics and features, determines our principal objectives, and shows us the way to reach those objectives.

### To Islam

I believe that there is no good for us in any of these systems as a whole, for each has its glaring defects as well as its evident merits; they are systems that arose in lands other than ours, for conditions other than ours, and among societies different from our society. Moreover, we have before us the complete system that leads to comprehensive reform in the guidance of upright Islam, and which has laid for the national economy fundamental principles that, if we comprehend and apply them in a sound manner, will solve our problems and enable us to attain every merit contained in these [other] systems while avoiding every defect attached to them; and we shall know how to raise the standard of living and give rest to all classes, finding the shortest way to the hoped-for welfare.

## Bases of the Islamic Economic System

The economic system of Islam may be summarized in the following principles:

1. Regard for lawful wealth as the mainstay of life, and the obligation of diligence in acquiring it, managing it well, and making it productive.

2. Making work and earning a duty for every able person.

3. Care for the sources of natural wealth, and the obligation of benefiting from every force and substance in existence.

4. Avoidance of unlawful means of earning.

5. Narrowing the gap between the various classes, putting an end to opulent wealth and destitute poverty.

6. Social guarantees for every citizen, securing his needs and working for his comfort and happiness.

7. Encouragement to spend in good causes, making mutual support obligatory among citizens, and obligating cooperation in righteousness and piety.

8. Affirmation of the sanctity of wealth and respect for private ownership.

9. Regulation of financial transactions by just legislation, with careful attention to monetary affairs.

Whoever examines the teachings of Islam will find these principles explained in the Noble Qur'an, the Pure Sunnah, and the books of Islamic jurisprudence in the broadest detail.

### Lawful Wealth Is the Mainstay of Life

Islam has praised lawful wealth, made diligence in acquiring it obligatory, and commanded that it be well managed and made productive. It has extolled the rank of the grateful rich person who uses his wealth for the benefit of people and for the pleasure of Allah. In Islam there is none of that meaning which drives people toward poverty and destitution, by a misunderstanding of asceticism. What is mentioned in condemnation of the world, wealth, riches, and abundance is aimed only at what incites to tyranny, temptation, extravagance, assistance in sin and disobedience, and ingratitude for Allah's blessings. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said in a ḥadīth:

> "How good is lawful wealth for the righteous person!"
>
> "And do not give the weak-minded your property which Allah has made a means of support for you." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:5]

This is an indication that wealth is the support of actions. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, also spoke about wasting wealth in an improper way, saying:

> "Allah dislikes for you three things: idle speech, asking much, and wasting wealth."

And one who dies defending his wealth is a martyr, as is stated in the ḥadīth: "Whoever is killed defending his honor is a martyr; whoever is killed defending his wealth is a martyr..."

### Work for Every Able Person

Islam encourages work and earning; it regards earning as a duty for every able person according to his knowledge, and praises work in the most emphatic terms. It forbids begging, declaring that among the best of worship is work, that work is from the sunnah of the prophets, that the best of earnings is from the work of the hand and cultivation of the land, and that idleness is a burden upon society whatever the cause of that idleness — even if it is seclusion in the worship of Allah; Islam does not recognize this kind of idleness. Reliance upon Allah (*tawakkul*) is only through taking the means and [then entrusting] the outcomes; whoever lacks one of these is not truly reliant. The provision that is decreed is accompanied by constant effort. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> "And say, 'Do [as you will], for Allah will see your deeds, and [so will] His Messenger and the believers; and you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform you of what you used to do.'" [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:105]

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "No one has ever eaten anything better than what he has earned by the work of his own hand." Indeed, the Prophet Dāwūd, peace be upon him, used to eat from the work of his hand, and he would say, "I would not sit with you seeking sustenance while He Who provides me with sustenance is on His Throne." And he knew that the sky does not rain gold or silver. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "A man will continue to ask people until he comes on the Day of Resurrection with no piece of flesh on his face."

### Care for the Sources of Natural Wealth

Islam also directs attention to the sources of wealth and resources of provision in existence, encouraging their exploitation and making it obligatory to utilize all that in this wondrous universe has been made subservient to humankind for its benefit. Allah the Exalted says:

> "Do you not see that Allah has made subservient to you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth and has showered upon you His blessings, apparent and hidden?" [Qur'an, Luqmān 31:20]

> "He has made subservient to you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth, all from Him. Indeed in that are signs for a people who reflect." [Qur'an, Al-Jāthiyah 45:13]

Whoever reads the verses of the Noble Qur'an will find this explained in the broadest and clearest manner.

### Avoidance of Unlawful Means of Earning

Among its teachings is the prohibition of unlawful means of earning. Islam defines unlawful earning as what is taken without a corresponding act of work, such as usury (*ribā*), gambling (*maysir*), and lotteries, or what is acquired without right, such as fraud, theft, and cheating, or what is given as a price for something harmful, such as wine, swine, carrion, and the like. All such means of earning are forbidden by Islam and not recognized by it.

### Narrowing the Gap Between the Classes

Islam has worked to narrow the gap between the classes by prohibiting hoarding and the outward displays of luxury for the rich, and by encouraging the raising of the standard of living among the poor. It has affirmed their right in the wealth of the state and the wealth of the rich, and has described the practical way to that. It has strongly encouraged spending in good causes and urged generosity, condemned miserliness, boastful expenditure, and causing harm, and laid down the path of zakāh and the goodly loan in quest of the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted, and in hope of His reward.
