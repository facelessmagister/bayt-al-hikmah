# The Muslim Brothers and the Caliphate

It is fitting, in completing this research, to present the position of the Muslim Brothers toward the caliphate and what relates to it. The Muslim Brothers believe that the caliphate is the symbol of Islamic unity, the manifestation of the connection between Islamic nations, and an Islamic rite whose consideration is obligatory upon Muslims. The caliph is the locus of many rulings in the religion of Allah. For this reason the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them all, gave precedence to attending to its matter over burying the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, until they finished with that matter and were reassured of its accomplishment.

The *aḥādīth* that have come concerning the obligation of appointing the Imam, the rulings of Imamate, and the details connected with it leave no room for doubt that it is obligatory upon Muslims to think about the matter of their caliphate from the time it deviated from its paths and was abolished completely until now. For this reason the Muslim Brothers have placed the idea of the caliphate and working for its restoration at the head of their program. Yet at the same time they believe that this requires many preliminary measures that are indispensable, and that the direct step toward restoring the caliphate must be preceded by steps: there must first be cultural, social, and economic cooperation among all the Islamic peoples; then follows the formation of alliances and treaties, the convening of conferences among these lands. The powerful Islamic Congress for the Palestine cause and the dispatch of Islamic delegations to London to proclaim the rights of the Arabs in the Blessed Land are two blessed, broad steps along this path.

After that comes the formation of a league of Islamic nations, so that when that becomes firmly established for the Muslims, the Imam will be the bond that gathers them, the meeting of scattered parts, the refuge of hearts, and the shade of Allah upon the earth.
