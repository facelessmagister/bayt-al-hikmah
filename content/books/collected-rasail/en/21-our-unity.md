# Epistle 5: Our Unity in the Light of the Islamic Orientation

> "Indeed, this, your Ummah, is one Ummah, and I am your Lord; so fear Me." [Qur'an, Al-Mu'minūn 23:52]

The matter, in our particular national cause, has been placed before my people as the orientation of Islam: the shortest path whenever it is decisive and noble, and which is nothing more than renunciation of enmity and preparation of the means of resistance. Once we do that, there is no doubt that we shall emerge from this bewilderment and find the right answer to what every tongue asks and what every citizen repeats in his mind: "What shall we do now?"

The truth is that we do not act not because we cannot, but because we do not wish to do anything. We flee from accepting work, flee from the weight of sacrifices and the cost of struggle, always seek ease and comfort, and think of nothing else. We imagine that freedom and independence fall from heaven without effort, but heaven does not rain gold or silver, nor does it pour down freedom or independence. If we were truly serious in seeking them, we would walk the path once we knew it in two words: "we renounce" and "we strive," and victory thereafter is from Allah.

Now I turn to another subject connected with our general national cause, which governs the issues of the Arab Ummah in all its aspects and the whole Islamic world, to see how it too is solved by the orientation of upright Islam.

Islam is a universal message that came for all nations and peoples alike, without distinction between Arab and non-Arab, East and West:

> "Blessed is He who sent down the Criterion upon His servant that he might be a warner to the worlds." [Qur'an, Al-Furqān 25:1]

It called for the elimination of racial and ethnic distinctions, for human brotherhood, and raised the banner of universality among people for the first time in human history:

> "O mankind, fear your Lord, who created you from one soul and created from it its mate and dispersed from both of them many men and women. And fear Allah, through whom you ask one another, and the wombs [that bore you]. Indeed, Allah is ever, over you, an Observer." [Qur'an, An-Nisā' 4:1]

Islam also established, as a matter of priority, the strongest meaning of brotherhood among those who believe in it, adhere to it, and believe in its message, until it made brotherhood one of the meanings of faith — indeed, the whole of its meanings:

> "The believers are but brothers." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

> "A Muslim is the brother of a Muslim: he does not wrong him nor does he forsake him." [Bukhārī, 2442; Muslim, 2580]

> "The believers in their mutual love, compassion, and sympathy are like one body: when one of its limbs complains, the rest of the body responds with sleeplessness and fever." [Bukhārī, 6011; Muslim, 2586]

> "The believer to the believer is like a solid building, one part strengthening another." [Bukhārī, 2446; Muslim, 2585]

When the Muslims faced the whole world as one rank and one word, under this true, sincere brotherhood, the kingdoms of bare administrative or political bonds vanished before them in an hour of a day, and they advanced together without the tyranny of the Romans and the Persians alike, forming a vast empire stretching from its heights to its depths, a state of knowledge and civilisation, power and radiance.

But on the day they turned away from the power of their book and did not follow its guidance —

> "And do not dispute and [thus] lose courage and [then] your strength would depart; and be patient. Indeed, Allah is with the patient." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:46]

— the disease of [earlier] nations crept into them: giving precedence to excessive material interests over abiding faith-brotherhood. That empire was torn to pieces, played with by internal and external ambitions, and its affair finally ended, after the First World War, in collapse and falling into the hands of non-Muslim adversaries who seized lands, took control of its affairs, and divided them among themselves. They thought that the matter of Islam was ended and that the Crusader war had ended in the best conclusion.

The great intrigue that burst upon the Muslims, first in minds and hearts, then in their lands and countries, was their infection with nationalism and chauvinism, each nation priding itself on its race, forgetting what Islam brought of the abolition of the partisanship of ignorance and of boasting in races, colours, and lineages.

The Second World War ended with the destruction of the modern racism in Europe — the racism of Nazism and Fascism. After it we saw the major European states striving eagerly to gather and unite, sometimes by treaties and sometimes by interests. Russia seeks to gather the Slavic bloc with all its peoples under the banner of the Soviet Union; England and America unite by language and race, then agree afterwards on [dividing] the nations of the world and regions of influence on earth by interests and vital necessities, covering this rivalry while the United Nations is formed to reassure people that it works for the public good and for the elevation of humankind. We have seen these same states themselves gathering against our national rights and failing us in every one of our foreign causes, whether presented to the Security Council or to the General Assembly of the United Nations, as happened in the cause of Egypt, in the cause of Palestine, and in the cause of Indonesia.

Before all these new, confused conditions, and before the similarity and conformity of our causes — for they are all one cause, whose meaning is the completion of freedom and independence and the breaking of the chains of exploitation and imperialism — we must return anew to what Islam imposed upon its sons from the first day, when it made unity one of the meanings of faith. We must rally and unite. We have begun with the Arab League; and although it has not yet attained complete stability, it is nevertheless a blessed, auspicious opening in every respect.

We must support it, strengthen it, and free it from every factor of weakness and instability surrounding it. Then we must widen the circle until the bond of the Ummah of Islam — Arab and non-Arab — is realised, so that it becomes the nucleus of (the League of Islamic Nations), Allah willing.

This method, added to our particular means for every nation — renunciation and *jihād* — is another meaning of power: unity and gathering. By it we can liberate ourselves and preserve the world balance among ambitious nations and states competing for gains and dominion.

I ask, for the realisation of these steps, the Arab and Islamic governments all together, and every caller to reform among these peoples, men and people of houses: today I direct the call:

> "And hold fast to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:103]

I have conveyed; so witness, O Allah.
