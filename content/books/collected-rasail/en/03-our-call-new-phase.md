# Epistle 3: Our Call in a New Phase — Part 1

## The Challenge and the Rush

The Muslim Brotherhood believe that Allah, Exalted and Glorified, sent down the Qur'an and commanded His servants to follow the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and that the happiness of nations, their revival, and their felicity are bound up with that. That is corroborated by the words of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered prophet, whom they find written [i.e., described] in what they have of the Torah and the Gospel, enjoining upon them what is right and forbidding them what is wrong and making lawful for them what is good and forbidding for them what is evil and releasing them from their burdens and the shackles which were upon them." [Qur'an, Al-A'rāf 7:157]

And in confirmation of the words of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, in the noble hadith whose meaning is: "By Allah, no people turn away from evil except that He punishes them for it."

If you contemplate the teachings of Islam, you will find that it laid down the most correct foundations, the best systems, and the soundest laws for the life of the individual—man and woman—for the life of the family in its formation and dissolution, and for the life of nations in their rise and decline. It also resolved the intellectual dilemmas that stood before reformers and leaders of nations.

Individualism, nationalism, socialism, capitalism, medicine, war, the distribution of wealth, the connection between producer and consumer, and whatever is near or far from these inquiries that occupy the statesmen and social philosophers—all of these, we believe, Islam entered into and laid down for humankind the systems that enable them to benefit from what is wholesome and to avoid the dangers and calamities that follow from its neglect. This is not the place to elaborate on all of that. Rather, we shall state what we believe and explain to people what we call them to, and we shall have further occasions to detail what we say.

Since the Muslim Brotherhood believe this, they are duty-bound to call people to act so that the principles of Islam become the foundations upon which the revival of the modern East is built in every sphere of life. They believe that every manifestation of modern civilization that conflicts with the principles of Islam and collides with the rulings of the Qur'an is a corrupt and evil experiment from which the Ummah will emerge only after great sacrifices, to no avail. Therefore, it is better for nations that seek revival to take the shortest path by following the rulings of Islam.

The Muslim Brotherhood do not restrict this call to one Islamic country rather than another. Rather, through their messages, they hope that it will reach the ears of leaders and eminent men in every country whose sons live under Islam. They are not slow to seize this opportunity in which the Islamic countries are united and are trying to build their future upon firm foundations of progress and civilization.

## Beware of Deviation

What the Muslim Brotherhood most fear is that peoples may be swept along by those systems. The soundest of those systems is for Islam to be a general constitution, and every general constitution must derive its provisions from the rulings of the Noble Qur'an. The nation whose religion is its foundation—every article that Islam does not allow and whose rulings do not sanction must be struck out, lest contradiction appear in the basic law of the state.

### Reform the Law

Every nation has a law by which its sons are governed; and this law must be derived from the rulings of Islamic Shariah, taken from the Noble Qur'an, in agreement with the principles of Islamic jurisprudence (*fiqh*, فقه). In the Islamic Shariah, and in what the Muslim legislators laid down, there is what fills gaps, meets needs, heals ailments, and produces the best results and most blessed fruits. Were the limits of Allah to be applied, they would restrain the transgressor, repel aggression even if it had taken root, and spare governments futile wars. Experience confirms and supports this, and the principles of modern legislation call for it and invite to it. Allah, Exalted and Glorified, has made it obligatory:

> "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed—then it is those who are the disbelievers." [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:44]

### Reform the Manifestations of Society

In every nation there are manifestations of social life that governments supervise, laws regulate, and authorities direct. Every Eastern Islamic nation must strive to ensure that all these manifestations are consistent with the manners and teachings of Islam and the spirit of its legislation. Official prostitution is a stain of shame on the brow of any nation that values virtue—how much more so upon Islamic nations whose religion imposes upon them a stern fight against fornication and adultery:

> "And do not let pity for them withhold you from [carrying out] the religion of Allah, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. And let there be [arising] from you a group inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:104]

The wine-shops on the most visible streets and prominent places, the large signboards for alcoholic drinks, and these open, clear advertisements for vices are manifestations contrary to religion and forbidden by the Noble Qur'an in the strongest terms.

### Fight Licentiousness

This alluring licentiousness, this captivating amusement, and this idle play in the streets, clubs, summer resorts, and places of recreation contradict what Islam has commanded of chastity, noble virtue, dignity, devotion to earnestness, and avoidance of triviality:

> "Indeed, Allah loves the lofty matters and hates the trivial ones."

All these manifestations and their like must be met by the Islamic nations with all the energy and resources of their authority and power; they must not be remiss in that and must not fear reproach.

### Organize Education

Every nation and every Islamic people has a policy in education and the upbringing of the youth, and in building the men of the future upon whom the new life of the nation depends. This policy must be founded upon wise principles that guarantee the youth religious immunity, moral protection, and knowledge of their religion, their past glory, and their expansive civilization.

This is some of the many principles that the Muslim Brotherhood wish the Islamic nations to observe in building modern civilization. They direct this call to all Muslims, peoples and governments alike. Their means of attaining these sublime Islamic goals is one: that they make known what Islam contains of excellence and wisdom, so that when people are reminded of it and convinced of its benefit, that will produce action for it and a departure from what contradicts it:

> "Say, \"This is my way; I invite to Allah with insight, I and those who follow me. And exalted is Allah; and I am not of those who associate others with Him.\"" [Qur'an, Yūsuf 12:108]

## Benefit from the Brotherhood of Muslims

Islam calls to unity among its sons and followers. Thus Allah says:

> "And hold fast to the rope of Allah all together and do not become divided. And remember the favor of Allah upon you—when you were enemies and He brought your hearts together and you became, by His favor, brothers." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:103]

The Noble Qur'an says in another verse:

> "Indeed, the believers are brothers." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

And in another verse:

> "And the believers, men and women, are protecting friends one of another." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:71]

The noble Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Be servants of Allah as brothers." Likewise, the first Muslims—may Allah be pleased with them—drew from Islam this brotherly spirit. Their creed in the religion of Allah stirred feelings of love and affection, and produced manifestations of brotherhood and mutual acquaintance, so that they became one man, one heart, and one hand. Allah praised them for that in His Book, saying, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And brought together their hearts. If you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have brought their hearts together; but Allah brought them together." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:63]

That Emigrant who had left his family, parted from his land in Mecca, and abandoned his wealth found the sons of Islam in Medina awaiting him with longing, love for him, and joy at his arrival. They did not know him previously, nor did any kinship, alliance, or worldly benefit draw them to him. Rather, it was the creed of Islam that made them love him, bind themselves to him, and count him a part of themselves and a partner in their souls. As soon as he arrived at the mosque, the Helpers of the Aws and Khazraj would vie with one another to take him to their homes, preferring him to themselves and their families, and devoting their lives and wealth to him. Each would cling to his request until the matter had to be decided by drawing lots. Imām al-Bukhārī narrated to this effect:

> "No Emigrant came to the Helpers except that he was assigned to one of them by lot."

And the Qur'an has immortalized this virtue forever, ever casting a shining light upon the pages of years in the words of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And those who came after them, saying, \"Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith and put not in our hearts [resentment] toward those who have believed. Our Lord, indeed You are Kind and Merciful.\"" [Qur'an, Al-Ḥashr 59:10]

Wait—the Qur'an has recorded that virtue in another place as well:

> "And [also for] those who were settled in the Home [i.e., al-Madinah] and [adopted] the faith before them. They love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given, but give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation. And whoever is protected from the stinginess of his soul—it is those who will be the successful." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥashr 59:9]

Upon this, the sons of Islam proceeded, especially the first generation, among whom was found this brotherly affection. There was no difference between Emigrant and Helper, between Meccan and Medinan, until the noble Messenger praised the Ashʿarites of Yemen with the words, peace and blessings be upon him, whose meaning is:

> "The best of people are the Ashʿarites: when they travel or settle, they gather what they have and place it in a garment, then divide it equally among themselves."

If you read the Noble Qur'an, the hadiths of the noble Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the biographies of the righteous Muslims of this religion, you will find in all of that what refreshes your eyes, lifts your head, and gladdens your heart.

## Brotherhood That Proclaims Humanity

This creed has sweet fruits that we must mention and speak to you about, for there is in them delight, pleasure, loftiness, and benefit.

The first of them is that Islamic conquest (*al-futūḥāt al-Islāmiyyah*, الفتوحات الإسلامية) is unlike any conquest in history, whether in its aim, its course and administration, or its results and benefit. For the Muslim conqueror only opens a land when he opens it in order to exalt in it the word of God and to raise its horizon by the sunnah of the Noble Qur'an. When the Muhammadan creed shines upon the souls of its people, differences vanish, love of Allah appears, justice and equity spread, and love and brotherhood abound. There is no victorious conqueror and defeated opponent; rather, they are brothers who love one another and are united. From this, the idea of communalism melts away like snow under the rays of a strong, shining sun—while the idea of Islamic brotherhood, which the Qur'an plants in the souls of all who follow it, remains.

That noble conqueror, before he conquers anyone he conquers and defeats anyone he defeats, has sold himself and his family, stripped himself of his tribalism and communalism, in the path of Allah. Thus he does not conquer for tribal fanaticism, nor defeat for communal glory, nor strive for kinship; rather, when he acts, he acts for Allah and for Him alone, without partner. Among the most magnificent things I have seen of sincerity in aim and purity of soul from death is what is reported in the noble hadith, whose meaning is: A man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Messenger of Allah, I love to fight in the path of Allah, and I love to have my station witnessed." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, remained silent and did not answer him. Then the noble verse was revealed:

> "So whoever would hope for the meeting with his Lord—let him do righteous work and not associate anyone in the worship of his Lord." [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:110]

See how Islam regards a person's desire for praise and commendation—which is a hidden form of polytheism in the nature of souls—as something from which the noble aim must be purified and exalted! Is there any purer sincerity than that a man forget himself in the path of his aim? Do you think that a religion which requires its adherent to strip himself of self, subdue his emotions, his ambitions, and his desires until his striving is purely for Allah alone—do you think such a religion can possibly be a religion of fanaticism or communalism? By Allah, no.

That defeated one, whom fate has willed to be blessed by Islam, gives with his own hands what he left of his country and his land so that no one may seize it, exploit it like a humiliated slave, or monopolize its good things apart from him. Rather, he left what he left because he mixed it with himself and his soul, and he calls out with sincerity: "For you is what is for us, and upon you is what is upon us; and the Book of Allah, Exalted and Glorified, will judge between us." Thus both vanish in their aim and sacrifice in the path of their principle, leaving what they left so that the light of Allah may encompass humanity and the sun of the Noble Qur'an may rise upon it. In that is the completion of its happiness—if only they knew.

## The Horizon of the Islamic Homeland

The second fruit is that Islamic brotherhood makes every Muslim believe that every inch of earth in which a brother lives by the religion of the Noble Qur'an is a piece of the general Islamic land upon which Islam obliges all its sons to work for its preservation and happiness. From this, the Islamic homeland expands beyond the limits of geographical and blood nationalism to a nationalism of sublime principles and pure, sound ideas—principles and ideas that adorned the world with guidance and light. When Islam makes its sons feel this meaning and establishes it in their souls, it imposes upon them a necessary duty to defend the land of Islam from the aggression of aggressors, to deliver it from the wrath of usurpers, and to fortify it against the ambitions of encroachers.

## A Long Road

I hope that these successive words have made clear to the honoured readers the aim of the Muslim Brotherhood and have explained to them, at least to some extent, their methods in advancing toward that aim. I have spoken before to many of our brothers who are absent from Islam on a long discourse similar to these words, which the readers saw under the title "To What Do We Call People?"

Those I spoke to listened with praiseworthy attention; and we used to follow the discussion point by point until we emerged from the conversation fully convinced of the nobility of the aim and the success of the means. My amazement was great when I saw among them something near to consensus that this path is long despite its sure success, and that the sweeping, continuous currents in the country are strong enough to carry despair to hearts and let despondency seize souls. So that the honoured readers may not find in themselves the feeling that those earlier speakers found, I wished this epistle to be filled with hope, overflowing with certainty of success, Allah willing, in the beginning and the end. I shall therefore confine the subject to two positive outlooks.

### A Sociological Outlook

The sociologists say that the realities of today are the dreams of yesterday, and the dreams of today are the realities of tomorrow. This is an outlook confirmed by reality and strengthened by reason. Who would have believed that scholars would reach the discoveries and inventions they have recently achieved only a few years before they occurred? Indeed, the great men of knowledge themselves denied them at first, until reality established them and proof confirmed them. The examples of this are many, and are so obvious that we need not lengthen their mention.

### A Historical Outlook

The revivals of nations all began in a state of weakness; history tells us that patience, steadfastness, trial, and hardship raised these weak beginnings, with limited means, to the peak of power and success. Who would have believed that the Arabian Peninsula—that barren, harsh desert—would spread light and knowledge, and would dominate by the spiritual and political influence of its sons the greatest states of the world? Who would have believed that Abū Bakr—that tender, gentle heart, whom the people rose against and whose supporters were few in a matter in which he could, in a single day, dispatch eleven armies to suppress the rebels, set right the crooked, chastise the tyrant, take vengeance upon the apostates, and restore to Allah the Zakah withheld by the deniers? Who would have believed that that weak, hidden family of Banū Hāshim and Banū al-ʿAbbās could overturn that mighty, vast kingdom in a night or a morning, when it was once exposed only to killing, expulsion, exile, and threat? Who would have believed that Ṣalāḥ al-Dīn al-Ayyūbī, for long years, would chase the kings of Europe before him, routed, when their numbers were plentiful and their armies conspicuous, until twenty-five kings from among their greatest gathered against him?

That was in ancient history. In modern history the wonder of it is even greater. Who would have believed that King ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz Āl Saʿūd, after his family had been scattered, his people exiled, and his kingdom seized, would recover this kingdom with a mere score of men, and that he would afterwards become one of the hopes of the Islamic world in restoring its glory and reviving its unity? Who would have believed that that German worker—Hitler—would reach the power and success he attained?

### Is There Another Way?

There are two negative considerations that lead to the same result and turn the heart of the worker to work with strength and soundness. The first is that this path is long. Experience has confirmed the correctness of this theory.

### Duty First

The second is that the worker works to perform his duty first, then for the reward of the Hereafter second, and then for benefit third. If he works, he has performed his duty and won the reward of Allah without doubt, so long as its conditions are met. As for benefit, it is left to Allah: an opportunity may come in His reckoning that produces the most blessed fruits. But if he sits back, he incurs the sin of negligence, loses the reward of the deed, and is certainly deprived of benefit. Which of the two parties is better in station and more excellent in standing? The Noble Qur'an pointed to this clearly and plainly in the noble verse:

> "So let them laugh a little and [then] weep much as recompense for what they used to earn." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:82]

This is one of the two proofs; the second is in the story of a nation that was moving from weakness.

## The Story of a Nation Moving from Weakness

Come now before a repeated scene, a lesson from the lessons of the ages, in which a tyrannical giant took a people as brutal servants, soldiers, and vassals, enslaving one of the noble, unified nations. Then Allah, Exalted and Glorified, willed to prepare for this people the means of its stolen freedom, its seized dignity, its lost glory, and its vanished might. The first ray of this people's dawn of freedom was the shining of a light upon the forehead of its great leader, Mūsā, while he was yet a child. We recite to you from the news of Mūsā and Pharaoh in truth, for a people who believe:

> "Indeed, Pharaoh exalted himself in the land and made its people into factions, oppressing a section among them, slaughtering their sons and keeping their females alive. Indeed, he was of the corrupters. And We wanted to confer favor upon those who were oppressed in the land and make them leaders and make them the inheritors, and establish them in the land…" [Qur'an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:4–6]

### Leadership

And we, after this, stand before this giant when he has reached his utmost strength and towered as the leader of nations, after his soul has grown accustomed to tyranny and his conscience to crime, so he has become haughty and fled from right. Then Allah fashioned him for Himself and for the burden of His message, and entrusted to him the deliverance of his people. So he comes, supported by certainty, to face that tyrant, asking him to restore freedom to his people, to leave dignity to them, and to follow him. How magnificent is that sharp reply when the Noble Qur'an relates the words of the great Messenger:

> "And mention in the Book [the story of] Mūsā. Indeed, he was chosen, and he was a messenger and a prophet. And We called to him from the right side of the mount and brought him near, confiding." [Qur'an, Maryam 19:51–52]

Is the favor upon you, O tyrant, that you were brought up among the Children of Israel—servants of Allah, not your servants—that you should enslave a people and humiliate my nation and degrade my community? It is a cry of truth issuing from the mouth of the noble Prophet, shaking the throne of Pharaoh and shaking his kingdom:

> "So go to Pharaoh and say, \"Indeed, we are messengers from the Lord of the worlds: Send with us the Children of Israel.\" He said, \"Did we not raise you among us as a child, and you remained among us for years of your life? And [then] you did your deed which you did, and you are of the ungrateful.\" He said, \"I did it, while I was of those astray. So I fled from you when I feared you. Then my Lord granted me judgment and prophethood and made me of the messengers.\"" [Qur'an, Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:16–21]

### Struggle

And we now witness the wrath of power upon right: how it rises up against it, devours from it, torments its people, and humiliates its supporters. Then how the hope of the oppressed gushes forth over all that, and how their leaders fill them with sweet hopes and pleasant expectations until a flood of yearning flows into their souls:

> "And the eminent among the people of Pharaoh said, \"Will you leave Mūsā and his people to cause corruption in the land and abandon you and your gods?\" He said, \"We will kill their sons and keep their women alive; indeed, we are subjugators over them.\" Mūsā said to his people, \"Seek help through Allah and be patient. Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah. He causes to inherit it whom He wills of His servants. And the [best] outcome is for the righteous.\"" [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:127–128]

### Faith

How magnificent it is to witness that eternal model of steadfastness, patience, clinging to the handhold of truth, and disdaining everything, even life itself, in the path of faith and creed, among the followers of this leader who believed in his call and challenged this giant with firmness and resolve:

> "So decide whatever you are going to decide. You can only decide for this worldly life. Indeed, we have believed in our Lord that He may forgive us our sins and what you compelled us to do of magic. And Allah is better and more enduring." [Qur'an, Tāʾ Hā 20:72–73]

### Victory

If we see all of that, we see its outcome in the fifth part—what will make you know what it is? Triumph, success, victory, achievement, and good tidings conveyed to the oppressed; hope realized for those who hope; and the morning of manifest truth resounding through the horizons of the earth:

> "O Children of Israel, We have saved you from your enemy…" [Qur'an, Ash-Shuʿarāʾ 26:65]

## An Address to the Rulers

In Rajab of the year 1351 AH, the Imām and martyr Ḥasan al-Bannā, the General Guide of the Muslim Brotherhood, sent this address to His Majesty King Fārūq the First, King of Egypt and the Sudan; to His Excellency Muṣṭafā al-Naḥḥās Pāshā, President of his government at that time; to Their Majesties and Highnesses the kings, princes, and rulers of the various Islamic countries; and likewise he sent it to a great number of prominent men of religion and worldly standing in these countries. Now, when this is being printed and distributed, many of the views it contains and the demands it includes still burn with zeal for every Arab and every Muslim. We ask Allah for their realization.

> In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.

All praise is due to Allah, and may Allah send blessings and peace upon our master Muhammad and upon his family and Companions.

> "Our Lord, grant us from Yourself mercy and prepare for us from our affair right guidance." [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:10]

Cairo, capital of the Egyptian lands, in Rajab al-Fard 1351 AH.

To His Majesty,

Peace, mercy, and blessings of Allah be upon you. Thereafter: I am pleased to present this epistle to your exalted station, with a firm desire to guide the nation whose affairs Allah has entrusted to you and whose concerns He has committed to you, in its new era, toward wholesome paths based on the best ways, to chart for it the finest courses, to protect it from lapses and disturbances, and to spare it lengthy, painful experiments.

We seek nothing from this except to have performed our duty and offered sincere advice… and the reward of Allah is better and purer.

### The Shepherd's Burden

Allah has entrusted to you the command of this nation, and placed its interests, its affairs, its present, and its future as a trust with you and a deposit in your hands. You are responsible for all of that before Allah, Exalted and Glorified. The present generation is a seed from your planting; what a great trust and heavier burden it is when a man is asked about a nation! The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Every one of you is a shepherd and every one of you is responsible for his flock." The just Imām ʿUmar—may Allah be pleased with him—said, "If a lost camel were to stumble in Iraq, I would fear being held responsible for it before Allah, Exalted and Glorified; how then could I be at ease over its roads?" And ʿUmar expressed the greatness of this burden in a word when he said, "I would wish to leave it neither burdened nor neglectful."

### Foundations

#### (a) The Era of Transition

The most serious thing in the life of a nation, and the first to require careful examination, is the transition from one state to another. For in it the course of the new era is laid down and its plans and foundations are drawn, by which the nation is to be raised and committed. If these plans, foundations, and courses are clear, sound, and upright, they will carry this nation to a long, far-reaching life and magnificent, glorious deeds. The leader will be glad tidings to this triumph, and its proofs in this report are great reward, lasting remembrance, fair history, and good repute.

#### (b) At a Crossroads

The matter has two aspects: first, the liberation of the nation from its political bonds so that it may attain its freedom and recover what it has lost of independence and sovereignty. Second, its renewal in order to take its path among nations and to compete with them in the ranks of social perfection. Now, as the noble treaty has laid down its burdens and the nation is entering a new era, you will see before you two paths, each of which calls you to direct the nation toward it and to walk it. Each has its characteristics, virtues, signs, results, calls, and ascents. The first is the path of Islam, its principles, foundations, civilization, and culture; the second is the Western path, with its manifestations of life, its systems, and its methods. Our creed is that the first path, the path of Islam and its principles and foundations, is the only path that must be taken and toward which the present and future nation must be directed.

#### (c) The Fruits of the Islamic Orientation

If we lead the nation along this course, we shall obtain many benefits. Among them is that the Islamic orientation is a tried method whose soundness history has witnessed, and which produced for humankind one of the strongest, best, most merciful, and most just nations toward humanity as a whole. In addition to pride in communalism and praise of pure nationalism, we build our life upon its principles and foundations and do not take from others. In that is the best measure of social and material independence after political independence. In following this method lies strengthening of Arab unity first, then Islamic unity, for it extends the whole Islamic world with its spirit, feeling, intellect, and help, and creates among us a brotherhood that renews itself, grows, and does not perish. In that is a great moral gain that no wise person would disdain.

This method is complete and comprehensive, guaranteeing the establishment of the best systems for the public life of the nation, materially and spiritually. This is the characteristic that distinguishes Islam: it comprises the systems of life for nations on two important foundations—taking what is wholesome and avoiding what is harmful.

If we follow this path, we shall be able to avoid the social problems that have befallen other nations which did not know this path or follow it. Indeed, we shall be able to solve many of the complicated problems that the present systems have failed to solve. Let me recall to you the words of Bernard Shaw:

> "How badly the world needs, in its modern age, a man like Muḥammad to solve its complicated problems as easily as one takes a cup of coffee."

And after all that, if we follow this path, Allah will be behind us with strength in weakness, delivering us in hardships, holding fast to the covenant, and guiding us forward:

> "And do not weaken in pursuit of the enemy. If you should be suffering—so are they suffering as you are suffering, but you expect from Allah what they do not expect. And Allah is Knowing and Wise." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:104]

#### (d) Western Civilization Now

To complete this discussion we say: Western civilization, which has dazzled the world for a period of time and subjected the whole world by the results of this science to its states and peoples, is now bankrupt and receding. Its foundations are crumbling and its systems and principles are collapsing. Its political systems are undermining its dictatorships, its economic systems are shaking with crises, and its unemployed and hungry millions bear witness against it. Its social systems are stained with deviant principles and revolutions erupting everywhere. Its people have puzzled over treating its condition and have lost the way. Its conferences fail, its treaties are violated, and its covenants are neglected. Its League is a phantom without spirit or influence, and the hand of the great powers is raised with others against the covenant of peace and tranquility.

In one direction, it inflicts a blow; in another, it receives a harsher blow. Thus the world, by these greedy, oppressive policies, has become like a ship in the midst of the sea, without a captain, tossed by waves from every side. Humanity is tormented, wretched, anxious, and disturbed, burned by the fires of ambition and materialism, and is in the greatest need of the gentle fountain of Islam to wash away the filth of misery and lead it to happiness.

The leadership of the world was once purely Eastern; after the Byzantines and Romans it became Western; then the Mosaic, Christian, and Muhammadan prophecies were conveyed to the East a second time. Then the East fell into its great slumber and the West launched its modern revival, according to the unchanging sunnah of Allah. The West inherited the general leadership of the world, and here it is now, wronging, harming, tyrannizing, and floundering. There is nothing for it but that a strong "Eastern" hand should overshadow it with the banner of Allah and raise over it the standard of the Qur'an, with it the strong, firm soldiers of faith. Then the world will become Muslim, secure; and all peoples will cry out:

> "Praise be to Allah, who has guided us to this; and we would never have been guided if Allah had not guided us." [Qur'an, Al-A'rāf 7:43]

That is not a fancy in any respect; it is the truthful law of history, for if it is not realized through us, "Allah will surely bring forth a people whom He loves and who love Him, humble toward the believers, mighty against the disbelievers; they fight in the path of Allah and do not fear the blame of any blamer. That is the favor of Allah; He bestows it upon whom He wills." [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:54]

But we are eager to be among those who obtain this favor and are recorded in this honor; and your Lord creates what He wills and chooses.

> "Our Lord, grant us from Yourself mercy and prepare for us from our affair right guidance." [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:10]


# Epistle 3: Our Call in a New Phase — Part 2

## Islam Is Sufficient to Sustain the Ummah

There is no system in the world that gives a rising nation the systems it needs for revival but Islam; and the Noble Qur'an has paid special attention to portraying this aspect, striking examples sometimes with property and sometimes with detail, and treating these aspects with a precise and clear treatment that no nation adopts until it reaches what it aspires to.

### (a) Islam and Hope

A rising nation needs broad hope and optimism. The Qur'an supplied it with this feeling in ways unknown to any dead nation — a nation wholly consumed by despair, while Islam is all hope and resolve. It is enough that He made despair a path to unbelief, and hopelessness one of the manifestations of misguidance. The weakest of nations, when it hears the word of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "He has promised those among you who believe and do righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [in authority] upon the earth, as He granted succession to those before them; and that He will surely establish for them their religion which He has approved for them; and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security." [Qur'an, An-Nūr 24:55]

And His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "So do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are [true] believers. If a wound should touch you — there has already touched the [opposing] people a wound similar to it. And those days [of varying conditions] We alternate among the people." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:139–140]

And His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "It is He who expelled the ones who disbelieved among the People of the Scripture from their homes at the first gathering. You did not think they would leave, and they thought that their fortresses would protect them from Allah; but [the decree of] Allah came upon them from where they had not expected, and He cast terror into their hearts [so] they destroyed their houses with their own hands and the hands of the believers. So take warning, O you who have eyes." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥashr 59:2]

And His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Or do you think that you will enter Paradise while yet there has not come to you the like of [what came to] those who passed on before you? They were touched by poverty and hardship and were shaken until [even] the Messenger and those who believed with him said, \"When is the help of Allah?\" Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:214]

The weakest of nations, if it receives all this glad tidings and reads the realistic, applied stories in it, must afterwards come forth as the strongest of nations in determination and spirit; and it must see in this hope what drives it to storm difficulties however severe and confront events however great, until it attains the victory it yearns for.

### (b) Islam and Communal Honour

Rising nations also need to take pride in their nationality (*qawmiyyah*, قومية) as a distinguished nation that glories in its principles and history, so that the image is imprinted in the souls of their children and they cherish that glory and honour with their blood and spirits, and strive to build up, exalt, and bring happiness to this homeland. This meaning will not be seen as clearly in any system as just, noble, and expansive as it is in pure Islam (*al-Islām al-Ḥanīf*, الإسلام الحنيف). For a nation that knows that its dignity and honour have been sanctified by Allah in His previous law, and recorded in His Mighty Book — as He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110]

And He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And thus We have made you a median [i.e., just] community that you will be witnesses over the people and the Messenger will be a witness over you." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:143]

And:

> "To Allah belongs [all] honour, and to His Messenger, and to the believers." [Qur'an, Al-Munāfiqūn 63:8]

Which nation is more deserving of sacrificing the vanities of this world and all that is in them for the sake of its heavenly dignity?

Modern nations have worked hard to entrench this meaning in the souls of their youth, men, and children alike; hence we have "Germany above all," "Italy above all," and "Sudan one with Britain." But the difference between the feeling produced by the Islamic call and the feeling produced by these words and principles is that the Muslim's feeling ascends until it connects with Allah, while the feeling of others stops at the mere slogan. On another side, Islam defined the goal of this feeling and firmly bound its obligation, clarifying that it is not racial fanaticism or false pride, but subjugation of minds to goodness. Thus He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind: you enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110]

This means supporting virtue, combatting vice, respecting the Highest Aim, and keeping it in view at every deed. Therefore this sentiment of sovereignty was implanted in the Muslim forefathers to the extent that they left upon nations the fairest traces of justice and mercy. As for the principle of sovereignty in Western nations, its goal is limited to no more than blind fanaticism, and this produces conflict and aggression against weak nations. Thus the Islamic principle took the best of this aspect, intending thereby to cultivate its sons and shield them from the evil and tyranny within it. Islam has widened the bounds of the Islamic homeland, charging [its people] to work for it and sacrifice in pursuit of its freedom and honour. For the homeland in Islam's understanding comprises: (1) the particular country first; (2) then it extends to the other Islamic countries, for all of them are a shared homeland and abode; (3) then it reaches the first Islamic empire which the forefathers built with their precious blood, raising the banner of Allah, so that no land is found in which mention is made of their favour and glory; all these regions will be asked about before Allah, Exalted and Glorified, what each did toward restoring them; (4) then the homeland of Islam after that, in its true sense, encompasses the entire world; and do you not hear the word of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And fight them until there is no fitnah and [until] the religion, all of it, is for Allah." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:39]

Thus Islam has reconciled particular national feeling with general national feeling in a manner that contains all good for humanity as a whole:

> "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13]

### (c) Islam and Military Power

Rising nations also need power, and to stamp their sons with the military spirit — especially in these ages in which peace is secured only by readiness for war, and whose slogan has become, among all nations: "Might is the surest path to securing right."

Islam did not neglect this aspect; rather, it made it a definite obligation among its obligations, not distinguishing between it and prayer and fasting in any way. There is no system in the entire world, ancient or modern, that has attended to this aspect as Islam has attended to it in the Qur'an, in the hadith of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and in his biography. You will see that clearly exemplified in the word of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:60]

And His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Fighting has been enjoined upon you while it is hateful to you. But perhaps you hate a thing and it is good for you; and perhaps you love a thing and it is bad for you." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:216]

Have you ever seen a military proclamation in a sacred book recited in prayer, remembrance, worship, and intimate supplication like this proclamation that opens with the decisive command of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Fight in the way of Allah those who fight you but do not transgress." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:190]

Then it builds courage afterwards:

> "And whoever fights in the cause of Allah and is killed or achieves victory — We will bestow upon him a great reward." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:74]

Then it follows that with arousing the noblest emotions in souls, namely the rescue of family and homeland:

> "And what is [the matter] with you that you do not fight in the cause of Allah and [for] the oppressed among men, women, and children who say, \"Our Lord, take us out of this city of oppressive people and appoint for us from Yourself a protector and appoint for us from Yourself a helper.\"" [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:75]

Thus it makes clear the nobility of its goal and the baseness of its opponents' goal, for they are offered at a dear price — life in exchange for a precious security that deserves it and is raised upon it, namely the pleasure of Allah — while others fight for no [worthy] goal, with weaker souls and more contemptible hearts. Thus His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Those who believe fight in the cause of Allah, and those who disbelieve fight in the cause of *ṭāghūt* (طاغوت). So fight against the allies of Satan. Indeed, the plot of Satan has ever been weak." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:76]

Then it scolds those who shrink from performing the duty, taking on the burdens of safety while leaving aside the burdens of heroism; and it shows them that this stance of theirs is wrong, that boldness will not harm them but will win them the great reward, while holding back will not avail them, for death is behind them in any case. His words follow immediately upon the preceding verses:

> "Have you not seen those who were told, \"Restrain your hands [from fighting] and establish prayer and give *zakāh* (زكاة)\"? But then when fighting was enjoined upon them, at once a party of them feared men as they fear Allah or with greater fear. They said, \"Our Lord, why have You decreed upon us fighting? If only You had postponed us for a short time.\" Say, \"The enjoyment of this world is little, and the Hereafter is better for he who fears Allah. And you will not be wronged so much as the thread of a date seed. Wherever you may be, death will overtake you, even if you should be within towers of lofty construction.\"" [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:77–78]

By your Lord, what military proclamation is like this in power and clarity? It rouses in the soldier's soul everything a commander desires of resolve, honour, and assistance.

If the foundation of military life rests on two visible pillars — discipline and obedience — Allah has established this in two verses of His Book. He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Indeed, Allah loves those who fight in His cause in a row as though they are a [single] structure joined firmly." [Qur'an, Aṣ-Ṣaff 61:4]

And He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "So they who are given their record in their right hand — those will read their record, and injustice will not be done to them, [even] as much as a thread [inside the date seed]." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:71]

Wait — the intended meaning of the second verse is rather the principle of obedience and good word as in:

> "So they are given their record [in their right hand], and they will read it, and they will not be wronged one whit." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:71]

If you read what Islam brought regarding the preparation of equipment, the acquisition of strength, learning archery, night watch, the excellence of martyrdom, the reward of *jihād* (جهاد), the recompense of spending in it, caring for its families, and comprehending its diverse forms, you will find what cannot be enumerated, whether in the Noble Qur'an, the noble hadiths, the pure biography, or the pure jurisprudence:

> "Our Lord has encompassed all things in knowledge." [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:89]

Modern nations have attended to this and built themselves upon these foundations; and we have seen the foundation of Fascism, Mussolini, Hitler's Nazism, and Stalin's Communism to be military foundations in essence. But the difference between all of that and Islamic military life is immense, for Islam — which sanctified power to this degree — is the same Islam that preferred peace, saying, Exalted and Glorified, immediately after the verse of power:

> "And if they incline to peace, then incline to it [also] and rely upon Allah." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:61]

And it is He Who defined victory and its manifestations:

> "Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [in authority] upon the earth, as He granted it to those before them; and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has approved for them; and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security. They worship Me, not associating anything with Me." [Qur'an, An-Nūr 24:55]

Wait — the intended verse is rather:

> "Indeed, Allah will defend those who have believed. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone treacherous and ungrateful." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:38]

And:

> "Allah has promised those who have believed and done righteous deeds that for them will be forgiveness and great reward." [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:9]

And it is He Who laid the foundation of international military law, saying, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And if you fear from a people betrayal, throw [their treaty] back to them, [putting you] on equal terms. Indeed, Allah does not like traitors." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:58]

No matter how intense the instructions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his successors after him to the commanders of their armies were, they were the most magnificent manifestations of mercy and gentleness: "Do not betray, do not act treacherously, do not mutilate, do not kill a child, an old man, or a woman, do not cut down a fruit-bearing tree, do not finish off a wounded person, and you will come upon people who have devoted themselves to monasteries; leave them and what they have devoted themselves to."

Thus military life in Islam was "the police of justice and the watchmen of law and order." As for the military life of Europe today, it has made itself known to all as "the army of injustice and the soldiers of greed." Which of the two parties is better in station and nobler in standing?

### (d) Islam and Public Health

Rising nations are also in need of this noble military spirit; and the foundation of this military spirit is the health of bodies and the strength of physiques. The Qur'an pointed to this meaning in explaining the story of a struggling nation that was roused to undertake the burden of striving for its freedom and independence, and whose soul needed [a leader], so Allah chose for it a leader strong in thought and strong in resolve, and made the strength of his body one of the pillars of bearing his burden. This is what the Noble Qur'an narrates about the Children of Israel in the upbringing of their leader Ṭālūt, saying:

> "Indeed, Allah has chosen him over you and has increased him abundantly in knowledge and stature." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:247]

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, explained this meaning in many of his hadiths, urging the believers to preserve the strength of their bodies as well as the strength of their souls. In *Ṣaḥīḥ* he said: "The strong believer is better and more beloved to Allah than the weak believer." And he said: "Indeed your body has a right over you." And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, clarified for the Ummah many principles of public health, especially in prevention, which is the better half of medicine. He said, peace and blessings be upon him: "Guard against filth," and in a hadith: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to seek refuge with Allah" — and he forbade urinating and defecating in stagnant water, and declared quarantine upon a plague-stricken land and its people, so that neither they leave it nor others enter it. He warned against contagion and ordered isolation from the leper. Finally, his concern, peace and blessings be upon him, for many branches of physical exercise such as archery, swimming, horsemanship, and running, and his urging his Ummah upon them and upon caring for them, is such that he said in a hadith: "Whoever learns archery and then abandons it is not of us." And he warned sternly against monasticism and monkery that exhaust the bodies and darken the hearts in drawing near to Allah, Exalted and Glorified, while guiding the Ummah to the path of moderation in all of that. All of this points to Islam's profound concern for public health, its determination to preserve it, and its openness to everything in which lies its good and happiness in this respect.

### (e) Islam and Knowledge

Just as nations need power, they also need the knowledge that supports this power and secures for them the best path, and extends to them what they need of inventions and discoveries. Islam does not reject knowledge; rather, knowledge is an obligation among its obligations, like power, and it is intertwined with it. It is enough that the first verse revealed from the Book of Allah was:

> "Read in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging substance. Read, and your Lord is the most Generous, Who taught by the pen — taught man that which he knew not." [Qur'an, Al-ʿAlaq 96:1–5]

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, made it one of the conditions of release for the captives at Badr that each of them should teach ten of the sons of the Muslims to read and write, so as to remove illiteracy from the Ummah. Allah does not equate the knowledgeable with the ignorant, as He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Are those who know equal to those who do not know? Only they will remember [who are] people of understanding." [Qur'an, Az-Zumar 39:9]

And Islam has weighed the ink of scholars against the blood of martyrs, and it has brought knowledge and power together in the two noble verses:

> "And it is not for the believers to go forth [to battle] all at once. For there should separate from every division of them a group [remaining] to obtain understanding in the religion and warn their people when they return to them that they might beware." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:122]

And:

> "And those who have believed and emigrated and fought in the cause of Allah and those who gave shelter and aided — it is they who are the believers, truly. For them is forgiveness and noble provision." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:74]

If you consider the verses that invite reflection upon the universe, you will find them a broad invitation to the study of the cosmos and its sciences, through which are the paths to fearing Him and the ways of knowing Him. Thus His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Do you not see that Allah sends down rain from the sky and We produce thereby fruits of varying colours?" [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:27]

And in that is an allusion to meteorology, the stars, and the connection between heaven and earth. Then He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "And in the mountains are tracts [of land] white and red of varying colours and [others] very black." [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:27]

And in that is an allusion to geology, the strata of the earth, and its stages and phases:

> "And [there are] among men and moving creatures and grazing livestock various colours likewise." [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:28]

And in that is an allusion to sociology and zoology in all its divisions — humans, insects, and animals. Do you not see that this verse leaves out no science of the universe? Then all of that is crowned by His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Indeed, those who fear Allah from among His servants are the learned." [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:28]

Do you not see, from this magnificent Qur'anic arrangement, that Allah urges people to study the universe and spurs them to it, and He makes those who know its subtleties and secrets the people of His knowledge and fear? This is the *fiqh* (فقه) of the Muslims in their religion.

### (f) Islam and Moral Character

A rising nation needs moral character (*khuluq*, خلق) more than anything: noble, strong, firm character, and a great, dominant, aspiring soul, for it will face from the demands of the new era what it cannot reach except through strong, sincere character, deep faith, firm youthfulness, abundant sacrifice, and profound modesty. The only one who creates this perfect soul is Islam; it guides to the purity of the soul and its purification as the foundation of success. Thus He said, Exalted and Glorified:

> "He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]." [Qur'an, Ash-Shams 91:9–10]

And He made the transformation of nations' affairs dependent upon the transformation of their character and the rectification of their souls:

> "Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves." [Qur'an, Ar-Raʿd 13:11]

If you listen to the noble verses on the elements of noble character, you will find them the power that is not defeated in rectifying souls, preparing them, purifying them, and refining them. Thus His word, Exalted and Glorified, regarding fidelity:

> "Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allah. Among them is he who has fulfilled his vow [to the death], and among them is he who awaits [his chance]. And they did not alter [the terms of their commitment] by any alteration — [it is] so that Allah may reward the truthful for their truth." [Qur'an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:23–24]

And regarding giving, sacrifice, spending, and endurance, and overcoming hardships:

> "That is because they were not afflicted by thirst or fatigue or hunger in the cause of Allah, nor did they tread on any ground that enraged the disbelievers, nor did they inflict upon an enemy any infliction but that it was registered for them that Allah may reward them for the best of what they used to do." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:120]

And:

> "And they give food, despite love for it, to the needy, the orphan, and the captive, [saying], \"We feed you only for the countenance of Allah. We wish not from you reward or gratitude.\"" [Qur'an, Al-Insān 76:8–9]

And the establishment of conscience over piety — and that is the best conscience — which does not miss a deed or the secrets of the hearts and the movements of the eyes.

### (g) Islam and Economics

A rising nation is most in need of organizing its economic affairs, for these are the greatest concerns in these times. Islam did not fall short in this aspect; rather, it laid down its general principles and did not stop at completing its detailed aspects. Thus you hear the word of Allah, Exalted and Glorified, on preserving wealth and respecting its value and the obligation of caring for it:

> "And do not give the weak-minded your property, which Allah has made a means of subsistence for you." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:5]

And He said on balancing expenditure and income:

> "And do not make your hand [as] chained to your neck or extend it in total excess." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:29]

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "There is no *man* that spends but that he is rewarded." And he was truthful in applying this to the individual and to the Ummah, with his saying, peace and blessings be upon him: "Wealth is the nourishment of the righteous man." Any just and expansive economic system is embraced by Islam and calls the Ummah to encourage it, and does not stand in its way; and Islamic jurisprudence is a sea of rulings on financial transactions that has explained them in such detail that it leaves nothing more to be added.

After all this, if a nation is provided with these supports — hope, nationalism, knowledge, power, health, and economy — then it is, without doubt, the strongest of nations and the most independent. Especially if, in addition to that, it has been purified of selfishness, aggression, injustice, and tyranny, and has become the worthiest of all people to bring good to the entire world. And Islam has done all this, leaving no excuse for a nation that desires revival to turn away from it or to transgress against its path.

This is one aspect from among the aspects of justice in some Islamic systems, namely the systems related to the revival of nations, considering that we have taken the supports of revival as our foundation. As for all the aspects of justice in all Islamic systems, we have contented ourselves with saying one word that covers all the systems: the system of Islam, whether it concerns the individual, the nation, the family, its government and people, or [its balance] between comprehensiveness and precision, preferring interest and clarifying it — it is the wisest and most beneficial system known to people, ancient or modern, and history testifies to it and careful research confirms it in every manifestation of the nation's life.

This was a special discussion that is now a general one, agreed upon by every fair-minded person; and whenever researchers have penetrated into their studies, they have discovered from the aspects of justice in these eternal systems what had not occurred to the predecessors. Allah, the Speaker, spoke the truth:

> "We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth. But is it not sufficient concerning your Lord that He is, over all things, a Witness?" [Qur'an, Fussilat 41:53]

## Islam Protects the Rights of Non-Muslims

My companion, people think that holding fast to Islam and making it the foundation of life contradicts the presence of non-Muslim minorities in the Muslim nation, and contradicts the unity between the elements of the nation — which is a strong pillar of revival in this age. But the truth is otherwise. Islam, established by the All-Wise, All-Knowing Legislator Who knows the past, present, and future of nations, has already taken precautions against that obstacle and removed it beforehand. It did not issue its constitution except with this text:

> "Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes — that you be righteous to them and act justly toward them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly." [Qur'an, Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8]

This text includes not only justice but also kindness and beneficence toward them. And Islam, which sanctified general human unity in His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13]

Then it also sanctified general religious unity, eliminating fanaticism and obliging its sons to faith in all the heavenly religions, in His word, Exalted and Glorified:

> "Say, \"We have believed in Allah and in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, the Descendants, and what was given to Moses and Jesus and what was given to the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and we are Muslims [in submission] to Him.\"" [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:136]

Then after that it sanctified the particular religious unity, without harshness or aggression, in the word of Allah, Exalted and Glorified:

> "The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your brothers. And fear Allah that you may receive mercy." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

This Islam, built upon this moderate and fair temperament, cannot be a cause for its followers to sever unity; on the contrary, it gives this unity the quality of religious sanctity after it had derived its strength only from a civil text.

And Islam has precisely defined those whom we are to fight, boycott, and cut off from, in His word, Exalted and Glorified, after the preceding verse:

> "Allah only forbids you from those who fight you because of religion and expel you from your homes and aid in your expulsion — [forbids you] that you make allies of them. And whoever makes allies of them — it is those who are the wrongdoers." [Qur'an, Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:9]

There is not a single fair-minded person in the world who would force a nation to accept a kind of entry that brings corruption and great evil to its people and disrupts the system of its affairs.

This is Islam's stance toward non-Muslim minorities — clear, unambiguous, without doubt. Its stance toward foreigners is one of security and honour as long as they are upright and sincere; but if their evil increases and their aggression multiplies, the Qur'an has defined our stance toward them in His word:

> "O you who have believed, do not take as intimates those other than yourselves, for they will not spare you [any] ruin. They wish you would have hardship. Hatred has already appeared from their mouths, and what their breasts conceal is greater. We have certainly made clear to you the signs, if you will use reason. Indeed, you have those who are like them among yourselves." [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:118–119]

Thus Islam has treated these aspects with the most precise, most comprehensive, and purest treatment.

## Islam Does Not Disturb the Purity of Relations Between Us and the Nations

People may also think that the Islamic system in its new phase creates distance between us and neighbouring states and disturbs the purity of political relations between us and them after they had nearly stabilized. But this too is an erroneous notion that quickly fades. For these states, if they harbour evil suspicions against us, will not be pleased with us whether we follow Islam or otherwise; and if they have dealt with us in sincerity and mutual confidence, their orators and statesmen have declared that every state is free in the system it follows within its own land so long as it does not harm the rights of others. It is therefore incumbent upon the statesmen of these states to realize that the international mission of Islam is the holiest mission in history, and that the principles Islam laid down in this regard have established honour, preserved rights, rooted principles, and spread them.

Islam is the one that says, in preserving treaties and fulfilling obligations:

> "And fulfill [every] covenant. Indeed, the covenant will be questioned [about]." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:34]

And:

> "Excepted are those with whom you made a treaty among the polytheists and then they have not been deficient toward you in anything or supported anyone against you; so complete for them their treaty until their term [has ended]. Indeed, Allah loves the righteous [who fear Him]." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:4]

And:

> "And if they incline to peace, then incline to it [also] and rely upon Allah." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:61]

And His word on honouring refugees and treating well those who seek protection:

> "And if any one of the polytheists seeks your protection, then grant him protection so that he may hear the words of Allah. Then deliver him to his place of safety." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:6]

And this [applies] to the polytheists, so what of those who are nearer [in kinship or treaty]?

Islam, which raises these principles, by following it with these methods the West will consider it another protector for them. Some even say that it is in the interest of Europe itself that these new principles prevail in the dealings of states with one another, for that is better for them all.

## The Roots of Deviation in the East and the Causes of the Western Example

Among the reasons that drove the Eastern nations to deviate from Islam and imitate the West was the study of their leaders of the Western renaissance and their conviction that no renaissance is possible except by shattering religion, demolishing churches, freeing themselves from papal authority, silencing priests and clergy, eliminating every manifestation of religious authority in the nation, and separating religion from the general foundation of the state altogether. If that was valid in Western nations, it is absolutely not valid in Islamic nations, for the nature of Islamic teachings is not like the nature of the teachings of any other religion; and the authority of Muslim religious men is restricted and limited, neither changing conditions nor overturning systems, since the foundational principles in Islam have, throughout the centuries, kept pace with the ages, called to progress, supported knowledge, and protected scholars. What was valid there is not valid here. This is a broad subject treated at length in the major books, and we do not intend in this epistle to do more than touch upon it briefly as a reminder and to dispel doubts. We are certain that every fair-minded person agrees with us in this principle. Therefore it is never permissible for this feeling to be a pioneer in our new renaissance, which ought to be founded first and foremost upon strong supports of noble character, abundant knowledge, and overflowing power — and that is what Islam offers.

Among the justifications taken by some who followed the Western path is that they were offended by the stance of some Muslim religious men opposed to national renaissance, their accusation of patriots as appropriators of the nation's wealth for aggressors, and their preferring private benefit and worldly ambition to the interest of the country and the Ummah. If that is true, it is a weakness in the religious men themselves, not in the religion itself. Does the religion command this? Does it not have the life history of the most virtuous scholars of the Islamic Ummah, who used to storm the doors of kings and princes, their fathers and their walls, to confront them, warn them, admonish them, refuse their gifts, and present to them the demands of the Ummah — even bearing arms in the face of injustice and tyranny? History has not forgotten the battalion of jurists in the army of Ibn al-Ashʿath in the East of the Islamic state, nor the revolt of the judge Ibn Muḥibb al-Laythī al-Mālikī in the West.

These are the teachings of the religion, and this is the history of its men, the jurists of the Muslims. Is there anything in this of what they allege? Is it fair that the religion bear the burden of men who deviated from it?

Moreover, if these claims are true of some people, they are not true of all; and if they occurred in a particular circumstance, they are not general to all circumstances. The history of modern renaissances in the East is full of stances of Muslim religious men in every nation. What of the stance of al-Azhar in Egypt, the Higher Council in Southern Syria (Palestine) and Northern Syria (Lebanon), the school of Abū al-Kalām and his brothers among the scholars of India, and the leaders of the Muslims in Indonesia? Such claims are therefore unjust and should not be taken as an excuse to turn the Ummah away from its religion to bare nationalism. Or is it not better for the Ummah to rectify its religious men and reform them, rather than to stand against them with a hostile stand? These expressions that have reached us by imitation and habit, "religious men," do not correspond with Islamic usage; for if in the West they specifically mean the clergy, in Islamic usage they include every Muslim — we are all, young and old, "religious men."

## A Bold Step, and Yet a Successful One

After all that has preceded, you will see that the path before us is the path of Islam, and that the path of desires and adornments — pride and hypocrisy — and the path of Europe's luxury and towers, and its chronic illness and fear, and its dissolution and passions, and all that souls desire of pleasure, and all that has been made attractive to people of love for women, sons, and hoarded heaps of gold and silver, branded horses, cattle, and tilled land — is but the enjoyment of worldly life.

But the path of Islam is honour, dignity, life, strength, purity, uprightness, steadfastness, resolution, anger [for truth], and patience. Compare it with what Allah has offered the Ummah:

> "Say, \"Shall I inform you of [something] better than that? For those who fear Allah will be gardens in the presence of their Lord beneath which rivers flow, abiding eternally therein, and purified spouses, and approval from Allah. And Allah is Seeing of [His] servants.\"" [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:15]

Luxury has destroyed nations, and Europe today is being shaken by its desires and ambitions:

> "And when We intend to destroy a city, We command its affluent people, but they defiantly disobey therein; so the word [i.e., decree] comes into effect upon it, and We destroy it with [complete] destruction." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:16]

Allah, Exalted and Glorified, has sent Islam to the worlds until the Day of Resurrection, and sent with it His Book as a light and guidance until the Day of Resurrection. The leadership of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, is the foundation of its constitution, and the authority of the Qur'an is powerful in its proof, and humanity is proceeding in any case to the exaltation of the mighty or the humiliation of the lowly, near or far, by the realization of the word of Allah:

> "It is He who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth to manifest it over all religion." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:33]

I advance, in the name of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, carrying the bottle of medicine from the pharmacy of the Qur'an to rescue the afflicted, sick world.

It is a bold step, but a successful one, Allah willing, and the Victorious over His affair:

> "And on that day the believers will rejoice in the victory of Allah. He gives victory to whom He wills, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Merciful." [Qur'an, Ar-Rūm 30:4–5]

## Some Practical Steps of Reform

My companion: after we have explained what we hope the Ummah will possess in its new phase of a spiritual feeling, it is fitting that we mention in conclusion some of the practical manifestations and steps of reform that must follow this feeling. We shall record here only headings, and we know well in practice that every one of these demands requires broad research and precise study in which the competence and capability of specialists are available. We do not claim to enumerate all the needs of the Ummah, its demands, and the manifestations of renaissance; nor do we believe that the realization of these demands is among the easy matters that can be accomplished in a night or a morning. We know that many of them have complicated obstacles ahead that require long patience, great wisdom, and much perseverance. All that we know and appreciate. Alongside it, we say that if resolve is sincere and the path is clear, a strong-willed Ummah that sets out upon the paths of goodness cannot but reach what it desires, Allah willing. So let us meet, and I am with you, at the headings of reform grounded in the sound Islamic spirit.

First: in the political, judicial, and administrative sphere:

1. Eliminate party spirit and direct the political forces of the nation in one direction and one form.
2. Reform the law so that it harmonizes with Islamic Shariah in every branch.
3. Strengthen the army and multiply youth brigades and direct them on the foundations of Islamic *jihād* (جهاد).
4. Strengthen ties between all the Islamic countries, especially the Arab ones, as a prelude to serious, fruitful thinking about the matter of the lost Caliphate (*al-Khilāfah*, الخلافة).
5. Infuse the Islamic spirit into the government bureaus, so that the employees are all people required to know the teachings of Islam.
6. Supervise the personal conduct of employees and do not separate the personal sphere from the work sphere.
7. Advance the times of work in the bureaus in summer and winter so that this helps with the obligatory prayers and puts an end to much staying up late.
8. Eliminate bribery and favouritism, and accustom people to efficiency and legal qualifications only.
9. Weigh every action of the government in the scale of Islamic rulings and teachings, so that the systems of celebrations, invitations, assemblies, official receptions, prisons, and hospitals do not conflict with the teachings of Islam; and let work shifts be arranged so as not to clash with prayer times.
10. Employ the Azharites in military, administrative, and teaching positions.

Second: in the social and scientific sphere:

1. Accustom the people to respect public manners, and put in place guidelines reinforced by legal protection in this matter, and stiffen penalties for moral crimes.
2. Treat the woman question with a treatment combining advancement for her and preservation of what Islam has settled, so that this question — which is one of the most important matters of social reform — is not left neglected.
