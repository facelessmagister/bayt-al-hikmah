# The Muslim Brothers' Position on National, Arab, and Islamic Unity

People's thoughts often diverge on these three sides: national unity, Arab unity, Islamic unity; and some add to them Eastern unity. Tongues and minds set to comparing them, the possibility or difficulty of realising them, the benefit or harm of them, clinging to some to the exclusion of others. What is the position of the Muslim Brothers amid this mixture of ideas and directions? Indeed, many people insinuate against the Muslim Brothers in their patriotism and consider their adherence to the Islamic idea as something that conflicts with sincere national feeling. The answer to this is that we will not depart from the principle we have laid as the foundation of our idea: travelling upon the guidance of Islam and the light of its exalted teachings. What is Islam's own position toward these sides?

Islam has made it a binding, unavoidable obligation that every person should love his country, devote himself to serving it, and offer the utmost he can in serving the Ummah in which he lives, beginning with the nearest in kinship and neighbourhood, until it is not permitted to move *zakāt* beyond the distance of the [local] division except out of necessity, out of kindness to near relatives. Every Muslim is obliged to fill the needs incumbent upon him and to serve the country in which he grew up. From this the Muslim is the most patriotic of people and the greatest in performing the duties of citizenship, because that is enjoined upon him by the Lord of the Worlds. The Muslim Brothers are the most eager of people to serve their homeland and the most devoted in serving their people. They wish this beloved, honoured land every glory, honour, advancement, progress, success, and prosperity.

The leadership of the Islamic nations has devolved to them through many circumstances that converged upon this noble position. The love of the city in which the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, dwelt made him yearn for Makkah and say, "I would return to my origin," and he would describe it:

> O dweller of the valley, be not deceived; > Leave the lowlands and the rugged heights! > Have I not lived a night at Wajj and Batḥā', > And seen at Dhī Tuwā the sweetest sights?

The Muslim Brothers are lovers of their homeland, eager for its national unity in this respect. They see no objection to anyone's devoting himself to his country, labouring in the ways of his people, and wishing his homeland every glory and honour. This is from the side of particular national feeling.

Then this upright Islam arose among the Arabs and reached the nations through the Arabs. Its noble Book came in a clear Arabic tongue, and nations united in its name upon this tongue when they were Muslims. It is related that when the Arabs are humbled, Islam is humbled, and this meaning was realised when Arab rule declined and power passed from their hands to non-Arabs — Persians and Daylamites, and from them to others. The Arabs are the bond of Islam and its bell. We wish here to note that the Muslim Brothers consider Arabism in the sense in which the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, described it in the *ḥadīth* reported by Ibn Kathīr from Muʿādh ibn Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him: "Indeed, the Arabs are the tongue; indeed, the Arabs are the tongue." From this, the unity of the Arabs is a necessary matter for reviving Islam, establishing its state, and exalting its authority. Hence every Muslim must work to revive Arab unity, support it, and aid it. This is the position of the Muslim Brothers toward Arab unity.

It remains for us to define our position toward Islamic unity. The truth is that Islam, as creed and worship, as homeland and citizenship, has abolished racial differences among people. Allah the Exalted said:

> "The believers are but brothers." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "A Muslim is the brother of a Muslim." The Muslims' blood is equal in value, and the lowest of them guarantees their security. In this condition, Islam does not recognise geographical borders or bloodline racial distinctions. It considers all Muslims one Ummah, and the Islamic homeland one homeland, however its regions diverge and its borders multiply. Likewise, the Muslim Brothers sanctify this unity, believe in it as a comprehensive whole, and work to unite the word of the Muslims and to exalt the brotherhood of Islam. They proclaim that their homeland is every span of earth in which a Muslim says, "There is no god but Allah; Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah." How splendid is what one of the poets of the Muslim Brothers said in this regard:

> I know no homeland other than Islam; > In it Syria and the Nile's valley are the same. > Wherever the faith of Allah is mentioned, > I count that land among my homelands.

Some people say that this contradicts the current of prevalent thought in the age — the idea of fanaticism for races and colours. The world today is swept by a wave of racial nationalities; how do you stand before this current, and how do you depart from what people agree upon? The answer is that people are mistaken, and the results of their error in that are plainly visible in disturbing the peace of nations and cruelly tormenting peoples, which needs no proof. It is not the doctor's task to imitate the disease but to treat it and guide them to the straight path. That is the task of Islam and of whoever connects his call with Islam.

Others say that this is impossible and that work for it is futile and frozen without benefit. It is better for those who work for this comprehensive [unity] to work for their own peoples and serve their particular homelands. The answer to this is that this is the language of weakness and resignation. These nations were previously divided, differing in everything — religion, language, feelings, hopes, and pains — and Islam united them and gathered them upon a single word. As long as Islam, with its limits and ordinances, exists, if there are found among its people those who bear the burden of its call and renew it in the souls of Muslims, it will gather these nations all together anew, as it gathered them before. Restoration is easier than origination, and experience is the truest proof of possibility.

Some people regard this as Eastern unity. I think that this term stirs in the souls of those affected by it nothing but the fanaticism of Westerners against them and the bad complex that has formed in the East and its sons regarding that. They are mistaken in this. If Westerners persist in this creed of theirs, it will bring down upon them woe and chastisement. The Muslim Brothers do not regard Eastern unity except through this sentiment alone. East and West are equal to them if their position toward Islam is equal, and they do not value people except by this measure.

Thus it is clear that the Muslim Brothers respect their particular nationality as the first foundation of the desired revival, and they do not consider it objectionable that every person should work for his country and prefer it in the homeland over others. Then after that they support Arab unity as the second link in the revival, and they work for the Islamic community as the complete enclosure of the general Islamic homeland. After this I may add that the Muslim Brothers wish good for the entire world, and they do not call for universal unity because that is the objective and goal of Islam, in accordance with the words of Allah, Blessed and Exalted:

> "And We have not sent you, [O Muḥammad], except as a mercy to the worlds." [Qur'an, Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:107]

There is no need, beyond this, for me to say that there is no contradiction between these degrees of consideration, and that each of them strengthens the other and realises the goal from it. If some peoples wish to use the call to a particular nationality as a weapon to arouse hostility against others, the Muslim Brothers are not with them. This is the difference between us and many of the people.
