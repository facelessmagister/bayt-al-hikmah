# Epistle 1: The Muslim Brotherhood on Parties, Egypt al-Fatah, and the European States

Another matter is the reckless rivalry [over power], and the harnessing of every party advertisement and every honourable and dishonourable means to attain it, and the vilification of all opponents of both factions without gaining anything from it. The Muslim Brotherhood likewise believe that this partisanship has corrupted for the people all their social activities, disrupted their interests, corrupted their morals, torn apart their bonds, and produced in their public and private lives the worst effect. They also believe that the constitutional order, indeed the parliamentary order, has no need of the party system in its present form in Egypt; otherwise coalition governments would not have arisen in democratic countries. Thus the argument that the parliamentary order cannot be conceived except through the existence of parties is a weak argument. Many constitutional parliamentary states operate under a single-party system, and this is possible.

The Muslim Brotherhood believe that there is a difference between freedom of opinion, thought, expression, disclosure, counsel, and sincere advice — which Islam requires — and fanatical attachment to an opinion, insurrection against the general welfare, and continual work to widen the chasm of division in the Ummah and to undermine the authority of rulers — which is what partisanship demands, and which Islam refuses and forbids in the strongest terms. Islam, in all its teachings, calls to unity and cooperation.

This is the full view of the Muslim Brotherhood on the question of partisanship and parties in Egypt. For this reason they have repeatedly asked the leaders of the political parties to set this rivalry aside and to join together. They proposed mediation in this matter to His Royal Highness Prince Muhammad ʿAli and His Highness Prince Jalāl Ṭūsūn. They also asked His Majesty the King to dissolve these parties legally so that they might all merge into a single popular bloc that works for the good of the Ummah on the basis of Islam.

If circumstances do not help in the realisation of this idea, we believe that this year bears witness to the truth of the Muslim Brotherhood’s view. The Muslim Brotherhood will continue their efforts in this field and will strive for what they desire, by the success of Allah and the vigilance of the Ummah, and by the successive failures of the men of parties in their endeavours. And Allah’s promise will surely be fulfilled: “As for the foam, it passes away as scum, but as for what benefits the people, it remains in the earth.” [Qur’an, Ar-Raʿd 13:17]

Some men of certain parties think that we mean, by this discourse, to destroy one party in the service of another, or to pursue some private gain. The clearest proof of the error of this view is that this doubt has spread to all parties alike. Many men of the Wafd suspect the Muslim Brotherhood of working against it and believe that it alone is intended by these descriptions and labels. They think that the Muslim Brotherhood have taken Islam as a pretext to disparage the Wafd and to dissociate from it, and that they intend by this to serve the government. At the very same time we hear this accusation from some government parties as well! Is there any proof clearer than this that the Muslim Brotherhood stand in one position regarding all of them, derive their stance from their creed, and act in it by inspiration from their faith and their reformist purpose?

I would like to say to our brothers among the callers to the parties and their men: the weapon which the Muslim Brotherhood use is none other than pure Islamic thought; it will not come later, and it has not come before. The Muslim Brotherhood do not harbour any special enmity toward any party whatsoever. But they believe, from the depths of their souls, that Egypt cannot be set right or saved except by the dissolution of all these parties and their reconstitution into a single general national bloc that leads the Ummah to victory under the banner of the Qur’an.

And on this occasion I say that the Muslim Brotherhood believe the idea of coalition between the parties to be barren and think of it as a palliative, not a cure. Soon the allies will attack one another and the conflict between them will return as strong as it was before the coalition. The decisive, successful cure is for these parties to depart gratefully, for they have done what they could and the circumstances that created them have passed. For every age there is a [new] state and men, as they say.

## The Muslim Brotherhood and Egypt al-Fatah

On this occasion I must set forth the position of the Muslim Brotherhood toward the Egypt al-Fatah group. The Muslim Brotherhood group has been in existence for ten years, and the Egypt al-Fatah group has been in existence for roughly five years; the audience of the Muslim Brotherhood is exactly twice the size of Egypt al-Fatah. Nevertheless, it has become widespread in many circles that the Muslim Brotherhood group is an offshoot of Egypt al-Fatah. The reason for this is that Egypt al-Fatah relied on advertisement and publicity, while the Brotherhood preferred work and activity. But none of this concerns us. Whether the Muslim Brothers are the ones who sowed for Egypt al-Fatah the way of *jihād* and struggle for Islam, or whether Egypt al-Fatah was the one that revealed the Brothers and made them known to the people — they were born before it and preceded it to struggle and to the field by five years, that is, by twice its age — all of this, in my view, gives the Muslim Brotherhood no weight. What I wish to draw attention to in this chapter is that the Muslim Brothers were not, at any time, in the ranks of Egypt al-Fatah, nor workers with it. I do not mean by this to disparage it or its founders. I say it as a statement of fact: the freedom of Egypt al-Fatah stirred the Brothers and attracted them, but it did not befit them, and they did not transgress against it or accuse it — and that is also not correct. We, the Muslim Brothers, do not boast over anything written against it, nor do we wish to be taken to task for any of it. I hope that this is the feeling of all the Brothers, and that many people wish Egypt al-Fatah would unite with the Muslim Brotherhood. This feeling, without doubt, is a genuine one. For nothing is more beautiful than unity and cooperation for good. But some matters are decided only by time. In Egypt al-Fatah there are those who see the Muslim Brotherhood as nothing but a preaching group and who deny all else of their program; and among the Muslim Brotherhood there are those who believe that the Egypt al-Fatah group has not matured in the souls of many of its members to the correct Islamic meaning that would spur them to call to Islam purely and soundly. Let us leave time to carry out its function and to issue its verdict; it is the best guarantor of refinement and preparation.

This does not mean that the Muslim Brotherhood will fight Egypt al-Fatah. Rather, it is easy for them, with the success [granted by Allah], to welcome every worker for good and to march alongside them. The Muslim Brothers do not disdain to mix the building [of cooperation] with them, and in the field of struggle all have a share.

That is our position toward Egypt al-Fatah, so long as it has declared that it is not a party and that it works, and will continue to work, for the Islamic idea. And every helper of Islam is, in that, a new victory for the Muslim Brothers.

There remains a final matter: the position of the Muslim Brotherhood toward Egypt al-Fatah in the question of the demolition of the taverns. Although no tavern can be established in any country whose inhabitants are proud that not a single wine-shop stands upon its soil, the Brothers have written laying the responsibility for this demolition upon the government before others, because it is the government that put the Muslims in this embarrassing position and did not perceive the psychological transformation and the new, strong tendency that have arisen in them through reverence for Islam and pride in its teachings. Before that, it used to order the tapster to strike with the stick when it wished the drunkard to stop drinking. We believe that this challenge is not appropriate to the present time, and that it is necessary to choose the suitable moment, or to use the utmost wisdom, and to carry it out in a way that is less harmful and more eloquent in conveying the purpose: namely, to draw the government’s attention to its Islamic duty. Although those arrested do not confess, the Muslim Brotherhood directed a letter to His Excellency the Minister of Justice, drawing his attention to the necessity of examining this question in a special manner appropriate to their noble motive, and to hastening the legislation that protects the country from these moral calamities.

## The Position of the Muslim Brotherhood Toward the European States

After this quick survey of the position of the Muslim Brotherhood, which relies on Islam in all internal matters, it is fitting that I speak to you about their position toward the European states.

Islam, as I have presented it, regards the Muslims as one Ummah united by creed, sharing with one another in sorrows and hopes, and any aggression that befalls one of them or any individual Muslim befalls them all. This made me laugh and weep when I saw it mentioned in the book *al-Sharḥ al-Ṣaghīr* on *Umdat al-Masālik*: it states, “If a Muslim woman is taken captive in the East, it is obligatory upon the people of the West to ransom her, even if that consumes all the wealth of the Muslims.” I saw something similar before that in the book *Bayʿ al-Anhār* in the commentary on *Maltaqā al-Aḥrār*, transmitted from the book *al-Baḥr* on the madhhab of the Ḥanafis. I saw this and laughed and wept, and said to myself: Do the eyes of those two writers not see all the Muslims in captivity at the hands of the disbelievers and the materialists? I wish to infer from this that the Islamic homeland is one, indivisible; and that aggression against a part of its parts is aggression against the whole.

This is one point. The second is that Islam obliges the Muslims to be a community in their lands, sovereign in their homelands. And not only that, but it is also incumbent upon them to call others to their call and to guide them by the lights of Islam by which they themselves were guided.

Hence the Muslim Brotherhood believe that every state that has aggressed and aggresses against the lands of Islam is an oppressive state that must cease its aggression, and the Muslims must prepare themselves and work together to rid themselves of its dominance.

As for England, it is not content to wrong Egypt despite its opposition; and it is of no benefit for us to say that the treaty is beneficial or harmful, or that it should be amended or rescued. That is futile talk. The treaty is a shackle around Egypt’s neck, and there is no doubt about that. Can Egypt free itself from this fetter except by work and good preparation? The language of power is the clearest language; so work for that, and gain time if it desires freedom and independence.

England still wrongs Palestine and attempts to diminish the rights of its people. Palestine is a homeland for every Muslim, because it is part of the land of Islam, the cradle of the Prophets, and the site of the Aqsa Mosque which Allah blessed around it. Therefore, the rights of the Muslims in Palestine are a debt upon England that it must fulfil until it gives them their due. England knows this well. This is what moved it to invite the kings of the Muslim lands to the London Round Table Conference. Let us seize this opportunity to remind it that Arab rights cannot be diminished, and that the cruel acts which its officials persist in committing in Palestine are not such as to inspire good opinion of it among the Muslims. It is better for it to stop these aggressive actions against innocent, free people. We shall send, from this pulpit of the Great Mosque of Muhammad ʿAli, the purest wishes of the Muslim Brotherhood to it, and they will harm neither its friendship nor its free people; that will only add honour to its honour and pride to its pride. We remind the Islamic delegations of England’s deception and of the necessity of standing firm for the rights of their lands, complete and undiminished.

On this occasion I remind the Brothers that a general committee has been formed at the Young Men’s Muslim Association, comprising the Islamic associations, to cooperate in issuing a unified stamp to be distributed from the beginning of the current year as aid to Palestine and its struggle, and this stamp will take the place of all the different stamps of the various associations. The Brothers are asked to exert their efforts in encouraging this committee by distributing its stamps when they are issued, and by settling whatever may exist with them of old stamp accounts and returning them to the office to be cancelled.

We also have, after that, an account with England in the Islamic lands it occupies without right, in which Islam obliges its people and us to work for their rescue and liberation.

As for France, which has claimed friendship for Islam for a long time, the Muslims have a long account with it. We do not forget its position toward noble Syria, nor its position in the question of the Aqsa disturbances and the barbaric bombardment, nor do we forget that many of our dear brothers, the youth of the far Maghrib, the free, struggling homeland, are today in prisons and the outskirts of exile, awaiting the day when this account will be settled and those wrongs made right among the people.

Our account with Italy is no less than our account with France. Tripoli — Arab, Muslim Tripoli, the dear, nearby neighbour — the Italians and their agents work to annihilate it, to exterminate its people, to erase every trace of Arabism and Islam from it. How can there remain in it any trace of Arabism and Islam when it is considered a part of Italy? It is futile for Italy to seek, under this title, the friendship of the Muslims.

O Muslim Brothers!

This is speech that rends the hearts and tears the livers! I have recited these calamities in this chapter, and the chain has no end. You know this. But you must explain it to the people and make them realise that Islam is not pleased with its sons for anything less than freedom and independence, let alone sovereignty; and the declaration of *jihād*, even if it costs them blood and wealth. For death is better than this life — a life of slavery, bondage, and humiliation! And if you do this and are true to Allah, the Mighty, the Exalted, then victory must come, Allah willing:

> “Allah has written, ‘I will surely overcome, I and My messengers.’ Indeed, Allah is Powerful and Exalted in Might.” [Qur’an, Al-Mujādilah 58:21]

O Muslim Brothers!

I have presented to you in this epistle a brief, sufficient summary of your idea in its present form. Today I would have liked to review with you some of the social and economic problems existing in Egyptian society — indeed, in every Islamic society — for the malady is one, were it not for lack of time, and were it not that the malady is summed up in one thing: weakness of morals, loss of ideals, preference for private interest over public welfare, flight from facing facts, escape from the consequences of treatment, and disunity that kills the soul. This is the malady. And the cure, likewise, is a single word: it is the opposite of these morals; it is the treatment of souls by the Muslim Brotherhood and the strengthening of the people’s character:

> “He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption].” [Qur’an, Ash-Shams 91:9–10]

O Muslim Brothers!

This religion was established by the *jihād* of your predecessors upon strong pillars: reliance on Allah, renunciation of the pleasures of this perishing world, preference for the abode of eternity, sacrifice of blood, soul, and wealth in support of the truth, love of death in the cause of Allah, and proceeding in all of that upon the guidance of the Qur’an. Upon these strong pillars build your renaissance, reform your souls, firm up your call, lead the people to good, and fear no blame from any blamer.

O Muslim Brothers!

Do not despair, for despair is not the character of the Muslims. The dreams of yesterday are the realities of today, and the dreams of today are the realities of tomorrow. There is still time, and the elements of safety are still mighty and great in the souls of believing peoples, despite the dominance of the manifestations of corruption. The weak does not remain weak all his life, and the strong does not remain strong forever:

> “And We wanted to confer favour upon those who were oppressed in the land and make them leaders and make them the inheritors, and establish them in the land…” [Qur’an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:5–6]

Time will bring forth many great events, opportunities will arise for mighty deeds, and Islam’s call shall again stand at the forefront of nations and the leadership of peoples:

> “Those are the days We alternate between the people…” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:140]

And you hope from Allah what they do not hope. So prepare and act today, for tomorrow you may be unable to act.

I have addressed the impassioned among you to be patient and wait for the turn of time. And now I address the idle: rise up and act, for there is no rest for the serious:

> “And strive for Allah with the striving due to Him. He has chosen you and has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty…” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:78]

And forward, always forward.

Allah is the Greatest, and praise be to Allah.

Ḥasan al-Bannā
