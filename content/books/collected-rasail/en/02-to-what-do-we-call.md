# Epistle 2: To What Do We Call People? (ilá ayy shayʾ nadʿū al-nās)

How, then, do we build the call of the Muslim Brotherhood from this whole complex mixture?

That compels me to speak to you about two things: first, the structure of our call in its plain, unadorned form, and then, after that, its position toward each of these calls.

Do not reproach me for this digression in the introduction. I have made it a rule for myself to write as I speak, and to treat my subject in this style of writing without affectation or effort. I want people to understand me while they are relaxed, and for my words to reach souls free of pretension and division.

## Our Call

Know, my brother: our call is most fully described as an *Islamic* call, and this word has a broader meaning than the narrow meaning people usually intend. Do not imagine that Islam is a comprehensive idea that organizes all the affairs of life, setting down for every matter a wise system, and that it does not stand inactive before the life-problems and systems indispensable for reforming people. Many have erred in thinking that Islam is restricted to forms of worship or modes of spirituality, confining themselves and their understanding to these narrow circles of isolated rites.

But we understand Islam differently: it is a broad field encompassing the affairs of this world and the Hereafter. We do not claim this as an assertion or expand it on our own authority; rather, it is what we have learned from the Book of God and the life of the first Muslims. If the reader wishes to understand the call of the Brotherhood by something broader than the word "Islamic," let him take his *muṣḥaf* (مصحف), purge his intention of the impurities of caprice and false aims, then understand what the Qurʾān requires of him, and he will see in that the call of the Brotherhood.

Yes: our call is Islamic in every meaning the word can bear. So understand it however you wish, provided your understanding is anchored in the Book of God, the Sunnah of His Messenger, and the life of the righteous predecessors (*al-salaf al-ṣāliḥ*, السلف الصالح) among the Muslims. For the Book of God is the foundation and support of Islam; the Sunnah of His Messenger explains and elucidates the Book; and the life of the righteous predecessors—may God be pleased with them—shows the practical application of these ideas and teachings, and is the living image of them.

## Our Position Toward the Calls

As for our position toward the different calls that have dominated this age, dividing hearts and confusing minds: we weigh them in the scale of our call; what agrees with it we welcome gladly, and what contradicts it we disown. We are convinced that our call is general and all-embracing, leaving no wholesome part of any call without touching upon it and pointing to it.

### Nationalism (*al-waṭaniyyah*, الوطنية)

People have been enchanted at one time by the call of nationalism and at another by the call of communalism (*al-qawmiyyah*, القومية), especially in the East, where peoples feel the injustice of the West—injustice that has touched their dignity, honor, independence, wealth, and blood. These peoples, feeling the yoke of the West that has been forced upon them, strive to free themselves from it with all the strength, firmness, struggle, and endurance they possess. The tongues of leaders moved, the pens of newspapers flowed, writers wrote, orators spoke, and callers cried out for nationalism and the glory of communalism.

That is fine and splendid; but what is not fine and not splendid is that when you try to explain to the Eastern peoples—while they are Muslim—that Islam contains something fuller, loftier, nobler, and more generous than what is on the lips of Westerners and in the writings of Europeans, they suspect you of imitating them and preach to them; they imagine that Islam is on one side and this idea on the other, and some of them think that this may split the unity of the *Ummah* and weaken the bond of youth.

This false notion has been dangerous for the Eastern peoples in every respect. Because of it I wished to present here the position of the Muslim Brotherhood and their call toward the idea of nationalism—the position they have chosen for themselves and which they desire and try to make people accept.

#### Nationalism of Longing

If the proponents of nationalism intend by it love for this land, attachment to it, yearning for it, and affection toward it, then this is a natural feeling in the souls of people on the one hand, and commanded by Islam on the other. Indeed Bilāl, who sacrificed everything in the path of his creed and religion, was the same Bilāl who sang in the house of Umayyah of his longing for Mecca in verses dripping with tenderness and sweetness:

> *Ah, would that I knew whether I might spend a night in a valley with dear and noble ones around me,*
> *And whether I might one day return to Mecca, and whether its shaded valleys might be my lot.*

And the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, heard ʿAṣṣāl describe Mecca, and tears flowed from him toward it, and he said, "O ʿAṣṣāl, let the weeper weep."

#### Nationalism of Glory and Freedom

And if the proponents of nationalism intend by it that every people should strive with all its power for honor among nations, and that tranquility, dignity, and freedom should prevail in its affairs, then we are with them in this too. Islam has emphasized this with the strongest emphasis, as the Exalted said:

> "And to God belongs [all] honor, and to His Messenger, and to the believers, but the hypocrites do not know." [Qurʾān, Al-Munāfiqūn 63:8]

And He said:

> "And never will God confer upon the disbelievers a way [to triumph] over the believers." [Qurʾān, An-Nisāʾ 4:141]

#### Nationalism of Community

And if they intend by nationalism strengthening the bond between the people of one country and guiding them to use that strength for their interests, then we agree with them in that too. Islam considers this a necessary duty, as the Messenger of God, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Be servants of God as brothers." And the Noble Qurʾān says:

> "Hold fast to the rope of God all together and do not become divided. And remember the favor of God upon you—when you were enemies and He brought your hearts together and you became, by His favor, brothers." [Qurʾān, Āl ʿImrān 3:103]

#### Nationalism of Conquest

And if they intend by nationalism the opening of lands and sovereignty over the earth, then Islam has made this obligatory and directed conquerors to the noblest conquest and most blessed opening. That is His saying, the Exalted:

> "And fight them until there is no fitnah and [until] the religion, all of it, is for God." [Qurʾān, Al-Anfāl 8:39]

#### Partisan Nationalism

But if they intend by nationalism dividing the *Ummah* into factions that fight one another, harbor malice, exchange accusations and suspicions, some of them devouring others, adopting ways and groups shaped by caprice, ambitions, and interests, dividing their days according to personal and party benefits—so that all this serves their party, increasing the fire blazing in their hearts against the truth, while requiring them to cut off ties and cooperation with others—then this is a false nationalism, useless to its proponents and to the people. So you see that we are among the proponents of nationalism, indeed with its most ardent advocates, in every wholesome meaning that benefits lands and people; yet at the same time you see that this broad nationalist claim is nothing but one part of the teachings of Islam.

#### The Limits of Our Nationalism

As for the point of difference between us and them, it is that we do not limit nationalism to the boundary-posts and the geographical lines drawn on maps. Every land in which a Muslim proclaims "There is no god but God; Muḥammad is the Messenger of God" is a homeland to us, possessing sanctity, inviolability, love, sincere devotion, and earnestness. All Muslims in these geographical regions are our people and our brothers; we belong to them, share in their feelings, and sense what they sense. The proponents of nationalism only are not like that: nothing moves them except the affairs of that narrow patch of earth. This practical difference appears when a non-Muslim nation tries to strengthen itself at the expense of others: we will not accept that at the expense of any Islamic country; rather, we seek strength for all of us together. The narrow nationalists see no harm in that, and from this bonds are torn, powers weakened, and the enemy strikes some of us through others.

#### The Goal of Our Nationalism

That is one point. The second is that the nationalists for the most part aim merely at liberating their country, and if they then seek to strengthen it afterwards in material ways, as Europe does now. But we believe that every Muslim bears on his neck a trust: he must expend his soul, blood, and wealth so that he may guide humanity by the light of Islam and raise its banner high over every corner of the earth—not seeking thereby wealth, status, or dominion over anyone, nor enslavement of a people, but seeking only the face of God, bringing happiness to people through His religion, and exalting His word. That is what drove the righteous predecessors, may God be pleased with them, to those holy conquests that astonished the world and surpassed everything history had known in speed, justice, nobility, and virtue.

#### Unity

I also wish to draw your attention to the fallacy of the claim that pursuing this path tears the unity of an *Ummah* composed of different religious elements. For Islam, the religion of unity and equality, guarantees these bonds among people so long as they cooperate in good:

> "God does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes—that you be righteous toward them and act justly toward them. Indeed, God loves those who act justly." [Qurʾān, Al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8]

So who, then, is the one who introduces division?

Do you see, after this, how we agree with the most fervent nationalists in love for their land, earnestness in seeking its liberation, welfare, and advancement, and how we cooperate with and support everyone who strives for that with sincerity? Indeed, I would have you know that if their goal is the liberation of the homeland and the restoration of its glory, that for the Muslim Brotherhood is only a part of the path, or one stage of it. After that they must work to raise the banner of the Islamic homeland over every part of the earth and to plant the standard of the *muṣḥaf* everywhere.
