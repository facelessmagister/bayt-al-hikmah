# Epistle 4: In Ten Years — The Fifth General Conference

Summary of the general address delivered by the Supreme Guide at the fifth general conference

## The Goal of the Muslim Brothers and the Characteristics of Their Call

O Brothers:

I wished that we might continue working without tiring, and that works alone might speak of the Brothers and their steps. I loved that your next step should follow your previous step in calm and quiet, without this pause that marks the boundary of ten years passed, that we might resume another stage of the stages of constant *jihād* in the paths of realising our exalted idea.

But you wanted this, and you loved to gladden us with this comprehensive gathering; so thanks to you. There is no harm in seizing this blessed opportunity to review our programs, survey the fields of our works, verify the stages of our path, and define the goal and the means, so that the vague idea becomes clear, the mistaken view corrected, the hesitant step firm, the missing link completed, and people may know the Muslim Brothers in the reality of their call without confusion or ambiguity.

There is no harm in this, and no harm in whoever presents to us from the people of soundness of this call, or whoever has heard or read this statement, offering his opinion on our goal, our means, and our steps, so that we may take what is wholesome from his opinion and descend to the truth in his counsel. For the religion is counsel: for Allah, His Messenger, His Book, the Muslim community, and its common people.

O Brothers:

I need not express my thanks to you, nor describe the happiness that fills me in this position among you, the joy and delight at meeting you, and the great hope in your support and Allah's success for us.

I need not explain all of this with this abundance of noble emotions that fills the atmosphere of this gathering, for everything in it spoke of deep love, strong attachment, sincere brotherhood, and firm cooperation. May Allah grant you success in what He loves and is pleased with.

## The Muslim Brothers — An Idea in Four Souls

O dear Brothers:

I have lived long, tried much, mixed with many circles, and witnessed several events, and I have come out of this short swim through long stages with a firm creed that does not waver: that the happiness which all people seek does not descend upon them from their own souls and powers, nor does it come to them from outside these powers at all. And the misery that surrounds them and from which they flee comes to them from these very souls and hearts. The Qur'an confirms and clarifies this meaning in the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> "Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves." [Qur'an, Ar-Raʿd 13:11]

And I have not seen any words more profound in social philosophy than the saying of that poet:

> "No land is straitened by its people; rather, the morals of men straiten the land."

I believe this, and I believe alongside it that there is no system or teachings that guarantee the happiness of these human souls and guide people to the practical, clear ways of this happiness except the teachings of pure, natural, clear, practical Islam. There is no room here to detail these teachings, nor to show that they guarantee this result and secure the happiness of humanity altogether — that is another occasion. Suffice it that we are all partners in believing in the soundness of this theory, and many non-Muslims acknowledge and confess the beauty and perfection in Islam.

From the time of my youth I have stood with a single goal: guiding people to Islam in its reality and essence. Hence the idea of the Muslim Brothers was purely Islamic in its goal, its means, and everything connected with it; it had no connection to anything other than Islam.

These thoughts remained a conversation with myself and a solitary communion, until they overflowed to many around me, appearing as an individual call, a sermon of exhortation, a lesson in the mosques whenever an opportunity to teach presented itself, or an urging of some friends among the scholars to arouse this zeal and redouble this effort in saving the youth and guiding them to the good in Islam.

In Egypt and other lands of the Islamic world there were events that moved me deeply and stirred the depths of feeling in my heart, turning my attention to the necessity of extending help and working, and to the way of action after contemplation, and foundation after teaching. I will not detain you with the details of events that have ended and whose traces have almost disappeared, and which led to right guidance or some right guidance for their people.

I approached many of the great ones of the nation concerning the necessity of rising and working and the way of earnestness and formation. Sometimes I found encouragement and arousal, sometimes postponement; but I did not find what I desired of concern for organising practical efforts. In fairness I mention here the late Aḥmad Bāshā Ḥilmī, may Allah make wide his grave in Paradise, for I never saw him except as an example of flaming zeal and burning jealousy; whenever I spoke to him about any matter concerning the nation in general, I found complete intellect, full readiness, comprehensive concern, and an awaiting of the hour of action. May Allah have mercy on him and reward him greatly.

I also found among friends and brothers from the university and the house of seeking knowledge sincere friendship, true affection, and a sense of duty, and I found a good readiness; the first to respond to my sharing the burden of reflection were some of the virtuous Brothers: Mr Aḥmad Afandī al-Ṣukkarī, the virtuous late Shaykh Ḥāmid ʿAskarī, may Allah make him dwell in the breadth of His Paradise, and Shaykh Aḥmad ʿAbd al-Majīd, and many others.

We made a pact and a strong covenant that each of us would work for this goal until the general custom in the nation should be turned toward a sound Islamic direction. We used to meet in the evenings and review the condition of the nation and what it had reached in its various aspects of life, analysing its diseases and ailments, thinking of the remedy and treating the disease, and we would be overcome by emotion to the point of weeping. We used to wonder when we saw ourselves in this intense spiritual preoccupation, while the young men were wandering about in cafés and frequenting clubs of corruption and ruin. If you asked one of them what drove him to this empty, wearisome sitting, he would say, "I am killing time" — little knowing that he who kills his time kills his soul, for time is life.

We used to wonder at the neglect of the people and of many of those more worthy than us to bear this burden; then one of us would say to another: Is this not one of the diseases of the nation, and perhaps the most dangerous, that it does not think of its disease and does not feel its own ailment?

For this and the like we used to work to reform this corruption, and our souls would find consolation in praising Allah that He had made us among those who call to Him and work for His religion. Time worked its effect, and we four were separated: Aḥmad Afandī al-Ṣukkarī went to al-Maḥmūdiyyah, the late Shaykh Ḥāmid ʿAskarī to Zaqāzīq, Shaykh Aḥmad ʿAbd al-Majīd to Kafr al-Dawwār, and I to Ismailia. I recall the words of the poet:

> "Syria is my family and Baghdad my homeland; I am at the two Romes and with the people of Fusṭāṭ my neighbours."

And in Ismailia, O Brothers, the first nucleus of formation for the idea gathered, and the first modest band arose to work and to raise its banner, pledging to Allah complete soldiership in peace under the name "the Muslim Brothers." That was in the month of Rabīʿ al-Awwal in the year 1348 AH.

## The Concept of the Muslim Brothers

Permit me, gentlemen, to use this expression — and I do not mean by it that the Muslim Brothers have anything new apart from the Islam brought by the master of creation, Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him, from his Lord. I mean that many Muslims in many places have stripped Islam of names, descriptions, limits, and forms they devised themselves, and have used its flexibility and breadth in a harmful way — though it was only for a sublime wisdom — so that they differed greatly in their conception of Islam, and many images of Islam were impressed upon the souls of its sons, some close to, some distant from, or not matching the original Islam represented by the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions, may Allah be pleased with them.

Some people see Islam as nothing beyond the limits of outward worship; if they perform it, or see someone perform it, they are content and think they have reached the core of Islam. That is the common meaning among most Muslims. Some see Islam only as noble morality and sublime spirituality, this philosophical nourishment satisfying the mind and spirit and lifting it above the filth of dominating matter. Some stop their Islam at the limit of admiring this practical humanitarian meaning in Islam, so that they do not look beyond it and are not attracted to thinking about anything else. Some see Islam as a kind of inherited beliefs and customary actions that require no effort and offer no benefit, so they are discontented with Islam and everything connected with it; and you find this clearly in the souls of many who have been educated in a foreign culture and have not had the opportunity of good contact with Islamic realities, so that they know nothing of Islam originally, or know it in a distorted image mixed by those who did not represent it well among the Muslims.

Beneath these categories are many other categories, according to each person's view of Islam in relation to another's, and few are the people who have grasped Islam as a complete, clear picture embracing all these meanings together.

These multiple images of the one Islam in people's souls made them differ greatly in their view of the Muslim Brothers and their conception of their idea. Some people conceive the Muslim Brothers as a preaching, exhortational association whose whole concern is to offer people sermons that admonish them in this world and remind them of the Hereafter. Some conceive them as a *Ṣūfī* path concerned with teaching people the forms of *dhikr* and the arts of servitude and what accompanies that of detachment and renunciation. Some think them a theoretical, legal group whose only concern is to stop at a certain body of rulings, dispute about them, defend them, and burden people with them, making peace or war with whoever accepts or rejects them. Few are those who know the Muslim Brothers in the reality of their call.
