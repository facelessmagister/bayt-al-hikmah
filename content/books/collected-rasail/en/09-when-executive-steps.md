# When Will Our Executive Steps Be?

O Muslim Brothers:

We are here in what I consider a critical, familial matter concerning the Muslim Brothers. I wish to be utterly frank with you, for nothing remains useful except honest counsel. The arena of speech is not the arena of action, the arena of action is not the arena of speech, the arena of *jihād* is not the arena of action, and the arena of mere *jihād* is not the arena of productive, wise *jihād* that yields the greatest profit at the least cost.

I know that thoughts occur to you, but not every thought that passes through the mind can be clothed in words on the tongue. Many can speak, but few of these many are firm at work. Many of this few can act, but few of them can bear the burdens of arduous *jihād* and violent action. These strugglers, the elite few of the supporters, have mistaken the way and will not attain their goal unless they pass through the cage of Saul, as I say — prepare yourselves, accept sound cultivation and precise testing, examine yourselves by action, by a strong, severe examination of the soul upon that, and wean it from its desires, familiarities, and habits.

At the time when there are, among you, O Muslim Brothers, three hundred battalions, each of which has prepared itself spiritually, in creed, in knowledge and culture, and physically through training and sport — at that time ask me to plunge with you into the depths of the seas, to storm the heights of the heavens, and to raid every obstinate enemy. I shall do so, Allah willing. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truth when he said: "Twelve thousand will not be overcome by a small company." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:65] I estimate for that a time not far off, after putting trust in Allah, seeking His assistance, and submitting to His decree and will. You may be able to shorten this term through your deputies and representatives when you reach your goal and double your efforts. You may err in this reckoning, and the expected results will vary. Therefore, make yourselves feel the responsibility, form the battalions, establish the units, apply yourselves to lessons, hasten to training, spread your call in places that have not yet been reached, and do not put a thought into anything except action.

I do not mean by this to disparage words or to belittle those present. This is not my meaning. The Muslim Brothers are many in their earnestness for Allah, and this gathering has thousands of members, each of whom stands for a complete company whose number cannot be belittled, whose efforts cannot be slighted, or whose right can be disregarded. But I mean what I first said: the man of speech is not the man of action; the man of action is not the man of *jihād*; and the man of mere *jihād* is not the man of productive, wise *jihād* that leads to the greatest profit at the least sacrifice.

## 5 — Preference for the Practical Side

As for preferring the practical side over propaganda and announcements: the Muslim Brothers were moved by this in their nature and called to it in their program for several reasons. Among them is what Islam especially brought concerning the hidden side of actions, and the fact that if these actions are mixed with the impurities of showing off, decay and corruption quickly rush to them. Balancing this with what is reported about publicising good deeds, hastening to announce them, and exceeding oneself in them is a delicate matter that can only be accomplished with divine success.

Among the reasons also is the natural caution of the Muslim Brothers, because people are accustomed to false propaganda and sensationalism behind which there is no action; and what this has produced in the Ummah of bad effect, great misguidance, and palpable corruption. Among them also is what the Muslim Brothers feared of the call's clashing with a sharp opponent or a harmful friendship, either of which causes obstruction to progress or suspension from the goal. All these matters placed the Muslim Brothers in the balance, and they preferred to proceed in their call with earnestness and speed, unperceived except by those near them, though their matter becomes known in their core.

Few people know the caller among the callers of Islam in the modern age who has emerged in the guise of a reformer. In the evening he is in Minya lecturing the people; in the Friday prayer he is a preacher travelling far and wide; in the afternoon he is a lecturer in Asyūṭ; after sunset he is a lecturer in Sohag. Then he returns, and in the early morning he is in Cairo at his job before his brothers from the employees — four public gatherings delivered by a caller of the Muslim Brothers' callers in the corners of the country within thirty hours. Then he returns, his soul serene, his heart content, praising Allah for what He has enabled him to do, perceived only by those whose religion has heard him.

This effort, if done by anyone other than the Muslim Brothers, would fill the world with morning clamour and propaganda. But the Muslim Brothers prefer that the people see nothing from them except practical deeds. Whoever is convinced by action, they have convinced; and whoever is not affected by action, words will not guide him.

One of them may spend a month or more away from his family — his household, wife, and children — calling to Allah. He is in the night a lecturer, in the day a traveller, one day in Suez and another in ʿAqma, delivering more than sixty lectures in a week from the east of the country to its west, attended by thousands from different classes; then after that he instructs that this should not be exploited for propaganda or advertisement.

The Muslim Brothers hold a model camp near Alexandria for about a month, a model camp in the true sense, from the sport of the mind and spirit to the sport of the body and physique, embodying in complete clarity and vividness the complete athletic and military form. This continues throughout the period and brings under its blessed tent a hundred pious, believing young men. It cannot but have an echo among the Muslim Brothers who attended it.

They hold a general conference like this one, which in reality is the truest mirror of Egypt, for in it are represented its schools, centres, villages, and towns from every class in truest form. You have all attended, impelled by nothing but firm desire for productive work. We direct the call to you and gather you in this blessed place with the Muslim Brothers.

The Muslim Brothers do this and other forms of reform that produce the best effects. In doing so they do not boast, show off, or even mention it beyond the truth, let alone exaggerate. If some of this activity and some of these works were done by anyone other than the Muslim Brothers, earthly parties would shout about it from East and West. No wonder, for we live in an age of propaganda.

O Brothers:

The meaning you intend is indeed a lofty meaning and a path derived from Allah and accepted by people. Be earnest in it and do not fear. But observe that you have now grown, and the call has raised you to a stage where you cross private barriers into wide horizons. The call has revealed itself, and people have begun to ask about it and about you. The curious have begun voluntarily to draw for others a picture of it, knowing little or much. Therefore, it is incumbent upon you to make your goal, your means, the bounds of your idea, and the program of your works clear to the people. Announce these matters to the people, not for boasting, but in guidance to what brings benefit and good to the Ummah and its sons. Write to *an-Nadhīr*, which is your tongue; write to the daily newspapers, and say: "Do not stand in my way." Take care to be truthful men who do not transgress the truth. Let your propagation be in the manners of the noble, virtuous, free, full eagerness to gather hearts and unite souls. And feel, as your call has appeared, that the favour in all of it is Allah's:

> "Rather, Allah has conferred favour upon you that He has guided you to faith, if you are truthful." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:17]

## 6 — The Attraction of Youth to the Call

As for the attraction of youth to the call and its growth in many circles, especially the most fertile soil for calls — the educated and middle classes — my joy is great, and we praise Allah for it. Youth everywhere have come to the call of the Muslim Brothers, believing in it, supporting it, aiding it, and pledging to Allah to rise with it and work in its paths.

Six young men from the university came forward years ago, and Allah made their presence sweet and their existence pleasant. Allah favoured them and aided them, and behold, the university became full of supporters of the Muslim Brothers, bearing their burden, protecting them, and wishing them success. And behold, among the university youth there is a pious, believing band that devotes itself to the call and spreads everywhere.

The same is true of noble al-Azhar. Al-Azhar is by nature the fortress of the Islamic call and the refuge of Islam. It is not strange that it should consider the Muslim Brothers' call its own, and count their goal as its goal. Its active, virtuous young men, teachers, and preachers fill the ranks and clubs of the Muslim Brothers, and in them is the greatest effect in spreading the call, supporting it, and proclaiming it everywhere. The attraction of youth was not limited to the classes of virtuous students and their like; rather, many from the classes of the people, believing young men, came to the call and were a great help in supporting it. Many of these young men were astray and Allah guided them; heedless and Allah directed them; accustomed to sin and Allah enabled them to obey; knowing no purpose in life and Allah clarified the purpose before them:

> "Allah guides to His light whom He wills." [Qur'an, An-Nūr 24:35]

We consider this among the signs of divine success, and every day we see new progress in this regard, calling to strong hope, perseverance, and redoubling of effort. Victory is only from Allah, the Mighty, the Wise.

## 7 — Rapid Spread in Villages and Towns

As for the rapid spread of the call in villages and towns: I have already told you that the call arose in Ismailia and grew up in its pure atmosphere. It walked upon its extensive sands, nourished and refreshed every morning and evening by the manifestations of foreign occupation and European monopoly of the good of this land — from the Suez Canal, the source and root of the affliction; in the west, the English camp with its equipment and supplies; in the east, the General Office of the Canal Company with its furniture, presidency, grandeur, and salaries. The Egyptian is a stranger amid all these atmospheres in his own land, deprived, while the foreigner enjoys the good of his homeland by night, and the foreigner boasts of seizing his means of livelihood. This bitterness was abundant nourishment and powerful support for the call of the Muslim Brothers. We spread our carpets in the Canal region, then crossed to the Small Sea, then to the governorate of Daqahlīya. There it occupies the hearts of the believers by a small, humble seed; then it is not long before it takes possession of these hearts and engrosses their feelings and thoughts, becoming the greatest of hopes and the dearest of concerns, so that the one who calls sacrifices, gives, and humbles himself.

The call proceeded to Cairo, joining with its Islamic civilisation, its innovations and customs, to the Muslim Brothers with a complete idea, preferring action over [empty] groupness, asceticism towards titles and names, and contempt for these individual vanities that have spoiled for us every work. After that the General Guidance Office was formed in Cairo, supervising the branches of the young group in the provinces and countries, working tirelessly to spread the idea and make it reach the lands that had not yet connected with it. Its members spend of their money, their time, and their efforts what they can afford to serve their creed, with the chastity of the lion and the purity of the water of the oasis, harming no one at all, asking no one for anything, neither taking from the government's money nor seeking help from anyone except Allah, until the web of the Muslim Brothers spread with astonishing speed throughout all parts of the Egyptian land — from Aswān to Alexandria, to Rashīd, to Port Said, to Suez, to Ṭanṭā, to Fayyūm, to Banī Suwayf, to Minya, to Asyūṭ, to Jirjā, to Qinā, and between them all the centres and villages. It did not stop at these Egyptian borders but crossed them to the southern part of the beloved homeland, to the noble Sudan, then to many parts of the Islamic East: Syria with its eastern and western sections, and then to other blessed Islamic lands. We used to direct the call and work to spread it from afar. But now the call drives us to villages and towns and compels us to pursue it and discharge its rights, whatever effort and fatigue that involves. The important thing is that the connection between these branches, beyond similarity in name or unity in general purpose — nay, rather the strongest of connections — is the connection of deep love, mutual cooperation, ancient bond, complete gathering around the axis of the call and its centre, comprehensive unity in love, hope, *jihād*, action, means, goals, programs, and steps. There is no increase beyond that.

These branches in the countries and villages are not restricted in their work to carrying out the instructions of the central office in Cairo. Rather, they find and work in wide fields of service. They establish their clubs, and many of them have built their own houses that have become property purely their own. Many of them have also undertaken many charitable, economic, and social projects, and their continued activity is productive activity. The connection of the office with its branches and diverse bodies is not that of a president to subordinates, nor of mere administration and general supervision; it is above all that: a connection of spirit, a connection of members of one family, each to the other, visiting one another for the sake of Allah. The callers of the Muslim Brothers visit their brothers, mix with them, and know everything connected with their lives and their private and public affairs. Such a thing is not found in a hundred existing parties, to my knowledge. That is a favour of Allah; He gives it to whom He wills.

O Muslim Brothers:

I do not hide from you that I am proud of this sincere brotherly unity. Be proud of this strong, firm divine bond; great is the hope in the future so long as you remain brothers in Allah, loving and cooperating. Be eager for this unity, for it is your weapon and your equipment.

Many people ask: From where do the Muslim Brothers meet the expenses of this call, which are great expenses that overwhelm the rich, let alone the poor? Let these and others know that the Muslim Brothers do not begrudge their call any day of their days, with their effort, their wealth, their sweat, their blood, and their lives, let alone their possessions and what is easy for them. When they took up this burden they knew very well that this religion is not satisfied with anything less than blood and wealth. They went out from all of that for Allah's sake, acting upon His words:

> "Indeed, Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties [in exchange] for that they will have Paradise." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:111]

They accepted the sale and offered the merchandise willingly, with contented souls, believing that all favour belongs to Allah. They became independent in Allah from the hands of people, and Allah blessed the little, so it produced much. Until now the General Guidance Office has not received a single aid from any government whatsoever, and it boasts and challenges all people to say that a single piastre from other than the pockets of its members has entered its treasury. We want nothing but this, and we shall not accept except from a member or a friend. We shall not depend upon governments for anything; do not occupy yourselves with arranging your affairs around that, do not look to it, do not act for it, but ask Allah of His bounty. Indeed, Allah is over all things Omniscient.

That, O Muslim Brothers, is some of the characteristics of your call. I have taken this opportunity to speak to you about it, and I now turn to an important side of the call concerning which the position of the Muslim Brothers has become confused in the minds of many people, and which perhaps some of the Muslim Brothers themselves do not fully know. Let us define it together and examine together what may be said concerning it.
