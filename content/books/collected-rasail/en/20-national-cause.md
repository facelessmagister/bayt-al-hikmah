# Epistle 4: Our National Cause and How It Is Solved by the Islamic Orientation

Our national rights are well known. The Ummah has declared them with full clarity and openness, on the tongues of its parties, bodies, groups, and individuals on every occasion. They are: the realisation of the unity of the Nile Valley, south and north, and the removal of all foreign troops from it, so that its freedom and independence may become a reality.

Upright Islam proclaims freedom, establishes it, and confirms it for individuals, nations, and groups in the finest of its meanings. It calls to pride in freedom and to preserving it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever accepts humiliation for himself willingly, without being compelled, is not of me." It fights that international banditry called imperialism with all its might, for imperialism will never be pleased for a nation to be sovereign or for one people to deprive another people. Still ringing in the ears are the words of ʿUmar al-Fārūq, may Allah be pleased with him, when he said to his governor ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ: "You were born free, so when you come to them, let them find you still free."

On the day the Muslim conqueror strapped the sword upon his shoulder and set out as a warrior to the ends of the earth, he did not seek worldly gain behind that, nor did he aspire to seize the wealth of nations and peoples for himself, even though his hands might be filled with it without intending it. Rather, he believed in a call, carried a message, and defended in the world the principles of freedom, justice, and peace.

The history of the first era of the rightly guided, rightly acting predecessors — the proof of Islam — gives you this picture clearly, its boundaries distinct.

Moreover, Islam regards the Muslim Ummah as the trustee of Allah's message on His earth, and in the world it holds the rank of leadership — not, we say, the rank of humbling itself before anyone, or enslaving itself to anyone, or softening its conviction for a winker, or submitting to an aggressor who exceeds the limit:

> "And Allah will never give the disbelievers a way over the believers." [Qur'an, An-Nisā' 4:141]

When Islam decreed this, it decreed the practical path to safeguarding this freedom: it prescribed *jihād* with soul and wealth, made it a collective obligation to secure the call, and an individual obligation upon every son of the Ummah when invading forces from non-Muslims appeared; it made martyrdom the highest of ranks; it promised the *mujāhidūn* victory and support in this world, permanence and abiding bliss in the Hereafter; and it declared *jihād* the best of deeds after faith:

> "Indeed, those who have believed and emigrated and fought in the cause of Allah with their wealth and their lives are greater in rank in the presence of Allah. And it is they who are the successful. Their Lord gives them good tidings of mercy from Him and approval, and gardens in which there is enduring pleasure, wherein they abide forever. Indeed, Allah has with Him a great reward." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:20-22]

Yet with all this, Islam welcomed peaceful means and the resort to arbitration whenever these means led to the full right of the claimants:

> "And if they incline to peace, then incline to it [also]." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:61]

When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was given a choice between two matters, he would choose the easier so long as it was not forbidden. Negotiation is one of the peaceful means if it attains the full right; the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, negotiated at Ḥudaybiyah. Arbitration is likewise one of the peaceful means if it leads to this right. We do not know that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, or any of the rightly guided, rightly acting caliphs, accepted arbitration by a disbeliever; but this is required by the generality of the verse and by the necessity of agreement upon good that does not harm Islam, between Muslims and others, when it contains their interest and no harm to them.

If these peaceful efforts fail, Islam's judgment is explicit on "renunciation" (*nabdh*) that includes declaring hostility. Then one proceeds at once to every means of *jihād*:

> "And if you fear from a people betrayal, throw [their treaty] back to them, [putting you and them] on equal terms. Indeed, Allah does not like traitors." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:58]

> "And let not the disbelievers think they have escaped. Indeed, they will not cause failure [to Allah]." [Qur'an, An-Nisā' 4:104]

> "And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:60]

The promise and covenant of the *mujāhidūn* for the truth is that Allah will defend them and grant them victory, no matter how numerous their enemies, with complete readiness, mighty numbers, abundant equipment, and strong means. They must not be concerned by that, but rely upon Allah alone:

> "And it was incumbent upon Us to support the believers." [Qur'an, Ar-Rūm 30:47]

These rulings are comprehensive and established in Islam; whoever has drunk from its spring and taken his share of its jurisprudence knows them with broader detail and stronger, more precise, and more correct evidence. By their light we can solve our national cause, which has reached a degree of irritation that has unsettled minds and disturbed ears.

Here is the explanation of that:

We negotiated, but reached nothing because of English obstinacy, rigidity, and manoeuvring.

We resorted to arbitration, but likewise reached nothing, in the face of the dominance of international interests and imperialist ambitions. A fine writer said that we need wide propaganda for our cause by presenting it before the eyes of the whole world and transferring it from the narrow field of bilateral understanding to the broad field of international arbitration; and that is true. But this literary gain will not compensate for the actual reality in the slightest, namely that we have no credit with the English, as if we had not advanced a step. On the contrary, this stagnation is a cause of questioning and affliction.

Therefore nothing remains but "renunciation on equal terms": to declare to them open, frank hostility; to resolve clearly the annulment of all treaties and agreements between us and them; to declare that the Ummah of the Valley is in a state of war with them (even if passive); and to organise our lives on this basis. Economically: by self-sufficiency and limiting ourselves to what we and our Arab and Muslim brothers have, and to friendly states if they exist. Socially: by encouraging the spirit of dignity and honour; by training all youth militarily so that every individual becomes a soldier; and by uniting the hearts of the people for that through complete, full, widespread propaganda, as nations do when they face a real state of war. All social conditions will change on this basis.

This work is not initially demanded of individuals or of bodies; rather, the government is responsible for it first and last. The strange thing is that the Prime Minister declared this clearly in the Security Council, then returned and did nothing and did not advance one step along this path. This is the duty of the government.

As for the people, we say to them with clarity, openness, and confidence: they are fully prepared to sacrifice everything if the government follows this path. They are prepared for hunger and nakedness... for death, struggle, and fighting in the severest forms of struggle and combat. But on condition that this be in the path of their freedom and independence, not in the path of the confusion of government committees, the weakness of administrative means, the floundering of economic policy, and standing before English intrigues and pressure in the posture of helpless, submissive people.

I heard a poor worker say, when the orders were issued to mix the grains: "I am ready, I and my children, to eat only once a day, if we are sure that this is in the path of freedom and deliverance from the English. But I am utterly indignant because I do not understand why we resort to this mixing when we are an agricultural country whose greatest produce is foodstuffs!"

The people are fully prepared to sacrifice, but along a clear, marked road leading to freedom or martyrdom, under the leadership of a firm government that traces for them, with strength and sincerity, the stages of that road. But if the government continues in its hesitation, laxity, and disorder, that will lead the people to one of two things: either they "revolt," or they "die" — and both are national wounds that history will never forgive.

So, O Prime Minister; O men of noble al-Azhar; O leaders of bodies and parties; O men jealous for this homeland; O sons of the Ummah, all of you:

This is the path, so walk in it in the light of Islam, and act.

I have conveyed; so witness, O Allah.
