# Introduction

Praise be to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger. To proceed:

This book is divided into two parts. The first part deals with the attributes that must be present in the Muslim—every Muslim—so that he may thereby be a Muslim. It explains the conditions that must be present in everyone who belongs to this religion.

For many people are Muslims by inheritance, or Muslims because they were born to Muslim parents. These and those like them do not, in reality, realize the meaning of their belonging to Islam, nor do they know the requirements of this belonging. That is why you see them in one valley and Islam in another.

The purpose of the first part of this book is to answer all these questions and to clarify what Islam requires of the Muslim so that his belonging to Islam may be sound and real, and consequently so that he may be truly a Muslim:

> He has chosen you and has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty. [It is] the religion of your father, Abraham. He named you "Muslims" before and in this [revelation] that the Messenger may be a witness over you and you may be witnesses over the people. So establish prayer (*salah*, الصلاة) and give *zakah* (الزكاة) and hold fast to Allah. He is your protector; and excellent is the Protector, and excellent is the Helper. [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:78]

As for the second part of the book, it explains the obligation of working for Islam and belonging to the Islamic movement (*al-harakah al-Islamiyyah*, الحركة الإسلامية), as it deals with the characteristics of the Islamic movement, its goals, its means, its philosophy, its way of working, and the attributes that must be present in those who belong to it.

## The Ummah's Crisis

And among what is worth mentioning at this point is that all the events taking place in the Islamic world in general and in the Arab region in particular confirm a great truth: that the *Ummah* (الأمة) is experiencing a fatal void in all aspects of its life.

The Ummah passed through the past period in harsh circumstances. Many systems and principles fell during it, and many movements and leaderships were exposed. When all these were placed on the front line of confrontation with the various challenges of the age, they fell because they did not possess, in the first place, the factors of survival and continuity.

They fell because they were empty, glittering slogans, having no value and no content.

They fell because they were not authentic; they did not express the personality of this Ummah. They were foreign, artificial, imported—as we import shoes and socks. And these did not last long. They were soon uncovered; soon their evils appeared.

For that reason the masses rejected them, because they were alien to them, not compatible with their principles and beliefs. They are like a kidney or a heart that is transplanted into the human body: if the body accepts them, it is for a short while full of suffering and pain, then the body soon wastes away and dies.

This is what actually happened to the Islamic Ummah when it woke to its feeble, sick self. It did not think about what it was doing. Rather, it hastened to import whatever it thought were sound principles and man-made systems—systems that carried within them factors of destruction and ruin, factors of chaos and corruption, factors of loss and wandering.

As for the first operation, it was planting Western civilization and capitalist thought into the entity of this Ummah. The result was a cancerous growth that appeared in every aspect of its social, economic, and political life. A cancerous growth that worked to distort its personality, to confuse its thoughts, to corrupt its morals—and finally to prepare it to receive the first defeat, the defeat of 1948.

And despite the fierceness of the experience and the severity of the catastrophe, the Ummah remained captive to its loss and wandering, drawn by its emotions, taken by false slogans and deceptive appearances. This is what made it, for the second, third, and fourth time, drink from the same cup of defeat one after another. Its progressive-revolutionary-socialist systems did not avail it at all; indeed, its intimate alliances did not shield it from harm.

If the defeat of 1948 was the harvest of Western imperialist humiliation, then the defeat of 1967 is the harvest of leftist proletarian dependency.

And the Ummah emerged from these events and experiences laden with worries, saturated with wounds. Its hopes in those on whom it had pinned its hopes were disappointed, and its trust in the leaders and commanders around it was shaken.

So did the Ummah wake up after that, do you think? Did it recover from the shock and the severity of the experience? Did it realize that the international powers—all the international powers—are conspiring against it?

It is indeed the utmost misguidance for the Ummah to be swept away by this current, [as if] it must be either communist or capitalist.

## Islam: The Only Filling of the Void

Rather, the Islamic Ummah must realize that it has an independent, distinctive personality: a personality that is neither rightist nor leftist, an authentic personality that derives its characteristics and features from Islam—the religion of the *fitrah* (الفطرة, the innate nature) and the message of the *fitrah*. And by virtue of this distinction and authenticity, it can assume a place of intellectual and political leadership in the world.

It must realize that this great void that it is living through is not filled by the projects of the American White House, nor by the projects of the Soviet Kremlin; not by the ideas of Marx and Lenin, nor by the principles of Guevara and Ho Chi Minh.

Indeed, this void is filled by nothing except Islam: as creed and system, as morals and legislation, as all of that. Therefore, it places the Islamic movement before a historic, fateful responsibility—a responsibility that requires an act of faith and a will that endures. Are the callers to Islam aware of their responsibility?

## The Subjects of This Book

The first part of this book, entitled *Mādhā Yaʿnī Intimāʾī lil-Islām* (What My Belonging to Islam Means), deals with the most important attributes that must be present in a person for him to be truly a Muslim.

For belonging to Islam is not belonging by inheritance, nor belonging by identity, nor is it belonging by outward appearance. Rather, it is belonging to Islam, commitment to Islam, and adaptation to Islam in all aspects of life.

And in what follows we will briefly explain the most prominent attributes that should be present in the Muslim so that his belonging to this religion may be a sound, truthful belonging:

> He named you "Muslims" before and in this [revelation] that the Messenger may be a witness over you and you may be witnesses over the people. [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:78]

1. That I be a Muslim in my *ʿaqeedah* (عقيدتي, creed)
2. That I be a Muslim in my *ʿibādah* (عبادتي, worship)
3. That I be a Muslim in my public life
4. That I be a Muslim in my family and home
5. That I overcome my self
6. That I be confident that the future belongs to Islam

That I be a Muslim.
