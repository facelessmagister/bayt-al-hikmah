# Chapter 13: Understanding the Conditions of Brotherhood and Membership

## The Meaning of Brotherhood in Islam

Brotherhood in Islam (*al-ukhuwwah*) is a great divine bond that ties hearts together. It is the bond of closeness for the sake of Allah, and it is among the strongest ties of faith, just as prayer and its connection are — for the Prophet said: “The strongest ties of faith are: love for the sake of Allah and hatred for the sake of Allah.” [Musnad Aḥmad]

Brotherhood is one of the supports upon which Islam relies to consolidate the structure of the Muslim society and strengthen the bonds among its children.

The Islamic movement is in the utmost need of deepening this bond, so that it may be like a solid building whose parts reinforce one another, or like a single body: when one of its limbs complains, all the other limbs respond with sleeplessness and fever.

So that brotherhood may play its required role in the formation of the community, Islam has established for it practical, tangible rights and duties — to emphasize that it is responsibilities and burdens, not merely words and theories.

## The Rights and Duties of Brotherhood

1. **Brotherhood helps one to obey Allah.** This is in fulfillment of the Prophet's saying, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever Allah intends good for, He grants him a righteous friend; when he forgets, he reminds him, and when he remembers, he helps him.” [Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi, Book of Asceticism, Hadith 2393] To this ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may Allah be pleased with him, pointed when he said: “Keep to the brothers of truth, and live in their midst, for they are an adornment in prosperity and a refuge in calamity.”

2. **Brotherhood is mutual support and concern for the needs of the brother, striving to fulfill them.** This is in fulfillment of the saying of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: “For one of you to walk with his brother in fulfilling a need is better for him than secluding himself in this mosque of mine for a month.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Good Manners, Hadith 6126; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2543] And brotherhood is the sharing of mutual confidence, reliance upon the truthfulness of the other, his sincerity, and a deep trust that produces affection, esteem, respect, and obedience.

3. **Brotherhood is material mutual support.** This is in fulfillment of the Prophet's saying, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever relieves a Muslim of a distress of this world, Allah will relieve him of a distress on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever makes things easy for one in difficulty, Allah will make things easy for him in this world and the Hereafter. Whoever conceals the faults of a Muslim, Allah will conceal his faults in this world and the Hereafter. Allah is ever helping His servant as long as His servant helps his brother.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Virtues, Hadith 2699; Sunan Abū Dāwūd; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi; Sunan al-Nasāʾī; Sunan Ibn Mājah]

4. **Brotherhood entails social duties that cover the simplest and most important obligations.** The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “The rights of a Muslim upon a Muslim are six: when you meet him, greet him with peace; when he invites you, respond to him; when he seeks your counsel, counsel him; when he sneezes and praises Allah, say *yarḥamuk Allāh*; when he falls ill, visit him; and when he dies, follow his funeral.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2162]

5. **Brotherhood is intimacy, affection, and solidarity.** The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Do not cut ties, do not turn away from one another, do not harbor hatred, do not envy, and be servants of Allah as brothers. It is not lawful for a Muslim to shun his brother for more than three nights.” [Muwattaʾ Imām Mālik; Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Manners, Hadith 6077; Sunan Abū Dāwūd; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi; Sunan al-Nasāʾī] And he said: “Do not belittle any good deed, even if it is to meet your brother with a cheerful face.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Virtue, Enjoining Good Manners, and Joining Ties of Kinship, Hadith 2626] And he said: “Every good deed is charity; and among good deeds is that you meet your brother with a cheerful face, and that you empty your bucket into the vessel of your brother.” [Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi, Book on Righteousness and Maintaining Ties of Kinship, Hadith 1911; declared ḥasan by al-Albāni] And he said: “Exchange gifts, you will love one another, and rancor will depart.” [Muwattaʾ Imām Mālik; al-Adab al-Sharʿīyah by Ibn Mufliḥ; declared ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albāni]

## The Obligations of the Muslim Brother

Among the obligations of the Muslim brother — the one who belongs to the Islamic movement — the Martyr Imām set forth, in the Message of Teachings, some twenty obligations. In them he summarized to the truthful brother: that binding yourself by this *bayʿah* (pledge) obligates you to fulfill these duties, so that you may be a strong brick in the structure.

1. **You should have a daily portion (*wird*) from the Book of Allah** no less than one *juzʾ*, and strive not to exceed one month — and not less than three days — in completing it.

2. **You should perfect your recitation of the Qur'an, listening to it, and reflecting upon its meanings.** You should study the pure Sīrah and the history of the righteous predecessors (*al-salaf*) to the extent your time allows; at minimum, the book *Ḥammāt al-Islām* is sufficient for this. You should read abundantly from the *aḥādīth* of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, and memorize at least forty *aḥādīth* — let them be the forty of al-Nawawī. And you should study a treatise on the foundations of creed and a treatise on the branches of jurisprudence.

3. **You should undergo a general medical examination,** and take every means of physical strength and health protection, and keep away from causes of bodily weakness.

4. **You should avoid excess in coffee and tea,** and reduce them from among stimulating drinks, drinking them only when necessary; and you should abstain entirely from smoking.

5. **You should attend to cleanliness in everything:** in your dwelling, your clothing, your food, your body, and your workplace. For Islam has emphasized cleanliness.

6. **You should be truthful in word; do not ever lie.**

7. **You should be faithful to the covenant, the word, and the promise; do not break them,** whatever the circumstances.

8. **You should be courageous and patient in bearing hardship.** The best of courage is speaking the truth, keeping the secret, admitting the mistake, being fair to oneself, and controlling oneself in anger.

9. **You should be dignified,** preferring seriousness always. This does not prevent you from good-natured joking and a smiling laugh.

10. **You should be strongly characterized by modesty,** sensitive in feeling, greatly affected by good and evil — rejoicing in the former and pained by the latter. You should be humble without humiliation, servility, or flattery. You should ask for less than your rank until you attain it.

11. **You should be just and sound in judgment in all situations.** Do not let anger make you forget good deeds, nor let satisfaction close the eye of criticism. Do not let dispute make you forget the burden; speak the truth even if it is against yourself, or against the closest of people to you, even if it is bitter.

12. **You should possess great activity and be trained in public service.** Feel happiness and joy when you are able to offer a service to another person. Visit the sick, help the needy, carry the weak, console the afflicted — even with a kind word — and always hasten to good deeds.

13. **You should be tenderhearted, kind, generous,** pardoning and overlooking, gentle and forbearing, merciful toward human beings and animals. Have good dealings and good conduct with all people, preserving the Islamic social etiquettes: mercy toward the young, reverence for the elderly, making room in gatherings, not spying, not backbiting, not shouting, and asking permission to enter and to leave, and so on.

14. **You should perfect reading and writing,** read extensively in the treatises, newspapers, and magazines of the brothers, and the like. You should have a personal library, however small. You should delve deeply into your knowledge and craft if you are a specialist, and you should be well-acquainted with general Islamic affairs to the extent that enables you to form a conception of them and judge them in accordance with the requirements of the idea.

15. **You should work with your hands, economically,** however rich you may be. You should take up free work, however humble it may be, and plunge into it yourself, whatever your scholarly talents.

16. **Do not be eager for a government position;** regard it as the narrowest of doors to livelihood. Do not reject it if it is made available to you, and do not turn away from it unless it conflicts completely with the duties of the call.

17. **You should be eager above all else to perform your craft in a manner characterized by honesty,** precision, avoidance of cheating, and punctuality.

18. **You should demand your right in a good manner,** and fulfill the rights of people completely, without shortcoming, without being asked or procrastinating.

19. **Keep away from gambling in all its forms,** whatever the motive behind it, and avoid means of unlawful earnings, however immediate their gain may seem.

20. **Keep away from usury (*ribā*) in all transactions,** and purify yourself from it completely.

21. **Serve the general Islamic economy** by encouraging Islamic manufactures and economic institutions, and be keen that your money does not fall into non-Islamic hands, whatever the circumstances. Do not wear or consume except what your Islamic homeland produces.

22. **Contribute a portion of your wealth to the call,** and pay the obligatory *zakāh* due on it, and allocate a known right from it to the asker and the deprived, however small your income.

23. **Set aside a part of your income for emergencies,** however little.

24. **Do what you can to revive Islamic customs** and suppress foreign customs in every manifestation of life: the greeting, language, history, dress, furniture, work and rest hours, food and drink, arrival and departure, sorrow and joy, and so on. Be especially careful to follow the pure Sunnah in all of this.

25. **Boycott civil courts and every non-Islamic judiciary,** and clubs, newspapers, societies, schools, and institutes that oppose your Islamic idea — boycott them completely.

26. **Keep constant watch over yourself before Allah,** remember the Hereafter and prepare for it. Traverse the stages of traveling toward Allah's pleasure with determination and resolve; draw near to Him, glorified be He, through supererogatory acts of worship, including the night prayer (*qiyām al-layl*), fasting at least three days of every month, abundant remembrance of Allah with the heart and tongue, and the authenticated supplication in all circumstances.

27. **Perfect your purification,** and remain in a state of *wuḍūʾ* for most of the time.

28. **Perfect your prayer and be constant in performing it at its appointed times,** and be eager for congregational prayer in the mosque whenever possible.

29. **Fast Ramadan,** and perform *Ḥajj* to the House if you are able to find a way to it; and work toward that if you are not yet able.

30. **Always keep with you the intention of *jihād* and love of martyrdom,** and prepare for that to the extent that preparation is possible for you.

31. **Be constant in repentance and seeking forgiveness,** guard against minor sins as well as major ones, and set aside an hour before sleep in which you take yourself to account for the good and evil you have done. Be keen about time, for it is life. Do not spend any part of it without benefit, and keep away from doubtful matters lest you fall into the forbidden.

32. **Strive against your soul with a mighty striving** until its reins become easy for you. Lower your gaze, restrain your emotion, resist the impulses of instinct in yourself, and elevate it always to the wholesome lawful, keeping it away from the unlawful, whatever that may be.

33. **Avoid wine, intoxicants, drugs, and everything of this sort,** shunning it altogether.

34. **Keep away from bad companions, corrupt friends, places of disobedience, and dens of iniquity.**

35. **Fight places of amusement,** let alone remaining in them; and keep away from all manifestations of luxury and softness.

36. **Know the members of your cell individually,** thoroughly, and let them know you thoroughly as well. Fulfill their rights completely: love, esteem, help, and preferring them. Attend their meetings and do not be absent from them except for an overwhelming excuse; let your treatment of them be affectionate.

37. **Do not sever your connection with any branch, organization, or group** whose connection is not contrary to your idea, especially when you are commanded to do so.

38. **Work to spread your call in every place,** and keep the leadership informed of all your circumstances. Do not undertake any action that would affect it essentially except by permission. Be in constant spiritual contact with it, and consider yourself always a soldier in the line awaiting orders.
