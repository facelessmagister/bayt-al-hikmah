# Chapter 12: Understanding the Pillars of Islamic Work

## Seventh: Realizing the Conditions of *Bay'ah* and Membership

- The duties of the Muslim brother

---

### To Realize the Conditions of *Bay'ah* and Membership

To realize the conditions of *bay'ah* (pledge of allegiance, بَيْعَة) and membership: every Muslim is able to share with the Islamic movement (*al-ḥarakah al-Islāmiyyah*, الحَرَكَةُ الإِسْلَامِيَّةُ) the burdens of working for Islam, by joining its ranks, once the qualities previously mentioned are found in him, and once he feels the bitter reality in which the Ummah lives, understands the secret of this backwardness, believes in Islam's ability to revive this Ummah and rescue it, and to elevate it to the highest human, civilizational, and moral levels—together with what this faith requires of total submission to the rulings and principles of Islam in all affairs of his life.

## Quality, Not Quantity

It is only natural that the Islamic movement should seek to expand its ranks and increase its numbers through the various legitimate means and methods. But this must not lead it to gather individuals haphazardly and without criteria. *Taw'iyah* (raising awareness/education, تَوْعِيَةُ) is more important than quantity. The call may exert effort to win an individual, spending time and effort in the hope that this individual will take his place in the community and bear his share of responsibility and his portion of obligations. But it refuses to let the community carry its members throughout their lives, for that is a waste of effort and a squandering of work.

An individual may be an entire Ummah; and he may be a harmful and burdensome weight that does damage and brings no benefit. Not placing him in the ranks is better and more proper. There are many lessons to be taken to heart:

> "[Remember] the day of Ḥunayn when your great number pleased you, but it did not avail you in any way, and the earth seemed narrow to you despite its spaciousness; then you turned back, fleeing." [Qur'an, Al-Tawbah 9:25]

Numerical quantity is not the first criterion. Rather, it belongs to the small group who know Allah, are steadfast, and sincere to Him. Quantity is sometimes a cause of defeat, because some of those swept into it—those who enjoy its abundance without having understood the reality of Islam or committed themselves to it—will have their feet slip and their hearts tremble in the hour of hardship. Thereby they spread disturbance and panic among the souls and in the ranks.

Therefore, evaluating and classifying individuals becomes one of the movement's duties, beneath the appearance of its health, so that it may draw its true manpower from the best human resources before beginning any work. This does not mean that the movement should abandon the rest of its members; rather, it continues to educate and raise them so that they become a permanent resource.

## *Bay'ah* and Its Legal Ruling

*Bay'ah* is a pledge of obedience. It is as if the one pledging makes a covenant with his leader that he will surrender to him the disposal of his own soul and the affairs of the Muslims, not contesting him in anything of that, and obeying him in whatever he is charged with, whether willingly or unwillingly.

*Bay'ah* is a Prophetic *Sunnah* that the Muslims gave to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ repeatedly in his life—such as the First and Second *Bay'ahs* of al-'Aqabah and the *Bay'ah* of al-Riḍwān—and it remained in effect after his death, given by the Muslims to those in authority over them.

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Junādah ibn Abī Umayyah, from 'Ubādah ibn al-Ṣāmit, who said: "He called us, and we pledged allegiance to him, and among the things he took from us was that we pledged to hear and obey, whether in ease or hardship, and in what we like and dislike, and that we would not dispute the command with its people unless we see clear disbelief (*kufr*) for which we have proof from Allah."

Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān said in *Al-Dīn al-Khāliṣ*: "This hadīth indicates that asking the Companions for *bay'ah* is a *Sunnah*, and likewise their giving *bay'ah* upon that is a *Sunnah*; and fulfilling it is obligatory, while breaking it is a serious matter."

That is, in a state of our activity and in the state in which we are incapable of doing what we are commanded. Al-Dāwūdī said: "What is meant are the things they dislike." Ibn al-Tīn said: "The apparent meaning is that he meant at the time of laziness and lethargy." Ibn al-Jazarī al-Mālikī said: "It is not permissible to rebel against the rulers even if they are oppressive, unless clear disbelief appears from them. They must be obeyed in what a person likes and dislikes, except if they command a sin; there is no obedience to a created being in disobedience to the Creator."

The sincere activist brother should draw from all these statements, and others besides them, what is obligatory upon him in dealing with his supervisors and the leaders of the call. He must be above devoting his concern to tracking down the slips and faults of his brothers, for *ḥusn al-ẓann* (having a good opinion of Muslims, حُسْنُ الظَّنِّ) is the original obligation to follow, not the partisan methods far from piety and religion. And every *bid'ah* (heresy/innovation, بِدْعَةُ) is misguidance.

## Obedience and Its Legal Ruling

Obedience is compliance with the command. If we do not obtain obedience, sin and *fitnah* (tribulation, فِتْنَةُ) take its place. The believer may not perceive the first steps of *fitnah*; he only senses it once it has actually occurred. Therefore, he must be vigilant in all his words, deeds, and conduct so that he does not become a *fitnah* for those who believe.

Obedience is obligatory unless it involves disobedience or leads to disobedience. Allah—exalted is He—says:

> "O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result." [Qur'an, Al-Nisā' 4:59]

And Abū Hurayrah—may Allah be pleased with him—reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah, and whoever disobeys me has disobeyed Allah; and whoever obeys my leader has obeyed me, and whoever disobeys my leader has disobeyed me." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Judgments, Hadith 2956]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Anas ibn Mālik—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Hear and obey, even if an Abyssinian slave is appointed over you." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Judgments, Hadith 7142] And he narrated from Ibn 'Abbās—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Whoever sees something from his leader that he dislikes, let him be patient. For no one separates from the community an arm's length and then dies except that he dies a death of *Jāhiliyyah*." In another narration: "He has removed the bond of Islam from around his neck." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 1849]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from 'Abdullāh ibn 'Umar—may Allah be pleased with them both—from the Prophet ﷺ: "Hearing and obeying are incumbent upon a Muslim in what he likes and dislikes, unless he is commanded with a sin. If he is commanded with a sin, then there is no hearing and no obeying." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Oppressions, Hadith 7144]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Abū Sa'īd al-Khudrī—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There were people in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ who used to delay [complying with] revelation. Revelation has ceased, and we will only take you according to what appears to us of your deeds. Whoever shows us good, we will trust him and draw him near; and we have nothing to do with what is hidden in his inner self. Even if he says his inner self is good." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 33]

## The Pillars of *Bay'ah*

For belonging to the Islamic movement is, in truth, actual belonging to Islam; the beginning of a new covenant with Allah; and a *bay'ah* upon working and striving (*jihād*, جِهَادُ) in the cause of Allah.

The martyr-Imam summarized the pillars of *bay'ah*, saying:

> The pillars of our *bay'ah* are ten; so preserve them:
> Understanding (*fahm*, فَهْمٌ) — sincerity (*ikhlāṣ*, إِخْلَاصٌ) — work (*'amal*, عَمَلٌ) — *jihād* — sacrifice (*taḍḥiyah*, تَضْحِيَةٌ) — obedience (*ṭā'ah*, طَاعَةٌ) — steadfastness (*thabāt*, ثَبَاتٌ) — selfless dedication (*tajarrud*, تَجَرُّدٌ) — brotherhood (*ukhuwwah*, أُخُوَّةٌ) — trust (*thiqah*, ثِقَةٌ).

The martyr-Imam explained each of these pillars, saying:

### 1. Understanding

That you be certain that our idea is an idea of pure Islam, and that you understand Islam as we understand it within the bounds of these concise fundamentals, which we summarize as follows:

- That Islam is a comprehensive system covering all aspects of life.
- That the Qur'an and *Sunnah* are the reference for every Muslim in learning the rulings of Islam.
- That *taqwā* (God-consciousness, تَقْوَى), sincere worship, and *jihād* are a light and sweetness that Allah casts into the heart of whichever of His servants He wills.
- That *kashf* (unveiling), visions, inspirations, and passing thoughts are not evidence in legal rulings and are not considered unless they do not conflict with the rulings and texts of the religion.
- That *tamā'im* (amulets), *tyarah* (evil omens), *wud'ah* (bird-omens), *raml* (geomancy), *ma'rifah* (clairvoyance), *kuhḥānah* (soothsaying), and everything of this kind are forbidden and must be combated—except what is from a Qur'anic verse or a transmitted *ruqyah* (incantation).
- That the opinion of the leader and his deputy in matters without a text, in matters admitting several possible aspects, and in unrestricted benefits is to be followed as long as it does not conflict with a legal principle. And everyone may be taken from or left aside in his speech except the infallible one. And it is obligatory upon every Muslim who has not reached the level of examining the evidences of legal rulings to follow an imām from the imāms of the religion.
- That juristic disagreement in branches is not a cause of division in religion.
- That every issue upon which action is not required is free of the burden that the Sharī'ah has absolved us from.
- That knowing Allah—blessed and exalted is He—declaring His oneness (*tawḥīd*, تَوْحِيدٌ), and declaring Him transcendent are the highest creeds of Islam; and that the verses of attributes and their authentic hadīths are to be believed as they came, without *ta'wīl* (figurative interpretation) or *ta'ṭīl* (negation of attributes).
- That every *bid'ah* in religion that has no basis is an innovation that must be combated with the best means.
- That the additional *bid'ah* and the omission or neglect of absolute acts of worship are matters of juristic disagreement in which every person has his view; there is no harm in scrutinizing the truth through evidence and proof.
- That visiting the righteous, respecting them, and asking Allah to have mercy upon them for what is known of their good deeds is a means of drawing near to Allah—blessed and exalted is He.
- That visiting graves, whatever they may be, is a legislated *Sunnah* according to the established manner.
- That supplication, when coupled with seeking a means to Allah through one of His creatures, is a subsidiary matter concerning the manner of supplication and not an issue of creed.
- That customary ignorance does not alter the realities of Sharī'ah terms.
- That creed is the foundation of action, and the action of the heart is more important than the action of the limbs; and perfection in both of them is required by the Sharī'ah, even if the levels of requirement differ.
- That Islam liberates the intellect, encourages reflection upon the universe, elevates the rank of knowledge and scholars, and welcomes every beneficial righteous thing.
- That *naẓar shar'ī* (legal investigation) and *naẓar 'aqlī* (rational investigation) may each address what does not fall within the sphere of the other; but they will not differ on decisive matters. A sound scientific reality will never conflict with a firmly established legal principle; the probabilistic aspect of either is interpreted so as to agree with the decisive. If both are probabilistic, then legal investigation is more worthy of following until the rational is established or refuted.
- Do not declare a Muslim an unbeliever who affirms the two testimonies, acts according to their requirements, and performs the obligations—whether with an [erroneous] opinion or a sin—unless he utters a word of disbelief or commits an act that cannot bear any interpretation other than disbelief.

### 2. Sincerity (*Ikhlāṣ*)

That the Muslim brother intend, in his words, his deeds, and all his *jihād*, the Face of Allah alone, without regard to wealth, appearance, fame, or title. Thereby he becomes a soldier of the idea and creed, not a soldier of interest and benefit.

### 3. Work

By it is meant: reforming the self, building the Muslim household, guiding society, liberating the homeland so that it is truly Islamic, restoring the international entity of the Muslim Ummah, and leading the world through spreading the call of Islam in its regions until there is no *fitnah* and the religion is all for Allah.

### 4. *Jihād*

By it is meant the enduring obligation until the Day of Resurrection, as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Whoever dies without having fought or considered fighting [in Allah's cause] has died upon a branch of hypocrisy." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 81]

### 5. Sacrifice (*Taḍḥiyah*)

By it is meant giving one's self, wealth, time, life, and everything in the path of the goal.

### 6. Obedience (*Ṭā'ah*)

By it is meant compliance with the command and carrying it out in hardship and ease, whether willingly or unwillingly.

### 7. Steadfastness (*Thabāt*)

By it is meant that the brother remain active and *mujāhid* (one who strives, مُجَاهِدٌ) in the path of his goal, however long the duration and however many the years, until he meets Allah upon that, having attained one of the two good things: the goal or martyrdom.

### 8. Selfless Dedication (*Tajarrud*)

By it is meant that you be devoted to your idea alone, nothing else, and that it be your beginning and end, your lowest and highest.

### 9. Brotherhood (*Ukhuwwah*)

By it is meant that hearts and souls be bound by the bond of creed; for creed is the firmest bond and the most precious. (See: The Duties of Brotherhood and Its Obligations.)

Brotherhood in Islam is the creedal bond that binds the Muslims to one another.

### 10. Trust (*Thiqah*)

By it is meant the soldier's assurance in his leader.
