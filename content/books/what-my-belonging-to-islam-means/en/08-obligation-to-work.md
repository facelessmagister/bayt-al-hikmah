# Chapter 8: Believing in the Obligation to Work for Islam

Working for Islam — in order to create the personality that embodies it as creed and as moral character, to create the society that adheres to it in thought and behavior, to create the state that applies it as *shari'ah* (الشريعة), methodology, and constitution, and carries it as a guiding call to establish truth and justice in all worlds — this work, and all that it needs, is connected to it, branches from it, and requires it, is an Islamic *shar'i* (شرعي) obligation. It does not fall until there arises the authority that undertakes this responsibility and oversees the affairs of Muslims. And as long as this authority is non-existent, every shortcoming in this regard is nothing but the failure to promptly rise to the burdens of working for Islam.

And among what confirms the obligation of working for Islam is that it is a charged duty and not voluntary: the certainty of its obligation stems from several aspects.

## First: Its Obligation as a Principle

Working for Islam is an obligation of principle, because it is the basis of Allah's charge to all human beings — to the prophets and messengers first, then to all people until Allah inherits the earth and all that is upon it. By the evidence of His saying:

> "By time, indeed mankind is in loss, except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience." [Qur'an, Al-'Asr 103:1-3]

And His saying:

> "O Messenger, announce that which has been revealed to you from your Lord, and if you do not, then you have not conveyed His message." [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:67]

And His saying:

> "Indeed, those who conceal what We sent down of clear evidences and guidance after We made it clear for the people in the Scripture — those are cursed by Allah and cursed by those who curse." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:159]

And the pure Sunnah is replete with what was narrated from the Messenger ﷺ of hadiths that encourage calling to the truth and combating falsehood. Among them is his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he is unable, then with his tongue; if he is unable, then with his heart — and that is the weakest of faith. And there is not beyond that [even] a mustard seed of faith." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 49]

And his saying:

> "O people, indeed Allah says to you: Command the good and forbid the evil before you ask Me for victory, and I will not help you." [Sunan at-Tirmidhi]

And his saying:

> "If you see my ummah fearing to say to the wrongdoer: 'O wrongdoer,' then it is as if it has departed from them." [Sunan at-Tirmidhi]

## Second: Its Obligation as a Legal Ruling

Working for Islam is an obligation as a legal ruling, because the suspension of Allah's rule on earth, and the dominance of man-made systems and legislations over human societies, imposes upon Muslims the work of establishing the Islamic society, resuming Islamic life, and making people's servitude to Allah pervade their beliefs, morals, and systems. By the evidence of His saying:

> "But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muhammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission." [Qur'an, An-Nisa 4:65]

And His saying:

> "And in anything over which you disagree — its ruling is [to be referred] to Allah. [Say], 'That is Allah, my Lord; upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn back.'" [Qur'an, Ash-Shuraa 42:10]

And His saying:

> "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed — those are the disbelievers," and the verse about the wrongdoers, and the verse about the defiantly disobedient. [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:44-48]

So if realizing the Islamic society and ruling by what Allah has revealed is obligatory in itself, then the work to establish it and unify it becomes obligatory as a legal ruling, by the evidence of the legal maxim: "Whatever is indispensable for the discharge of an obligatory duty is itself obligatory."

Indeed, most — if not all — of the Islamic lands are governed by man-made systems that are a mixture of Roman, Greek, French, and other legislations. And the prevailing economic systems in these lands are capitalism and socialism, which makes working to demolish these *jahiliyyah* (الجاهلية) entities and resume Islamic life a *fard 'ayn* (فرض العين) — an individual obligation — upon every Muslim, until leadership and guardianship return to Islam.

Furthermore, many of the legal obligations depend for their execution and practice upon the establishment of a *khilāfah* (الخلافة) or an *imām* (الإمام), and this in turn is tied to the existence of an Islamic authority. Thus all the legislations related to the Islamic social, economic, and political systems, all the legislations related to governance, punishments, peace, war, *jihad* (الجهاد), reconciliation, treaties, and social and economic transactions — these and other aspects of Islamic legislation cannot be implemented except through a state based on the foundation of Islam.

## Third: Its Obligation by Necessity

Working for Islam is, likewise, an obligation by necessity in order to confront the challenges of the age and the conspiracies of the enemies of Islam, and to stop the materialistic waves and atheistic advances that threaten the Islamic existence with eradication and disappearance.

Indeed, a penetrating look at the conditions of the lands of the Islamic world confirms the necessity of an Islamic struggle — indeed, it makes undertaking it imperative. For there are lands that complain of the control of non-Muslims over them and their rule therein, such as Palestine, Kashmir, Eritrea, Cyprus, Bukhara, Samarkand, and others. And there are lands that complain of the domination of sectarian minorities filled with hatred, ruling over the necks of Muslims by force of iron and fire. And there are other parts of the Islamic world that complain of the domination of leftist or rightist parties over them, just as other parts experience bloody and terrible conflicts due to the clash between the forces of right and left over power.

In addition to all of this and that, the lands of the Islamic world all experience a state of loss… a state of chaos… political chaos, social chaos, economic chaos. They experience a terrifying deterioration in morals and values as well as in ideas and beliefs. All of that, and more, confirms the obligation of work, if only by necessity, to confront these challenges that Islam faces — whether from colonialism and international forces from without, or from its agents, hirelings, and collaborators with it from within:

> "And fight them until there is no *fitnah* (الفتنة) and [until] the religion, all of it, is for Allah." [Qur'an, Al-Anfal 8:39]

## Fourth: Individually and Collectively

Indeed, the responsibility of working for Islam — insofar as it is a charged *shar'i* obligation — is considered an individual responsibility, like all the other obligations and *shar'i* responsibilities upon which performing them entails reward and leaving them entails punishment:

> "Every soul, for what it has earned, will be retained." [Qur'an, Al-Muddaththir 74:38]

> "And all of them are coming to Him on the Day of Resurrection alone." [Qur'an, Maryam 19:95]

> "If you do good, you do good for yourselves; and if you do evil, [you do it] to yourselves." [Qur'an, Al-Isra 17:7]

> "And no bearer of burdens will bear the burden of another." [Qur'an, An-Najm 53:38]

> "And whoever strives, indeed he strives for [the benefit of] himself." [Qur'an, Al-Ankabut 29:6]

So Islam, in order to involve all people in the process of building and developing — building life upon truth and populating it with good — made every person responsible for a share of giving and contribution within the limits of his capabilities and energies, as long as this person is mature, rational, and capable. This makes society a living cell in which every individual builds and is keen on building, and every person in it gives and is motivated in [the path of Allah]. From here, the obligation of working for Islam is individual, as a *shar'i* charge and an Islamic obligation.

And if working for Islam is an individual obligation from this angle, it is a collective obligation from the perspective of its executive and movement responsibility. And this is confirmed by facts and considerations that are basically indisputable. Among them:

**First:** The burdens of working for Islam are too great for one person to undertake alone. For working for Islam aims to demolish *jahiliyyah* in its entirety and establish Islam in its place. This requires burdens, capabilities, and efforts that an individual is incapable of bearing, and he cannot rise to them with effort, struggle, and suffering except through a movement organization that is at the level of confrontation in awareness, organization, and capability.

**Second:** The work of the Messenger ﷺ in confronting *jahiliyyah* and establishing the Islamic society and resuming Islamic life is a legal proof of the obligation of *jama'ah* (الجماعة) — collectivity — in working for Islam. For the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, never relied on the method of individual work; rather, from the first day he was keen to establish a *jama'ah* (الجماعة), choosing its elements selectively and its individuals on the basis of loyalty, so that it would be a tool of Islam in the process of change. And this is what the events of the Prophetic *sīrah* (السيرة) state at every turn and on every level.

**Third:** Then indeed the path of working for Islam is strewn with thorns and surrounded by ordeals. The challenges that obstruct the path are great, and the forces that lie in wait for Islam and its people are many. This imposes the existence of a broad movement organization capable of confronting every stage of the work, its conditions, and its possibilities. And within the Quranic verses there are clear indications and evidences, just as in the Prophetic hadiths, confirming the necessity that the work for Islam be collective, movement-based, and organized:

> "And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression." [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:2]

## Fifth: Whoever Strives, Indeed He Strives for Himself

Then indeed the first thing incumbent upon those working for Islam to realize deeply is that they are the ones in need of Islam. And that when they work, strive, and struggle, it is for the purification of themselves, the cleansing of their souls, the fulfillment of some of the rights of Allah upon them, and so that they may reckon it with Allah on the day when sights will dart and hearts will reach the throats:

> "The day when their plan will not avail them at all, nor will they be helped." [Qur'an, Al-Ahzab 33:10]

So they are the winners if they come forward, and they alone are the losers if they turn away:

> "And indeed Allah is free from need of the worlds." [Qur'an, Ali 'Imran 3:97]

> "And if you turn away, He will replace you with a people other than you; then they will not be the likes of you." [Qur'an, Muhammad 47:38]

Likewise, it is incumbent upon them to realize that their true worth is through Islam, and they have no worth without it; then they are:

> "like livestock, but even more astray." [Qur'an, Al-A'raf 7:179]

And that true honor and true might cannot be realized except through participation in the caravan of Islam and persistence in this participation, and that:

> "Whoever separates himself, he will fall into the Fire." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Prayers, Hadith 477]

And whoever is encompassed by the caravan has indeed connected to the noblest lineage and tied himself to a caravan of guided ones among those whom Allah has blessed from among the prophets, the steadfast affirmers of truth, the martyrs, and the righteous. And excellent are those as companions. [Qur'an, An-Nisa 4:69]

Likewise, it is incumbent upon those working for Islam to realize that remaining in the caravan is a condition for steadfastness:

> "Indeed, the wolf eats only from the sheep that strays away." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Prayers, Hadith 477]

For a society in which trials surge like the dark portions of the night — it is not possible for one to live in it without being affected by its filth, whoever is far from *dhikr* (الذكر), remembrance:

> "And remind, for indeed, the reminder benefits the believers." [Qur'an, Adh-Dhariyat 51:55]

Or far from mutual exhortation and enjoining truth and patience:

> "By time, indeed mankind is in loss, except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience." [Qur'an, Al-'Asr 103:1-3]

So there must be living in the expanse of the family of faith, and joining the caravan of the Most Merciful and living in its shelter always and forever:

> "And keep yourself patient [by being] with those who call upon their Lord in the morning and the evening, seeking His countenance. And let not your eyes pass beyond them, desiring the adornments of the worldly life, and do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his desire and whose affair is ever [in] neglect." [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:28]

Likewise, it is incumbent upon those working for Islam to realize that they only work for Allah and strive in His path, and that this path is long and arduous, and that Paradise is surrounded by what is disagreeable and Hell is surrounded by desires. [Sahih Muslim, Book of Heart-Melting Traditions, Hadith 2822]

It is a path that cannot be traveled by one for whom the footsteps of the breeze wound his cheeks and the touch of silk makes his fingers bleed. And it is a path that cannot be traveled by one who worries about tomorrow for his provision and his life. And it is a path that cannot be traveled by one of mood and whim, narrow chest and feeble strength, one who cannot bear a word let alone a calamity, one who relies on his own opinion while not knowing and not knowing that he does not know, and one who finds it difficult to step aside at the group's decision and adhere to the opinion of the *jama'ah* (الجماعة).

It is the path of purification, chastity, and cleanliness; the path of mercy and nobility; the path of perseverance and steadfastness; the path of detachment and elevation; the path of truthfulness and sincerity. And a path whose characteristics are such that none can remain steadfast upon it except believers whose hearts are attached to the One, the Refuge, looking with their souls to the Eternal Refuge:

> "And whoever fears Allah — He will make for him a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect. And whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him. Indeed, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has already appointed for everything a [decreed] extent." [Qur'an, At-Talaq 65:2-3]

> "And whoever breaks [his oath] — indeed, he breaks it only to the detriment of himself. And whoever fulfills that which he has promised Allah — He will grant him a great reward." [Qur'an, Al-Fath 48:10]
