# Chapter 4: Being a Muslim in My Family and Home

My belonging to Islam must make me a bearer of a message in life—nay, it must make my life, all of my life, directed in accordance with this message. If my belonging to Islam obliges me to be a Muslim in myself—in *‘aqeedah* (عَقِيدَة), worship, and morals—then it also obliges me to work to make the society in which I live Muslim around me. That is because among the effects that Islam instills and pours into the human soul, if it has *iman* (إيمان) and does well, are concern for others, calling them, advising them, and protective jealousy (*ghayrah*, غَيْرَة) over them, in fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever spends the night while not concerned with the affairs of the Muslims is not one of them.” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Faith] From here is laid upon me a new responsibility: the responsibility of establishing the Muslim society and the responsibility of carrying Islam to society.

And the first step in this field—the first, nearest step—is that my home be Muslim: that I carry the message of Islam to my small society, to my family, to my wife, and to my children; then to the nearest and nearest of kin. This is what the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, adhered to at the beginning of his call: “So do not invoke with Allah another deity, lest you be among the punished. And warn, [O Muhammad], your closest kindred, and lower your wing to those who follow you of the believers.” [Qur’an, Ash-Shu‘ara 26:213-215]

And from here, the first duty entrusted to the Muslim immediately after *iman* (faith) is his responsibility toward his family, his home, and his children—by the word of Allah, the Exalted: “O you who have believed, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones, over which are [appointed] angels, harsh and severe; they do not disobey Allah in what He commands them but do what they are commanded.” [Qur’an, At-Tahrim 66:6]

## The Responsibility of Marriage

Indeed, in order for Islam to help me succeed in establishing the Muslim home, it guided me to the way and pointed to a set of factors and causes that facilitate my task and achieve my goal, among them:

- That my marriage be for Allah—that is, for establishing the Muslim home, for producing righteous offspring (*dhurriyyah*, ذُرِّيَّة) who undertake the bearing of the trust (*amanah*, أَمَانَة) and through whom guidance continues to be handed down, “offspring, some of them from others.” [Qur’an, Aal Imran 3:34]

- That among the objectives of my marriage be that I lower my gaze, guard my chastity, and fear Allah, my Lord. In this regard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Three for whom Allah is bound to help: the *mujahid* (مُجَاهِد) in the path of Allah, the *mukatab* (مُكَاتَب) who desires to fulfill his contract, and the one who marries seeking chastity (*‘iffah*, عِفَّة).” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Marriage] And he said: “Whoever marries has completed half of his religion, so let him fear Allah regarding the remaining half.” [Al-Tabarani, Al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat]

- That I make a good choice in my wife, the partner of my life and companion of my path, because the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Choose carefully for your sperm (*nutfah*, نُطْفَة), for indeed offspring is inherited.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage] And in another narration: “Marry the compatible ones (*al-akfa’*, الأَكْفَاء) and give your daughters in marriage to the compatible ones.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Marriage]

- That I choose a woman of *khuluq* (character, خُلُق) and *din* (religion, دِين), even if she be below others in wealth and beauty. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Do not marry women for their beauty, for their beauty may fade; and do not marry them for their wealth, for their wealth may make them rebellious; but marry them for their religion. And a black slave woman (*amah*, أَمَة) with religion is better.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage]

- That I beware of opposing Allah’s command in this and guard against His anger and vengeance. For the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever marries a woman for her wealth, Allah will increase him only in humiliation; and whoever marries her for her beauty, Allah will increase him only in poverty; and whoever marries her for her lineage, Allah will increase him only in baseness. And whoever marries a woman intending only to lower his gaze, guard his chastity, or maintain ties of kinship (*rahim*, رَحِم), Allah will bless him through her and bless her through him.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage]

## Responsibility after Marriage

Indeed, my good choice of a wife does not exempt me from continuing my responsibility toward her after marriage; rather, the greater responsibility begins from the first moment of my marriage. From here are laid upon me a number of consequences, among them:

- That I treat her well and honor my conduct toward her, in order to realize trust between her and me, in fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “The best of you is the one who is best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family.” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1162] And his statement: “Indeed the best of you in character are the kindest of you to their families.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1978]

- That my relationship with her not be limited to the relationship of bed and desire; rather, before this and above it there must be realized between us intellectual, spiritual, and emotional harmony. We read together, perform some acts of worship together, manage the affairs of the home together, and then we have some opportunities for play and amusement. In the sphere of worship, Allah the Exalted says: “And enjoin prayer upon your family [and people] and be steadfast therein.” [Qur’an, Taha 20:132] And [He says about Ismail]: “And he used to enjoin on his people prayer and zakah and was to his Lord pleasing.” [Qur’an, Maryam 19:55] In the sphere of play and recreation, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to race with Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, by running. [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage] And in the sphere of domestic cooperation, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to take up many burdens, among them mending the sandal. [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Good Manners]

- That my relationship with my wife—what has been mentioned and what has not—acquire the character of legality, so that it is not at the expense of Islam or in what Allah has forbidden. For it is reported from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: “Indeed, there is no one who obeys a woman in disobedience to Allah except that Allah casts him into the Fire.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage] And his statement: “Allah will not confront a servant with a sin greater than the ignorance of his family.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Wills] And he said: “Wretched be the slave of luxury.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Riqaq]

## My Responsibility with Her in Raising the Children

In reality, success in marriage—in choosing a righteous woman and in the fusion of the two spouses in the crucible of Islam—helps to a great extent in achieving the desired Islamic upbringing (*tarbiyyah*, تَرْبِيَّة) of the children. As for failure in achieving the Islamic marriage and a poor choice of the woman, that leads to ruinous consequences and heralds widespread evil for the whole home. Any contradiction that occurs in the life of the spouses will inevitably, directly, and quickly reflect upon the upbringing of the children and upon their souls; and consequently they will bear many complexes and deviations. Therefore, the first factor in achieving the Islamic upbringing of the children is achieving the Islam-ness of marriage, as we mentioned earlier.

The truth is that the hoped-for fruit of establishing the Muslim home is producing righteous offspring. [As the believers pray:] “Our Lord, grant us from among our wives and offspring comfort to our eyes and make us an example for the righteous.” [Qur’an, Al-Furqan 25:74] The child is born on the *fitrah* (الفِطْرَة); if sound upbringing is provided for him, he becomes righteous, and if he grows up between contradictory or deviant parents, he becomes accordingly a fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Every child is born on the *fitrah*; then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Beginning of Creation, Hadith 1292]

For this reason Islam emphasized and insisted on the good upbringing of children, and on providing all the causes, supports, atmospheres, and climates that realize good upbringing. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “For a man to discipline his child is better for him than giving a *sā‘* (صاع) in charity,” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Adab, Hadith 3668] and he said: “No father has given his child a gift better than good character (*adab*, أَدَب),” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Knowledge] and he said: “Honor your children and refine their manners,” [Al-Tabarani, Al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat] and he said: “When the son of Adam dies his deeds are cut off except for three: an ongoing charity (*sadaqah jariyah*, صَدَقَةٌ جَارِيَةٌ), knowledge benefited from, or a righteous child who supplicates for him.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Wills, Hadith 1631]
