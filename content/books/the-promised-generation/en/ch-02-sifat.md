## Page 24

Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — informed us in His Book that there are attributes and conditions that must be present in the group or generation that He — Glorified is He — will empower, support with His aid and strength, and grant victory over its enemies. And this is not conditional on a specific place or time; rather, the matter is open to everyone across time and place. What is required is simply fulfilling the qualifying conditions for entering into Allah’s companionship (al-ma’iyyah), protection (al-kifayah), and victory (al-nasr).

### It Is Not Mere Theoretical Talk

And before the discussion moves on to the attributes of the promised generation, there is a matter worthy of attention: that the talk about the promised generation and what Allah can realize for it is not theoretical, hypothetical talk used to blacken pages, nor is it wishes or dreams that we dream and flee through from our bitter reality. No, by Allah; rather, they are realities that can occur, and faster than some might imagine, if we all strive to complete the required conditions and rise up to realize the attributes of the promised generation in ourselves and in those around us:

> “Whoever thinks that Allah will not help him in this world and the Hereafter, let him extend a rope to the sky, then cut it, and let him see whether his plan will remove what enrages him.” (Quran, Al-Hajj 22:15)

## Page 25

The history of our ummah tells us that there was a generation among the generations that actualized those conditions within itself, so Allah fulfilled His promise with it. That is the generation of the Companions — may Allah be pleased with them — who, before their Islam, were at the height of ignorance (jahiliyyah), division, and fragmentation. Then Islam came, and its light entered their hearts, and inner change took place, so their personalities were reformed anew, and they fulfilled what Allah required of them. Swift and noble fulfillment came from Him — Glorified is He — upon the earth for them, so they were able to destroy oppressive empires and within a few years become the foremost power in the world, and to carry humanity into a new, bright era.

This generation, before its Islam, was far farther from Allah than we are now; yet it changed what was within itself, fulfilled the conditions qualifying for victory (al-nasr) and empowerment (al-tamkin), dominated the earth, and produced great glory for Islam.

So the matter now is in our hands. We do not need huge preparations, or external assistance, or…; rather, we need to change what is within ourselves so that the promised generation may appear among us, leading the ummah to honour and sovereignty, just as happened with the first generation.

### The Loss of Glory

## Page 26

Because the promise of Allah is never broken: when a group of people actualized the attributes and conditions qualifying for victory — despite their lack of material means — Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — granted them victory and empowered them on earth; and when the later generations began gradually abandoning those attributes, there came abandonment (al-khizlan), loss, and a return once more to backwardness, so the status of the disbelievers rose again and the situation reached the misery and loss in which we now find ourselves.

### Allah’s Way with the Children of Israel

And indeed the promise of Allah and His way (sunnah) were fulfilled in both aspects upon the Children of Israel: when a generation among them fulfilled the conditions qualifying for entering into divine companionship (al-ma’iyyah), there came from Him — Glorified is He — aid, support, and victory:

> “And We caused the people who had been oppressed to inherit the eastern regions of the earth and the western ones, which We had blessed. And the good word of your Lord was fulfilled upon the Children of Israel because of what they had patiently endured. And We destroyed [all] that Pharaoh and his people were producing and what they used to erect.” (Quran, Al-A’raf 7:137)

And time never ceased revolving until
other people took the glory from us,
and my people are no longer seen among the caravans,
though their leaders lived for many years.

## Page 27

This divine empowerment (al-tamkin) only occurred for the Children of Israel when they fulfilled what Allah required of them. So did they preserve it? The Quran tells us that after that they began deviation and gradual distancing from the state that had qualified them to enter the circle of companionship (al-ma’iyyah) and divine favour. Arrogance (al-kibr), oppression (al-zulm), and even association (shirk) began to appear among them. So Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — granted them respite and pardoned them time after time, hoping they would return to their former covenant. But they persisted in their misguidance and insisted on their oppression and arrogance, so their painful punishment from Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — came:

> “They have been put under humiliation wherever they are found, except by a rope [of protection] from Allah and a rope [of protection] from the people. And they have drawn upon themselves wrath from Allah, and poverty was struck upon them. That is because they disbelieved in the signs of Allah and killed the prophets without right. That is because they disobeyed and were transgressing.” (Quran, Aal Imran 3:112)

### Honour Is in Proportion to Uprightness

Thus, entering the circle of divine support (al-ta’yid) requires conditions, and remaining within it requires steadfastness (al-thabat) upon these conditions. The honour of servants with their Lord is according to their uprightness (al-istiqamah) and piety (al-taqwa), and the continuation of honour is through the continuation of uprightness.

Did He not say — exalted is He — to His beloved Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him:

> “And if you follow their desires after what has come to you of knowledge, you will not have from Allah any protector and any intercessor.” (Quran, Ar-Ra’d 13:37)

It is a heavenly law:

> “And your Lord does wrong no one.” (Quran, Al-Kahf 18:49)

### With the Attributes of the Generation of Change

From what has preceded it becomes clear to us that there must exist a generation in whom the attributes that qualify it to enter the circle of companionship (al-ma’iyyah), victory (al-nasr), and divine support (al-ta’yid) are realized. Yes, these attributes may be found in individuals here and there, but that alone is not enough; rather, there must exist a bonded generation (jeel mutarabit) upon which victory and empowerment descend.

As for the attributes of the generation of empowerment (al-tamkin), the Quran has spoken of them in many of its verses, so we must seek them out, reflect upon them, and extract from it what they indicate of attributes.

Here are some of these verses for you, dear reader:

1. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [in authority] upon the earth just as He granted it to those before them, and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has preferred for them, and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security, [for] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that — then those are the defiantly disobedient.” (Quran, An-Nur 24:55)

In this verse we find that Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — has promised a group of His believing servants succession (istikhlaf) and empowerment (al-tamkin), and linked this promise to worshipping Him and not associating partners with Him. Thus among the attributes of the promised generation are worship (al-‘ibadah) and complete sincerity (al-ikhlas) to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He.

## Page 29

2. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “O you who have believed, whoever of you should revert from his religion — Allah will bring forth [in place of them] a people He will love and who will love Him, [who will be] humble toward the believers, mighty against the disbelievers; they will strive in the path of Allah and will not fear the blame of a critic. That is the favor of Allah; He bestows it upon whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing. Your ally is none but Allah and [therefore] His Messenger and those who have believed — those who establish prayer and give zakah, and they bow [in worship]. And whoever is an ally of Allah and His Messenger and those who have believed — indeed, the party of Allah will be the predominant.” (Quran, Al-Ma’idah 5:54–56)

These verses speak of some attributes of the party of Allah (hizb Allah) whom He — exalted is He — honours with predominance and victory. These attributes are:

First: love of Allah (mahabbah).

Second: humility (tawadu’).

Third: continuous jihad (jihad).

Fourth: worship (al-‘ibadah).

Fifth: loyalty and brotherhood (al-muwalah wa al-ta’akhi).

3. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy…” (Quran, Al-Anfal 8:60)

## Page 30

This verse addresses the attribute of jihad in its broad sense, comprising exertion in material preparation in all its available forms and within the limits of ability.

4. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “And those who disbelieved said to their messengers, ‘We will surely drive you out of our land, or you must return to our religion.’ So their Lord inspired to them [the truth]: ‘We will surely destroy the wrongdoers. And We will surely cause you to dwell in the land after them. That is for whoever fears My station and fears My threat.’” (Quran, Ibrahim 14:13–14)

Here the verses focus on fear of Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — as a fundamental attribute of empowerment.

5. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “And We have already written in the book [of Psalms] after the [previous] mention that the land is inherited by My righteous servants. Indeed, in this is a message for a people devoted [to Allah].” (Quran, Al-Anbiya 21:105–106)

Through these verses we find that worship (al-‘ibadah) and righteousness (al-salah) must be among the attributes of the generation of empowerment.

6. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “And the good word of your Lord was fulfilled upon the Children of Israel because of what they had patiently endured.” (Quran, Al-A’raf 7:137)

And He — exalted is He — said:

> “And We made from among them leaders, guiding by Our command, when they were patient and [when] they were certain of Our signs.” (Quran, As-Sajdah 32:24)

In these verses we find patience (sabr) and steadfastness (thabat) on the truth among the most important attributes of the victorious group:

> “That is from the news of the unseen which We reveal to you. You knew it not, neither you nor your people, before this. So be patient; indeed, the [best] outcome is for the righteous.” (Quran, Hud 11:49)

## Page 31

7. Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “So be steadfast as you are commanded, and those who turn in repentance with you, and do not transgress. Indeed, He is All-Seeing of what you do. And do not incline toward the wrongdoers, lest the Fire touch you, and you would not have any protectors besides Allah; then you would not be helped.” (Quran, Hud 11:112–113)

Here we find the divine face of the believing group in the necessity of being characterized by the attribute of moderation (al-wasatiyyah) and balance (al-i’tidal); for transgression (al-tughyan) represents extremism (al-tashaddud), and inclination (al-rukun) represents negligence (al-tafrit) and laxity (al-tarkhis), while uprightness (al-istiqamah) lies between them; without it the believers depart from the circle of divine guardianship (al-wilayah) and support (al-nasr), as the verses have clarified.

As confirming this meaning, He — exalted is He — said:

> “And thus We have made you a middle nation, that you may be witnesses over mankind and the Messenger be a witness over you.” (Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:143)

8. And in contrast to the verses that speak of the attributes of the promised generation of victory and empowerment, we find verses that speak of the causes of defeat and departure from the circle of companionship (al-ma’iyyah) and divine support (al-ta’yid). From these verses one understands the necessity of abandoning these attributes for whoever desires victory and empowerment. Among them is His — exalted is He — saying:

> “O you who have believed, what is [the matter] with you that, when you are told to go forth in the cause of Allah, you adhere heavily to the earth? Are you satisfied with the life of this world rather than the Hereafter? But what is the enjoyment of the life of this world compared to the Hereafter except a [very] little. If you do not go forth, He will punish you with a painful punishment and will replace you with another people, and you will not harm Him at all. And Allah is over all things competent.” (Quran, At-Tawbah 9:38–39)

## Page 32

Thus attachment to this world is among the most dangerous causes leading to departure from the circle of companionship (al-ma’iyyah) and divine victory (al-nasr). Hence it is necessary that asceticism (zuhd) in this world and non-attachment to it be among the attributes of the promised generation.

### The Ten Attributes

So if we wanted to gather the attributes indicated by the preceding verses, we would find them to be:

1. Sincerity to Allah (al-ikhlas lillah).
2. Love of Allah (mahabbah Allah).
3. Fear of Allah (khawf Allah).
4. Worship (al-‘ibadah).
5. Humility (tawadu’).
6. Asceticism toward the world (al-zuhd fi al-dunya).
7. Jihad in the path of Allah (al-jihad fi sabil Allah).
8. Patience and steadfastness (al-sabr wa al-thabat).
9. Loyalty and brotherhood (al-muwalah wa al-ta’akhi).

## Page 33

The tenth attribute remains, one of great importance and that should be present in the generation of change — namely, the attribute of moderation (al-wasatiyyah) and balance (al-i’tidal). Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — demanded of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, and those with him that they realize it while they were still in Mecca, being pursued and persecuted. He — exalted is He — said:

> “So be steadfast as you are commanded, and those who turn in repentance with you, and do not transgress. Indeed, He is All-Seeing of what you do. And do not incline toward the wrongdoers, lest the Fire touch you, and you would not have any protectors besides Allah; then you would not be helped.” (Quran, Hud 11:112–113)

For transgression represents extremism, inclination represents negligence and laxity, while uprightness lies between them; without it the believers depart from the circle of divine guardianship and support, as the verses have clarified.

### Between Individualism and Collectivism

What is observed in the preceding attributes is that some of them have an individual character, meaning that they concern each individual separately and it is upon him to realize them in himself, such as sincerity to Allah, love of Him, and fear of Him. Some other attributes have a collective character, meaning that they cannot be realized except through the group of individuals, some with others, such as preparation and brotherhood. This is what the Quran emphasizes in its address to the believing group, and what we observe is that it addresses them in the plural form, considering that many of the principal attributes of the generation of empowerment cannot be realized except through the cooperation of individuals

## Page 34

with one another, and their melting in a single crucible. Reflect with me on His — exalted is He — saying:

> “Those who, if We give them authority in the land, establish prayer and give zakah and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. And to Allah belongs the outcome of [all] matters.” (Quran, Al-Hajj 22:41)

And His saying — exalted is He —:

> “The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and establish prayer and give zakah and obey Allah and His Messenger. Those — Allah will have mercy upon them. Indeed, Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise.” (Quran, At-Tawbah 9:71)

### Can Other Attributes Be Added?

Before moving on to speak about these ten attributes in some detail, there is an observation worthy of drawing attention to: some of us may see that there are other attributes that can be added to these, whether individual such as knowledge (al-‘ilm) and reliance on Allah (al-tawakkul ‘ala Allah), or collective such as discipline (al-inzibat), obedience (al-ta’ah), enjoining good (al-amr bil-ma’ruf), and forbidding evil (al-nahy ‘an al-munkar).

Yes, there is no objection to that, especially since these attributes — as will be explained — are but fruits that issue from several axes. When these axes are realized in the promised generation, they will — by Allah’s will — bear these ten attributes and others that may be added, in a natural and smooth manner.

This means that these attributes are not exhaustive; although they form the most important distinguishing traits of the promised generation.

### First: Sincerity to Allah, Mighty and Exalted

(A Generation of Sincere Devotion)

Allah — exalted is He — said:

> “Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [in authority] upon the earth just as He granted it to those before them, and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has preferred for them, and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security, [for] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that — then those are the defiantly disobedient.” (Quran, An-Nur 24:55)

The verse addresses the believers and gives them the glad tidings of the divine promise of succession (istikhlaf) and empowerment (al-tamkin) on earth, on the condition that they worship Him and do not associate anything with Him.

It is noteworthy that the verse mentions the word “anything” (shay’an), to exclude shirk from the narrow circle — the circle of manifest shirk — to the wide circle that includes all forms of shirk, whether in direction and intention or in seeking help.

### Shirk of Intention and Purpose

Shirk of intention and purpose is that a person intend from his actions Allah’s pleasure from one side, and from another side seek by those actions people’s pleasure and love for him, and the raising of his status among them; or he intend by his actions Allah’s pleasure and also the attainment of booty, prestige, or any worldly benefit.

## Page 37

Thus everything that contradicts complete and absolute orientation to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — is shirk.

On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, say:

> “Allah — exalted is He — said: I am the One Who has no need of any partner. Whoever does a deed in which he associates with Me someone other than Me, I abandon him and his association.”(1)

And on the authority of Shaddad ibn Aws, who said: I saw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, weeping, so I said: What makes you weep, O Messenger of Allah? He said:

> “I fear for my ummah the hidden shirk. They do not worship an idol, nor the sun, nor the moon, nor a stone, but they show off with their deeds.”(2)

### Shirk of Seeking Help

And just as we are required to devote our intention to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — and that His pleasure alone be the purpose in all our deeds, we are also required to seek His help alone in performing any deed, for there is no might nor power except with Allah. When a person seeks help from other than his Lord and thinks he will reach his goal without Him — Glorified is He — then he has associated partners with Him.

Among the hidden forms of seeking help from other than Allah:

(1) Seeking help through the self and believing that what Allah has endowed it with of abilities is the servant’s own property, which he may favour others with, or that he can use and rely on it whenever he wishes. So when he reaches his goal he rejoices in himself and looks at it with approval and admiration. This is self-admiration (‘ujb), which is counted among the most dangerous types of shirk with Allah.

## Page 38

Thus shirk with Allah includes everything that shares in directing the servant’s deeds to Allah, and also includes everything by which the servant seeks help to perform those deeds.

Ibn Taymiyyah said: “Riyā’ is from the gate of associating partners with the creation, and ‘ujb is from the gate of associating partners with the self.”

And he said: “The self-admiring person cannot realize ‘You alone we ask for help,’ just as the ostentatious person cannot realize ‘You alone we worship.’”(1)

## Page 39

### The Danger of Hidden Shirk

As for the danger of hidden shirk, whether riyā’ or ‘ujb and what they lead to of delusion, arrogance (al-kibr), and hypocrisy (nifaq), it is great. The least of it is the nullification of deeds, for every deed mixed with riyā’ or ‘ujb is nullified and becomes scattered dust. He — exalted is He — said:

> “O you who have believed, do not invalidate your charities with reminders [of it] or injury, like the one who spends his wealth to be seen by the people…” (Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:264)

And alongside the nullification of deeds, ‘ujb leads to Allah’s anger and contempt. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> “Whoever magnifies himself in his own soul and struts in his walk will meet Allah while He is angry with him.”(2)

And he said:

> “The one who repents awaits mercy, while the self-admiring person awaits wrath.”(3)

And ‘ujb also leads to abandonment (al-khizlan), deprivation of success (al-tawfiq), and exposure to trials. We have in the story of the Battle of Hunayn the clearest example:

> “Already Allah has given you victory in many regions and [even] on the day of Hunayn, when your great number pleased you, but it did not avail you in any way; and the earth was confining for you with what broadened it; then you turned away, fleeing.” (Quran, At-Tawbah 9:25)

Thus the matter is not only in devoting one’s intention to Allah, but true help-seeking from Him — Glorified is He — is also necessary, and not viewing the deed or the self with joy, approval, and admiration.

From here our certainty grows in the necessity of the presence of the attribute of complete sincerity (al-ikhlas al-tamm) to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — in intention and help-seeking among the children of the promised generation; otherwise there is no victory and no empowerment.

### Manifestations of Sincerity

## Page 40

When the servant proceeds on the path of sincerity to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — this is such that it leaves effects and signs upon him which he feels inwardly and which those around him observe.

- Among these signs is that the servant will not wait behind his deed for the approval of his superiors, or for praise to be directed to him, or for titles to be conferred upon him, or for him to be preferred over his colleagues.

- Among them is that he will strive to hide his position and his work as much as possible, so he will not speak of it allusively or explicitly.

- Among them is that he will not seek to know people’s opinion of his deeds; rather, he does the deed, exerts effort in forgetting it, concealing it, and not speaking of it.

- Among them also: that he fears for himself the trial of fame and spotlight, so he repels it from himself as much as possible.

- Among them: that work in the front ranks is equal to work in the rear ranks for him; indeed, his work in the rear will be dearer to him, because there he is not exposed to people’s gaze or their praise.

- Among them: that praise and blame are equal to him, to the point that he repels people’s praise for fear of its negative effects upon himself.

- Among them: that he is not distressed when his deed is attributed to someone else.

- Among them: that he helps others in their work and works for its success without anyone sensing his involvement.

## Page 41

- Among them: that he has a secret of righteous deeds between himself and Allah that no one but He knows.

- Among them: that he will not feel distressed when the choice passes him by for undertaking a work in which he has more prior experience than others; rather, he assists the one chosen for it and offers him advice and experience.

- Among them: that he will not be concerned with people’s pleasure or displeasure with him.

- Among them: that he will not speak about himself in a way that purifies it, nor will he often say “I… for me… I have…”

- Among them: that he will not boast to anyone over something Allah has favoured him with.

- Among them: that he will not try to display all his capabilities and knowledge before others.

- Among them: that he will not look for gain from behind his work, nor will he exploit his position to achieve worldly benefits for himself or his relatives and friends.

- Among them: not reminding others of favours, and not recalling them to them in any form.

- Among them: that he increases in seeking forgiveness (istighfar) after his righteous deeds.

- Among them: that he strives against his soul and does not surrender to thoughts of self-admiration; rather, he treats them and gives them the antidote that restores balance and places it in the mould of servitude (al-ubudiyyah).

## Page 42

- Among them also: that he does not see himself as sincere; rather, he always has a bad opinion of himself, accuses it of hypocrisy and… and… and many other things that indicate complete sincerity to Allah — Mighty and Exalted is He — in intention and help-seeking.

And Allah will complete this affair until a rider will travel from Sana'a to Hadramawt fearing none but Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, and the wolf over his sheep. But you are impatient." (3)

And in the Battle of the Trench, and with the severity of the trial, and the gathering of the polytheists against the believers from every side, and the strangling of them, these people remembered what Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, had informed His Messenger, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, that they would meet of toils on their path to establishing the global Islamic state, so that increased them in faith, submission to Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, patience, and seeking reward for what is with Him: "And when the believers saw the confederates, they said: This is what Allah and His Messenger promised us. And Allah and His Messenger spoke the truth. And it increased them only in faith and submission." (Quran, Al-Ahzab 33:22).

**(3)** Narrated by al-Bukhari.

## Page 88

**Ninthly: Moderation and Balance (al-iʿtidāl wa al-tawāzun)**

Among the most important characteristics of this generation is that it is a moderate and balanced generation in all its affairs — in its worship and its dealings, in its understanding and its movement. It gives every possessor of a right his right, does not magnify what is small or belittle what is great, and places everything in its proper place: "And whoever is given wisdom has certainly been given much good." (Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:269).

It balances between the requirements of reforming itself and the requirements of its household and society, and it knows that the first circle is itself, then its household, then its society.

It does not exaggerate where exaggeration is not appropriate, nor does it burden itself with what it cannot bear.

**A Scholar of His Time (ʿālim bi-zamānihi)**

The generation of empowerment is not a generation isolated from people, so you do not see it living in seclusion far from them. Rather, it is the child of its environment, knows its time well, deals well with the variables of life and modern means of knowledge, cultured in thought. It lives the concerns of its ummah, and knows well how its enemies plot.

It uses everything new to convey its call and reach its goal.

**Occupied with Building the Truth (mashghūl bi-bināʾ al-ḥaqq)**

## Page 89

The promised generation is likewise occupied with building the truth, and pours its effort into rebuilding the ummah and reviving spirit in it. This task requires that it not be too occupied with demolishing falsehood. For if you say: And why should it not also be occupied with demolishing falsehood? The answer comes: Falsehood, even in its violence, has many helpers, and if it were occupied with demolishing it, its strength would be exhausted and its effort wasted. And we have in the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, the best example in that, for he, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, used to circumambulate the Ka'bah and around it were idols numbering three hundred and sixty, and despite that he did not try to destroy them. Rather, his work, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, was devoted to building tawhid in souls and establishing the Islamic state, so that after that the demolition of falsehood would come with ease and facility. He, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, was occupied with building the ummah, and day after day the Islamic edifice rose and falsehood diminished, until the day came in which it vanished completely and idols were removed from the entire Arabian Peninsula. And this is what is required: "And say to those who do not believe: Act according to your position; indeed, we are acting. And wait; indeed, we are waiting. And to Allah belongs the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and to Him all matters will be returned. So worship Him and rely upon Him. And your Lord is not unaware of what you do." (Quran, Hud 11:121-123).

**With the First Generation (maʿa al-jeel al-awwal)**

## Page 90

Indeed, the issue of correct understanding of Islam is of great importance to the generation of empowerment, for if faith is the true motive for deeds, then correct understanding is what prevents these deeds from deviating outside the bounds of uprightness. And this attribute was well represented in the generation of the companions — may Allah be pleased with them — and its manifestations appeared in many of their sayings and actions. Perhaps one of the strongest manifestations was their delay in burying their Messenger and beloved, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, until they finished choosing a caliph and imam for them, fearing that they would spend a night without an imam, because of the great corruption in that for the ummah.

And this Abu Bakr al-Siddiq — may Allah be pleased with him — at his death, when the hour came and death visited him, he bequeathed to Umar ibn al-Khattab — may Allah be pleased with him — after appointing him his successor as caliph of the Muslims, saying to him: "Indeed, Allah has a right by day that is not accepted at night, and a right by night that is not accepted by day. And voluntary deeds are not accepted until obligatory deeds are performed." (1)

And Uthman ibn Affan — may Allah be pleased with him — shortened the prayer at Hajj, although the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, used to shorten at Hajj, lest that become a means for the common people to believe that the prayer is two rak'ahs at all times and circumstances.

**(1)** Al-Zuhd by Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak 119.

## Page 91

And Abu Hurayrah — may Allah be pleased with him — said: "To stand guard for a night in the path of Allah (ribāṭ) is more beloved to me than to spend the Night of Decree in prayer at the Black Stone." (2)

**(2)** Ibn Taymiyyah, Majmu' al-Fatawa 28/6.

## Page 92

**Tenthly: A Generation Bound Together in Brotherhood (jeel mutarābiṭ mutaʾākhī)**

This generation — the generation of empowerment — is not merely scattered individuals here and there, none of them feeling the other. Rather, it is a generation bound together in mutual brotherhood like a solid structure, each part supporting the other. For brotherhood is the bond that binds its individuals so that they form together a single straight row with no crookedness in it: "Indeed, Allah loves those who fight in His path in a row as though they are a solid structure." (Quran, As-Saff 61:4).

This bonding that should exist among the children of the promised generation has a great role in achieving the desired goal, for it is a tremendous means of protecting individuals from lethargy or negative influence by the civilizational attacks to which the ummah is exposed. It also helps them to remain steadfast and continue the journey, and through it comes competition in good, mutual counsel in truth, and mutual counsel in patience.

**Noble Models (numādhij mushrifah)**

And because the first generation is the model that we see before us and in whom Allah's promise of victory and empowerment was fulfilled, it is natural that we see this attribute clearly manifested in it, and its effects appeared in the sayings and deeds of its children. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, established, after the emigration from Mecca to Medina, the brotherhood (al-mu'ākhāt) between the Muhajirun and the Ansar in the house of Anas ibn Malik.

## Page 93

And they were seventy men, half of them from the Muhajirun and half from the Ansar, forming brotherhood based on mutual consolation and on inheriting from one another after death instead of relatives, until the Battle of Badr. Then when Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, revealed: "And those of kinship are closer to one another in the Book of Allah." (Quran, Al-Anfal 8:75), inheritance by the bond of brotherhood was revoked. (1)

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, made this brotherhood a contract, not an empty phrase, and a deed binding in blood and wealth, not a greeting that tongues chatter with and has no effect. Feelings of selflessness, consolation, and companionship mingled in this brotherhood, filling the new society with the most wonderful examples. (2)

Al-Bukhari narrated that when the Muhajirun came to Medina, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, formed brotherhood between Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' — may Allah be pleased with them both. Sa'd said to Abd al-Rahman: "I am the wealthiest of the Ansar, so divide my wealth in half, and I have two wives, so look at whichever of them pleases you and name her to me, I will divorce her, and when her waiting period ends, marry her." He said: "May Allah bless you in your family and wealth. Where is your market?" So they directed him to the market of Banu Qaynuqa', and he returned with some butter and tallow…

**(2)** Fiqh al-Sirah by Muhammad al-Ghazali 179-180.

## Page 94

**Selflessness with Need (al-ithār maʿa al-ḥājah)**

The bond of brotherhood and love for Allah's sake is among the strongest bonds that gather the believers in general, and the generation of empowerment in particular. This bond can intensify and intensify until it reaches horizons so high that the mind cannot believe them.

Reflect on Allah's description of the Ansar and how they dealt with their Muhajirun brothers: "And those who settled in the home and in faith before them love those who emigrated to them and find not in their breasts any want of what they were given, and prefer them over themselves, even though they are in privation." (Quran, Al-Hashr 59:9).

Al-Qurtubi said: The Muhajirun were in the houses of the Ansar. So when the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, obtained the wealth of Banu al-Nadir, he called the Ansar and thanked them for what they had done with the Muhajirun in giving them their homes and sharing their wealth, then he said: "If you wish, I will divide what Allah has bestowed upon me from Banu al-Nadir between you and them, and the Muhajirun will remain as they are in your homes and your wealth. And if you wish, I will give it to them and they will leave your homes." So Sa'd ibn Ubadah and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh said: "No, we will divide it between the Muhajirun, and let them remain in our homes as they were." And the Ansar cried out: "We are content and submit, O Messenger of Allah." So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "O Allah, have mercy on the Ansar and the children of the Ansar." (1)

**(1)** Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Quran by al-Qurtubi 18/17-19.

## Page 95

They had preferred them over themselves with their wealth and homes, not out of plenty, but while they themselves needed them. (1)

And among what confirms that selflessness (al-ithār) was a general characteristic in the dealings of the first generation with one another is what Ibn Umar — may Allah be pleased with them both — reported, that a sheep's head was presented to a man from the companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, and he said: "So-and-so my brother is more in need of it than I." So he sent it to him. Then that person sent it to another, and it kept being sent from one to another until it returned to the first after it had circulated among seven. (2)
