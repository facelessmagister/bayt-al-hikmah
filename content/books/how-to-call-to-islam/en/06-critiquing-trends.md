## Marxist Theory

If we wish to investigate the causes of all wars since the capitalist organization of society and the state became known, we trace the causes of every war back to this law: the law of competition and rivalry.

### 3. The Law of the Concentration and Monopoly of Production

The law of competition that drives peoples and nations to plunge into the waves of wars waged by capitalism and the capitalists also drives companies to struggle and fight against one another. Large companies try to liquidate small or medium companies, either by swallowing them through the purchase of a majority of their shares, or by competing with them so fiercely that the small or medium companies go bankrupt and then collapse completely.

Banks play a large role in achieving the aims of large companies in liquidating small or medium companies. In times of crisis, the banks withhold loans from small or medium companies and demand payment of their debts, thereby pushing them into bankruptcy and collapse.

### 4. The Law of Low Prices

This law calls for preserving profits while increasing them, by means of the mechanical expansion of production and the reduction of the workforce. What results from the lowering of the cost of the commodity through reducing wages arising from replacing workers with machines leads to a lowering of the price of the commodity on the scale of profits; thus, the capitalists preserve their profits without the lowering of the price of the commodity affecting the amounts of profit that come to them. All of this is because the problem has been solved by reducing the workforce and thereby lowering costs through dispensing with paying wages to workers whose place the machine has taken.
