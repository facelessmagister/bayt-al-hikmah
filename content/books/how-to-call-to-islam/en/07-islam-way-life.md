# Proving Islam is a Way of Life

## Third Investigation: Proving Islam is a Way of Life

It is incumbent upon the caller, in this chapter, to prove to the one being called that Islam is a *manhaj* (منهج)—a way of life—and to demolish the prevailing belief among the general body of Muslims that it is merely a devotional religion having no relation to life or to its social, economic, and political affairs.

The caller may treat this topic along the following lines:

### First: The Origin of This Conception

Because the Islamic world, under the influence of Western civilization and its harmful influences, regarded Europe’s revolt against the Church and the Western world’s separation of ecclesiastical authority from temporal authority as a universal, constant rule, it concluded that religion—every religion—must be separated from the state, and that one must render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s and to God the things that are God’s.

In reality, the masses were right in their revolt against the Church in Europe and in their demand that religious clerics be excluded from the political stage after they had tasted from them the worst of suffering. For oppression, killing, hanging, burning, and dragging were among the means to which the Church resorted in order to consolidate its dominion. The masses were also right because the Church, by departing from the circle of spiritual and moral teachings brought by Jesus (peace be upon him), and by entering the political arena and exploiting its spiritual authority in the field of public life, had exceeded the limits of its jurisdiction and intervened in what was not its concern. So it was the right of the masses, when that occurred, to rise up to correct conditions and restore matters to their proper order.

### Second: Ignorance of Islam

Likewise, due to the Muslims’ ignorance of the characteristics of their religion and the horizons of their Shariah, and the transfer of intellectual and political leadership from them to others, and the collapse of Islamic rule in the Ottoman era—which, despite its faults and evils, was a manifestation of Islamic *ḥākimiyyah* (حاكمية) and the total existence of Islam—for these reasons and others, the conception and understanding of Muslims regarding Islam became bad and distorted, not matching the essence of Islam in content or in history.

Islam is a way of life. This is how it should be understood, and this is how it must be applied. It is a comprehensive revolution: a revolution not limited to one aspect of life’s aspects, but extending to every aspect; a revolution not expressed by a word or slogan, but by a qualitative transformation of society and a radical change of its basic foundations at every level.

And the history of Islam likewise bears decisive witness that it never accepted any separation between state and church, nor between a class of religious men and another class of statesmen. This clear, consistent picture ran through the lives of the Muslims: for they were men of prayer and men of struggle. They would lead people in prayer in the mosques, then return with them to the battlefields of war and combat.

Therefore, the nature of Islam and its history are entirely different from the nature of Christianity and its history. But the error of many who judged Islam by the guilt of another is due to their ignorance of Islam as a message, and to the fabricated conspiracies against Islam which worked to spread these ugly opinions and conceptions about the religion, and planted them in educational curricula and in the media, and through missionary and foreign cultural institutions, as a studied plan aimed at distancing Islam from the leaders of the *ummah* (أمة), for it is the secret of its strength and the backbone of its existence.

### Islam Is a Way of Life

For Islam is a way of life. It is a creed that clarifies the correct conception of the universe, humanity, and life. It is a creed that defines this human being: where he came from, why he came, what his destiny is, and his limits. By this creed, man enters into the most dangerous covenant in human life. And upon the basis of this creed there arise a Shariah and a system that organize human life in all its affairs.

Islam is a complete *manhaj* for human life: comprehensive in outlook, vast in horizons, and wide in scope. It regulates the individual’s relationship with himself, his family, and his society, and it expounds the principles and rules upon which rest the systems and laws that govern the conduct of society and people.

Thus it combines the gentleness of guidance with the precision of legislation, the majesty of creed with the beauty of worship, and the leadership of the prayer-niche with the leadership of war. In this way it is a way of life in the full meaning of the word.
