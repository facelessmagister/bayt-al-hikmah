# Sources of the Islamic Methodology

## Third Investigation: Sources of the Islamic Methodology

It is fitting for the caller, in this subject, to emphasise that restricting the sources of Islamic legislation to the Qur’an and the *Sunnah* (سنة) is a fundamental factor in its stability, firmness, and immunity from distortion, alteration, and arbitrary interpretation. It likewise exposes the falsehood of positive systems and doctrines, for these are intrinsically deficient and subject to the diverse human interpretations and hermeneutics across ages and epochs.

The caller should therefore address the sources of the Islamic *manhaj* (منهج) with some detail according to the following order:

### First: The Original Sources

#### 1. The Qur’an

It is the Book of Allah, revealed to His Messenger Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him, in the Arabic language, transmitted to us from the Prophet by an indisputably *mutawātir* (متواتر) narration.

##### Its Revelation and Compilation

The Qur’an was revealed to the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, piecemeal over twenty-three years. Its verses were written down immediately upon revelation, and what was written was placed in the house of the Messenger. The Qur’an had well-known scribes, said to number forty-two.

The Qur’an was first compiled during the caliphate of ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may Allah be pleased with him. This compilation was distinguished by important characteristics: they collected the entire Qur’an with the most precise standards of research, examination, and scholarly verification; they restricted it to what had not been abrogated in recitation; and they ensured that the *ummah* heard it and that its contents were transmitted by *tawātur*.

During the caliphate of ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān, may Allah be pleased with him, the second compilation of the Qur’an took place. This compilation was distinguished by the following: it restricted the text to what was established by *tawātur*, excluding what had been transmitted by solitary reports; it omitted what had been abrogated in recitation and had not become fixed in the final codex; it arranged the *sūrahs* and verses in the well-known order known today; and it stripped away everything that was not Qur’an—such as what some Companions wrote in their personal copies to clarify a meaning, indicate an abrogating or abrogated verse, and the like.

##### Its Characteristics

**First:** Its wording and meaning are both from Allah. The Messenger had no part in it except to convey it. Therefore, the Prophet’s *aḥādīth* (أحاديث) are not considered Qur’an, for their wording is not from Allah, even though their meanings were revealed to him. Likewise, a translation of the Qur’an into a language other than Arabic is not considered Qur’an.

**Second:** It has been transmitted to us by *tawātur*, meaning that it was transmitted from the Prophet by groups whose number precludes the possibility of collusion upon a lie, and then transmitted from these by others in the same manner, until it reached us in successive generations, preserved in people’s hearts without addition or omission. For Allah, the Exalted, has undertaken to preserve it. He, Mighty and Majestic, said:

> Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur’an and indeed, We will be its guardian. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥijr 15:9]

**Third:** It is miraculous, in the sense that all created beings are incapable of producing anything like it. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Say, "If mankind and the jinn gathered in order to produce the like of this Qur’an, they could not produce the like of it, even if they were to each other assistants." [Qur’an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:88]

##### Its Rulings

The rulings of the Qur’an are of three kinds:

1. **Rulings relating to creed**, such as faith in Allah, His messengers, and the Last Day, and so on.
2. **Rulings relating to the purification and refinement of souls**, and to the explanation of the noble virtues that one must adorn oneself with, and the vices that one must shun.
3. **Rulings relating to the words and deeds of accountable persons** beyond the two preceding categories. These are the practical rulings that fall within the sphere of *fiqh* (فقه), and they are of two divisions: devotional acts and transactions.

#### 2. The Sunnah

It is the second original source of Islamic legislation. What is meant by it is whatever issued from the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, in word, deed, quality, or tacit approval.

##### Recording the Sunnah

The Sunnah was not officially recorded during the lifetime of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, as the Qur’an was, because the Companions relied on their memories and were occupied with recording the Qur’an during that period. But this does not mean that nothing of the Sunnah was written down; rather, it is established that some Companions did so for their own benefit.

In the first and second centuries *hijrī*, repeated attempts at recording the Sunnah were undertaken. In the third century, a group of eminent scholars took up the task of recording the Sunnah, compiling it, and distinguishing its sound reports from others. At the forefront of these were the two great Imāms, al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

Thus the Sunnah came to have refined, sound compilations, foremost among which were the Six Books: *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, *al-Muwaṭṭaʾ* of Mālik, *al-Jāmiʿ* of al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan Abī Dāwūd*, *Sunan al-Nasāʾī*, and *Sunan Ibn Mājah*.

##### Sciences of Ḥadīth

Among the fruits of the efforts that accomplished the recording of the Sunnah was the establishment of scientific rules and principles for handling *aḥādīth* and classifying them. Of these are:

1. **The science of ḥadīth terminology** (*ʿilm muṣṭalaḥ al-ḥadīth*). This science deals with the foundational rules for accepting a *ḥadīth*, assessing it, and classifying it in light of that as *ṣaḥīḥ* (صحيح), *ḥasan* (حسن), *ḍaʿīf* (ضعيف), or what branches from each of these categories.

2. **The science of jarḥ and taʿdīl** (الجرح والتعديل). It is the science of evaluating men; it investigates the conditions of transmitters, their trustworthiness, reliability, uprightness, and so forth.

##### Evidence for the Authority of the Sunnah and Its Being a Source of Legislation

1. **The explicit statement that the Prophet does not speak from whim.** Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Nor does he speak from [his own] inclination. It is not but a revelation revealed. [Qur’an, An-Najm 53:3–4]

2. **The command to obey the Messenger.** Allah said:

> Say, "Obey Allah and the Messenger." [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:32]

3. **Making obedience to the Messenger obedience to Allah:**

> He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:80]

4. **The command to follow what the Messenger brings us:**

> And whatever the Messenger has given you — take; and what he has forbidden you — refrain from. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥashr 59:7]

5. **The obligation to refer disputed matters to Allah and to His Messenger:**

> And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:59]

6. **The obligation to submit the dispute to the Messenger and to accept his judgment:**

> But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muḥammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:65]

7. **The warning of painful torment for those who oppose the Messenger:**

> So let those beware who dissent from the Prophet's order, lest fitnah strike them or a painful punishment. [Qur’an, An-Nūr 24:63]

8. **The Messenger was given the authority to explain the rulings of the Qur’an**, for it contains general rulings that require elaboration before legal responsibility can be validly imposed. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And We revealed to you the message that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them and that they might give thought. [Qur’an, An-Naḥl 16:44]

##### Types of Sunnah with Regard to Its Chain

By its chain is meant its transmitters. In this respect, if the chain is connected to the Prophet, it is of three types—unless the chain is not connected to the Prophet because the *tābiʿī* (تابعي) did not mention the name of the *ṣaḥābī* (صحابي) from whom he narrated the *Sunnah* from the Prophet; in that case it is called a *murassa*l (مرسل) hadith. The three types are:

1. **A *mutawātir* Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by a group whose collusion upon a lie is inconceivable and whose number cannot be counted, then transmitted from them by another group with the same quality until it reached us. Of this type are the practical *sunan* that explain the amounts of *zakāh*, the acts of *ḥajj*, and the form and pillars of *ṣalāh*. These convey certain knowledge.

2. **A *mashhūr* (مشهور) Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by one or two individuals—i.e. a number falling short of *tawātur*—then it became widely known and was transmitted by *tawātur* groups in the era of the *tābiʿīn* and the successors of the *tābiʿīn*.

3. **An *āḥād* (آحاد) Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by a number that did not reach the level of *tawātur* and did not later become widely known—i.e. it is neither *mutawātir* nor *mashhūr*. This type conveys probable but preponderant knowledge of its sound attribution to the Prophet, and it is obligatory to act upon it.

##### Types of Rulings Brought by the Sunnah

1. **Rulings that agree with and confirm Qur’anic rulings**, such as the *ḥadīth*: “The wealth of a Muslim is not lawful to another Muslim except by his good pleasure.” [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Financial Transactions] It agrees with and confirms the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> O you who have believed, do not consume one another's wealth unjustly but only [in lawful] business by mutual consent. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:29]

Likewise, the *Sunnah* prohibits disobedience to parents, false testimony, and killing the soul, and the like, just as they are mentioned in the Qur’an.

2. **Rulings that explain and detail what is general in the Qur’an**, such as the *Sunnah* that explains the amounts of *zakāh* and the amount of stolen wealth for which the thief’s hand is cut off.

3. **Rulings that restrict an absolute statement of the Book and specify its general wording.** Some rulings come in the Qur’an in absolute terms, and the *Sunnah* restricts them. For example, the cutting off of the thief’s hand came in absolute terms, but the *Sunnah* restricted it to the wrist, i.e. the hand is cut off from the wrist.

4. **New rulings not mentioned in the Qur’an**, for the *Sunnah* is independent in legislating rulings, and in this it is like the Qur’an. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> Indeed, I have been given the Qur’an and something like it with it. [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Sunnah, Hadith 4604]

Of this type are the prohibition of the blood of domesticated donkeys, the prohibition of eating every predatory animal with a fang and every bird with a talon, judgment by a single witness together with an oath, the obligation of blood-money on the *ʿāqilah* (عاقلة), the inheritance of the grandmother, and the like.

### Second: Subordinate Sources

#### 1. *Ijmāʿ* (الإجماع)

It is, in the terminology of the jurists and the scholars of *uṣūl* (أصول الفقه), the agreement of the *mujtahidīn* (المجتهدين) of the Muslim *ummah* in a given age, after the death of the Prophet, upon a legal ruling.

*Ijmāʿ* is a source of legislation and one of the evidences of rulings. This is established by numerous texts from the Book and the *Sunnah*.

**The basis of *ijmāʿ*:** *Ijmāʿ* must rest upon an evidence, for a statement on legal matters without evidence is error, and the Muslim *ummah* does not agree upon error. The basis of *ijmāʿ* may be a text from the Book of Allah or a *Sunnah* of His Prophet, or it may be analogical. For example, the prohibition of marriage to granddaughters no matter how far down the line is based on the text of the Qur’an:

> Prohibited to you [for marriage] are your mothers, your daughters… [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:23]

And the consensus on the prohibition of pork fat is by analogy to the prohibition of its meat, and so on.

#### 2. *Qiyās* (القياس)

It is, in the terminology of the scholars, the extension of a ruling from an original case, concerning which a text has come, to a new case on account of the equality of the two cases in the effective cause (*ʿillah* (علة)) of the ruling. Thus, when an original case exists concerning which a text has come and we know the *ʿillah* of the ruling, and then a new case occurs concerning which no text has come, the new case takes the ruling of the original case.

**Examples of *qiyās*:**

- The ruling on drinking wine is prohibition, because a text has come to that effect. The *ʿillah* of the ruling is intoxication; therefore, every beverage containing this *ʿillah* is likewise prohibited by analogy to wine.

- Killing the heir to take his inheritance: a text has come concerning this case, and its ruling is the exclusion of the killer from inheritance. The *ʿillah* of the ruling is the heir’s haste to take the inheritance before its proper time, so he is punished by being deprived of it. The killing of a beneficiary named in a will by the testator is a case concerning which no text has come, but it contains the same *ʿillah*—haste to take a thing before its due time. Therefore it takes the same ruling: the beneficiary-killer is deprived of his right in the will, just as the heir-killer is deprived of his right in the inheritance.

#### 3. *Istiḥsān* (الاستحسان)

It is, in the terminology of the scholars, the departure from an obvious analogy to a hidden analogy for an evidence that requires this departure. An example is that donations are not valid from one legally interdicted because of imbecility, and among these donations is the endowment (*waqf* (وقف)). Yet by way of *istiḥsān* an exception is made to this principle, allowing his endowment upon himself for his own benefit. The reason for this is the preservation of his wealth and preventing his means of livelihood from becoming a burden upon others.

#### 4. *Maṣāliḥ Mursalah* (المصالح المرسلة)

It is a jurisprudential source whose authority is established by an inductive survey of the texts and rulings of the *Sharīʿah* in the Book and the *Sunnah*, and by the practice of the jurists among the Companions. This source is broad enough for new events and evolving occurrences; it makes *fiqh* flexible and dynamic, enabling it to embrace the problems of life in every age. The jurists adopted this source and relied upon it.

*Maṣāliḥ mursalah*, then, are interests for which the Lawgiver has not laid down specific rulings to secure them, and for which no particular evidence establishes or negates them. Therefore, every occurrence that contains no text, no *ijmāʿ*, no *qiyās*, and no *istiḥsān*, but contains a benefit for people, permits the *mujtahid* to find the appropriate ruling to secure it—that is, to secure this benefit for the people.

**Examples of *maṣlaḥah mursalah*:**

- The compilation of the Qur’an into a single *muṣḥaf* by Abū Bakr.
- The registration of the *dawāwīn* (الدواوين) by ʿUmar.
- Making artisans liable for the loss of people’s property caused by their hands.
- The right of the ruler to levy taxes upon the wealthy in case of need, and the like.

#### 5. *Sadd al-Dharāʾiʿ* (سد الذرائع)

*Dharāʾiʿ* means means. If a means leads to what is forbidden and corrupt, that means is forbidden, and it is obligatory to block it—that is, to prevent it in order to cut off the material of corrupt means. If, on the other hand, a means leads to something desired in the *Sharīʿah*, that means is likewise desired. Thus *dharāʾiʿ* are blocked and prevented when they lead to corruption, and are required and opened when they lead to benefits.

Because the term *dharāʾiʿ* is most often applied to actions and methods that lead to evil and corruption, it has come to be called *sadd al-dharāʾiʿ*.

**The authority of this principle:**

The principle of blocking means is attested as sound by evidences from the Book, the *Sunnah*, and the practice of the Companions. From the Book is the saying of Allah, the Exalted:

> And do not insult those they invoke other than Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge. [Qur’an, Al-Anʿām 6:108]

And from the *Sunnah* is the Prophet’s prohibition, peace and blessings be upon him, of *iḥtikār* (احتكار) as a means of blocking the way to usury.

Blocking the means is therefore a valid principle and a jurisprudential source from which rulings are drawn. The leading *mujtahid* Imāms adopted it, and the most committed to this source were Imām Mālik and Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal.
