# Major Outlines of the Islamic Method

In the topics of *sharīʿah* (الشريعة):

- In the worship system
- In the social system
- In the economic system
- In the political system

## The Worship System

It is the system that regulates the relationship between the servant and his Lord, and the acts that draw one near to Allah the Exalted, purifying the soul, elevating it through the stations of devotion and perfection, and realizing the innate balance between the needs of the soul and the needs of the body in human life. It comprises the Five Pillars of Islam. On the authority of ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is none worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakāh, fasting Ramaḍān, and performing Hajj to the House if one is able.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 8; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 16]

### Prayer (*al-ṣalāh*)

**Its ruling:** Prayer is the pillar of religion and one of the pillars of Islam; it is an individual obligation upon every legally responsible person. Allah the Exalted says:

> “Maintain with care the [obligatory] prayers and [in particular] the middle prayer and stand in devout obedience to Allah.” [Qur’an, An-Nisa 4:103]

**Its wisdom:** Prayer is the *miʿrāj* (معراج) of the believer to Allah, and his spiritual space that illumines his heart, purifies his soul, and disciplines his limbs, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater. And Allah knows that which you do.” [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45]

And in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

> “Tell me, if there were a river at the door of one of you in which he bathed five times a day, would any filth remain on him?” They said, “No filth would remain on him.” He said, “That is the likeness of the five prayers: by them Allah effaces sins.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Times of Prayer, Hadith 528; Sahih Muslim, Book of Purification, Hadith 671]

**The ruling on one who abandons it:** One who deliberately abandons prayer in Islam is a disbeliever according to the imams of the legal schools, and it is obligatory to kill him. According to some others, he is a transgressor if he leaves it deliberately without denial, and he is to be disciplined or imprisoned until he prays. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “The covenant between us and them is the prayer; whoever abandons it has disbelieved.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 82]

And he said:

> “Between a man and disbelief is the abandonment of prayer.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 82]

**Its conditions:** Purification of the body, garment, and place; entry of the prayer time; facing the *qiblah* (قبلة); covering the *ʿawrah* (عورة); and *wuḍūʾ* (وضوء).

**Its pillars:** Intention; the *takbīrat al-iḥrām* (تكبيرة الإحرام); standing for one who is able; recitation of *al-Fātiḥah* (الفاتحة) in every cycle; bowing with tranquility; rising from bowing with tranquility; prostrating with tranquility; sitting between the two prostrations with tranquility; the final *tashahhud* (تشهد); sending blessings upon the Prophet; and the *taslīm* (تسليم).

**Its nullifiers:** The exit of anything from the front or back; deliberate speech; excessive movement; and leaving a pillar from its pillars or a condition from its conditions.

**Its times:** Fajr, Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, and ʿIshāʾ.

**The *adhān* and *iqāmah*:** These are two *sunnahs* (سنتان) for every obligatory prayer after the time has entered.

**The Friday Prayer (*ṣalāt al-jumuʿah*):** It is an individual obligation upon resident legally responsible persons; it consists of two *rakʿahs* (ركعتان), preceded by two sermons.

**The Prayer of the Traveler:** The traveler may shorten the four-*rakʿah* prayers to two. He may also combine Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr, and Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ. If he intends residence for four days or more, shortening is not permitted for him. If he stays in a place awaiting his need, he may shorten for up to eighteen days. The distance for shortening is forty-eight miles.

**The Funeral Prayer (*ṣalāt al-janāzah*):** It consists of four *takbīrāt* (تكبيرات): *al-Fātiḥah* is recited in the first; blessings upon the Prophet are sent in the second; supplication is made for the deceased in the third; and *taslīm* is given after the fourth.

**The ʿĪd Prayer:** The ʿĪd prayer is two *rakʿahs*: in the first, three additional *takbīrāt* are made after the opening *takbīr* and before recitation; in the second, three additional *takbīrāt* are made after recitation—according to the Ḥanafī school.

### *Wuḍūʾ*

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; washing the face; washing the arms including the elbows; wiping part of the head; washing the feet including the ankles; and sequence.

**Its *sunnahs*:** saying *bismillāh*; washing the palms; rinsing the mouth; inhaling water into the nose; wiping the entire head; wiping the ears; running fingers through the beard; running water between the fingers of the hands and feet; repeating each washing three times except the head; and performing the acts in immediate succession.

**Its nullifiers:** the exit of anything from the two passages; deep sleep; loss of reason; touching the private parts; and touching a strange woman.

### *Ghusl* (ritual bath)

**Its occasions:** penetration of the glans into the vagina; emission of semen through a wet dream or otherwise; cessation of menstrual or postpartum bleeding; and death.

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; causing water to reach the entire body; rubbing; and performing *wuḍūʾ* before *ghusl*.

### *Tayammum* (dry ablution)

**Its conditions:** absence of water; fear of harm from using it; and entry of the prayer time.

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; wiping the face and hands with clean earth.

**Its *sunnahs*:** saying *bismillāh*; shaking off dust; and performing the acts in immediate succession.

**Its nullifiers:** the same things that nullify *wuḍūʾ*; the availability of water; and the disappearance of the excuse.

### Fasting (*al-ṣawm*)

**Its ruling:** Fasting is one of the pillars of Islam and an obligation upon every legally responsible person able to fast. The traveler and the sick may break their fast and must make it up. One unable to fast due to old age or an illness from which recovery is not hoped for may break the fast and must provide *fiḍyah* (فدية) for each day.

Just as nations train and mobilize their sons to endure hardship and struggle in anticipation of needing them on the day of conflict, so too Islam prescribed fasting at one appointed time, including children and adults, men and women. It is training for the greater and perpetual struggle: the struggle of life waged by all, for which all bear responsibility.

**Its definition:** Abstaining from the things that break the fast from the break of dawn until sunset.

**Its nullifiers:** sexual intercourse; deliberate ejaculation; and deliberate vomiting.

**Permissible matters while fasting:** bathing; taking medicine by injection in the vein or muscle; kissing one’s wife; swallowing saliva; tasting food; and sexual relations between sunset and dawn.

**Recommended voluntary fasts:** It is recommended to fast ʿĀshūrāʾ, the tenth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, three days of every month, Mondays and Thursdays, and six days of Shawwāl.

### *Zakāh*

**Its ruling and wisdom:** It is one of the pillars of Islam, an obligatory act of worship, and also a social and economic necessity, for it fosters solidarity in Muslim society. Allah the Exalted says:

> “Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them to increase.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:103]

And the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “Indeed Allah has made obligatory upon the wealthy Muslims in their wealth a portion for the poor among them sufficient for their needs. The poor will not be exhausted by hunger or nakedness except because of what the wealthy do. Beware, Allah will call them to a severe reckoning and punish them with a painful torment.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Zakah, Hadith 1676; Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Zakah, Hadith 609]

**Its thresholds (*niṣāb*):** The threshold of gold is twenty *dīnārs*; the threshold of silver is two hundred *dirhams*; the rate on each is one-fortieth, that is, two and a half percent.

The threshold of camels begins at five, for which one sheep or goat is due.

The threshold of sheep begins at forty, for which one sheep or goat is due.

The threshold of cattle begins at thirty; when this number is reached, a *tabīʿ* or *tabīʿah* (a calf in its second year) is due.

**Zakāh on trade goods:** Trade goods are valued at the end of the lunar year at their price in the local currency; their zakāh is paid in cash at the rate of one-fortieth.

**Zakāh on crops and fruits:** One-tenth is due on what is irrigated by rain, and one-twentieth on what is irrigated by machines. According to most jurists, the threshold for these is five *wasqs*, i.e., 585 kilograms.

**Its beneficiaries:** Zakāh is disbursed to the eight categories specified in the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler—an obligation [imposed] by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:60]

### *Ḥajj* (pilgrimage)

**Its ruling:** It is obligatory once in a lifetime upon every Muslim who is adult, sane, and able, whether male or female. Evidence for its obligation is the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House—for whoever is able to find thereto a way.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:97]

And the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

> “O people, Hajj has been made obligatory upon you, so perform Hajj.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Hajj, Hadith 1337]

**Its wisdom:** It realizes the benefit of the gathering of Muslims and what results from it of consultation on common affairs and issues, devising solutions for existing and emerging problems, and realizing unity and solidarity. In it human equality is realized, for all stand on one plain and in one garment, with no distinction or preference among them. In it the elevation of the spirit is realized, as the soul strips away its earthly attachments and desires, devoting itself to Allah the Exalted and keeping watch over Him. This has an effect in disciplining and purifying the soul from its stains and burdens.

**Its types:** Hajj is performed in three ways:
- *al-Ifrād*: entering *iḥrām* (الإحرام) for Hajj only from the *mīqāt* (الميقات).
- *al-Tamattuʿ*: entering *iḥrām* for Hajj upon ascending to ʿArafāt.
- *al-Qirān*: entering *iḥrām* for Hajj and ʿUmrah together from the spatial *mīqāt*; the *qārin* (القارن) remains in *iḥrām* until he completes the rites of both ʿUmrah and Hajj.

**Its pillars:** intention at *iḥrām*; standing at ʿArafāt; *ṭawāf* (طواف) of the House in completion; *saʿy* (سعي) between Ṣafā and Marwah in completion; and shaving or cutting the hair.

**Its obligations:** *iḥrām* from the *mīqāt*; spending the night at Muzdalifah; spending the nights in Minā during the days of *tashrīq*; and the farewell *ṭawāf*.

**Its *sunnahs*:** the *talbiyah* (تلبية); the arrival *ṭawāf*; combining night and day at ʿArafāt; bathing upon donning the *iḥrām*; and sacrifice.

**Its prohibitions:** sexual intercourse; kissing; masturbation; perfuming; contracting marriage; wearing stitched garments; and hunting game.

## The Social System

The social system in Islam aims to support family cohesion, regulate relations among members of a single family, a single neighborhood, and a single quarter, and realize cooperation among them for the reform and purification of society from everything that corrupts its thought and character. This includes realizing material solidarity among citizens, which strengthens bonds and makes society a cohesive unit in which the words of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) are embodied:

> “The believers in their mutual kindness, compassion, and sympathy are like one body: when one of its limbs complains, the rest of the body responds with sleeplessness and fever.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Beginnings of Creation, Hadith 6011; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2586]

To realize this objective, Islam establishes the following:

1. **The righteous individual is regarded as the cornerstone in building the Islamic society.** Therefore Islam is concerned with his upbringing, building his mind and soul upon the basis of *sharīʿah* rulings and the requirements of *fiṭrah* (فطرة), without excess or neglect. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption].” [Qur’an, Ash-Shams 91:7–10]

2. **The family is regarded as the first cell in the structure of the Islamic state.** The man and woman are its two elements upon which it stands and depends. Therefore Islam legislated many matters, among them:

   - Regulating the reproductive instinct through marriage and considering any other relationship outside it forbidden except with one’s spouse or what one’s right hand possesses. Thus Islam forbade fornication, sodomy, and masturbation. It encouraged young men to marry, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

     > “O young men, whoever among you is able to marry, let him marry; and whoever is not able, then let him fast, for it is a shield for him.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage, Hadith 5065; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1400]

   - Ensuring that virtue is realized through the cooperation of the spouses, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression.” [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:2]

   - Ensuring the freedom of each party to choose their spouse. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

     > “Choose carefully for your sperm, for marry those who are suitable and marry them to suitable matches.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1968]

     And he said:

     > “Do not marry off a previously married woman until she is consulted, and do not marry off a virgin until she is asked for permission.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage, Hadith 5136; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1419]

     It is narrated that al-Mughīrah ibn Shuʿbah proposed to a woman, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to him:

     > “Look at her, for that is more likely to create affection between you,” meaning compatibility and harmony.

   - Ensuring the woman’s right to work in what does not conflict with the interest of the family or the limits of *sharīʿah*, does not lead to moral or social harm, and does not burden her beyond her capacity. The women of the Muslims used to participate alongside men in expeditions and wars, and in some trades and commerce. Such was Umm Ḥakīm bint al-Ḥārith, who entered battle between the Romans and the Muslims while still a bride, the scent of her wedding not yet faded. Her husband was martyred before her eyes, yet she neither wept nor wailed; rather, she tightened her garments and killed seven of the enemy at the bridge still known today as “Qanṭarat Umm Ḥakīm.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Military Expeditions, Hadith 4323]

   - Ensuring separation between the society of women and the society of men and preventing mixing except for a need proportionate to the *sharīʿah*, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “And if you ask anything of them, ask them from behind a partition. That is purer for your hearts and their hearts.” [Qur’an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:53]

     And the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

     > “Whenever a man is alone with a woman, Satan is the third among them.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Beginning of Creation, Hadith 5233; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1341]

   - Ensuring the *qiwāmah* (قوامة) of the man over the woman in leading the family and caring for its affairs, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “Men are in charge of women by [right of] what Allah has given one over the other and what they spend [for maintenance] from their wealth.” [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:34]

     And His words:

     > “And they [i.e., women] have rights similar to those [of men] over them in kindness, and men are a degree above them.” [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:228]

   - Ensuring equality in rights and duties.

   - Ensuring ease in dowries and marriage expenses, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

     > “The marriage that brings the most blessing is the one with the least expenditure.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1905]

     And his words:

     > “The best dowry is the easiest.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1908]

3. **Human equality is regarded as the essential characteristic of Muslim society.** All people are equal in the sight of Islam, returning to one origin; there is no distinction between one individual and another, and no superiority of one over another except through the good one brings to himself and society. This is what the Qur’an expressed in the words:

   > “O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Acquainted.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13]

   And what the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) meant when he said:

   > “O people, your Lord is one, and your father is one; all of you are from Adam, and Adam is from dust. The noblest of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous among you. There is no superiority for an Arab over a non-Arab, nor for a non-Arab over an Arab; nor for a red-skinned person over a black-skinned person, nor for a black-skinned person over a red-skinned person, except by righteousness. Have I conveyed the message? O Allah, bear witness. Let the one who is present convey it to the one who is absent.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Musnad al-Mukththirīn min al-Anṣār, Hadith 23489; al-Mustadrak, Hadith 2348]

4. **Social solidarity is regarded as a sacred religious obligation in Muslim society.** This solidarity is not limited to securing food, clothing, and shelter for the poor and needy; rather, it is comprehensive enough to guarantee the natural human rights of life, freedom, knowledge, dignity, and ownership. The fundamental principle from which this solidarity in Islam proceeds is regarding the members of society as brothers, for Allah the Exalted said:

   > “The believers are but brothers.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

   They are obliged to fulfill the duties of brotherhood among them, as the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “None of you [truly] believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 13; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 45]

5. **Noble character is regarded as the force that regulates energies and the spirit that spreads goodness, security, and virtue.** The state is the first party responsible for purifying society from corruption and its causes, and it is among its duties to spread virtue and combat vice, until the purpose for which Islam came is realized in society. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “I was only sent to perfect noble character.” [Sunan al-Muwaṭṭaʾ, Book of Good Character, Hadith 1614]

   The realization of this comes through concern for instilling *ʿaqīdah* (عقيدة) in souls and applying the rulings of Islam in general. Thus every individual will feel responsible for safeguarding society from grave sins, in fulfillment of the hadith of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Every joint of the son of Adam must give a charity every day the sun rises. It was said: O Messenger of Allah, from where can we give charity every day? He said: The gates of goodness are many: saying *subḥānallāh*, *al-ḥamdulillāh*, *allāhu akbar*, and *lā ilāha illallāh*; enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong; removing harm from the road; giving ear to the deaf; guiding the blind; showing the one who asks for what he needs; carrying with the strength of your leg the one who asks for help in distress; and carrying with the strength of your arm the weak.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Zakah, Hadith 1009]

## The Economic System

The economic system in Islam aims to develop general production and multiply it, so as to ensure the satisfaction of people’s essential needs and enable them to secure their complementary needs without extravagance or waste, narrowing the gap between the different classes in a manner that realizes justice, prosperity, and well-being for all members of society.

To this end, Islam establishes the following:

1. **Human ownership of wealth is accidental and temporary; the entire universe belongs to Allah the Exalted.** Allah the Exalted said:

   > “To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:189]

   > “And He has subjected to you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.” [Qur’an, Al-Jāthiyah 45:13]

   > “And spend out of that over which He has made you trustees.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥadīd 57:7]

2. **Ownership is of three kinds:** individual ownership, public ownership, and state ownership.

3. **Lawful individual ownership is inviolable in the Islamic economic system.** Whatever an individual acquires through lawful means becomes his right, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Whoever reaches something that no other Muslim has reached first, he has the greater right to it.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Division of Spoils, Hadith 3078]

   And his words:

   > “Whoever revives a dead land, it belongs to him.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Division of Spoils, Hadith 3078; Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Division, Hadith 1368]

   To emphasize the sanctity of individual ownership in Islam, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “The wealth, blood, and honor of every Muslim are sacred to every Muslim.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Sanctity of Blood, Hadith 6296; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 45]

   And he said:

   > “Whoever is killed defending his wealth is a martyr.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Blood Money, Hadith 2580]

4. **Public ownership** is permission granted by the Lawgiver for the community to share in benefiting from the thing itself. This includes pastures, forests, firewood collection, mines, oil wells, roads, and water, in accordance with the hadith of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “People are partners in three things: water, pasture, and fire.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Agriculture, Hadith 3477; Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 18005]

5. **State ownership** is what the public interest requires the state to own or what the state consumes for public interests, such as shipbuilding, weapons, aircraft, and the like.

6. **Ensuring the provision of means of work for every individual in the *ummah* (أمة), guaranteeing every person shelter to live in, livelihood that keeps disease at bay, and health, social, and cultural security for him and his children.** Many *aḥādīth* of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) point to this, among them his words:

   > “If one of you has work but has no home, let him acquire a home; if he has no wife, let him marry; and if he has no mount, let him acquire a mount.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 12481]

7. **Ensuring the worker’s right to receive appropriate wages commensurate with his competence, so that they do not fall below the minimum needed for a moderate standard of living.** Evidence for this is the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Allah says: I will be the opponent of three people on the Day of Resurrection: a man who gave in My Name and then betrayed, a man who sold a free person and consumed his price, and a man who hired a worker and took full advantage of him but did not pay him his wages.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2224]

   And his words:

   > “Your workers are your brothers whom Allah has placed under your authority. So whoever has a brother under his authority, let him feed him from what he eats and clothe him from what he wears.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2356]

   And his words:

   > “Whoever hires a worker, let him pay him his wages.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2443]

   And his words:

   > “Give the hired worker his wages before his sweat dries.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2443]

8. **Zakāh in all its kinds is the minimum right in wealth**, directed to its specified beneficiaries:

   > “Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:60]

9. **The state has the right to impose just taxes on citizens when need calls for it**, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): “Indeed, in wealth there is a right beyond zakāh.” Al-Shāṭibī said: “If we establish an authoritative imam in need of increasing troops to meet the needs of the frontiers and protect a vast kingdom, and the treasury is empty, and the needs of the soldiers rise to what is beyond their means, then a just imam may levy upon the wealthy what he deems sufficient for them—that is, for the soldiers—in the present circumstances.”

10. **The prohibition of every transaction that harms society or is based on usury, monopoly, and fraud.** Therefore Islam forbade many things, among them: selling forbidden items; *gharar* (غرر) sales; price manipulation; monopoly; fraud; giving short measure; usury; and the legal maxim in all of this is: “There shall be no harm nor reciprocal harm.”

11. **Encouragement to spend in avenues of goodness and making mutual solidarity obligatory among citizens.** The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

    > “The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim; he does not wrong him or desert him. Whoever attends to the need of his brother, Allah will attend to his need; whoever relieves a Muslim of a difficulty, Allah will relieve him of one of the difficulties of the Day of Resurrection; and whoever covers the fault of a Muslim, Allah will cover his fault on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Oppressions, Hadith 2442; Sahih Muslim, Book of Piety and Joining Ties, Hadith 2580]

12. **Affirming the state’s responsibility to protect these foundations**, based on the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

    > “Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Administration, Hadith 893; Sahih Muslim, Book of Oaths, Hadith 1829]

## The Political System

The political outlook of Islam is summarized in the following points:

1. **The Islamic *ummah* is one.** The Islamic lands existing in the world are regarded as if they were one country, subject to one leadership, in accordance with the words of Allah the Exalted:

   > “Indeed this *ummah* of yours is one *ummah*, and I am your Lord, so worship Me.” [Qur’an, Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:92]

2. **The Islamic *ʿaqīdah* is the foundation of the state**, so that all its affairs proceed from it.

3. **Carrying the Islamic *daʿwah* (الدعوة) is the essential work of the state**, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

   > “We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥadīd 57:25]

   And His words:

   > “[And they are] those who, if We give them authority in the land, establish prayer and give zakāh and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. And to Allah belongs the outcome of [all] matters.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:41]

   And His words:

   > “You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind. You enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110]

4. **Sovereignty in the Islamic state belongs to Allah**, and the Book and the *Sunnah* are the only authoritative proofs for *sharīʿah* rulings. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “And in anything over which you disagree—its ruling is [to be referred] to Allah. [Say], ‘That is Allah, my Lord; upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn back.’” [Qur’an, Ash-Shūrā 42:10]

   And His words:

   > “Indeed, the ruling is only for Allah.” [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:40]

   And the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “I have left among you two things: you will never go astray so long as you hold fast to them: the Book of Allah and my *Sunnah*.” [Muwatta’ Imam Malik, Book of the Decree, Hadith 1655]

   It is narrated that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) asked Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān when he appointed him over Yemen: “By what will you judge, O Ḥudhayfah, if a case is presented to you?” Ḥudhayfah said: “By the Book of Allah.” He said: “And if you do not find it?” He said: “By the *Sunnah* of the Messenger of Allah.” He said: “And if you do not find it?” He said: “I shall exert my own judgment and spare no effort.” Thereupon the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) patted him on the shoulder and said: “Praise be to Allah.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Qādī, Hadith 3585; al-Mustadrak, Hadith 3585]

5. **Consultation (*shūrā*, الشورى) in the Islamic state is a right of all Muslims over the head of state**, and he is required to consult them and not act on his own opinion alone. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. So pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter. And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him].” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:159]

   And He says:

   > “And those who have responded to their Lord and established prayer and whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves, and from what We have provided them, they spend.” [Qur’an, Ash-Shūrā 42:38]

   And the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “Whoever consults others is never disappointed, and whoever seeks counsel is never misguided.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 15308]

   And he said:

   > “The one consulted is trusted.” [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Seeking Permission, Hadith 2650]

   And he said:

   > “No people ever consult one another except that they are guided to the best course for their affair.” As for the result of consultation, it is not binding—and Allah knows best.

6. **The ruler in the Islamic state is responsible before Allah and before the people.** He is the *imām* (الإمام) charged with establishing the command of Allah, and the *ummah* is required to listen to him and obey him so long as he does not command disobedience. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result.” [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:59]

   And the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah, and whoever disobeys me has disobeyed Allah; whoever obeys the commander appointed over him has obeyed me, and whoever disobeys him has disobeyed me.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Jihad, Hadith 2955; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1833]

7. **Counseling the rulers is one of the rights of Muslims and a collective obligation upon them.** This is expressed in the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Hearing and obedience are due in what one likes and dislikes, unless one is commanded with disobedience. If he is commanded with disobedience, then there is no hearing and no obedience.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Obedience, Hadith 7137; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1839]

   And his words:

   > “Religion is sincere counsel. We said: To whom? He said: To Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims, and their common people.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 55]

   And his words:

   > “If you see my *ummah* afraid to say to the oppressor, ‘You are an oppressor,’ then it has been stripped of blessings.” And his words: “The best *jihād* is a word of truth spoken to a tyrannical ruler.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Trials, Hadith 4344; Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Trials, Hadith 4012]

   And his words:

   > “There is no obedience to the created in disobedience to the Creator.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Beginning of Creation, Hadith 6697; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1840]

8. **Non-Muslim communities enjoy the public rights enjoyed by Muslims and bear the same duties as Muslims.** They have autonomy in personal status and some special matters. The legal maxim in this regard is: “They have what we have, and upon them is what is upon us,” in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Whoever harms a *dhimmī* (ذمي) has harmed me, and whoever harms me has quarreled with me on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Trials, Hadith 2834]

   And his words:

   > “Beware, whoever wrongs a person under covenant, or cheats him, or burdens him beyond his capacity, or takes something from him without his consent, I will contend for him on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Jihad, Hadith 3052]

   ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from Ibrāhīm (may Allah have mercy on them both) that a Muslim man killed a man from the People of the Covenant. When that was raised to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), he said: “I am the one most entitled to fulfill my covenant,” and then he ordered the Muslim to be killed.
