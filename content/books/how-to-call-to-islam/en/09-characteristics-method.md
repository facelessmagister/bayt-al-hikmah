# Characteristics of the Islamic Methodology

## Third Investigation: Characteristics of the Islamic Methodology

It is incumbent upon the caller, in this chapter, to clarify for the one being called the characteristics by which the Islamic *manhaj* (منهج) is distinguished from all positive (man-made) methodologies. For these characteristics are radical dividing lines and essential differences between it and the other trends and schools of thought.

Among these characteristics are the following:

### First: Its Divine Origin

The Islamic *manhaj* is a Divine method; it is not a human product like the positive schools of thought. This attribute confirms its suitability for life and its ability to embrace its constantly renewed, evolving problems in perpetual harmony with the "Power" from which it issued, and from which "not the weight of an atom escapes," and which "has encompassed all things in knowledge." Then is He who creates like one who does not create? So will you not be reminded?

> Then is He who creates like one who does not create? So will you not be reminded? [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:17]

And the human origin of positive methodologies is the first seed of their demise, and the point from which originate and multiply all the defects and faults of those methodologies.

### Second: Its Comprehensiveness

The Islamic *manhaj* is distinguished by its comprehensiveness, totality, and all-embracing scope, for its outlook and conception encompass every affair of life. In it is the regulation of the individual's relationship with himself, his relationship with his Creator, his relationship with his family, his relationship with his society, and society's relationship with him. And in it is an exposition of the principles and rules that govern the conduct of society and people in accordance with Islam's view of the universe, humanity, and life.

The greatness of this comprehensiveness lies in the fact that it is harmonious and consistent in its particulars and totality—first with itself, and then with the fundamental creedal foundation from which it emanates.

One may find in the modern age positive systems that possess some comprehensiveness—but not complete comprehensiveness. Yet even this comprehensiveness is artificial, neither homogeneous nor consistent. For it is a mixture of outlooks, theories, systems, and judgments of various kinds gathered together by a recent, contingent accident, not naturally issuing from a single creedal concept.

The greatness of this comprehensiveness also lies in the fact that it is a comprehensiveness whose particulars are distributed with the true balance, achieving equilibrium among the different bodily and psychological needs and demands, such that no enlargement can occur in one aspect at the expense of another. Thus it is a natural distribution that realizes harmony, consistency, tranquility, and stability in the life of the human being.

### Third: It Is a Revolutionary Method That Rejects Patchwork and Partial Reform

Among the characteristics of this *manhaj* also is that it is a revolutionary method which rejects patchwork and partial reform.

It refuses to be one source of legislation in any country, and it is not satisfied except that it be the sole source of legislation.

It refuses that any part of its legislative details be implemented, and it is not satisfied except that all of its legislation be implemented.

It refuses to stipulate merely that the religion of the state be Islam, and it refuses merely to state that Islam is the system of this country and its method in life.

For the nature of the Islamic *manhaj* rejects fragmentation and is not satisfied with anything less than totality in its acceptance or abandonment:

> Then do you believe in part of the Scripture and disbelieve in part? Then what is the recompense for those who do that among you except disgrace in worldly life; and on the Day of Resurrection they will be sent back to the severest of punishment. And Allah is not unaware of what you do. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:85]

### Fourth: Its Continuity

Among the characteristics of the Islamic *manhaj* is its suitability and ability to embrace life, lead it, and direct it at every stage of its stages.

It may happen that a positive system succeeds for a specific period of time and within certain limits of success; then it soon falters, fails, and its defects and faults become apparent. As for the Islamic *manhaj*, it contains the elements of continuity and the qualities of flexibility that make it suitable to lead humanity in the various ages and epochs.

### Fifth: Its Universality

Among the characteristics of the Islamic *manhaj* is that it is a universal method, in the sense that in its legislation lies the ability to embrace life and its constantly renewed problems in every place, and consequently the ability to organize life in any part of the earth.

The Islamic *manhaj* is not the product of a particular environment, nor a reaction to the bad conditions of a particular environment, as is the case with all human positive methodologies. Rather, it is the method intended to be the method of all humanity in its various homelands and regions. Its Divine source established in its principles and foundations the breadth of horizons and the totality of comprehension, making it universal, capable of life, fertility, and creativity in every place.

Through these characteristics by which the Islamic *manhaj* is distinguished from all other positive methodologies, it becomes the only method qualified to solve humanity's problems and save it from the contradiction in which it lives, and consequently to save humanity from the deep decline into which it is plummeting:

> And it was not [possible] for this Qur'an to be produced by other than Allah, but [it is] a confirmation of what was before it and a detailed explanation of the [former] Scripture, about which there is no doubt, from the Lord of the worlds. [Qur'an, Yunus 10:37]
