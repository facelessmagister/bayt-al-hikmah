Introduction

The Department of Islamic Culture of Al-Azhar University requested me
to contribute in an academic undertaking to write easily readable books or
pamphlets which, when translated into English, should introduce Islam and
its teachings to Europe and America, educating the Muslims who reside
there and attracting the non-Muslims toward Islam.

Certainly, this assignment to produce books and pamphlets has both a
lofty aim and a broad scope. It should have been initiated long ago, since,
many Muslims in Europe and America have very little knowledge of Islam,
and even that little is not safe from distortion and confusion. Recently a
friend from Al-Azhar who was sent to the United States of America wrote to
us, saying, "Many Muslims in this state run bars and earn their living
through the sale of liquor, not even knowing that this is a major sin in
Islam." He adds, "Muslim men marry Christian, Jewish and sometimes
mushrik women, passing over the Muslim women, who remain unmarried
and have to fend for themselves."

If this is the condition of the Muslims, what about the non-Muslims?
People in the West have a very distorted and ugly picture of Islam, its
Prophet (pbuh) and its followers, a picture painted by Christian missionaries
and the representatives of the imperialist powers. They have used all means
of propaganda at their disposal to malign Islam and to prejudice the minds
of people against it, while we, at the same time have been heedless and
neglectful of combating this malicious propaganda.

It is indeed high time to launch an educational project in order to remedy
this situation, to inform people about the truth of Islam, and to invite them to
it. We welcome this blessed step, commending these devoted people of
Al-Azhar; we request them to redouble their efforts in this direction, and
pray to Allah ever to bless and help them.

The Department of Islamic Culture assigned me the task of writing on
the subject of the lawful (halal) and the prohibited (haram) in Islam,
requesting that I present the subject in a simple and easily understandable
manner which was also to include a comparison with other religions and
cultures.

At first glance the subject of the lawful and the prohibited may seem
easy to write about, but it is, in fact, extremely difficult. No previous author,
whether early or modern, has compiled a book specifically treating this
topic. The researcher will find material pertaining to this subject scattered
throughout various chapters in the books on Islamic Jurisprudence (Figh),
and between the lines in the commentaries of the Qur'an and in the books of
the Prophet's hadiths.

Such a subject, moreover, compels the writer to be precise concerning
many matters about which earlier scholars differed and over which
contemporary scholars are confused. Consequently, to prefer one opinion to
another in matters relating to the halal and the Aaram in Islam requires
patience, thoroughness in research, and intellectual exertion on the part of
the researcher, all this after he has cleared his heart of all desires except that
of pleasing Allah (Glorified and Exalted be He) and to seek the truth.

It is my observation that most contemporary researchers and writers on
Islam fall under one of two groups. The vision of one group has been
blurred by the glamor of Western civilization. Overawed by this great idol,
they worship it, approach it imploringly, and stand before it humbly, with
downcast eyes, accepting Western principles and customs as unassailable
and proven beyond doubt. Accordingly, if one aspect of Islam is agreeable
they accept it while if another opposes them, they try to find similarities and
agreements, offer excuses and apologies, or resort to far-fetched
explanations and distortions, as if Islam had no choice except to surrender to
the philosophy and customs of Western civilization. When we examine their
views, we find that they permit things which Islam has prohibited, such as.
Statues, lotteries, interest, privacy with a non-mahram woman, a man’s
wearing gold and silk, and so on. They frown upon things which Islam has
permitted, such as divorce and polygamy, as if, in their view, whatever is
legal in the West is halal and whatever is illegal is haram. They forget that

The other group is frozen in its fixed opinions concerning questions
about the halal and the haram, following a statement in a text which they

one of them were to be asked his opinion concerning music, singing, chess,
women's education, a woman's showing her face and hands, and similar
matters, the most likely answer would be haram. This group has forgotten
the caution exercised by our righteous forebears in such matters, who never
applied the word haram to anything unless they knew it to be definitely

I have endeavored to follow neither. I cannot compromise my religion by
taking the West as my god after accepting Allah as the Lord, Islam as the
religion, and Muhammad (pbuh) as the Messenger. At the same time, I
cannot compromise my intellect by following one particular school of
jurisprudence in all its judgements, right or wrong, suspending my own
reasoning faculty and discernment. "A blind follower,” in the words of Ibn
al-Jawzi, "does not have any trust in what he follows. To imitate is to
suspend the use of the intellect, whereas the intellect is created for thinking

This is why I have not confined myself to any one of the several schools
of jurisprudence (madhahib, singular madhhab) prevalent in the Islamic
world, for the truth is not the monopoly of any one's school. The leading
scholars of these schools never claimed that they were infallible; they were,
in fact, researchers who sought to know the truth. If they erred in a ruling
they would have a reward, and if they were correct, their reward would be
twice as great.

Imam Malik said, "The word of any person other than the Prophet (pbuh)
is Sometimes acceptable and sometimes objectionable." And Imam Shafi'i
commented, "My opinion is correct with the possibility of its being
erroneous. An opinion different from mine could be erroneous and could be
correct.”

While it is not worthy of a Muslim scholar who is capable of comparing
and choosing to tie himself to a single school of jurisprudence or to submit
to the opinion of a particular jurist; he must give weight to arguments and
proofs. The jurist with stronger arguments and a valid proof deserves to be
followed, while the one with weaker arguments and stale proofs should be
rejected, regardless of whomever he is. On this point, Imam “Ali said,
"Truth is not to be learned on the basis of authority. Learn the truth and then
you will know who the truthful ones are."

I have tried, to the best of my ability, to follow the guidelines of the
Department of Islamic Culture. Thus I have presented material supported by
arguments, proofs, and comparisons, employing the latest scientific ideas
and contemporary ideas. Praise be to Allah, Islam is like the radiant sun,

‘Definitions

Al-Halal (The lawful):

That which is permitted, with respect to which no
restriction exists, and the doing of which the
Law-Giver, Allah, has allowed.

Al-Haram (The prohibited):
That which the Law-Giver has absolutely prohibited;
anyone who commits it is liable to incur the
punishment of Allah in the Hereafter as well as a
legal punishment in this world.

Al-Makruh (The detestable):

That which is disapproved by the Law-Giver but not
very strongly. The makruh is less in degree than the
haram, and the one who commits it is not punished
as he is punishes for a haram act, except when he
does it in excess and in a manner which leads an
individual toward what is haram.
