# Chapter 6: The Correct Beginning in Islamic Upbringing

## After Intellectual Faith and the Duty of the Hour, the Focus Is the Heart

The correct beginning in Islamic upbringing, after intellectual faith (*īmān*) and after the duty of the hour, is to focus on the heart, and to realize how grave it is to fail in rectifying it.

The starting point in Islamic upbringing is *īmān* (إيمان, faith). It is reported in more than one narration from the Companions — may Allah be pleased with them — that they said: "We used to be given *īmān* before the Qur'an." We spoke about the secret behind that in the book *Jund Allāh Thaqāfatan wa-Akhlāqan*. Here we shall say briefly: the Qur'an has its own characteristics, and among its characteristics is that a human being does not receive any share from it unless he is a believer; it does not touch the hearts unless those hearts are believing. Therefore Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And whenever a sūrah is revealed, there are among the hypocrites those who say, 'Which of you has this increased in faith?' As for those who believed, it has increased them in faith, while they are rejoicing. But as for those whose hearts is disease, it has [only] added defilement to their defilement, and they die while they are disbelievers." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:124-125]


Notice that the sūrah has an opposite effect upon those whose hearts contain disease: instead of increasing their *īmān*, it becomes a factor that increases their disease. For this reason, if we want the Qur'an to touch the human heart in a correct manner so that this heart benefits from the Qur'an, we must first treat this heart by making it a believer with pure faith.

Therefore, the first point on which the educator (*murbī*) should concentrate from the very start is the rectification of the heart. Failure in this matter indicates either ignorance on the part of the educator, lack of sincerity on the part of the seeker (*murīd*), or that the method (*manhaj*) is fundamentally wrong.

The correct starting point is to focus on the heart until it reaches health, because this kind of journeying (*al-sayr*) depends upon the state of the human being and upon his exit from the circle of Satan's temptation, whispering, and trial — whether that Satan be a satan among the jinn or among mankind. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And thus We have made for every prophet an enemy — devils from mankind and jinn, inspiring to one another decorative speech in delusion. But if your Lord had willed, they would not have done it, so leave them and that which they invent. And [it is] so the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter will incline toward it and that they will be satisfied with it and that they will commit that which they are committing." [Qur'an, Al-An'am 6:112-113]
+

Notice what the last verse indicates: the one who inclines his heart toward the whispering of Satan — both the satans of mankind and the jinn — is the one who is tempted by it. Therefore, if we want to remove a human being from the circle of the satans' whisperings, we must begin with the heart and its rectification.

## The Meaning of the Heart and the Sincere Impulse

When we say "the heart," we do not mean only the mind (*fikr*). Rather, among the things by which the heart is rectified are knowledge, thought, recognition combined with remembrance (*dhikr*) and conduct (*sulūk*), and other matters which we have seen in the life of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and his Companions.

You will observe a clear phenomenon: you will find a Companion at the beginning of his Islam in a state of extreme impetus toward action. Indeed, the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — would often intervene to bring some Companions back within the circle of moderation. And this is a state you find in everyone who is sincere with Allah: when a person turns to Allah sincerely after a life of *jāhiliyyah*, or after accepting a sound understanding of the religion of Allah — Exalted is He — you find him coming to Allah eagerly, impelled upon the road to Him. Therefore the educator, during this stage of sincere impetus, must direct his efforts toward transferring the human heart from disease to health; for if we fail in that, we expose this person either to interruption of his journey to Allah, or to abandoning the call of Allah, or to deviation from the command of Allah. In short, we expose him to accepting the satans' suggestions. And how dangerous that is!

## The Meaning of Sūrat Al-Ḥajj

To clarify this station it is necessary to understand these verses. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And We did not send before you any messenger or prophet except that when he spoke [or recited], Satan threw into it [some misunderstanding]. But Allah abolishes that which Satan throws in; then Allah makes precise His verses. And Allah is Knowing and Wise. [He makes it but] a trial for those within whose hearts is disease and those hard of heart. And indeed, the wrongdoers are in extreme dissension. And so those who were given knowledge may know that it is the truth from your Lord and [therefore] believe in it, and their hearts humbly submit to it. And indeed is Allah the Guide of those who have believed to a straight path." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:52-54]

Notice in these verses His words — Exalted is He: "[He makes it but] a trial for those within whose hearts is disease and those hard of heart." If there is disease in a person's heart, or if his heart is hard, it is this person who is tempted by Satan's casting. Thus, if we want to protect a person from Satan's trial, we must transfer his heart from its disease to its health, and from its hardness to its humility.

Then notice in these verses His words — Exalted is He:

> "And so those who were given knowledge may know that it is the truth from your Lord and [therefore] believe in it, and their hearts humbly submit to it."

You find in this text that knowledge is the path to the soundness and rectification of the heart. The people of knowledge are the ones who emerge from Satan's castings with greater humility, higher certainty, and more delicate modesty.

This confirms what we have already mentioned: one of the two pillars of the journey to Allah is knowledge; and whoever does not acknowledge this is wrong and in error.

We have hastened over these two observations concerning the verses in order to reach the purpose for which we cited the verses; yet the verses require a broader pause. Let us try to present it, for these are among the verses most debated as to their meaning. In these few lines, by Allah's grace, we shall present a summary on a matter that a person can scarcely grasp without effort.

Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And We did not send before you any messenger or prophet except that when he spoke [or recited], Satan threw into it [some misunderstanding]."

What is the wish of the messenger or prophet? The wish of the messenger or prophet is for his people and followers, and that they be raised to the station of complete servitude, that is, to the station of great *ṣiddīqiyyah*. This is the wish of every messenger and prophet — peace and blessings be upon them all. So what does Satan do in such a situation? He tries to cut off the wish of the messenger and the prophet by casting his evil suggestions into the hearts of those in whom the messenger's wish resides. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "except that when he spoke [or recited], Satan threw into it [some misunderstanding]."

That is, into the hearts of those in whom his wish and the wish for his people and followers reside — and this is what the context indicates. When Satan casts his suggestions, it is from the sunnah of Allah — Exalted is He — that Allah abolishes what Satan casts and makes His verses precise in the hearts in accordance with knowledge and wisdom. Allah — Exalted is He — clarified this sunnah of His in the following two verses. He said:

> "[He makes it but] a trial for those within whose hearts is disease" — that is, the hypocrites — "and those hard of heart" — that is, the polytheists, or those diseased with hardness of heart. Even if there is no polytheism, these and those are the ones who accept Satan's castings and are thereby tempted.

Then Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "And indeed, the wrongdoers are in extreme dissension."

This verse indicates that those with diseased hearts and those with hard hearts are wrongdoers, and are in far dissension from the truth. These are the ones who accept Satan's castings. Then Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "And so those who were given knowledge may know that it is the truth from your Lord and [therefore] believe in it, and their hearts humbly submit to it."

That is, Satan's castings into the hearts of the people of knowledge result only in increase of *īmān* in the Qur'an, increase of humility before the Qur'an, and reassurance. Then Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "And indeed is Allah the Guide of those who have believed to a straight path."

That is, in understanding and conduct.

From these verses we know that when the human heart accepts the truth it is impelled within it; then a counter-assault from Satan comes upon it. This assault either causes a person to fall, or to rise because of it. Those with diseased and hard hearts fall; the people of knowledge and the people of sound hearts succeed. The educator who does not perceive these matters, anticipate them, observe them, and know how to act before them is a failed educator.

## The Hadith of the Spots on the Heart

If we understand the meaning of these verses, we can understand the meaning of the hadith reported by Imām Muslim:

> "Temptations are presented to the hearts as a reed mat is woven, straw by straw. Any heart that accepts them is marked with a black spot, and any heart that rejects them is marked with a white spot, until the hearts become of two kinds: a white heart like a smooth stone which no temptation harms so long as the heavens and the earth endure, and a black, weathered heart like a vessel turned upside down, which neither recognizes good nor rejects evil, except what it absorbs of its own caprice." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 106]

Temptations are presented to the hearts continuously. Which heart is it that rejects these temptations and does not accept them? The verses have pointed us to this kind of heart: it is the heart that is safe from disease and not hard; for the diseased heart and the hard heart both accept Satan's castings.

From this we clearly understand that the correct starting point in Islamic upbringing is to focus on the heart until it reaches a state of health, and that every failure in this is a failure in producing the Muslim who is sound, steadfast in the command of Allah, and constant in his religion.

## The Danger of Failure in Rectifying the Heart

Failure in rectifying the human heart produces diseased models of human beings. Indeed, it may produce a kind of extremists (*ghulāt*) that cannot be borne, like the *Khawārij*. In the authentic hadith:

> "There will arise at the end of time a people, young in years but short in judgment, who will speak the best words of creation; they will recite the Qur'an, but it will not pass beyond their throats; they will pass out of Islam as an arrow passes through its game; so wherever you meet them, kill them; for in killing them there is a reward with Allah on the Day of Resurrection." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 68; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 107]

Notice that the *īmān* of this kind of people "does not pass beyond their throats," that is, it has not reached their hearts.

Failure in rectifying hearts produces kinds of sinners, hypocrites, liars, and apostates. Where there is no sound heart, there is no share in this world or the Hereafter. The Qur'an will not serve as a reminder, for the Qur'an requires a sound heart. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "Indeed, in that is a reminder for whoever has a heart or gives ear while he is a witness." [Qur'an, Qāf 50:37]

Where there is no sound heart there is no salvation with Allah, and no admonition is of benefit. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And among them are those who listen to you, until when they depart from you, they say to those who were given knowledge, 'What has he said just now?' Those are the ones of whom Allah has sealed over their hearts and who have followed their [own] desires." [Qur'an, Muhammad 47:16]

Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "The Day when there will not benefit [anyone] wealth or children, but only one who comes to Allah with a sound heart." [Qur'an, Ash-Shu'arā' 26:88-89]

## Continuous Effort Toward the Sound Heart

There must be continuous effort in ourselves to reach the sound heart, and continuous effort with every Muslim — indeed with every human being — to reach the sound heart. We must focus, from the very beginning, on everyone who turns to Allah, in order to bring him to the sound heart. That is the correct beginning.

A human being is in one of two conditions: either he directs his heart with all his conduct, or his heart is directed by many things. When the heart is dim in light, weak in *īmān* or certainty, diseased, or hard, in all these states it is directed by things that overpower it; you find it yielding before the soul's lusts, surrendering to its diseases. Arrogance directs his heart and then his self; envy directs his heart away from his self — and likewise for each of his other diseases. Sexual desire dominates his heart, so he surrenders to it; the desire of the belly dominates him, so he surrenders to it; the attractions of this worldly life dominate him, so he surrendes to them; and the suggestions of the satans — satans of mankind and jinn — dominate him, direct him, make him submit to them, and tempt him. His actual decisions become diseased and influenced by all of these meanings.

All of this is part of what follows from the failure to rectify the heart. But if the heart is rectified, it becomes the director: on the one hand it is freed from the satans' suggestions; then it refuses to surrender to the soul's lusts; and at the same time it becomes the director of the human being's conduct in light of the Sharīʿah of Allah — Exalted is He. The difference between the two states is very great: the state in which the heart is the director, and the state in which the heart is directed. Hence the saying: "Ask your heart, even if the people give you fatwas and the muftis give you fatwas."

Therefore we say: the first thing the educator is eager to do is to transfer the human heart to its highest horizons of *īmān* and light. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "So is one whose breast Allah has expanded to [accept] Islam and he is upon a light from his Lord [like one whose heart rejects it]? Then woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of Allah. Those are in manifest error." [Qur'an, Az-Zumar 39:22]

## The Many Litanies at the Beginning of the Journey

From this we understand the importance of the many, varied litanies (*awrād*) for a person at the beginning of his journey; the importance of his immersion in *adh-kār* (remembrances); the importance of retreats (*iʿtikāfāt*) and spiritual seclusions filled with devotion, piety, remembrance, knowledge, and the like. For some reason the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — used to worship during the odd nights in the cave of Ḥirā', and the revelation came to him there. For some reason Allah made an appointment with Moses — peace be upon him — for forty nights upon the mountain. Since the messengers — peace and blessings be upon them — and the purest of Allah's creation in natural disposition, with the most delicate hearts, used to travel in such a path, what then of the rest of creation?

If the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and his Companions were charged with standing in prayer for nearly a year, except a little, that was only because the upbringing of that great generation required it. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "O you who wraps himself [in clothing], arise [to pray] the night, except a little — half of it, or subtract from it a little, or add to it, and recite the Qur'an with measured recitation. Indeed, We will cast upon you a heavy word." [Qur'an, Al-Muzzammil 73:1-5]

Notice the connection between His words — Exalted is He — "Indeed, We will cast upon you a heavy word," and the command to stand in prayer at night.

## The Sufis and the Correct Beginning

The correct beginning in Islamic upbringing is to focus on the heart. Because the Sufis (*ṣūfiyyah*) were the first to begin with this matter, you find them the most successful people in educating the human being who is steadfast in the command of Allah. Whether or not they do so, the Prophetic Sunnah and divine revelation have pointed us to this starting point.

When you begin with a seeker of Allah, saying to him: "Brother, the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — said: 'Whoever persists in seeking forgiveness, Allah will make for him from every anxiety a relief, and from every constriction a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect.'" You ask this brother to persist in seeking forgiveness for days, long or short according to the need of his heart. Let no one think that the matter requires hundreds; rather, it requires thousands and tens of thousands, until the meaning and reality of seeking forgiveness settle in the heart, and until seeking forgiveness becomes a disposition (*khuluq*) of the person, performing its lasting role in polishing the heart.

Ibn Kathīr said: "Ibn Jarīr, al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasā'ī, and Ibn Mājah reported through various chains… from Abū Hurayrah from the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: 'When a servant commits a sin, a black spot is placed upon his heart; if he repents from it, his heart is polished. If he increases in sin, it increases.' That is the words of Allah — Exalted is He —

> 'No! Rather, the stain has covered their hearts of that which they were earning.'" [Qur'an, Al-Mutaffifīn 83:14]

Al-Nasā'ī's wording is: "When a servant commits a sin, a black spot is struck upon his heart; if he desists, seeks forgiveness, and repents, his heart is polished; if he returns, it increases upon his heart, and that is the *rān* which Allah — Exalted is He — spoke of: 'No! Rather, the stain has covered their hearts of that which they were earning.'"

So when a person busies himself with seeking forgiveness until its effects appear upon him, turn the brother's attention to turning toward sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — for it is an excellent path to the illumined heart. The noble hadith says:

> "Whoever sends one prayer upon me, Allah sends ten prayers upon him." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Blessings, Hadith 384; Abu Dawud, Book of Prayer, Hadith 936]

When Allah sends prayers upon us, He brings us out of darknesses into light. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "It is He who confers blessing upon you, and His angels [ask Him to do so], that He may bring you out from darknesses into the light." [Qur'an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:43]

Ask him to persist in sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — for long days, and to repeat it tens of thousands of times, until it bears fruit in rectifying and illuminating the heart. The matter has no limit except the appearance of the effects.

When those effects appear in the illumination of the wayfarer (*sālik*), turn his attention to the noble hadith reported by Aḥmad, al-Nasā'ī, and Ibn Mājah:

> "Renew your *īmān*." They said: "O Messenger of Allah, how do we renew our *īmān*?" He said: "Say frequently: 'There is no deity except Allah.'" [Ahmad, Musnad; Al-Nasā'ī, Book of Purification, Hadith 49; Ibn Mājah, Book of Supplication, Hadith 3814]

The brother then begins the remembrance of "There is no deity except Allah" for long days and tens of thousands of times, until his heart becomes purely, fully, and completely unitarian and illumined.

Then turn the brother's attention to immersion in reciting the Qur'an and contemplation of its meanings. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers." [Qur'an, Yūnus 10:57]

Notice His words — Exalted is He: "and healing for what is in the breasts." He completes the many litanies with contemplation and reflection; and throughout all of this he accustoms himself to a constant litany (*wird*), like the litany of supplication mentioned by al-Ustādh al-Bannā at the end of the *Māthūrāt*: one hundred times seeking forgiveness, one hundred times prayer upon the Messenger of Allah, one hundred times "There is no deity except Allah," and *Qul Huwa Allāhu Aḥad* three times; along with persisting in reciting whatever is easy of the Qur'an — a *juz* per day is considered a moderate litany. Add to this some standing in prayer at night, observing the prescribed prayers in congregation, performing the *rawātib* prayers, and the *ḍuḥā* prayer. If knowledge gathers for the brother along with all of this, we hope that he will reach the sound heart, by Allah's permission. Then he must arrange his litanies so that his heart takes its medicine and necessary nourishment, and his heart remains at a high level of *īmān*.

## The Need for the Most Advanced to Educate Beginners

It is appropriate here to say that those who are strongest in state (*ḥāl*) and most righteous should take charge of educating the beginner, for a luminous beginning is what leads to a luminous end. Ibn ʿAṭā' said: "Whoever does not have a luminous beginning will not have a luminous end." We have not restricted the *adh-kār* we mentioned in the first stage to a specific number for a reason: the heart-diseases differ, and the need of each one differs from the need of another. A heart whose thirst is great is not satisfied by a little, while another heart may be transferred from one state to another by a little remembrance. Specifying a number in a matter for which there is no text is a matter of much give-and-take among the people of knowledge. Al-Ustādh al-Bannā recorded the difference of opinion on this matter and did not give preference to any view. Therefore we prefer to leave this to the perspicacity (*firāsah*) of the educator and his view of the need of the sound person, and to the feeling of the wayfarer himself. Some of them hold that seventy thousand of each kind of unrestricted remembrance is sufficient in the beginning stage to transfer a person from one state to another, especially in the three *adh-kār* we mentioned: seeking forgiveness, prayer upon the Prophet, and "There is no deity except Allah."

## The Supreme Aspiration and the Remembrance of the Divine Name

Some of those occupied with *tasawwuf* and some writers about it hold that for the high aspiration toward knowledge of Allah, the remembrance of the Single Name is necessary — that is, the utterance of the Divine Name (*Allāh*). They consider that for the heart to become acquainted with Allah, His attributes, and His names, in a manner in which the heart is not absent from Allah, it must have the remembrance of the Single Name. They mention arguments for this, and they consider the remembrance of this Name to be like the complete medicine for the heart. According to these people, the remembrance of the utterance of the Divine Name (*Allāh*) in a disclosed manner is the path of the heart's tasted acquaintance with Allah. Then after that the seeker begins to feel the meaning of his prayer and his litanies. This is a topic we shall address later; here we mention it only so that this subject may knock upon us from one angle, and in order to mention here that knowledge of Allah is not dependent upon this particular [practice]. The high *īmān* and the illumined heart can be reached by a person through this remembrance and through other paths, although this remembrance has swift practical effects by experience.

## Recapitulation: Intellectual Knowledge, the Duty of the Hour, and the Heart

Since we have focused on the point that the correct beginning is to focus on the heart, and so that no one understands us incorrectly, we say: the first obligation upon a human being — as the people of *tawḥīd* mention, with disagreement among them on some details — is intellectual recognition of Allah. Then after that, among the ranks of obligations, comes the duty of the hour (*wājib al-waqt*). This does not contradict what we have said: intellectual recognition, then the obligations of the hour, then the rectification of the heart and its soundness — for all of this is heart-journeying in origin.

We must understand the meaning of the duty of the hour; it is a precise meaning that escapes many people, and this is an allusion to it: When a person enters Islam, the first thing obligatory upon him is to seek the duty of the hour and establish it. A person may enter Islam at the time of *ḍuḥā*, for example, and at that moment the duty of the hour in his case may be *jihād*, so he must strive. He may be in debt and *jihād* may be an individual obligation in his case, so the duty of the hour becomes the matter of debt and the command of *jihād*. He may enter at the time of *ẓuhr*, so the duty of the hour in his case is to learn purification and how to perform the prayer, especially the *ẓuhr* prayer. If the time is Ramaḍān, the duty of the hour in his case is added to that: abstaining from the things that break the fast for the rest of his day. If he is on the verge of committing a sin, the duty of the hour is added to that: leaving the sin. If his father comes to him at that time and asks him for permissible things, it becomes among the duties of his hour to fulfill them. If at the same time he is engaged in some money-earning occupation, the duty of his hour is to know the Sharīʿah ruling of this occupation and adhere to what Allah — Exalted is He — has obliged him.

Thus we find that the matter of the duty of the hour is among the very important matters, and rarely does anyone pay attention to it. You observe in the hadiths of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — preference given to *jihād* over other things, or to remembrance over other things, or to prayer over other things, or to *ḥajj* over *jihād*. The reason for that, as the people of knowledge say, returns to a group of states: a state in which something is the duty of the hour for a person, so that thing becomes the best for him; or a state in which something for a person is the most urgent obligation; or a state in which something is a condition of acceptance, or a condition for realizing sincerity in something else. These are precise matters that only a wise jurist perceives.

There are other situations in which the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — delayed the prayer past its time because of *jihād*, as happened on the Day of the Trench; and he said to his Companions on one occasion: "None of you should pray the ʿAṣr prayer except at Banū Qurayẓah." From this last hadith you observe how the duty of haste in the *jihād* movement was the duty of the hour because of which the prayer was delayed. It is a topic we may research elsewhere. We have alluded to it here only so that it is not imagined — while we are speaking of the correct beginning in Islamic upbringing being the focus on the heart — that we are heedless of the first obligations.

## Errors in This Chapter

Through what has passed we have understood a group of errors that people fall into in the subjects of this chapter. Among them is neglect of intellectual recognition of Allah; among them is error in knowing the duty of the hour, especially in some matters that are considered in our age among the most dangerous matters — such as the duty of working to establish Islamic rule, restoring Islamic unity, and the Caliphate. These are duties of the age; yet you find among many Muslims — we seek refuge with Allah — those who act in the opposite direction: fighting those who work for that, and befriending those who work night and day to corrupt wealth and honor and to annihilate Islam. The error in all of this is due to neglect of heart-education, and we have seen all of that in this chapter.
