# Chapter 11: The Journey to Allah From Its Beginning to Its End

## The Issue of Treating the Diseases of the Human Soul as Part of Spiritual Struggle, and the Types of Wayfarers

The journey to Allah usually begins with the stirring of resolve—that is, the turning of the will toward Allah. Let us suppose that this stirring of resolve, this turning of the will, is accompanied by finding a perfect guide. What, then, does the perfect guide—that is, the perfect prophetic inheritor—do with those who desire to turn toward Allah?

The simple answer is that the guide directs every person according to what befits his condition. If a person has a high readiness, he admits him to the path of prophetic inheritance, or near to it. If he is of a lower level, he deals with him according to his capacity. Thus we automatically find circles: the first is the circle of the wayfarers on the path of prophetic inheritance, and the last is the layer of beginners and attendants, those in between, and those orbiting in the circle of the shaykh. All of this is regulated by controlling factors; the perspicacity of the shaykh is of great importance. This is one picture.

But the perfect guide may not exist. What, then, does the one who desires to turn toward Allah do? All of this we would like to travel through together in this chapter. Later chapters will take up the issue of the shaykh and the perfect guide, and there we shall see the gravity of this matter, the ugliness of the errors committed in it, and the abundance of those who claim such a station. Indeed, if we were to say that errors in this subject are the cause of all the errors of *tasawwuf* (تصوف), we would not be exaggerating much. So let us postpone discussion of this subject to that place, and speak here assuming only two conditions: the first, the existence of the perfect guide—that is, the perfect prophetic inheritor, that is, the *walī* (ولي) guide in Qur’anic terminology; and the second, his non-existence.

If a person comes to a perfect guide, he is supposed to have a readiness to obey in what is good—and I emphasize the words "obedience in what is good," for anything other than that is not permissible. Accordingly, the *walī* guide shows him what he should do in a manner appropriate to his condition. Normally, he commands him with action and *dhikr* (ذكر): action appropriate to his condition, and *dhikr* appropriate to his condition, measure, and time. Through action and *dhikr*, in the atmosphere of exhortation and circles of *dhikr*, and through mutual reminder, the signs of sincerity appear upon him, the marks of acceptance become evident in him, and he sees the extent of his readiness for a journey that is subtler and higher. At this stage it is necessary to alert him to the conditions of repentance, to abundant seeking of forgiveness, and to freeing himself from the rights of people by the appropriate means.

In this stage one must also understand the issue of the Islamic rank and the obligation of liberating one’s loyalty for its sake, for without that one does not smell the fragrance of faith. What the shaykhs observe is that whoever comes to them, whoever he may be, they accept him, in the hope that if he lives in the atmosphere of faith he will pass from one stage to another. This is the first stage of the journey, and it is like tilling the earth and sowing it for the wayfarer.

Some of them expressed this stage with the words: "If a new brother comes to the people, they accept him whether he is dirty or clean, warn him against riding conceit, command him to adhere to obedience, and settle in him the conditions of repentance." It was also narrated that a highwayman came to them and said, "O people, do you accept me?"—for it had become incumbent upon them as a duty. They commanded him to seize the rope of Allah and His covenant altogether, to face the *qiblah* (قبلة), and to practice ritual purification, and they commanded him to keep company until his inner state became upright. Thus this stage ends with the appearance of marks of righteousness in the *murīd* (مريد), and the second stage begins. The *murīd* is then required to practice organized *mujāhadah* (مجاهدة) against himself: becoming accustomed to silence according to wisdom, purposeful moderate hunger, nurturing solitude, and wakefulness full of good, as has passed before with us. This is like tending the sown earth. Here the *murīd* begins—as a result of *mujāhadah*, devotional litanies, and action—to see the traits of his soul and its diseases. Then the shaykh begins alerting him to that. This stage is like pulling out harmful weeds from the earth and leaving the wholesome plants in it, or like pruning trees and ridding them of their thorns and unformed branches. For man it is refinement and pruning; this is the third stage.

How few are the knowers in this action who know health from sickness, who know what should be pruned and what should be kept in this stage! Rather, how many there are who uproot the wholesome and keep the foul. We shall return to this subject. In explaining these two stages of the journey, some of them said:

> For every novice there are works such as silence, fasting, and wakefulness, according to the difference of states, for the sake of what is in them of spiritual gain; for the *murīd* has no fixed limits. When he reaches his limits, he is returned to the devotional litanies and treated with the works, but they refer him back to action. Then comes ecstasy, then expansiveness, then states that are hoped for, until when their knowledge of him becomes firm and they see acceptance in him manifestly, they cast upon him the traits of the soul. It may be one disease or ninety-one, or it was said seventy.

In all these stages, indeed in every stage, the general journey continues, *mujāhadah* remains in effect with variation in intensity, and *adh-kār* (أذكار), *aw-rād* (أوراد), and actions remain required. Thus Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said: "Were it not for the fields of souls, the journey of wayfarers would not be realized. There is no distance between you and Him except that your journey traverses it, and no separation between you and Him except that your connection effaces it."

Then comes the fourth stage: the appearance of the fruits of the seeds—the seed of innate disposition, the seed of learning, the seed of *tawḥīd* (توحيد), and the seed of knowledge of Allah, Mighty and Majestic. The more complete one’s knowledge of Allah is, the more hopeful the fruit. Therefore, in this stage the focus is on two things: deepening experiential knowledge, and causing the fruits of *tawḥīd* to appear in a person’s conduct. "*Tasawwuf* is character; so whoever surpasses you in character has surpassed you in *tasawwuf*." In the field of knowledge of Allah, they emphasize reaching annihilation in acts, attributes, and essence. In the field of the fruits of that, they emphasize being characterized by the names of Allah along with complete servitude to Allah. In these stations errors occur, deviations occur, and ecstatic utterances occur.

The secret of the journey is for man to reach the station of the subtlest interaction with the Creator and with creation at the same time, in accordance with the Sharīʿah. If, in addition to all this, he possesses knowledge of the Book and the *Sunnah* (سنة), purification and education of the soul, and every science that a Muslim needs for himself and for others, among many other things, then this person deserves to cross the rank of guidance. The wayfarers after that are of levels: among them are those who qualify to reach the degree of a lieutenant, being like the intermediary between the shaykh and the rest of the *murīdīn* (مريدين); among them are those whose task is receiving instruction and carrying it out; and among them are those who remain among the masses as wayfarers, with little formal commitment but abundant love. Each has his place in the journey.

Let us suppose that the perfect guide does not exist—which is the prevalent case in our age. In that case, the etiquette of the wayfarer to Allah is insistence upon action, abundance in praying for peace upon the Messenger of Allah, taking from whoever one can take something, good comportment with all who step forward to give guidance, testing everything one hears by the light of knowledge and *fiqh* (فقه), and not binding oneself to a specific person by giving him a pledge of obedience except after knowing the conditions of the pledge recognized among the *ṣūfiyyah* (صوفية). In the end, by Allah’s permission, he will reach every good, and he will not be deceived by the claims of the ignorant *ṣūfiyyah*, each one of whom claims that if he does not tread the path of character under the hand of their shaykh, they do not know Allah and do not reach Him. This is compounded ignorance. For the great knowers of Allah, such as Shaykh al-Rifāʿī, may Allah have mercy on him, say: "The end of the scholars and the *ṣūfiyyah* is one; what the *ṣūfiyyah* reach through abundant worship with little knowledge, the ordinary people reach through abundant knowledge with little action." Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says: "Your arrival at Allah is your arrival at knowledge of Him; otherwise, our Lord is too exalted for anything to be joined to Him or for Him to be joined to anything. Your nearness to Him is for you to be a witness to His nearness."

In all these matters there are errors, mistakes, confusions, and false paths. Through presenting error and correctness, we shall come—by Allah’s permission—to understand the subject of the journey to Allah in its correct form.

1. The name *murīd* among the *ṣūfiyyah* is taken from the word of Allah, the Exalted:

> And keep yourself patient with those who call upon their Lord morning and afternoon, seeking His face. And let not your eyes pass beyond them, desiring the adornment of the worldly life, and do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his desire and whose affair is ever in neglect. [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:28]

So the will is for Him, and the *murīd* is the *murīd* of Allah. The sign of the *murīd* of Allah is that he worships Allah morning and evening—that is, at all times. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was commanded to adhere to these people, to be patient with them, and not to let his eyes look toward others out of desire for the adornment of the worldly life. He was commanded not to obey one who is heedless of Allah’s revelation, and not to obey those who follow their desires and those who walk behind them. We have seen shaykhs who regard the *murīdīn* as slaves to them, and who deepen the meaning of will for the shaykh without alerting their students to the essence of will and its true meaning. We have seen some of them, the higher they rise above their students, the more they humble themselves before the people of the world. We have seen some of them obeying the disbelievers against believing Muslims, drawing near to the disbelievers by waging war against the people of Islam. All of that is among the greatest calamities in Islamic education.

2. There is no journey to Allah except by withdrawing loyalty from the people of disbelief, hypocrisy, and transgression, and giving it to the people of faith and the Islamic rank. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Hold fast to the congregation of the Muslims and their imām." [1]

Allah the Exalted says:

> The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your brothers. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

We have seen shaykhs whose only concern is that their *murīd* be loyal to disbelief and its people so long as he is committed to them. Indeed, we have seen shaykhs who, if a *murīd* gave his loyalty to those working for Islam, abandoned him and even expelled him; and if he gave his loyalty to other than Islamic orientations, they remained silent toward him, indeed encouraged him in that.

3. There is no journey to Allah without knowledge and *dhikr*. We have seen shaykhs who do not give the *murīd* knowledge or *dhikr* throughout his life, but rather attach him to their persons, as if that alone were Islam.

4. In the subject of *mujāhadah* one either finds negligence or excess. Either there is an extreme *mujāhadah* not in accordance with the *Sunnah*, or the soul is given its whim, to the point that we have seen among those who claim journeying to Allah such transgressions that the earth groans because of them. We seek refuge in Allah from the evils of our own souls and our bad deeds.

5. In the subject of shaykhs and guidance, how many claims, how many errors, and how much blind fanaticism there are! This is a subject we shall treat in detail. Every shaykh who demands from his *murīd* absolute obedience, while he is not fit to be called a sound mind or heart in a bygone age—how then in an age in which no one is fit to step forward for guidance except one who has gathered of knowledge, education, and awareness what embraces the age and its people? And how can that be except... [2]

6. In the subject of treating diseases, how rarely this is discussed! Rather, how rare are those who perceive the roots of diseases; rather, how many there are who consider health to be sickness and sickness to be health, and how rare are those who focus on the diseases of the age and the diseases of those journeying. Sometimes the aspiration for *jihād* (جهاد) becomes a sickness in some of them, and working for the word of Allah to be supreme becomes something filthy requiring ritual bathing like that of major ritual impurity. May Allah fight ignorance.

7. In the subject of the journey to Allah in general, how abundant is ignorance, how abundant is error! The essence of the journey to Allah is as follows: the transfer of the soul from a lower state to a higher state, and sound knowledge of Allah. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says:

> Were it not for the fields of souls, the journey of wayfarers would not be realized. Your arrival at Allah is your arrival at knowledge of Him; otherwise, our Lord is too exalted for anything to be joined to Him or for Him to be joined to anything. Your nearness to Him is for you to be a witness to His nearness.

Many people consider disbelief to be a journey to Allah; imaginary notions overcome them, and how many they are.

8. Alongside the journey to Allah, many people have a conceit by which they despise all the people, from ascetics to commoners. Along with it comes pretension, pomposity, and a desire to philosophize matters, which reminds us of the words of Muʿādh, may Allah be pleased with him:

> There will come after me trials in which wealth will be abundant and the Qur’an will be opened, so that the believer, the hypocrite, the man, the woman, the slave, the free person, the young, and the old will take it. Then one who says, "What is the matter with the people that they do not follow me, though I have read the Qur’an?" And nothing will turn the people away from him except that he invents for them other than it. So beware of newly invented matters, for every newly invented matter is misguidance. [3]

Knowledge of Allah gives rise to a particular morality and a particular commitment, all of which is detailed in the *Sunnah*. How often this morality and this commitment are missing!

These are the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, who spent their lives in *jihād* until they were buried in every land and under every sky. Yet among many of those who claim this path, thinking of *jihād* becomes recklessness, and the morality of the Companions is well known—yet among many of those one does not find most of the concerns of the Companions; indeed, those concerns are nearly nonexistent among them.

10. How often do the most ignorant of people step forward to the stations of guiding creation! This is a station in which it is not correct for one to step forward unless he has inherited from the Messenger of Allah knowledge, practice, and spiritual state.

11. The journey to Allah requires acquisition and seeking, and it is accompanied by matters and requires etiquettes. In each one of these we find great calamities among many of those connected to these affairs. So let the following chapter be about the aids of the journey, its rectifiers, and the errors therein.
