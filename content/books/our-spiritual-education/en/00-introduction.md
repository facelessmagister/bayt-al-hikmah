# Introduction to the Series

## Preface to the Series on Spiritual Education, Purification, and *Sulūk*

This series, entitled *"In Spiritual Education, Purification, and Sulūk"* (في التربية والتزكية والسلوك), consists of three books, in the following order:

1. *Our Spiritual Education* (*Tarbiyyatunā al-Rūḥiyyah* — تربيتنا الروحية), which is this book.
2. *Al-Mustakhlas fī Tazkiyat al-Nufūs* (*The Extract Concerning the Purification of Souls*).
3. *Mudhakkirāt fī Manāzil al-Ṣiddīqīn wa-l-Rabbāniyyīn* (*Notes on the Stations of the Truthful and Godly*).

Several things prompted me to write this series.

### 1. The Need of the Contemporary Islamic Movement

The contemporary Islamic movement — represented in the generality of Muslims and its *duʿāt* (دعاة, callers) — needs a clear theory concerning *tasawwuf* (تصوف, Sufism) and the spiritual path (*al-sayr al-rūḥī*) together and as one. A clear understanding of *tasawwuf* protects one from drifting into either its extremist current or its hostile current without insight. As for spiritual journeying, it is indispensable for the sons of the Islamic movement; hence, deliberation concerning it is like deliberation concerning matters of organization and planning.

### 2. The Scarcity of a Sound Sufi Book

Sound Sufi literature, composed in light of the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah and their jurisprudential schools, is rare. Indeed, I used to feel embarrassed to recommend a book on *tasawwuf* to anyone, because many books of *tasawwuf* contain what the common scholar would not be at ease with: unregulated expressions, imbalanced ecstatic utterances (*shataḥāt*), or exaggeration of one matter at the expense of another. Therefore, there had to be a book that puts things in their proper places, serving as a standard by which a Muslim could set out to read books of *tasawwuf* with insight, knowing what to accept and what to reject in light of sound criteria that the hearts of the fair-minded would find restful.

### 3. Making the Matter General, Not Exclusive

Many who wrote in this field made it a specialty, whereas it is a general concern required of every human being, connected with issues demanded of every person — such as the soundness of the heart, the purification of the soul, and other matters all of which are binding obligations upon the generality of creation. Therefore, there had to be books that present the matter in its proper, universal scope.

### 4. Historical Confusion

In its historical course, this science became entangled with more things than any other science — things that made it like riddles, that sometimes made it appear to be something other than science and other than the sacred texts, that sometimes made it independent of the sciences of *tawḥīd*, *fiqh*, and *uṣūl al-fiqh*, and that sometimes even made it appear to possess the force of revelation in legislation or declaration. All of this is strange and astonishing in a science that ought to be like the rest of the Islamic sciences: refined and purified.

It is strange that the reader of books of *tasawwuf* usually feels that there are mysteries behind religion. Instead of this science being a path to realizing the texts, it has become something *behind* the texts — and that is what wounds the very liver of the jurist (*faqīh*). Therefore, I never felt comfortable — except rarely — directing someone to a book of *tasawwuf* unless that person were himself a jurist, possessing the jurist's circumspection in weighing matters as he reads, and unless what he read conformed to the texts.

Since it is my nature not to speak in a way that would hurt the feelings of a Muslim in an issue that has more than one aspect, I do not like to engage in fault-finding, criticism, and refutation. Perhaps the ugliest aspect of the matter is that you find many pretenders coming to a verse of Allah that has only one correct meaning and trying to give it other interpretations, piling mountains of issues and questions upon such efforts — when the entire matter is no more than whim or distortion. All of that could have been avoided by simply standing at the texts, striving to understand them, making them understood, and journeying to realize them. Had that been done, it would have been good — nay, excellent — and that is what we intend to achieve in this series, and what we attempted, together with others, in the series *Al-Usus fī al-Minhāj*.

### 5. The Inadequate Islamic Conception Among Many Practitioners

Most of those who occupy themselves with this science have a deficient Islamic conception and narrow concepts; they are often far removed from their own age and from self-evident matters of Islam that ought not to be absent from any contemporary Muslim. If this science remains confined to such people, then those who seek to journey to Allah will remain in an unhealthy atmosphere. Hence, the pure Islamic movement had to liberate this subject, just as it liberated other topics that constitute the ABCs of understanding Islam and contemporary work for its sake.

And because ages passed in which ignorant *tasawwuf* and ignorant *ṣūfiyyah* played a role in neglecting *jihād* (جهاد), the time has come for *tasawwuf* to return to its natural place: in the service of the cause of *jihād* — as has often happened when Sufi education gave rise to *jihādī* action. Let us not forget the revolt of Shaykh Saʿīd al-Kurdī al-Naqshbandī in Turkey, the revolt of Shaykh Shāmil al-Naqshbandī in Turkistan, the movement of ʿĀmir Khān in India — a result of the efforts of Shaykh Aḥmad al-Fārūq al-Sirhindī, the Sanūsī movement in Libya, and the Darwish movement in Sudan.

These meanings and many others were motives for the composition of this series.

## The Meaning of the Complete Spiritual Path

Every Muslim, in truth, is a traveler to Allah so long as he does what Allah — Exalted is He — has commanded him. He has his share of the stations of journeying by virtue of that. But *searching for the complete path*, reaching it, entering the houses by their doors, knowing the sources and supplies, the beginnings and ends, the limits and restrictions of all its lower and higher stations — this is what is termed "the complete spiritual path" (*al-sayr al-kāmil*). From this we realize the error of those who do not conceive of any journey to Allah except through *tasawwuf* and traveling in it; and we realize the error of those who reject the very existence of a path of *tasawwuf* and journeying within it — a point we mentioned in the book *Jawlat* in reply to those who deny the existence of the science of *tasawwuf*. Here we want to reply to the extremists among the *ṣūfiyyah* who do not conceive of a journey to Allah except by the hands of the people of the *ṭarīqah* (path). For the Companions — may Allah be pleased with them — and those who came after them, until the foundations of the science of *tasawwuf* were established, had nothing but the study of the Book and the Sunnah and the application of that. If that is not journeying, then what is journeying?

From these simple points, the reader can grasp some features of this series. Let this suffice in this introduction.

## A Subject That Will Stir Controversy

There is no doubt that writing on this subject will stir many for whom *tasawwuf* has become the very foundation and apex of matters. And there is no doubt that many causes led these people to such conclusions. With these causes present, and with such people present, this series was written, and I consider its composition a duty (*farīḍah*). We live in a materialistic age, and this requires us to confront it with equivalent thought and with high spiritual activity. We live in a hedonistic, *jāhiliyyah* age, and this requires us to confront it with elevated spiritual longing, while securing lawful desires and keeping their outlets open. We live in an age in which scarcely anyone restrains himself according to the refined Islamic etiquette (*adab*), and this requires us to insist upon refined psychological education. If all of this is the path of sound, healthy *tasawwuf*, then writing about it has become necessary.

Furthermore, the modern Islamic movement — being a movement that is supposed to be both renewed (*tajdīdiyyah*) and grounded in its original roots — has, as one of its essential characteristics, the *Sufi reality* (*ḥaqīqah ṣūfiyyah*). This was stated by its greatest founder, al-Ustādh Ḥasan al-Bannā — may Allah have mercy on him — and one of the most important reasons for writing in this subject is to renew it: restoring it to its sound roots and pure sources, removing from it the great dross that has attached to it, and putting things in their proper places in this science and others.

If the followers of this science have sensitivities that make them reject any discussion of an expression of their people or a deed of theirs, and if the deniers of it have sensitivities that make them reject its name, its people, its subject, or any speech about it — then the renewers (*mujaddidūn*) of this *ummah* cannot confront all of this except with the word of truthful justice that puts things in their proper places. That alone is what reforms and benefits the *ummah*.

If the scholar does not do that, he has not discharged the trust of knowledge in his generation. For ninety percent of the Islamic *ummah*, over many centuries, were connected to *tasawwuf* and its people in one form or another: either through engagement with it, reliance upon its people, connection with them, trust in them, or nominal affiliation to those they relied upon. And *tasawwuf* and its people, to this very day, are the ones who reach environments and regions that no one else reaches. If that is so, then this fact alone is sufficient to give great justification for writing on this subject, purifying it, refining it, and putting things in their proper places within it.

It is not enough to mention the error in something; rather, you must show the correct view in it. It is not enough to demolish; rather, you must build. And you must always present the sound alternative to what is being replaced, especially when what you are dealing with is something indispensable, something that cannot be neglected or ignored. There must be a sound formulation as an alternative to the weak or feeble foundation, and it is necessary to clarify the truth in every matter. Among the most important of such matters are the discussions of the science of *tasawwuf*, the actions of its people, and their statements. This is a sufficient justification for writing on this subject — although the matter is broader than that, and the necessities for writing on it are far greater than one might suppose. The heart, the spirit, the soul, the mind, and the body are many great things, all of which require clarification from those working in the call to Allah. If they do not fulfill this duty of sound clarification, error will retain its dominion over souls through false explanation, and exploiters of the higher aspirations of hearts and spirits will retain their dominion over their hearers — without the hearer having a correct standard, or sound knowledge through which he may know what he hears and what he does not, what he should accept and what he should reject, what requires rejection and what permits acceptance, what is appropriate to what is presented to him, and what he is called to in the law of Allah…

## The Issue of the Name "Tasawwuf"

I believe that most of the denial directed at this series will concern the *name* itself. There are people who cannot bear to hear the words *tasawwuf* and *ṣūfiyyah*. To these I say: first of all, this is a matter of history between you and me. No one has denied the name *tasawwuf* over the centuries, because it is a technical term for a science, just as are grammar (*naḥw*), rhetorical embellishment (*badīʿ*), meanings (*maʿānī*), *fiqh*, and so on. There is no quarrel over terminology, as the scholars say. Even in our own time, the *fatāwā* of Ibn Taymiyyah were published in two volumes under the title *Al-Taṣawwuf wa-l-Akhlāq*, and I do not know anyone who denies that. I hope, therefore, for deliberation before denying an issue in which there is no basis for denial in the first place. What is the justification for denying a permissible name given to a noble science until it became a science in its own right?

If people pass beyond this point — and they should pass beyond it — then the *content* is what should be the subject of discussion. Let the goal be reaching the truth in the content, instead of quarreling over a side issue from which no benefit follows.

It is necessary, in this series, to proceed in the study of the truth according to the Book, the Sunnah, and the madhāhib of the people of truth, because we believe that this alone is what ought to be and what the name should ultimately return to. Journeying to Allah cannot be abolished; rather, it must be invigorated. But it must be liberated, refined, and its issues precisely articulated. Neither the *ṣūfiyyah* nor others are infallible; the only infallible sources are the Book and the Sunnah.

It was reported that one of the greatest Sufi scholars of his age, Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī — may Allah have mercy on him — said:

> "When a subtle point from the speech of the people falls into my heart, I do not accept it except with the testimony of a just witness from the Book and the Sunnah; for Allah — Exalted is He — has guaranteed my protection in the Book and the Sunnah, and He does not guarantee it for me in anything besides that."

From this we recognize the error of the *ṣūfī* who wants to make every word uttered by a *ṣūfī* infallible, or who wants to give books of *tasawwuf* the same infallibility as the Book and the Sunnah. People such as these are no different from the extremists among the Jews and Christians concerning whom Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "They have taken their scholars and monks as lords besides Allah, and [also] the Messiah, the son of Mary." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:31]

If our view of people such as these is thus, then our view of those who reject the very foundation of the science of *tasawwuf* and what it contains, finding only error in it, is that these are people who have deviated from the correct opinion in this matter. They confront one error with another, and act with an emotional, irrational, unmeasured reaction.

## The Goal of the Series

In this series we have tried to place the reader's foot on a sound and error-free journey to Allah, and we have tried to pave the way for the existence of a class of complete heirs to the Messenger of Allah — carrying the call of Allah in its entirety, educating people outwardly and inwardly upon the truth. If we are correct, to Allah belongs all glory; if we err, then we seek Allah's forgiveness, and we are prepared, if the proof is established against an error of ours, to retract it openly. For the truth alone is what we are eager for, and eager to speak according to. In the word of Allah — Exalted is He —

> "And We will regard what they have done of deeds and make them as dust dispersed." [Qur'an, Al-Furqān 25:23]

there is a lesson for us and for others that prevents us from swerving from the truth out of fear of any created being.

We want to emphasize that if, in this series, we have tried to clarify the essence of a refined Sufi path, and in doing so we have supported a deviation, corrected an error, or affirmed a truth, we have not introduced anything novel into the matter. For the scholars throughout the ages have continually affirmed the journey to Allah, supported it, attacked false or innovating or ignorant *tasawwuf*, and the *ṣūfiyyah* themselves have continually highlighted the positive aspects of this science and addressed errors in its application.

Let us give two examples, one from the scholars and one from the *ṣūfiyyah*:

### Example A: From the Scholars

In the introduction to his book *Kifāyat al-Akhyār*, a work of Shāfiʿī *fiqh*, the author says:

> "Know that seekers of knowledge differ according to their objectives, and their objectives differ according to their ranks. Some require deep diving into the ocean and the like to obtain the great pearls, while some are content with a very brief summary. Then this contented group is of two kinds: one burdened by family and overcome by concern for livelihood, and another who turns to Allah — Exalted is He — with sincerity and earnestness, so the former is unable to keep company [with scholars], while the seeker (*sālik*) is occupied with his own struggle, devoting his night and day to himself in distress. Therefore I wanted…"

Notice his words: "while the *sālik* is occupied with his own struggle, devoting his night and day to himself in distress." Here is speech about *sālikūn* turning to Allah — Exalted is He. And in another passage of his book he criticizes the *ṣūfiyyah*. From all this we recognize the adab of the scholars: *sulūk* (spiritual wayfaring) toward Allah is required, and the aspects of error among the *ṣūfiyyah* are addressed by them as people of Allah.

### Example B: From the Ṣūfiyyah

In the poem *Al-Mabāḥith al-Uṣūliyyah* by Ibn al-Bannā al-Sarqisṭī, a poem well-known among the *ṣūfiyyah*, he says — after mentioning the path for the sake of the paths, so understand his guidance and follow his speech —

> "This is a path that has been sown; it was once running streams…" [Qur'an, Ya-Sin 36:12 reference to running streams, or the like]

Then he says:

> "O seeker of the ancient path, none of them is upon the [true] objective. They have not known the reality of the path, and the tree… They have replaced it with contemptible doctrines, and those who travel it today are a perishing party whose rising is of no benefit. The people are ignorant of the truth and have left a path trodden by slips."

Thus what we are doing is the ongoing practice of both the scholars and the *ṣūfiyyah* together throughout the ages. We say this, seeking refuge with Allah from the scheming of the schemers, and from the enmity of the envious, and from every harm. The standard is precise verification, and Allah is the One whose help is sought.

Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is better [for you] and best in result." [Qur'an, An-Nisāʾ 4:59]

And the breast is open to every truthful word, whether spoken by a *ṣūfī* or a *salafī*, without sensitivity from anyone. It is not fitting for a seeker of knowledge to be anything but a lover of truth, searching for it; when truth becomes clear to him, he embraces it. As for anything else, that is the concern of the people of caprice (*ahl al-ahwāʾ*).

## Two Aspects of Tasawwuf

*Tasawwuf* has two aspects: a practical aspect and a theoretical aspect. The practical aspect includes what agrees with the Sunnah and what contradicts it. The theoretical aspect includes matters of unveilings (*kashf*) and intimations (*ilhāmāt*), and also what is a condition of the path of realizing creeds and the morals of the soul. Most of the battles waged around *tasawwuf* revolve only because of innovated practices, and because of unveilings and intimations. We will try, Allah — Exalted is He — willing, to put things in their proper places in many of these matters in this series.

We have two duties concerning *tasawwuf*:

1. To guide the human being to the sound path to Allah — Exalted is He.
2. To liberate *tasawwuf* from its dross, so that the reader will have immunity against falling into the captivity of an ignorant person or ignorance.

All of this is for the sake of reaching a refined and realistic spiritual education (*tarbiyah rūḥiyyah*). This is what we have tried to do — though I know this will plunge me into conflicts with various parties: some *ṣūfī*, some *salafī*, and some with particular sensitivities regarding these matters.

Some *ṣūfiyyah* will say that this work does not smell of the *Sufi taste* (*dhawq ṣūfī*), that I do not know their technical terms, and that one has no right to speak about something one does not know. Some enemies of *tasawwuf* will say that this book serves deviant *ṣūfī* groups because those who read it will be captivated by its cover, and the outcome will be that they go to a *shaykh* from the *ṣūfiyyah* who is not truly grounded in the way I mentioned, who raises them upon falsehood, leads them astray from their religion, or stops them short of duties that are at the very pinnacle of Allah's obligations in this age.

I am not heedless of life, of people's varying conditions, and of the many reasons that would make one hesitate to speak much about these matters. More than once I reached the conviction that it was necessary; more than once I reached the conviction that I should not do it, and that the series *Al-Usus fī al-Minhāj* sufficed. But in the end, Allah opened my breast to speak, and to Allah belongs all help; and there is no longer in life any room to make reckoning for the creation. I do not claim to say for this *ummah* everything that ought to be said.

In summary, I say to the categories of people I mentioned:

- **a.** I have studied *tasawwuf* under those I consider the greatest scholars of *tasawwuf* in our age, the most deeply realized in it; some of the *ṣūfī* shaykhs have authorized me to guide and train seekers (*murīdīn*), on the condition that I not bind myself to a *ṭarīqah* and that I be bound in this matter only by the Book and the Sunnah. I say this so that the *ṣūfiyyah* may know that I speak — by Allah's grace — about knowledge and taste (*ʿilm wa-dhawq*), and so that others may know that nothing moves me except truth and justice.
- **b.** Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "And say, 'The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills — let him believe; and whoever wills — let him disbelieve.'" [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:29]

Our task is to clarify. And Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "Whoever is guided is only guided for [the benefit of] himself." [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:15]

I am keen that there should exist a kind of *salafī tasawwuf*, with its own shaykhs and circles of knowledge and remembrance. There is no other path before me.
- **c.** I am not keen that people abandon their shaykhs, nor that goodness be cut off. On the contrary, I am convinced that wholesome connections among people should increase, and that circles of goodness and those who work in them should multiply — but on the condition that all of that be grounded in the roots and branches of the Sharīʿah, and not at the expense of other obligations.
- **d.** It has become clear, through the experience of the contemporary Islamic movement, that when a thing's foundations are not clear, its effects are not wholesome. Some of the sons of the contemporary Islamic movement fled from Sufi education as thought and practice in a boring manner. Al-Ustādh al-Bannā, for example, in *Risālat al-Taʿālīm*, mentioned how one stage of his call had a *ṣūfī* character on one side; and in *Risālat al-Muʾtamar* he mentioned that among the characteristics of his call was the Sufi reality. In his memoirs he left the person seeking the special education free to follow whichever path he wished, and he mentioned that in the context of speaking about his position on *tasawwuf*. But what happened was that a *salafī* interpretation of the journey to Allah prevailed; and among its consequences was that many of the sons of al-Ustādh al-Bannā's call felt an emptiness and spiritual void, which led some of them to follow a *shaykh* or shaykhs who did not know the reality of the contemporary Islamic call and its necessities, so they diverted them or turned them away from duties that are at the very summit of Allah's obligations in this age.
- **e.** Finally, our age is the age of desire, the age of whim, and the age of entertainment; we must confront these things with what matches and opposes them. I say with certainty: Sufi education alone confronts that. For desire is not solved by speech alone; rather, it requires a *ḥāl* (state), an environment, and education. Entertainment is not matched by speech alone; rather, it requires feeling, taste, and spiritual sensations alongside speech. Whim is not treated by speech alone; rather, it is treated by humility before Allah, *taqwā*, *warʿ*, and *adab* — and this is the path whose name is *tasawwuf*.

When all this is clear, nothing remains but discussion: why the name *tasawwuf*? And the answer, as I said before, is: why the name grammar? why the name *badīʿ*? why the name *ṣarf*? It is merely a technical term for a science, arising as other technical terms arose and becoming established over the centuries.

## From the Beginning: Notes on This Series

From the very beginning, I would like to record — even if I repeat — several things about this series.

1. I want, in this series, to place the reader's foot on the path of the journey to Allah, so that he may taste the reality of faith. At the same time, I want the reader to understand the meaning of the Sufi reality, which is one of the characteristics of al-Ustādh al-Bannā's call — may Allah have mercy on him — and the one who presented the purest *ijtihād* in the fourteenth Hijrī century. I do not intend to encompass the subject of *tasawwuf* from beginning to end; for that is a study befitting higher academic research and specialists. I write for every human being.

2. I also want, in this series, to place the student's foot on the path of Sufi studies, so that he may read books of *tasawwuf* with a standard or a lamp in hand by which he walks and by which he weighs what he reads. Therefore, I do not consider this series anything more than a ladder for reading books of *tasawwuf*, especially the books of al-Muḥāsibī and al-Ghazālī — may Allah have mercy on them — and the *Risālat al-Qushayriyyah* of the knight scholar and mujāhid Abū al-Qāsim al-Qushayrī.

3. This series is not a substitute for companionship (*ṣuḥbah*) and consultation (*ijtimāʿ*), nor does it dispense with the guidance of knowledgeable, active, aware, and insightful shaykhs, capable of transporting a person from a lower state to a higher state in righteousness. Rather, it points to the kind of person one ought to seek to learn from, and it indicates the nature of taking from him, and it warns of the aspects of error. At the same time, it is sufficient as signposts on the path to Allah if a person cannot find such people, or as provision for the path for one who has found someone in whom his scholarly mind finds rest for taking from, and his jurist's heart feels at ease, and then, when he takes from him, he takes with insight. Indeed, if a person adheres to what is in it, I am confident that it will suffice him and be enough for his journey to Allah — and in it is his salvation with Allah, Allah willing.

I authorize every Muslim who feels in himself a sound intention to apply what is in it, and upon whom the effects of application appear, to read it and to educate others by it — especially students of knowledge graduating from a Sharīʿah college, Azhar, or trained under shaykhs.

4. I have not built this series in a vacuum, nor have I invented a science from myself. I have taken much of what I have read from the books of the *ṣūfiyyah*, and I have my own experience, and we are in an age in which much is mixed with dross for this *ummah* — as Ḥudhayfah — may Allah be pleased with him — asked the Messenger of Allah:

> "Is there any good after this evil?" He said, "Yes, but in it is dross." [Reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

I mention this because someone may say: "The author of this series has transmitted such-and-such a passage from such-and-such a book, which contains this and that, which I consider in myself to be much dross. He does this in order to discredit the series and demolish the value of this part that he transmitted." I hope that the fair-minded person will not fall into such a thing; for good is mixed with dross. We may find a book containing much dross, yet also containing much good. If that is the case, it is not right that the existence of this dross should prevent us from taking the good.

5. It is not right for anyone to hold me to every word an author said in a book as if all his words are my opinion, simply because I quoted an expression or followed the course of the author of that book in something from it.

6. I understand that the contemporary Islamic call tries to gather all inherited good, purifying it from its dross, and all good necessary for this *ummah*, provided it be without dross. Indeed, I understand that this is the first duty of the contemporary Islamic movement. Political action in the Islamic world began without controls or limits, and we want it to be a construction disciplined by Islam, free from dross, launched upon a sound foundation. The *salafī* movement set out in most countries with a vague and sometimes mistaken understanding, and with methods in which demolition is mixed with construction; and we want it to be a disciplined, clear *salafiyyah* that knows what should be demolished and what should be built. The Islamic *ummah* inherited a huge legacy of books of *tasawwuf*, and its circles represented by the *ṣūfī* orders; and within this legacy one finds much good and much dross, and we want from it a Sufi reality purified from dross. Say the same for everything. Ḥasan al-Bannā — may Allah have mercy on him — was not mistaken when he made the Sufi reality one of the characteristics of his call, presenting the purest *ijtihād* in the fourteenth century, because *tasawwuf* is an innate tendency in the human soul; therefore it must be part of any sound call.

7. There is no choice between absolute rejection of the Sufi legacy and absolute acceptance of it; therefore, there had to be a standard for taking and a standard for rejecting.

8. Without benefiting from the Sufi experience, we may be unable to treat many of the diseases of the human soul that have complicated the course of life and the nature of the age. Just as we needed the jurist's opinion to answer many everyday questions, so we need the experience of the people of experience in many intellectual, spiritual, and psychological questions — and what we wrote in the treatise *Jawlat* and in this series is enough to convince.

9. I consider the starting point for the health of our *ummah* to be the existence of a class of complete heirs covering all that the *ummah* needs. I consider this step indispensable, and any failure in it is failure in the very foundation; and there is no inheritance unless knowledge, action, and state of heart are gathered together.

I have tried much and seen much; rarely have I found completeness in the soul, or excellence in conduct, or ability in dealing with others, except where I found a pure Islamic Sufi education. That is because the keys of the human soul are in this education, its foundations and rules; for the *ṣūfiyyah* are those who inherited from the Messenger of Allah the education of the soul, its purification, specialized in that, devoted themselves to it, and discerned what others did not discern. They have established, in this field, markets of rich experience in every age. If a person does not take from them, his soul remains distant from the prophetic inclinations. The true people of *tasawwuf* are those who have mastered the science by which human souls are refined — in their relationship with Allah — Exalted is He — and in their ability to deal with people.

The Masonic movements have been accustomed to call a person who does not join the Masonic lodge a "rough stone," because he is not hewn so as to take his place in the building. What we say is: Freemasonry may hew stones, but the stones remain stones in hardness. Allah — Exalted is He — says:

> "Then your hearts became hardened after that, being like stones or even harder." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:74]

But *tasawwuf* and Sufi environments are capable of producing the human being in all his dimensions: the human being who performs the obligations of servitude to Allah, and the human being who offers the greatest giving in his dealings with others — doing all that with *adab*, mercy, gentleness, affection, love, preferring others, and kindness… But some *ṣūfiyyah* mixed the good with much dross, which harmed the general form of the structure. Our task in this age is to bring about the complete, pure Sufi education, planting pure Sufi environments, while keeping *tasawwuf* in the context of Islam, so that it does not become a refuge for laziness or an escape from *jihād*.

## On the Question: Is Not the Book and the Sunnah Sufficient?

There are people who, when arguments fail them, ask: "Is there not in the Book and the Sunnah enough to dispense with such a science?" The answer is: Yes, but this science gathers the like with the like. Moreover, not every person is capable of reading, comprehending all of it, and connecting the topics. The human being needs a clear foundation and an accessible starting point. Hence this series.

If this series is bound by the Book and the Sunnah and passes in light of that, then denial of it is wrong; for the one who denies it should deny every book ever written, since is there not in the Book and the Sunnah what suffices? This very answer contains, in fact, the secret of the origin of this science and every science. The science of *tasawwuf* came into being and became established — and we affirmed in the treatise *Jawlat* that it could not but come into being and become established — because when you read the Book and the Sunnah you find much speech about the heart, faith, taste, diseases of the hearts, and remedies for these diseases; you find speech about the deafness of the heart, its blindness, its soundness and sickness, its *taqwā* and *fisq*; about the human soul, its purification, and its wickedness; and similar meanings. It is natural that the scholars of the Muslims should record everything related to these meanings and issues in a special register, and that a special science should arise as a result, concerning everything related to the details of these meanings. This science was the science of *tasawwuf* and *sulūk*.

So it is not surprising that this science exists; what is surprising is that it would *not* exist, since the habit of the scholars of the Muslims is to write on every topic separately, classifying a thing with its like and counterpart, explaining and detailing, and answering every question related to a single topic. And so the science came into being, developed, and experienced what happens to every science: being addressed by those not qualified for it, written about by those who master it and those who do not, by the deviant and the upright. It is not strange that there should exist a science in which the wayfarers record, throughout their history, their observations and particular experiences in the subject of journeying — from heedlessness of Allah to wakefulness, from wandering to commitment, from the sickness of the soul and heart to their health. But what is strange is that it should *not* exist. If the science exists, and specialists in it exist, and takers from it exist, then its market is established. How could it not be, when it is a science needed by every Muslim?

If that is so, it is natural that schools should arise for it, that taking and rejecting should occur in it, and that many things should accompany this science and be considered its extensions or accessories. It is natural that the shortest path for one who wishes to learn, acquire, or obtain this science should be its books, taking it from its mine. In this respect it is like what is said of other sciences: the Book and the Sunnah contain the explanation of everything, and among that is what relates to this science — but does every person encompass the Book and the Sunnah, and possess the ability to gather the like with the like, to know the details of generalities, and to put things in their proper places? Are people equal in understanding, depth of reflection, and acuity of perception?

Those who turn the ordinary Muslim away from taking the sciences from their books and their people lengthen the path for him, indeed they cut him off from reaching. Just as one does not say to a student: "Trace the topic of *al-nāsikh wa-l-mansūkh* from books of *tafsīr* if you want it," nor does one say to a student: "Trace the occasions of revelation (*asbāb al-nuzūl*) from the lengthy books of *tafsīr*, though they exist there"; rather, one says to him: "Read *Al-Nāsikh wa-l-Mansūkh* by so-and-so, and *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* by so-and-so." So it is with this science and every science: that is the shortest path to acquiring the science and becoming acquainted with it.

And if a science must exist, then it must also be liberated and refined. How much more so when this science has experienced what the science of *tasawwuf* has experienced: the theoretical science (*ʿilm al-taṣawwuf*) proceeding in one valley, and practical *tasawwuf* in another? By "the science of *tasawwuf*" here we mean the general, refined *tasawwuf* in light of the Book and the Sunnah, approved by the firmly rooted scholars of the science. When all this is clear, our excuse for writing this series becomes strong.

If we have prolonged the apology for writing this series, and prolonged the explanation of the necessities that compelled us to write it, it is because many of our brothers whom we love and who love us, and who hope for us and for themselves that we remain aloof from the scholarly battles constantly raging among the Muslims today, so that we may be an instrument of gathering all upon good and a common denominator for all in the battle of Islam — we are as eager for that as they are. But the right of our own education does not exempt us from knocking on these topics, and the process of purification (*tazkiyah*) always comes first.

## Scope and Method of the Series

I have omitted, in this series, discussion of many matters that I consider, from both the theoretical and practical standpoints, to provide only negligible, exceptional benefit; because I believe that such matters are found by a person in any book, and reading about them in these books would not cause harm. Therefore I have spared myself reference to many topics, eager that this series remain very concise, neither boring its reader nor causing him to lose the essential core amid details. It is my nature that I do not like to write on a matter except where I find a necessity for it, and to the extent that necessity requires. Here the matter is thus.

If a reader finds that I have not followed, in this composition, the usual path of authors who begin by mentioning the name [of the science], the reason for its naming, and other things they consider pillars of authorship in any science, that is because I believe that this is available in many books. What I am eager for is more than merely adding this series as another book in a science for which there is some justification. Rather, my task is to exert effort to the extent of my ability in what revives the sunnah of the Prophets — upon them be peace and blessings. I say this apologizing for any shortcoming for which a reader of this series may take me to task if he does not find in it some things he would like. Yet I do not think I have neglected any core matter that ought to be known. Nor will it be difficult for the reader to reach for the *Risālat al-Qushayriyyah* of Abū al-Qāsim al-Qushayrī, or the book *Qawāʿid al-Taṣawwuf* by Shaykh Aḥmad al-Zarrūq, to find an answer to any topic I have omitted or wished to expand upon. I would be happy if these two books were printed together with a brief commentary by a *ṣūfī* jurist.

Finally, I say: writing on the subject of the journey to Allah is a necessity demanded by multiple necessities. For this human being has what is called the soul, what is called the mind, what is called the heart, and what is called the spirit — and each and every one of these meanings comprises strange and wonderful worlds, some of whose features are not disclosed to the human being except through the journey to Allah — Exalted is He. Hence the journey to Allah — Exalted is He — is necessary for the human being, so that he may know himself and what is hidden within him. Therefore, the human being who does not journey to Allah does not know much about the horizons of the soul and the horizons of the self. This is the first cause impelling the human being toward the journey to Allah — Exalted is He.

The journey to Allah — Exalted is He — is the only path to the correct experiential, sensory knowledge of Allah — Exalted is He. For the human being remains ignorant of much concerning his Creator — Exalted is He — unless he journeys to Allah — Exalted is He — even if he is a believer. There is a great difference between…

taste (*dhawq*)… and this is a second cause impelling the human being to journey to Allah — Exalted is He. The human soul is sick and does not become sound except by traversing the correct path to Allah — Exalted is He. The human soul is charged with great moral obligations, does not attain success without them, and is not realized by them without the journey to Allah — Exalted is He. This is another cause impelling one toward the journey to Allah — Exalted is He.

Hence the journey to Allah — Exalted is He — is obligatory in degrees varying according to readiness. Journeying is indispensable, and the degrees of the wayfarers will be according to their readiness. Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]." [Qur'an, Ash-Shams 91:9-10]

And He said:

> "Their meat will not reach Allah, nor will their blood, but what reaches Him is piety from you." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:37]

And the Prophet — upon him be peace and blessings — said in the *ṣaḥīḥ* hadith:

> "If faith were in the Pleiades, men from among the sons of Persia would reach it." [Reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim and al-Tirmidhī]

The journey to Allah — Exalted is He — requires the conscious implementation of the rulings of Allah's commands. Therefore, one who does not know the foundations of the journey to Allah and its purpose misses much in implementing divine commands, such as His saying:

> "Read in the name of your Lord who created." [Qur'an, Al-ʿAlaq 96:1]

And His saying:

> "And remember the name of your Lord and devote yourself to Him with [complete] devotion." [Qur'an, Al-Muzzammil 73:8]

He will lack the tasting of the Islamic meanings contained in the Book and the Sunnah, such as His saying:

> "Everything will be destroyed except His Face." [Qur'an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:88]

And His saying — upon him be peace and blessings:

> "Worship Allah as if you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He sees you." [Reported by Abū Nuʿaym in this wording, and it is a *ḥasan* hadith; its meaning is in the two *ṣaḥīḥ* collections.]

The journey to Allah is necessary; writing about it is necessary; dispelling illusions about it is necessary; repelling exaggeration in it is necessary — all of which drove the writing of this series. As we said before:

> "We believe that every Muslim is a traveler to Allah so long as he does what Allah — Exalted is He — has commanded him, and he has his share of the stations of journeying by that. But searching for the complete path, reaching it, entering the houses by their doors, knowing the sources and supplies, the beginnings and ends, the limits and restrictions of all their lower and higher stations — this is what is termed the complete spiritual path."

From this we realize the error of those who do not conceive of any journey to Allah — Exalted is He — except through *tasawwuf* and journeying in it — a point we mentioned before, in reply to those who deny the existence of the science of *tasawwuf*, and in reply to the extremists among the *ṣūfiyyah* who do not conceive of a journey to Allah except by the hands of the people of the *ṭarīqah*. For the Companions — may Allah be pleased with them — and those after them, until the foundations of the science of *tasawwuf* were established, had nothing but the study of the Book and the Sunnah and the application of that. If that is not journeying, then what is journeying?

From these simple points the reader can grasp some features of this series. Let this suffice in this introduction.
