# Chapter 3: The Journey to Allah

It is likewise a part of this science. And since the relied-upon principle in this science is that sound *fiqh* (فقه) is the decisive criterion, there is no objection to a matter being relied upon when a sound, well-grounded *fatwā* permits it. As for what is otherwise, it is rejected upon its author, whoever he may be. If we wish to be brief in defining this science after having understood its dimensions, horizons, and main fields, we can say: *tasawwuf* (تصوف), in brief, is the *sayr* (سير) to Allah upon the path. So let the third chapter be on this topic; and from now on we must give action its due attention after understanding.

## What Does It Mean? What Are Its Pillars? What Is Its Starting Point?

The *sayr* (سير) to Allah means moving from an unpurified *nafs* (نفس) to a purified one, and from an *ʿaql* (عقل) not grounded in the *sharīʿah* (شريعة) to an *ʿaql* grounded in the *sharīʿah*, and from a *qalb* (قلب) that is unbelieving, hypocritical, transgressing, diseased, or hard to a *qalb* that is tranquil and sound, and from a *rūḥ* (روح) scattered away from the door of Allah, not mindful of its *ʿubūdiyyah* (عبودية) and not realizing this servitude, to a *rūḥ* knowing Allah, observant of the rights of *ʿubūdiyyah* to Him, and from a body not disciplined by the limits of the *sharīʿah* to a body disciplined by the *sharīʿah* of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and, in sum, from a *dhāt* (ذات) of lesser perfection to a *dhāt* of greater perfection in its righteousness and in its emulation of the Messenger of Allah in word, deed, and spiritual state. All of this enters into their expressions in defining the *sayr* to Allah, and in its entire course it is a journey to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.

Some of them restrict the *sayr* to Allah to a single state, namely the transition from intellectual faith to tasted faith, and from the heart’s feeling for the *afʿāl* (أفعال) of Allah to feeling for His *ṣifāt* (صفات), to spiritual immersion, or what they call the station of *fanāʾ* (فناء), then the station of *baqāʾ* (بقاء); but this is in reality one manifestation of the *sayr*, one of its parts, and a stage within it.

How numerous are the errors that accompany this topic among many people! How numerous are the fancies that affect people’s conceptions in this matter! How often the essential is mixed with the accidental, and the truth with error in this topic! Therefore, speaking about this topic is difficult and bewildering; approaching and simplifying it is a great effort. For often means become ends, beginnings become final goals, and what is merely a prelude to what follows becomes as if it were everything. Let us give an example of that.

Some consider reaching the sound, tranquil heart to be the summit of the *sayr* to Allah, and they consider that the goal of goals, and they forget many obligations. Indeed, reaching the sound heart is a goal; but the sound heart is the one that receives the commands of Allah with complete submission and contentment, and drives the body thereby according to the commands of Allah with full strength, vitality, and earnestness. Among the commands of Allah is the command to *jihād* (جهاد) and to make the word of Allah supreme. So if you see a Sufi preoccupied with the matter of the sound heart throughout his life while he is neglecting the commands of Allah to exalt His word, and heedless of many obligations of his time, considering what he is in as everything despite his neglect of many obligations—that is a great error.

To say no more than this: the difference between the one with the sound heart and others is, as it is in the essence of the heart, so in the soundness of action and the strength of holding fast to the Book of Allah and His rulings. Indeed, often the claim of *maʿrifah* (معرفة) of Allah was a factor in fleeing from scrupulousness. What kind of *maʿrifah* is this that extinguishes in a person a type of scrupulousness? This is the Messenger of Allah, the most knowing of creation of Allah; he was the most fearful of Allah among creation. Hence he said, peace and blessings be upon him: “Indeed, I am the most mindful of Allah among you, and the most fearful of Him among you.” [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim]

Speaking about the *sayr* to Allah is not easy. First, because it is difficult to delimit and define; second, because people in this matter are of different sorts, and each has his *mashrab* (مشرب) that he has grown accustomed to, and he comes to view all matters through his own special lens. This leads one to be held to account by its owner for that. And the latter is a manifestation of error in this matter.

It is strange that you find in many people the sublime principle but the contradictory practice. One of the well-known expressions of the Sufis on the tongue of each of them is: “Allah has ways to Him according to the number of created beings.” This is an expression of clear meaning, indicating that the ways of reaching Allah are very many. Yet you find many people tying reaching Allah to specific concepts; these are concepts they may not be able to establish evidence for. So how can one hang the matter of reaching Allah—which is among the most sacred religious matters—on issues whose texts are not perfectly clear? For this reason I find myself compelled to discuss the issue of the *sayr* to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, again and again, in one form after another, so that the matter becomes clear in this regard, so that the Muslim may avoid errors, and—more important than all this—so that he may take his share of the *sayr* to Allah with insight.

Many people have tied *tasawwuf* to mysteries and made it full of secrets; they exaggerated and indulged until *tasawwuf* became a label for something that cannot be understood at all. They made *tasawwuf* something particular to a class of people, while in its origin and content it is required of all people. Was any one of the Companions without his sincere *sayr* to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic? And are the Companions not the model for creation in everything? So why the claims and boastings? This is ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib, may Allah be pleased with him, to whom most Sufi paths trace themselves back in time. Most of them, when one of them asked him, “Do you have anything of revelation beyond what is in the Book of Allah?” he said: “No… nothing has aided me except a comprehension that Allah gives a man regarding the Qur’an, and what is in this scroll.” And there was in the scroll nothing but some legal rulings. Thus the matter does not go beyond a person whose condition with Allah is sound being a man of understanding regarding Allah and His Messenger. Therefore the reference point is the Book and the Sunnah. [Agreed upon]

*Tasawwuf* must be returned to its correct roots so that it may be provision for everyone; then each person has his own ceiling and understanding, and the Messenger of Allah singled out some of his Companions with knowledge, but it is not of the nature of a general obligation upon the Ummah. Moreover, the interpretation of these meanings is known, so it is not permissible for anyone to interpret them in a way that contradicts the *sharīʿah*. Ḥudhayfah, may Allah be pleased with him, was singled out by being made aware of the hypocrites, and the secret behind that is clear: that there might be in the generation of the Companions one who would place matters in their proper places if one of the hypocrites were to assume a position that could affect the Islamic community. And Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, used to say: “I learned from the Messenger of Allah two satchels; as for one of them, I have spread it among you, and as for the other, were I to mention it, this throat of mine would be cut.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Knowledge, Hadith 120] The second satchel was alluded to by Abū Hurayrah when he would say: “I seek refuge with Allah from living to see the year sixty and the rule of youths,” and it later became clear that he meant the rule of Yazīd ibn Muʿāwiyah. Thus the matter is connected to specific political events that would occur in this Ummah; were he to speak of them, he would be killed because of the effects his words would leave. If what Abū Hurayrah had was a general obligation upon the Ummah, he would have made it public. And in any case, his words are not a basis for anyone to claim that what he was specially entrusted with is a particular kind of thing that does not agree with a *sharʿī* foundation. For in that case every pretender, every enemy of Islam, every heretic, and every esotericist could claim that what he is in and what he calls to is of the likes of this satchel: this is worthless talk that does not constitute proof.

There is in Islam no outward that contradicts an inward, nor an inward that contradicts an outward; whoever claims that is a disbeliever by the consensus of Muslims.

> Say, “This is my way; I call to Allah upon insight, I and whoever follows me. And exalted is Allah; and I am not of those who associate others with Him.” [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:108]

And in another hadith: “Indeed, I have left you upon clear guidance, its night like its day.” [Sunan Ibn Mājah, Book of Sunnah, Hadith 43]

By the pretext of secrets, every matter acquired its symbols, and by the pretext of knowledge of the secrets of the Divine Essence, some of them cast aside the issue of *waḥdat al-wujūd* until, in the view of these people, a Sufi is not considered a knower of Allah until he believes that the universe is the very Divine Essence, and some of them hedge on this matter: if an outwardly legalistic person comes to them they interpret it one way, and if a submissive person comes they interpret it another way. We do not hold people accountable for their intentions, but for their words.

One of the Sufis said:

> What is the universe in its manifestation but like ice, while You are the water that is its source? For the ice in our representation is other than its water, but in the reality of its essence it is its water.

What does the common person understand from these two lines except that this universe is the very Divine Essence, but it has solidified and become a universe, just as water solidified and became ice? The revealed laws say: the universe is other than its Creator, while the universe is Allah in these people’s claim—or it is a part of the Divine Essence that has solidified. Another one of them expressed this topic with the following analogy: “This universe in relation to the Divine Essence is entirely like the waves of the sea: it is neither the very sea, nor other than it.” And you say: the waves of the sea are a part of the sea. We say: Explain to us the word of Allah, the Exalted:

> But they have attributed to Him from among His servants a portion. Indeed, man is clearly ungrateful. [Qur’an, Az-Zukhruf 43:15]

What is meant by this? Is not this verse explicit in its denunciation of whoever ascribes a portion to Him? Whoever ascribes a portion to Him is a disbeliever manifest in disbelief. So are the alleged secrets of *tasawwuf* such that their outcome is that we stray as previous nations strayed? We seek refuge with Allah from that.

We know that there are states for the *sālik* (سالك) in which he senses the oneness of the Divine Essence and senses in them the name of Allah, *al-Ṣamad* (الصمد), and it is a state in which the *sālik* senses the annihilation of everything; but this sensation must be accompanied by the conviction that Allah is the Creator, and that there is a created being, and that the Creator is other than the created. *Tasawwuf* is tasting the creed, not interpreting it in a way that contradicts the texts and correct understanding.

What we must not omit to say here is that there are people who interpret such speech as we have mentioned with interpretations whose outward appearance agrees with the *sharīʿah* of Allah, and we have heard some of our shaykhs carry the two lines upon a meaning acceptable in the *sharīʿah*. Such people we hold to account for their words and leave their intentions to Allah. If their words in this matter are like their belief in it, then I hope for their safety; otherwise, the texts of the Book are manifest in judgment against them.

*Tasawwuf* is a science needed by all people, and it embraces all people. Some wayfarers may delve into the understanding of certain texts, and some wayfarers may understand from the meanings of the texts correct subtleties that others do not perceive, and all of that is unobjectionable as long as it does not nullify a text, or contradict a text, or consensus. But we see much speech among certain classes of Sufis that has no parallel in the era of the Companions, nor in the era of the Followers, nor in the era of the Followers of the Followers, and it contradicts texts and contradicts consensus. Then after that they present *tasawwuf* to the Ummah as though it were this, and these proponents want the Ummah to pardon them for that. Whoever does not pardon them—woe to him from sharp tongues and disapproving hearts.

We say to you, go gently: Allah has set limits, sent down revealed laws, and revealed texts; they are the criterion between truth and falsehood, and they alone are the truth and the balance, and whatever contradicts that is error and fancies.

In light of what we have mentioned, we shall present the issue of the *sayr* to Allah, but we would like to point out that as we attempt to present *tasawwuf* properly for everyone, we must be deliberate in judgment. For many of the people of *tasawwuf* have transmitted some meanings that may have a jurisprudential, general, and *sharʿī* aspect; therefore we must be deliberate in our judgment so that our judgment on a matter is a judgment correct in the *sharīʿah*. If we are reassured that our judgment on a given issue is correct in the *sharīʿah*, and that what we judge to be error cannot be otherwise, then at that point one should not hesitate to judge. In this treatise we shall try to put many matters in their proper places so that the aspect of error or correctness related to *tasawwuf* and its people becomes clear. Let us begin with the intended point.

The two pillars of the *sayr* to Allah, without which no *sayr* is possible, are *ʿilm* (علم) and *dhikr* (ذكر). There is no *sayr* to Allah without *ʿilm*, and no *sayr* to Allah without *dhikr*. *ʿIlm* is what clarifies the path; *dhikr* is the provision of the path and the instrument of ascent. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “This world is cursed, and what is in it is cursed, except the remembrance of Allah and what is connected to it, or a scholar and one who learns.” [Sunan Ibn Mājah, Book of Zuhd, Hadith 4112]

We need *ʿilm* to know the divine commands and to know their wisdom, so we carry out the commands and realize the wisdom. And we need *dhikr* so that Allah may be with us in our *sayr* to Him. Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, said in the *ḥadīth qudsī* (حديث قدسي): “And I am with him when he remembers Me.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Supplications, Hadith 7405] We shall discuss the issue of *dhikr* and its place in the *sayr* to Allah clearly in the details to come.

So the two pillars of the *sayr* to Allah are *ʿilm* and *dhikr*, and it is impossible that there be a *sayr* without them. However, the *sālik* is of two types: one dominated by *dhikr* while taking his share of *ʿilm*, and one dominated by *ʿilm* while taking his share of *dhikr*; each of them, in the end, by the permission of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, reaches his destination. There is no doubt that by *ʿilm* here is meant *ʿilm* of the Book and the Sunnah and what the *sālik* needs in his *sayr*, and by *dhikr* is meant the transmitted *dhikr* or that which is encouraged and falls within the commands of Allah and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, in the chapter of *dhikr*.

People in general are of two kinds: one whose desire for action is great and whose ability to act is present; and one whose ability to act is limited but whose energy for worship, action, and *dhikr* is great. So this latter one’s path is to increase in *dhikr*, though he must still have *ʿilm*; and the former one’s path is *ʿilm*, though he must still have *dhikr*. Ibn al-Bannāʾ al-Saraqusṭī said: “The people in this are divided into two groups, and their judgment concerning it is of two kinds.

“The first group: their path is built upon creeds and good intention.” This requires *ʿilm* and a good orientation toward Allah. “They said: The soul is like a mirror in which the Divine is impressed as it is in itself”—that is, what is established in the origin of the *fiṭrah* (فطرة) for the spirit of knowledge of Allah, *ʿubūdiyyah* to Him, and surrender to His command, past, present, and future. “And sometimes things hinder it”—that is, things hinder the spirit from its origin of knowledge: “neglect of guarding or rust”—that is, they hinder it from its knowledge and *ʿubūdiyyah* due to heedlessness, or the rust accumulated upon it because of sins or heedlessness. Since the matter is thus, the remedy is to remove the rust by a good orientation toward Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and that is only accomplished by *dhikr*. “They said: The eye may lose its water”—that is, its water disappears, and by the eye here is meant the origin of the *fiṭrah*; “and sometimes the spring returns it”—that is, the water returns to the eye after the spring has dried up, and that is by way of *dhikr*. “And this is the path of *ishrāq* (إشراق).” That is, this kind of *sayr* to Allah is called the path of *ishrāq*. Ibn ʿAjībah said: “It is called the path of *jalāʾ* (جلاء) and *taṣfiyah* (تصفية), because it is built upon purifying the hearts and the *sarāʾir* (سرائر) by stripping them of vices and adorning them with virtues.” I say: And all of this is only accomplished by *ʿilm* and *dhikr*. “It existed and will remain as long as existence remains,” so it is a path in the *sayr* to Allah made easy, for many people, after taking their share of *ʿilm*, can immerse themselves in *dhikr* and worship.

“The second group: a group said that knowledge is acquired from outside, by effort, superior”—that is, loftier and greater. So the original basis of this path is *ʿilm*, though *dhikr* is still necessary. “And they made knowledge a condition in their terminology”—that is, in the terminology of this kind of *sayr*—“for the door has no need of its key.” *ʿIlm* is the key to reaching Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. But what kind of *ʿilm*? “So the aspirant has no hope unless he has four knowledges.” Thus these knowledges, together with *dhikr*, are a condition of arrival, and he specified them: “They are the knowledges of the *dhāt* and the *ṣifāt*”—that is, knowledge of the *dhāt* of Allah, His *ṣifāt*, and His Names; “and *fiqh* (فقه), *ḥadīth* (حديث), and states”—that is, knowledge of *fiqh* and knowledge of *ḥadīth*—that is, the Qur’an as well—and the *aḥwāl* (أحوال), *maqāmāt* (مقامات), and *manāzil* (منازل), and the deceits of souls and their stratagems, and what is needed for the course of that. “And this is the path of *burhān* (برهان).” That is, this kind of *sayr* is a journey standing upon detailed evidence in every matter. “And it is for every resolute, vigilant one.”

Thus each of the two paths requires *ʿilm* and action, and the first of action is *dhikr*, as we said before. In the path of *ʿilm*, *ʿilm* has the first rank in terms of the proportion of action, and *dhikr* has the second rank; and in the path of *dhikr*, *dhikr* has the first rank in terms of the proportion of time spent on it, and *ʿilm* has the second rank. So each of the two paths requires *ʿilm* and action. Therefore Ibn al-Bannāʾ himself said: “For the path is knowledge, then action, then gifts after it to be hoped for.” If we restrict priority in the two paths to the proportion of time given to each, that is because absolute priority in each of the two paths belongs to *ʿilm*, for *ʿilm* is the leader. That is why they said: “Whoever acts without knowledge, his action is rejected and not accepted.” *ʿIlm* is the beginning of every kind of *sulūk* (سلوك) to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. Therefore Ibn al-Bannāʾ said: “Indeed, the brother of a people is a brother of infatuation.” *Al-fatūn*: it is said in *Mukhtāṣ al-Ṣaḥāḥ* that it is *al-iftitān*—that is, if the shaykhs come to a man who is infatuated, that is, distracted from Allah and with what busies the heart away from Him, by sin or otherwise, “and he said, ‘O my people, do you accept me?’” “Accept him, truthful or lying.” That was their etiquette with Allah, the Exalted:

> And when those come to you who believe in Our verses, say, “Peace be upon you. Your Lord has decreed upon Himself mercy: that any of you who does evil out of ignorance and then repents after that and reforms—indeed, He is Forgiving and Merciful. And thus do We detail the verses; and [so] the way of the criminals will become evident.” [Qur’an, Al-Anʿām 6:54-55]

Therefore he said: “Since taking charge of him was obligatory upon them, however he might be.” Note the issue of *ʿilm* as a beginning: “And they commanded him to adhere to obedience, prayer, the *qibla* (قبلة), and the congregation; and they stipulated for him the conditions of repentance and commanded him to keep company; then they commanded him with outward *ʿilm*,” until his inner secrets became upright.

The two pillars of the path, as we said, are *ʿilm* and *dhikr*; and there must be a definition of the issue of *ʿilm* and a definition of the issue of *dhikr*. Every person is required of *ʿilm* according to his state and according to his need, and it is a matter that differs according to people, environments, and ages. There are matters required of every person, and matters required of one person but not another. A Companion, for example, did not need to study the sciences of the Arabic language, for he understood and spoke it instinctively; and he did not need a science of recitation, because he received the Qur’an from the Messenger of Allah as it was revealed and conveyed it likewise. Many doubts, innovations, kinds of disbelief, and their trappings are encountered by one generation and not another, or by one person in a place and not by another person in another place. Many matters are required of one generation and not of another. For example, a generation living under an Islamic state is not required to work to establish an Islamic state, nor the sciences necessary for that; but a generation that has lost the Islamic state—for example—and no longer lives in a land where Allah’s religion is dominant, such a generation needs to know the sciences necessary to fulfill this obligation of Allah.

The issue of *ʿilm* and *dhikr* as two pillars in the *sayr* to Allah must be understood correctly, especially in our era in which people have neglected obligations and wasted much of their energies defending issues that are not of the essence, being either from permissible matters or from *bidʿah* (بدعة). All of that does not deserve that the contemporary Muslim should stand long at it. When the issue of *ʿilm* and *dhikr* as two pillars in the *sayr* to Allah has become somewhat clear, it is time for us to enter the core of the matter more broadly. The core of the matter in the *sayr* to Allah is reaching the sound heart.

For in the *ḥadīth*: “Indeed, in the body there is a morsel; if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupted, the whole body is corrupted. Indeed, it is the heart.” By the soundness of the heart is the soundness of the *nafs*, the soundness of the body, and the soundness of the *rūḥ*; it is the starting point of uprightness. With this soundness the human being’s reception from Allah is complete, and the ability to be saved from *fitnah* (فتنة) is made available by the permission of Allah. Therefore the correct starting point for a complete Islamic life is the soundness of the heart and its rectification. The *sayr* to Allah, in its essence, is the *sayr* of the heart toward its soundness; then remaining in that state of soundness and fulfilling the rights of pure *ʿubūdiyyah* to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, until death. In this circle there are many errors into which the *sālik* to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, falls; they will become clear to us little by little. [Sahih al-Bukhari]
