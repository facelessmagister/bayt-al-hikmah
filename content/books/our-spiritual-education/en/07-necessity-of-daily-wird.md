# Chapter 7: The Necessity of the Daily Wird and Spiritual Rotations

## The Necessity of Action, Spiritual Rotations, and Daily Awrād

97 — Chapter Seven: On the necessity of the daily *wird* (ورد) and spiritual rotations. By Allah, the last chapters have made clear the necessity of some matters. So that the seeker may not keep away from action in this study — and it is, in origin, a practical study — we wished to bring out something practical after we came to know many of the theoretical foundations that help us understand these practical aspects.

In short, we call the Muslim to action, and to have in his life spiritual rotations, and to have daily *awrād* (أوراد). It is not difficult for us to recognize the necessity of that from what has passed with us. For further emphasis and clarification, we mention some meanings:

## A — Action

On this subject there is a hadith reported by al-Bazzār and al-Ṭabarānī in *al-Kabīr* with a chain whose men are the men of *ṣaḥīḥ*, from ʿĪsā ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī from his father, who said: "When a man first embraced Islam, the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — would instruct him first in prayer," or he said: "Instruct him in prayer." And in this subject there are many sound hadiths. From such a text we recognize the necessity of understanding what is obligatory upon a person. We have already seen the necessity of knowledge, and spoke of beginnings and endings and what is between them. Beginning in the comprehensive practical path, whether by studying, personal reading, receiving instruction, or attending general or special Islamic circles, is something indispensable. And some matters have their cautions that the seeker must observe. In our books there is an exposition of the cautions related to the practical path. Here we say, on the subject of action:

1. Make it before your eyes to reach a purposeful, directed, and integrated Islamic culture, so that you do not become absorbed in a matter to the point of leaving another, and do not waste what you have studied.

2. You will find many people who want to confine you to a certain form of their thinking. We shall see that the matter is not with them, so be very patient and very firm. Do not let fanaticism drive you to abandon part of the truth, and do not let love of men prevent you from reaching the Pure Truth and knowing Him in every matter.

3. Whatever you have studied, do not remain distant from the Book and the Sunnah, and strive for the correct understanding of their texts, and make the memorization of the Book and the Sunnah a share of your time and effort.

4. You will encounter many ignorant people who will dissuade you from action, or from some of its kinds, or turn you away from useful kinds of it to the neglect of other kinds, or belittle some doors of action so that you abandon them. Do not listen to them, no matter how righteous you see them to be; for righteousness is one thing, and a person's worthiness of the station of guidance in your heart is another. Hence in history there is what is called the perfect guide, whose characteristics include that he be learned in the four schools, able to give fatwā according to them — and among the qualifications that make a person fit to be given the station of guidance in one's heart is a subject we shall come to in this epistle.

If you take heed of these four points and proceed on the path of action, you will reach good, by Allah's permission.

## B — Spiritual Rotations

We call the seeker to have spiritual rotations in his life as much as he is able, to the extent that is feasible for him. If he is able to make his rotation forty days, let him do so; if three days, or seven days, or eight days, or more or less, or months, let him do so. If he is able to devote this rotation to what does not cause him to lose a right or an obligation, let him do so; otherwise, let him do what he can without neglecting his dependents, or his craft by which he earns his livelihood, or his daily duties. If he is able to connect the rotation with certain months, such as Ramaḍān, the sacred months, the first ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, or others in which texts indicate a special character, then let that be; otherwise, what is easy is best. He should organize his rotation so that its spiritual return is high. If he is able to combine fasting, standing in prayer, *qunūt* prayers, Qur'an recitation, and kinds of *adh-kār* (أذكار), then that is excellent; otherwise, whatever he can do. If he restricts himself to one kind of *dhikr* (ذكر), such as sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah, or "There is no deity except Allah," or seeking forgiveness, or glorifying, exalting, magnifying, and praising Allah, that is good; and if he combines all of these, it is better.

Indeed, such rotations elevate a person greatly and transfer his heart from one state to another. And in the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — there is much that causes us to feel at ease with this, such as his *iʿtikāf* (اعتكاف). It is established that he performed *iʿtikāf* in Ramaḍān and at other times, and in some years he performed *iʿtikāf* for twenty days. And like his seclusion in the cave of Ḥirā', and that was before prophethood, except that it was from Allah's favoring of His Messenger. And like the command at the beginning of Islam making the standing of the night obligatory upon every Muslim, then the obligation was abrogated and the recommendation remained.

There are texts indicating numbers, such as the hadith reported by Ibn Mājah and al-Tirmidhī: "Whoever prays in congregation in a mosque for forty nights, not missing the first *takbīr* of the ʿIshā' prayer, Allah writes for him a grant of freedom from the Fire." Just consider: if a seeker resolves, between himself and his soul, to perform a spiritual rotation for himself lasting forty days, or less, or more, what would result from that? There is no doubt that his faith would be strengthened, the meanings of *tawḥīd* would become firmly rooted in his heart, and this would give him clarity of thought and good reflection — apart from many other meanings, all of them necessary in an age dominated by materialism and overcome by desires. If he repeats that every period of his life, it is hoped that the light of faith in his heart will remain strong, and that faith in his heart will remain fresh.

If we wish to propose a schedule for one of these rotations, it is possible, for example, for it to contain the following:

1. Performing all the prescribed *rawātib* prayers.
2. Maintaining the *ḍuḥā* prayer, the night prayer, and the *witr* prayer.
3. It is possible for the program to include *Ṣalāt al-Tasābīḥ* (صلاة التسابيح) daily.
4. He may set for himself a program for completing the Qur'an during the rotation.
5. He should include in his plan engagement with the *awrād* of *dhikr*, from seeking forgiveness, to sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah, to *tawḥīd* (توحيد), and other unrestricted *adh-kār*, and let him try to mention each of them seventy times, for the number seventy realizes abundance.
6. He should include in his plan the application of *awrād* connected with things, such as the *awrād* of prayer, and the *awrād* of morning and evening, and so forth. If he finds boredom in himself with one kind, let him turn to another kind.
7. Fasting whatever days are feasible, while reducing food, speech, and mixing with people.

Some people may say: "This is idleness," and some say: "This is laziness," in order to turn the seeker away from such things. These people, all of them, have ruined their scales, and their faith-based thinking is diseased. A speck of faith is not weighed against anything; if a speck of faith can bring a person out of the Fire and protect him from eternal abode in it, then how much more when these rotations make a person's faith like mountains, giving him tranquility of heart, raising him above the anxieties of the soul, and protecting him from the whisperings and trial of Satan?

Every Muslim must reflect on such a matter, and the educators in the Islamic Ummah must give it special attention. It is enough for every seeker to realize the soundness of what we have mentioned by remembering these two hadiths: "Indeed, faith wears out in the heart of one of you as a garment wears out, so ask Allah to renew faith in your hearts"; and "Renew your faith." They said: "O Messenger of Allah, how do we renew our faith?" He said: "Increase in saying: 'There is no deity except Allah.'"

If faith, while it exists, needs renewal, then how much more the heedless hearts, the hard hearts, the hearts in which there is darkness, the hearts in which there are whisperings, the confused hearts, the anxious hearts, the doubtful hearts, the hearts that diseases and desires have invaded. All of these need intensive spiritual rotations with a spiritual program. The program we proposed here is only a model; if a seeker were to devote days to himself in which he busied himself, for example, with sending prayers upon the Messenger alone, while performing the obligatory duties, that would have wholesome effects upon his heart, and the same is true of the Noble Qur'an. The important thing is that a person should not despair of a spiritual rotation or rotations in his life.

## C — Daily Awrād

A seeker must have a daily spiritual nourishment. This nourishment consists in performing the obligatory and required daily duties and persevering in whatever supererogatory acts are possible, to the extent that gives the heart its needs of nourishment and medicine, and by which the Muslim remains in continuous ascent. This daily *wird* that the Muslim arranges for himself should have a minimum limit that he must not fall below; then, beyond that, if he finds free time or eagerness from the soul, he should increase. If he sees in himself laziness or boredom, he should deal with it in the best way, with wise management of the soul. If his soul overcomes him and he becomes lazy for some reason, then if he is able to make it up, let him make it up; otherwise, let him begin anew at the first moment his soul returns to him, and let him return to what he arranged.

The texts concerning daily *awrād* are numerous. Among them:

1. Shu'ayb said: "ʿAbd Allāh fell ill, so we visited him, and he began to weep. He was rebuked, and he said: 'I am not weeping out of grief, but the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — told me: "Sickness is an expiation," and I weep because it has befallen me in a state of slackening, not in a state of striving; for what is written for the servant of reward before he falls ill is taken from him when he falls ill.'" From such a text we realize that the active seeker has his own daily *awrād*. Hence we find ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd weeping that his illness came upon him while he was not in the state he wished to be in.

2. From a sound hadith of ʿĀ'ishah — may Allah be pleased with her — narrated from the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: "Take up deeds you are able to do, for Allah does not grow weary until you grow weary." And in a narration from her: "And the family of Muḥammad, when they performed a deed, they would persist in it." This indicates that there were certain deeds in the life of the family of the Messenger of Allah in which there was a kind of daily commitment. Also, his saying — peace and blessings be upon him — "Take up deeds you are able to do" points to the fact that the Muslim should arrange for himself a daily work within his capacity.

3. He — peace and blessings be upon him — said: "Indeed, my heart becomes covered over, so I seek forgiveness one hundred times in a day."

4. His — peace and blessings be upon him — persistence in standing at night and his constancy in certain works all indicate that he — peace and blessings be upon him — had his own daily *awrād*, and he is the model for every Muslim.

So the daily *awrād* in the life of a Muslim are his daily provision, which he must not neglect. Every Muslim should arrange for himself a daily *wird*. This includes organizing his times for performing the obligatory and supererogatory prayers, and we especially mention standing at night and the *ḍuḥā* prayer, because people are heedless of them; it includes the *awrād* of the prayers; and it includes reading the Qur'an. The moderate limit in that is a *juz* (جزء), because of his statement — peace and blessings be upon him — in the sound hadith of ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ concerning the Qur'an: "Recite the Qur'an in every month."

This includes daily seeking forgiveness, daily sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah, daily exalting and glorifying Allah. It includes observing the days in which we have been recommended to do a special act, so that we dedicate them to a deed, such as sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah on Friday and its night, and reading *Sūrat al-Kahf* in it. It includes observing the *awrād* and *adh-kār* connected with an occasion, and it includes observing the days on which we have been recommended to fast. And finally, it includes action, and every deed required by the right of action.

There are also *awrād* in which we have been recommended to increase without limit; these a person can arrange for himself according to the need of his heart, without causing hardship, and without conflicting with the performance of other obligations.

If we wish to present a model for the daily *awrād* of a seeker, we can say:

1. The *qunūt* prayers, the *rawātib* prayers, their *adh-kār*, standing at night, and the *ḍuḥā* prayer.
2. Daily seeking forgiveness not less than one hundred times.
3. "There is no deity except Allah, alone, without partner; to Him belongs sovereignty and to Him belongs praise, and He is over all things competent," not less than one hundred times.
4. Sending prayers upon the Messenger, not less than one hundred times.
5. Reading *Qul Huwa Allāhu Aḥad* three times.
6. Reading a *juz* of the Qur'an.
7. The *adh-kār* of times and conditions, such as the *adh-kār* of eating, sleeping, entering, and leaving.
8. Then, increasing in the unrestricted *adh-kār* we have been recommended to, such as seeking forgiveness, or sending prayers upon the Messenger of Allah, or exalting Allah's oneness, or saying *ḥawqalah* (حوقلة), or glorifying Allah, or praising Allah, or otherwise.

Here are some texts that encourage some of what we have mentioned:

From Abū Dharr al-Muẓannī, raised to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: "Indeed, my heart becomes covered over, so I seek forgiveness in a day one hundred times." And in a narration: "Repent to your Lord, for by Allah I repent to my Lord one hundred times in a day."

From Abū Hurayrah, raised to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: "Whoever says: 'There is no deity except Allah, alone, without partner; to Him belongs sovereignty and to Him belongs praise, and He is over all things competent,' one hundred times in a day, it will be for him the equivalent of freeing ten slaves, and one hundred good deeds will be written for him, and one hundred evil deeds will be erased from him, and it will be a protection for him from Satan that day until evening. And no one brought anything better than what he brought except a man who did more than him."

And: "Whoever says: 'Glorified is Allah and by His praise,' one hundred times in a day, his sins will be dropped from him even if they are like the foam of the sea."

Al-Tirmidhī reported from Abū Ṭalḥah — may Allah be pleased with him — that one day the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — came to them with good news in his face, and they said: "We see good news in your face." He said: "Indeed, an angel came to me and said: 'O Muḥammad, indeed your Lord says: Does it not please you that no one sends prayers upon you except that I send prayers upon him ten times, and no one sends blessings upon you except that I send blessings upon him ten times?'"

Al-Ṭabarānī reported in *al-Awsaṭ* and *al-Ṣaghīr* from Anas, raised to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: "Whoever sends one prayer upon me, Allah sends prayers upon him ten times; whoever sends ten prayers upon me, Allah sends prayers upon him one hundred times; and whoever sends one hundred prayers upon me, Allah writes between his eyes innocence from hypocrisy and innocence from the Fire, and Allah will settle him on the Day of Resurrection with the martyrs."

Abū Dāwūd reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, raised to the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — that he said: "Whoever persists in seeking forgiveness, Allah will make for him from every distress a way out, and from every hardship relief, and provide for him from where he does not expect."

Al-Ṭabarānī reported in *al-Kabīr* from ʿUbayd ibn Yaḥyā ibn Ḥayyān from his father from his grandfather that a man said: "O Messenger of Allah, shall I make a third of my prayer upon you?" He said: "Yes, if you wish." He said: "Two-thirds?" He said: "Yes." He said: "All of my prayer?" He said: "Then Allah will suffice you for what concerns you of the affair of your worldly life and your Hereafter."

Finally, we say: It is incumbent upon the seeker to arrange for himself a daily program, a weekly program that folds up the daily program, a monthly program that folds up the daily and weekly programs, and a yearly program that folds up all three, so that he does not forget any obligation, and his life is filled with good, and he remains in a state of continuous ascent — through spiritual rotations, through the daily program, through establishing what has been recommended to us or imposed upon us weekly, such as the rights of the day of Jumuʿah, or what has been legislated for us annually, such as the fast of Ramaḍān, or monthly or weekly, such as supererogatory fasting, or what has been imposed upon us once in a lifetime, such as *ḥajj* (حج); and through establishing the obligation of the time, the obligation of place, and the obligation of occasion, such as the funeral prayer, visiting the sick, feeding the hungry, showing kindness to the neighbor, dutifulness to parents, maintaining ties of kinship, obligatory *jihād* (جهاد), or recommended *jihād*. Through all of this, the Muslim is completed, and he meets Allah while Allah is pleased with him. Indeed, action, spiritual rotations, and daily *awrād* are the provision indispensable for establishing all of this.

And by means of this chapter, many aspects of traveling to Allah have become clear to us; and it is now time for us to move to other aspects of this subject related to the world of the soul and its purification, which is the side of the heart. Therefore this topic will remain with us for several chapters to come in this epistle.
