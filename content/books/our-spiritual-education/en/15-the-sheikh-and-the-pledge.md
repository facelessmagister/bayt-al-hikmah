# Chapter 15: The Question of the Sheikh and the Pledge

Allah the Exalted says:

> And whomsoever Allah sends astray, for him you will never find a *walī* (ولي) to guide him to the right way.

[Quran, Al-Kahf 18:17]

This verse indicates that the farthest reach of ability to guide is the *walī al-murshid* (الولي المرشد) (the guiding ally). For the verse shows that the *walī al-murshid* himself cannot breach the will of Allah when Allah intends to send a person astray. Hence, the call to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, is only as complete as the presence of the *walī al-murshid*. When a person places his hand in the hand of the *walī al-murshid*, that is the finest possible state in the matter of guidance to Allah and to His path. Since the Messengers, peace be upon them, are in origin the true guides to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, the guiding *awliyāʾ* (أولياء) are the complete inheritors of the Prophets in building the call to Allah. From this meaning we realize the importance of the existence of the *walī al-murshid* for the soundness of the call to Allah. Since this matter is surrounded by much error, false claim, and misleading fancy, we must say a great deal about it. We shall present scattered meanings in successive paragraphs, each of them connected to the chapter's title, each clarifying an aspect of this subject.

## 1

Allah the Exalted says:

> O you who have believed, fear Allah and be with the truthful.

[Quran, At-Tawbah 9:119]

Many Sufis cite this verse as proof that Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, commanded being with the *ṣādiqīn* (الصادقين) (truthful), considering in principle that their *awliyāʾ* are the *ṣādiqīn*. What we say is that Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has precisely defined the qualities of the truthful; whoever is characterized by these qualities is truthful, and whoever is not is not. As for these qualities, Allah the Exalted says:

> The believers are only those who have believed in Allah and His Messenger and then doubt not but strive with their properties and their lives in the cause of Allah. It is those who are the truthful.

[Quran, Al-Hujurat 49:15]

And He says:

> Righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east or the west, but [true] righteousness is [in] one who believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets and gives wealth, in spite of love for it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask [for help], and for freeing slaves; [and who] establishes prayer and gives zakah; [those who] fulfill their promise when they promise; and [those who] are patient in poverty and hardship and during battle. Those are the ones who have been true, and it is those who are the righteous.

[Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:177]

And He says:

> Among the believers are men true to what they promised Allah. Among them is he who has fulfilled his vow [to the death], and among them is he who awaits [his chance]. And they did not alter [the terms of their commitment] by substitution.

[Quran, Al-Ahzab 33:23]

And He says:

> For the poor emigrants who were driven out from their homes and their properties, seeking bounty from Allah and [His] approval and supporting Allah and His Messenger—those are the truthful.

[Quran, Al-Hashr 59:8]

Thus the *ṣādiqūn* are the believing, striving, certain, praying, purifying, God-fearing, patient, fulfilling of covenants, and those who await being killed in the cause of Allah. The common people seeking knowledge also enter among the *ṣādiqīn*; for in the context of the verse Allah the Exalted says:

> And it is not for the believers to go forth [to battle] all at once. For there should separate from them a party [of remaining behind] to study religion and warn their people when they return to them that they might be cautious.

[Quran, At-Tawbah 9:122]

Therefore the educating *shaykh* (شيخ) must possess all these qualities and educate upon them.

## 2

Allah the Exalted says:

> Unquestionably, [for] the *awliyāʾ* of Allah there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve. * Those who believed and were fearing Allah.

[Quran, Yunus 10:62-63]

This is the definition of the *walī*. He is one who combines the two qualities of faith and *taqwā* (تقوى) (God-consciousness). The *shaykh* must be a *walī murshid*, that is, he must possess the quality of *irshād* (إرشاد) (guidance) in addition to the quality of *wilāyah* (ولاية). For how can one who is not a believer with *taqwā* be called a *walī*, let alone a *walī murshid*? Thus *wilāyah* is part of *shaykh-hood*, and the two foundations of *wilāyah* are faith and *taqwā*; and there is no faith and no *taqwā* without commitment to the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, peace be upon him.

## 3

From the two preceding paragraphs we understand some essential qualities with which the *shaykh* must be characterized. Since the *shaykh* is a guide, his guidance must certainly be from the directives of the Quranic verse:

> And it is not for the believers to go forth [to battle] all at once. For there should separate from them a party [of remaining behind] to study religion and warn their people when they return to them that they might be cautious.

[Quran, At-Tawbah 9:122]

From this verse we understand that guidance entails *fiqh* (فقه) in the religion of Allah and warning. If he is not a *faqīh* (فقيه) (jurist), he is not fit for the station of warning; and one who does not undertake the task of warning does not fulfill Allah's right in his *fiqh*. This is an aspect of the complete inheritance of the Messengers of Allah, peace be upon them:

> [We sent] messengers as bringers of good tidings and warners so that mankind will have no argument against Allah after the messengers.

[Quran, An-Nisa 4:165]

*Fiqh* in the religion of Allah requires *fiqh* in the Book and the Sunnah, *fiqh* in faith, Islam, *iḥsān* (إحسان), *taqwā*, and gratitude. Whoever does not combine *fiqh* in all of this, its details, and what is required of him is not a *faqīh* in the religion of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. One who is not skilled in upbringing upon all of this is not fit for the station of guidance; and one who cannot teach all of this and more is not fit for the station of complete guidance—that is, the station of the *shaykh* who renders a complete service in the matter of *sulūk* (سلوك) (traveling) to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.

## 4

Allah the Exalted says:

> Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and argue with them in a way that is best.

[Quran, An-Nahl 16:125]

This verse defines some of the duties of prophethood, and therefore some of the qualities of the inheritor—that is, the *shaykh* in Sufi terminology, or the *walī al-murshid* in Quranic terminology. Hence the *shaykh* must be wise, calling to the path of Allah with wisdom. Wisdom is a meaning beyond mere knowledge; Allah the Exalted says:

> And whoever has been given wisdom has certainly been given much good.

[Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:269]

Thus wisdom is a gift from Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. A person may be knowledgeable in the Book and the Sunnah, yet not say the appropriate word at the appropriate time, nor act in the appropriate manner within the limits of the *sharīʿah* (شريعة). Among that is the matter of *daʿwah* (دعوة). Wisdom is a divine bestowal, and it requires divine success in one's breaths and movements. The *shaykh* must be as we have described. It is not enough that he be capable of exhortation; and how many are those who speak yet do not exhort, and how many are those who do not exhort at all. The *shaykh* must be capable of discussion and establishing proof—not only by the goodly argument but by the best argument. All of this is part of the *shaykh's* adab (etiquette), and should be part of his constitution. This is not completed for the *shaykh* except through knowledge, upbringing, companionship, and abundant remembrance. Allah the Exalted says:

> There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent pattern for anyone whose hope is in Allah and the Last Day and who remembers Allah often.

[Quran, Al-Ahzab 33:21]

Indeed, hope in Allah and the Last Day and abundant remembrance lead to complete emulation of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and all the rest follows from that.

## 5

Allah the Exalted says:

> Certainly did Allah confer [great] favor upon the believers when He sent among them a Messenger from themselves, reciting to them His verses and purifying them and teaching them the Book and wisdom, although they had been before in manifest error.

[Quran, Al Imran 3:164]

Thus the inheritor—that is, the *shaykh*—must inherit this from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. He reminds people of Allah's verses in the universe and in history; he nurtures the human soul, purifies it from its faults, and cures it of its diseases; and he teaches people the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, peace be upon him, for that is the very essence of wisdom. He teaches people everything they need in the matter of their religion, from *fiqh* onward. This cannot be attained by the *shaykh* unless he is a scholar of the Book and the Sunnah, capable of nurturing the human soul, proficient in the sciences of Islam and Islamic culture, and knowing his age and history.

Here people raise an idea: they say that it is not a condition for the *shaykh* to possess all of that, because many great *awliyāʾ* studied under great scholars. We say: We do not deny that there may exist a *walī* capable of education and guidance despite deficiency in the sciences of the Book, the Sunnah, *fiqh*, and so forth; nor do we deny that such a person may benefit great scholars in this regard. But this is one thing, and the complete inheritor is something else. The complete *shaykh* and the complete guide are what we are speaking about. The great problem is that many consider their *shaykhs* to be complete inheritors while they do not inherit from the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, except in part. And the *shaykhs* themselves remain silent about the exaggeration of their disciples toward them, arguing that a person benefits according to his trust in the *shaykh*. Yet this leaves bad effects in the Islamic body, for it is not known where people like these *shaykhs* stand among those who form the true leadership of the Muslims. And because of their *shaykhs'* deficiency in knowledge, they issue fatwas to them that are deficient in public or private affairs; and in that there is disorder.

Imam Muslim narrated from Ḥanẓala ibn al-Rabīʿ al-Asadī—may Allah be pleased with him—in the Book of Faith: He said, "Abū Bakr met me and said, 'How are you, Ḥanẓala?' I said, 'Ḥanẓala has become a hypocrite.' He said, 'Subḥān Allah! What are you saying?' I said, 'When we are with the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, he reminds us of the Fire and Paradise as though we were seeing them with our very eyes. When we leave him, we encounter our spouses, children, and estates and forget much.' Abū Bakr said, 'By Allah, we encounter the same.' So I and Abū Bakr went until we entered upon the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. I said, 'O Messenger of Allah, Ḥanẓala has become a hypocrite.' He said, 'And what is that?' I said, 'When we are with you, you remind us of the Fire and Paradise as though we were seeing them with our very eyes. When we leave you, we encounter our spouses, children, and estates and forget much.' He said, 'By Him in whose hand is my soul, if you remained in the state in which you are with me and in the remembrance, the angels would shake hands with you in your beds and on your roads. But, O Ḥanẓala, [there are] moments and [there are] moments,' three times."

[Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith of Ḥanẓala]

From this hadith we understand that the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, had a state by which he elevated his Companions, to the extent that steadfast sitting with the Messenger of Allah could reach what the constant rememberer reaches—that is, a state in which angels could shake hands with him. The Companions of the Messenger of Allah, may Allah be pleased with them, also narrated in authentic reports something like that their hearts were diverted after they had buried the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him. All this indicates that the spiritual states were sensed through sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, and his presence among the Companions; and that among the manifestations of this state was that the Companion would feel as though he were seeing Paradise and the Fire with his very eyes. From all this we understand that the *shaykh* who is an inheritor—if he has none of this state—cannot be a complete prophetic inheritor. And in reality we find that those who are not upon a *ṭarīqah* (طريقة) of *tasawwuf* (تصوف) cannot transmit these feelings to others; indeed, they themselves do not sense them.

Therefore we say: Every seeker of knowledge must verify, with insight, the soundness of the path and its people. We have clarified that in this book, together with its two companion works, *al-Mustakhlaṣ* and *Mudhakkirāt fī Manāzil al-Ṣādiqīn wa al-Rabbāniyyīn* (Notes on the Stations of the Truthful and the Lordly), with explanations for all aspects of this *sulūk*.

From the texts we have mentioned, we understand some of the qualities of the *walī al-murshid*, or the complete inheritor, or the complete guide, or the *shaykh*. He is a wise, guiding *walī*, a caller to Allah, a teacher of Allah's verses, a teacher of the Book and the Sunnah, capable of purifying souls, capable of transporting the human heart to horizons of sensing many unseen matters, capable of conveying it to the *maqāmāt* (مقامات) (stations) of Islam. All of this requires that a certain knowledge, action, and state be combined in him, so that he becomes a teaching educator through example and instruction, with clarity and guidance. And he must realize the qualities of the truthful, chief among which is *jihād* (جهاد) of the soul and wealth when they are required, and we have already seen the evidence for that.

These are matters clear from the outset: the complete inheritor must realize them because they appear in the texts and are evident.

Now we shall mention some of what the Sufis themselves say about the question of the *shaykh*, quoting from the poem *al-Mabāḥith al-Asliyah* with some comment, drawing on the explanations of some commentators:

"He is a knower who is not satisfied with the sciences"—that is, he does not stop and toil in them until they become submissive to his hand, so that he is on clear insight from his Lord.

"And he knows the existent and the non-existent"—that is, he knows necessary existence and contingent existence, necessary non-existence and contingent non-existence.

"And he was not, at the outset, a *faqīh*"—that is, *fiqh* should precede everything, for it is not fitting for a person to embark on anything before he knows Allah's ruling in it.

"And the rest of rulings he does not know"—that is, he does not know Allah's ruling in the matters that confront, befall, or test him.

"And logic, principles, and language"—by *al-ḥadd* is meant the science of logic; by *al-uṣūl* the science of the principles of *fiqh* and the science of the principles of religion, i.e., creed; and by *al-lisān* the sciences of the Arabic language, such as grammar, morphology, rhetoric, and so forth.

"And remembrance, hadith, and proof"—by *al-dhikr* is meant the Quran; by *al-ḥadīth* the Sunnah; and by *al-burhān* establishing proof in Allah's religion over creation.

"And he was not a master of the science of the state"—by *ʿilm al-ḥāl* is meant *tasawwuf*; that is, the *shaykh* must also master *ʿilm al-ḥāl* and the science of the *maqāmāt*, such that he has traveled the path of *aḥwāl* (أحوال) (states) and then dwelt in the *maqāmāt*.

"And he does not understand the intentions of men"—that is, he cannot understand the expressions of common people in their explicit statements, clarifications, allusions, symbols, riddles, and purposes in all of that.

"And he does not declare transcendent the attribute of the Worshipped"—so that he knows Allah, the Exalted, in due gnosis, declaring Him transcendent from contingency, motion, incarnation, resemblance, likeness, or anything improper for Him, glory be to Him.

"And he does not know the levels of existence"—that is, the levels of contingent existence, necessary existence, witnessed existence, and absent existence.

"And the soul, the intellect together, and the spirit"—that is, he does not know what is meant by the word *nafs* (نفس), what is meant by the word *ʿaql* (عقل), and what is meant by the word *rūḥ* (روح), and when the referent is one and when it differs. It is not meant knowledge of the essence, as we have mentioned before.

"And he knows the opening of his breast"—that is, he also does not know the meaning of the breast opened by Islam, and what its signs are: turning away from the abode of delusion, returning to the abode of eternity, and so on.

"And the knowledge of the secret of abrogating and abrogated"—that is, he does not know the matter of the abrogating and abrogated in the Book and the Sunnah; for without this knowledge he goes astray and leads astray.

Then the *shaykh* said:

"That he should assume the station of the shaykhs"—that is, one for whom all the preceding does not apply is not fit to assume the station of *shaykh-hood*. Here the intended meaning is complete guidance; as for advice, mutual reminding, teaching, and benefiting by word or state, that door is open to the individuals of the *ummah* (أمة). For in the hadith:

> Convey from me, even one verse.

[Bukhari, Book of Knowledge, Hadith of Conveying]

And the author of *al-Mabāḥith* said elsewhere in his poem on the matter of the *shaykh*—we shall mention it with a light comment:

"The people are only travelers"—here travel is an expression for moving from one station to another, such as moving from the station of Islam to the station of faith, then to the station of *iḥsān*, then to the station of *taqwā*, then to the station of gratitude; and from seeing the acts of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, to sensing His attributes and names.

[Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith of Iḥsān; Tirmidhi, Book of Faith, Hadith of Iḥsān]

"Traveling to the presence of the Real and departing"—that is, they are travelers to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and move in their journey to Him from station to station: from the station of heedlessness to the station of wakefulness, from the station of wakefulness to the station of presence, and so forth.

"So in it they are in need of a guide"—that is, they are in need in this journey of a guide who shows them the path; he is the *shaykh* whose qualities will be mentioned after this line.

"Possessing insight into traveling and resting"—that is, the *shaykh* must be insightful regarding the states of travel and its halting-places, so that he directs every *murīd* (مريد) according to his capacity and effort, and takes into account the traveler's need for rest.

"He has traveled the road, then returned"—that is, the *shaykh* must have traveled the path of *sulūk* from beginning to end, then returned after knowing it in order to guide others; hence he said:

"To inform the people of what he gained"—that is, to inform the *murīdīn* of what he has gained from the sciences of tastes and the lights of witnessing. Therefore they said: the *shaykh* must have sound gnosis, a clear taste, a high state, and a wholesome condition.

"And he crossed its lowlands and hills"—*al-wahd* is the low place, and *al-akām* is the plural of *akkah*, the high place; *jāb* here means to traverse and cut through, i.e., to enter and travel. The meaning is that the *shaykh* must have tasted the flavor of humiliation and lowliness before the believers, and of withdrawal that is voluntary, and the like, which are like the low places on the path to Allah; and he must have tasted the hardship of the path in enjoining good, forbidding evil, and *jihād*—when required—and struggling.

"And he knows its sand and its dust"—*rāḍa* means to know a place by experience; *al-righām* is dust. The meaning is that the *shaykh* must know the path's easy parts, which resemble sand, and its difficult parts, which resemble hard dust; and thus he makes every *murīd* travel according to his condition and according to the appropriate method, whether it is long or short, hard or easy.

"And he moved in it morning and evening"—that is, it is a condition for the *shaykh* to be skilled in the path, having traveled it morning and evening, alluding to knowledge of beginnings and endings.

"And he traveled every rugged ground and valley"—*al-fadfid* is a place of roughness and elevation; *al-wādī* is the riverbed. By *fadfid* and *wādī* he alludes to the examinations, easements, divine assists, and gifts that the *murīd* encounters.

"And he comprehends fear and safety"—that is, he comprehends the matters from which one should fear for the *murīd*, so he commands him to avoid them, such as leaning toward self-exaltation, seeking honor, comfort, laziness, and the world; and he knows the matters by which the *murīd* attains Allah's pleasure, so that he becomes among those for whom there is no fear and who will not grieve.

"The wells and the springs"—*al-ābār* here are the sciences of the *sharīʿah*; *al-ʿuyūn* here are the springs of *fiṭrah* (فطرة) (innate disposition). Thus the *shaykh* knows the sciences of the *sharīʿah* and knows how the springs of *fiṭrah* gush forth and how to make them gush.

"He has crossed the deserts and the wildernesses"—*al-baydāʾ* is the desert; *al-mafāwiz* is the plural of *mafāza*, the vast desert. By *baydāʾ* here is meant the land of the soul in its lustfulness and recklessness; by *mafāwiz* are meant the distant spaces far from Allah's pleasure.

"And he reconnoitered every obstacle and barrier"—*al-irtiyād* is advancing before the group to choose places and what is in them; *al-ḥābis* is what holds you back from reaching the desired goal; *al-ḥājiz* is what bars you from your object. So the *shaykh* must know what stops the journey, such as pausing at some manifestation of the universe, and what bars arrival at Allah, such as weariness of struggle, leaning toward rest, and so on.

"And he alighted at the stations of the drinkers"—*al-manhal* is the place where the caravan halts, on condition that there is water. The meaning is that it is a condition for the *shaykh* to have alighted at the stations of the travelers: certainty, scrupulousness, *zuhd* (زهد), fear, hope, reliance, patience, contentment, surrender, witnessing, purification, annihilation in everything other than Allah, and subsistence in Allah.

"And every drinker drank from him"—*al-nāhil* is the drinker. That is, it is a condition for the *shaykh* to have drunk from the waters of these stations, that is, to have tasted them and realized them.

"When he fulfilled this weighty matter"—*al-khuṭb* is the weighty affair. That is, when he has realized all these matters that we have passed over from the beginning of these verses.

"They all said, 'You are the shaykh of the caravan'"—his brothers, *shaykhs*, and knowers said to him: you have reached the station of *shaykh-hood*, and it is time for you to be rewarded with guidance to the Kingdom of the Unseen.

"And the journey mentioned is with the hearts"—that is, the journey we have mentioned in what preceded is the journey of the hearts to the presence of the Knower of the Unseen; and in detail it is from four places to four places: from the place of sin and heedlessness to the place of repentance and wakefulness; from the place of eagerness for the world to the place of *zuhd* in it and seeking the Hereafter; from the place of the vices of souls and faults of hearts to the place of emptying from them and adorning with their opposites; and from witnessing the universe to witnessing the Lord of the universe. "Worship Allah as if you see Him." Then after that comes journeying with Him.

"And the shaykh is like the physician"—just as the *shaykh* is like the leader of the caravan in knowing the path, he is also like the physician for hearts.

"He knows in them the lean and the fat"—*al-ghath* is lean meat; by it here is meant the heart weak in knowledge, action, and state, and weak in certainty; and by *al-samīn* is meant the heart filled with knowledge, action, state, and gnosis. Thus the *shaykh* must be insightful, directing this and that according to what suits each.

"And he perceives the hard and the soft"—*al-ṣulb* is that which is hard and dry; *al-layn* is its opposite. By *ṣulb* here is meant the heart hardened by many sins and heedlessness, or the heart stern toward Allah's enemies; and by *layn* is meant the humble heart or the merciful heart toward Allah's creatures. So the *shaykh* knows the nature of this and that, and directs each person according to what he is qualified for or what suits his condition.

"He has mastered dissection and separation"—by *al-tashrīḥ* here is meant knowledge of treating cardiac, psychological, and spiritual diseases; by *al-fiṣl* here is meant knowledge of treating the limbs. The meaning is that the *shaykh* knows the obligations of the heart and the obligations of the body, and knows how to treat deviation of the heart and deviation of the body.

"And the science of medicine became acquired in him"—that is, he has acquired the whole of religious medicine, until he has come to possess the whole of medicine; that is, he is capable of treating every human condition at every level, whether it is the person's heart and conduct so that it conforms to the *sharīʿah*, or this person's place in the Islamic ranks, or this person's stance toward others through fatwa, guidance, advice, upbringing, discipline, and so on.

"And he was a herbalist and a pharmacist"—*al-ʿashshāb* is one who knows the varieties of herbs and their benefits and properties; *al-ṣaydalānī* is one who knows the types of medicines and drugs. The meaning is that the *shaykh*—since he is a physician who diagnoses the disease and prescribes the cure—is at the same time one who knows medicines and their properties and knows how to compound them; thus he is physician and pharmacist at once in matters of the diseases of hearts.

"An oculist, a kohl-applier, and a hospital-master"—*al-qaḍḥ* in medical terminology is eye surgery; a surgeon of the eye was called *qadḥ*; *al-kuḥḥāl* is one who knows eye medicines and treats them with kohl; *al-maristānī* is the director of the general hospital for varied diseases. The meaning is that the *shaykh* must be expert in surgery of the eye of insight and treating it, knowing the totality of diseases, and able to treat all their people.

"More skilled in symptoms and humors"—*al-aʿrāḍ* are the states that befall the body; *al-akhlāṭ* are what collect in the stomach of ailments arising from the mixing of different foods. By *aʿrāḍ* the author means what befalls the *murīd* of obstacles and distractions, such as seeking leadership and precedence in a matter before speaking about it, and so forth; and by *akhlāṭ* he means base thoughts and low worldly aims that may confuse the state of some *murīdīn*.

"Than the books of Galen or Hippocrates"—Galen and Hippocrates are physicians. The author means that the *shaykh* must be more skilled in diagnosing and treating hearts than these two physicians are in treating bodies.

"And he knows the simple and the compound"—*al-basīṭ* here is the heart that is not complicated; *al-murakkab* here is the complicated heart; or *al-basīṭ* is what is closer to *fiṭrah*, and *al-murakkab* is that in which something has mixed with *fiṭrah* and troubled it. Thus the *shaykh* must be knowing, able to rectify all, and know how to direct each of the possessors of these two hearts.

"And what appears outwardly in it and what is hidden"—some traits of hearts appear clearly in a person's conduct, and thus are easy to discover; and some matters of hearts are obscure and require acute discernment to perceive. The *shaykh* must have insight and discernment by which he knows the outward and hidden condition of his *murīd*.

"And temperament, humor, and constitution"—*al-ṭabʿ* is what a person is innately disposed to, such as fear, courage, generosity, or miserliness; *al-mizāj* here is the psycho-physical constitution of the person, such as being cold or hot in temperament, sharp or calm in *mizāj*; and *al-tarkīb* here is the mixture of one thing with another, such as the native with the foreign, the sick with the healthy. Thus the *shaykh* must know temperaments, natures, *mizāj*s, and psychological and cardiac mixtures, and in light of this knowledge he directs their possessors toward what rectifies them and brings them to Allah with wisdom in accordance with the *sharīʿah*.

"And existence, dissolution, and softening"—by *al-kawn* here is meant the person's condition, health or sickness; by *al-taḥlīl* here is meant dissolving what has coagulated in the person's heart of ailments; by *al-tarṭīb* here is meant knowing ways to soften what has hardened and dried of hearts. The meaning is that the *shaykh* must be skilled in the states of hearts, knowing their diseases, knowing their treatment whatever their condition. For the cardiac diseases dissolve through his guidance, and the dryness of hearts vanishes through his companionship and mutual reminding.

"When attainment became sound for him"—that is, after he has acquired all these stations in full completeness.

"He went to the sick and the ailing"—that is, he goes to the sick of every kind of disease.

"And he healed them of diseases"—that is, he heals them by Allah's permission from the cardiac and psychological diseases, some of which we have mentioned.

"And the displeased heart returns pleased"—that is, one whose heart was displeased becomes, after healing, pleased. For among the signs of healing is contentment with Allah in every condition; hence it was among the supplications of the Muslim: "I am pleased with Allah as Lord, with Islam as religion, and with Muhammad as Messenger," and we seek refuge with Allah from the state of the people of the Fire.

"This is not the medicine of Galen; it is confined to souls"—this is a note by the author that the medicine mentioned in the verses is not the medicine of bodies but the medicine of souls, so that they conform to Allah's command, and the medicine of hearts so that they are healed of diseases and faults and join the company of those who come to Allah with a sound heart.

Then he said:

"Such were the shaykhs, alas, when they passed and disappeared"—the *shaykh* means that none of this type of *shaykhs* remains, a phrase of regret and an exhortation to aspire to reach the station of *shaykh-hood* in truth. Otherwise, the *ummah* is never devoid of complete inheritors in every age, and grace is from Allah. Whoever knew our *shaykh*, the guide Ḥāmid—may Allah have mercy on him—knows what the second group of verses we quoted from the author of *al-Mabāḥith* contains.

The author of *al-Mabāḥith* mentions three main points concerning the *shaykh*:

1. The *shaykh* must have traveled the path from its beginning to its end and known all its hidden places, until he became able to guide all classes of people upon this path.
2. The *shaykh* must be insightful regarding the types of hearts and the types of their diseases, and able—by Allah's permission—to treat them.
3. He must know the types of cardiac remedies and what is compounded from them.

Now let us look at some of Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh's sayings on the *shaykh*:

Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh said:

"Do not keep company with one whose state does not uplift you and whose speech does not direct you to Allah; perhaps you are erring and he will show you the good in yourself. Keep company with one who is in a worse state than you."

"For keeping company with an ignorant person who is not pleased with himself is better for you than keeping company with a scholar who is pleased with himself. For what knowledge is there in a scholar pleased with himself, and what ignorance is there in an ignorant person not pleased with himself?"

"Whoever you see hastening to answer every question, expressive about everything he witnesses, and mentioning everything he knows—deduce from that the presence of his ignorance."

"Often the lights of the wise precede people; so when illumination occurs, expression follows. Every word emerges clothed with the heart from which it emerged. If permission to express is granted, their expression is understood in the hearing of creation and their allusion becomes clear to them. But if realities appear with their lights veiled, and permission to manifest them is not granted to you, their expressions are either from the overflow of ecstatic finding or from the intention to guide a *murīd*. The first is the state of the travelers; the second is the state of the people of power and realization. Expression is the sound of the family of one who seeks help. You have only what you are capable of. Whoever glimpsed a station, expressed it; and whoever reached it, humbled himself to it, and that is acquired only by the possessor of insight."

After we have seen a model from the texts and a model from the words of the Sufis on the question of the *shaykh*, let us ask: If this is the *shaykh's* mission in educating any *murīd* absolutely, then what is the *shaykh's* mission beyond that in our age, in which apostasy has deepened and unbelief has prevailed, and what are the effects and repercussions of that on the education of *murīdīn*? Then what is the *shaykh's* mission in an age in which Muslims no longer have a central caliphate? And how should the relation be between him and others, so that the Muslims become one rank, one word, and one *ummah*? I shall explain my conception of this subject, and then we shall pause at stations.

The journey of the diseased *ummah* to health begins with the existence of the *mujaddid* (مجدد) (renewer) and the complete inheritors, who transport the human being to health in four aspects: commitment, characteristics, culture, and specialization. In our epistle entitled *For a Step Forward on the Path of Blessed Jihad* we mentioned some manifestations of disease in the Muslim *ummah*, or some of them; and we said there, briefly, that the path to health begins with the existence of the first model of health represented for the Muslim *ummah* in every age, century, or generation by the *mujaddid*. Then through complete inheritors who embark on the process of renewal to its furthest ends, beginning with creating the complete Muslim and ending with raising the word of Allah, according to their capacities. In other books of ours we spoke at length about the motives that make the starting point of health the *mujaddid*. In light of the theories of the *mujaddid* in the renewal of the life of Islam and the Muslims, the inheritors must set out to mold the Muslim in a complete molding and raise every Muslim to the level that befits his class, condition, and readiness.

This means, in principle, that there must exist a stratum of inheritors covering the needs of this *ummah*, whether this inheritor is called *shaykh* or by whatever other name people conventionally use as a symbol for a working, educating scholar. We have spoken extensively in our books about Islamic work and Islamic education. Here we wish to emphasize a point: What is the first mission of the inheritor in forming the Muslim in our age?

There is no doubt that there are four circles the contemporary Muslim needs, and they encompass everything that can be imagined in the matter of forming the Muslim, whether the one imagined is a Sufi, a *faqīh*, or a *mujāhid*. These four circles are: knowledge, specialization, basic ethics and what branches from them, and adherence to the Islamic ranks and what that requires of upbringing, awareness, conduct, and commitment.

The great ailment is that the contemporary Muslim misses one of these, or two, or three, or all four, or takes some of them weakly. Imagine a Muslim who has knowledge but lacks the basic ethics, or one of them; the matter cannot stand on that. Imagine that what adherence to the Islamic ranks requires of upbringing, awareness, and so forth is not present; the matter likewise cannot stand. The great ailment lies in the loss of one of these four, or taking it deficiently.

What enters under knowledge, in our view, is Islamic culture with all its branches, which we enumerated in the book *Jund Allāh Thaqāfatan wa Akhlāqan* (Soldiers of Allah in Culture and Ethics); and under knowledge is acquiring contemporary culture, so that a person is not a stranger to his age and what happens in it. What enters under specialization is preparatory culture either for a life specialty or for a specialty within Islamic work. As for the basic ethics, they are those about which the verses on apostasy in Surat al-Māʾidah spoke, and we detailed speech on them in the book *Jund Allāh Thaqāfatan wa Akhlāqan*; they are: love of Allah, lowliness before all the believers—all the believers, might over all the disbelievers—all the disbelievers, *jihād* in the cause of Allah, and liberating loyalty for Allah, His Messenger, and the believers. As for adherence to the Islamic ranks, it requires knowing the reality of these ranks in our age and their characteristics specified in the texts, and the conditions that must be present so that they become the saved group working for the truth and acting upon it; it requires knowledge of the foundations on which contemporary Islamic work rests; it requires a consultative mentality that accepts *shūrā* (شورى) and acts upon its requirements in light of the rules of Islamic *shūrā*.

If all this is clear—and all of this is necessary—then what is the reality? You find a *shaykh* who claims to guide the *murīd* on the path to Paradise, yet misses upbringing in lowliness before the believers and might over the disbelievers; or one calling to *tawḥīd* (توحيد) who forgets to teach the Book, the Sunnah, the *sīrah* (سيرة), the life of the Companions, the history of the Muslim *ummah*, or anything else required for a complete Islamic culture. You find one calling to some sound call but missing many things in culture, ethics, or Islamic group upbringing. If the defect lies in one of these circles, the condition remains as we see.

The mission of the *shaykh* is very vast, and no doubt people's readiness varies; but there is a minimum that every person must have, and our mission is to raise people, not to lower ourselves to what they want. If we understand these few lines, we can understand the points of defect in the contemporary station of *shaykh-hood*, and we know what is required to ascend to that station. And every Muslim who is below this peak that I mentioned should travel under the hand of one who can bring him to this peak, or set for himself a program by which he can remedy his deficiency.

Long ago, the *ijāza* (license) that *shaykhs* gave was a certificate of a person's attainment and ability to teach; and how good it would be if this existed in detailed form in our age, especially for the station of complete inheritance or educating *shaykh-hood*. I consider that the first mission of the Islamic movement is to bring into existence a stratum of complete *shaykhs* who encompass the educational, pedagogical, and behavioral needs of the Muslims.

On the occasion of mentioning the word *ijāza*, I say briefly about it: The *ijāza* is a testimony to the qualification of a person for a kind of knowledge. The *ijāza* in a science is a testimony from its people that a person has reached maturity or the minimum level in that science. The *ijāza* in education is a testimony that a person has reached maturity or the minimum level that qualifies him for education. There is no doubt that testimony from its people inspires reassurance; therefore the *ijāza* is stipulated for independence in knowledge and education. As for assisting in knowledge and education, that is broad if the sound foundation exists.

If we comprehend all this, we will have understood the station of *shaykh-hood* as our age needs it, and understood the state of *shaykh-hood* in its present condition. Imagine now a person assuming the station of *shaykh-hood* while he does not know his age, and is not capable of comprehensive fatwa suited to time, place, and persons. Imagine this person being approached by a *murīd* asking him for fatwa in his public or private affairs, or in the affairs of Islam and the Muslims—how far can his fatwas reach? Therefore we warned in this book against absolute commitment to a *shaykh*. Here we advise the following:

1. Absolute commitment should be to the Muslim community and their living Imam; that is, if there exists a Muslim community with a rightly guided *khalīfa* (خليفة) at its head. If the Muslims do not have a community with a rightly guided *khalīfa* at its head, then the Muslim should be of the group mentioned in the hadiths, and that is by being with the Islamic ranks in general.
2. Let him oblige himself to cooperate in good according to his ability, and let him weigh everything he hears in the scales of sound knowledge. If a Muslim comprehends these two matters and has the correct balance in his hand—and that is sound knowledge—then there is no harm in his sitting with everyone and benefiting from everyone. No doubt he will find a more complete person, a more knowledgeable one, a person of deeper longing and purer aspiration; all of that is good. But beware of absolute commitment except to the Muslim community and their rightly guided *khalīfa* if one exists. For if we allow each of us to commit absolutely to a *shaykh*, how can the Muslims have one community and one unity? Therefore al-Suyūṭī answered a question: "A man gave a pledge to a *shaykh*, then gave it to another; which of the two pledges binds him?" He said, "Neither, and there is no basis for that." The only basis is adherence to the Muslim community and their rightly guided *khalīfa*.

We mentioned earlier that the Sufis, when they found a situation in which a person did not find a complete guide, said that knowledge together with sending blessings upon the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, is sufficient for a person. For Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, promised whoever sends blessings upon the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, that He will send blessings upon him. In the hadith:

> Whoever sends blessings upon me once, Allah sends blessings upon him ten times.

[Bukhari, Book of Invocations, Hadith 6340; Muslim, Book of Salutations, Hadith 408]

And when Allah sends blessings upon a person, He brings him out of every darkness into light:

> It is He who confers blessings upon you, and His angels [do so], that He may bring you out from darkness into light.

[Quran, Al-Ahzab 33:43]

Absolute submission to other than the complete guide, and absolute commitment to other than the community of the travelers and their rightly guided *khalīfa*, are two great errors. Most Sufis today are heedless of these two issues; so every Muslim must review himself in this matter and leave blind fanaticism for his *shaykh*, not giving him a station beyond his station. We do not wish to cut a *murīd* off from his *shaykh*; rather we wish to open the way for *shaykhs* to cooperate and for *murīdīn* to benefit wherever they find benefit.

What may be relied upon in keeping company with the righteous during tribulations and misguidance is the keeping company of Salmān al-Fārisī before Islam with those who remained upon the true religion of Jesus, peace be upon him; and what al-Tirmidhī narrated in a *ṣaḥīḥ* hadith concerning the monk—that seven soldiers pledged to him and stayed with him.

[Tirmidhi, Book of Asceticism, Hadith of the Monk]

There is an issue we must know: one of the expressions of the Sufis is, "Whoever has no *shaykh*, his *shaykh* is Satan." This expression is transmitted from one of the great Sufis. We would like to discuss this matter, and we begin by saying: The scholars of *uṣūl* do not consider the *ijtihād* of a Companion binding on the *ummah* unless there is consensus upon it. So how much more so the opinion of anyone else? Every person's statement gains strength in proportion to the texts that support it. Once this principle is clear, we say: This expression is correct in one case only, namely: if there is an ignorant person who has no ability to learn the religious sciences for himself, a person who proceeds in his worship, dealings, and conduct without knowledge—then if this person does not adhere to a scholar, his *shaykh* is Satan. As for a person capable of learning by himself, who proceeds in light of sound knowledge, his *shaykh* is sound knowledge and his *shaykh* is the Book. As for a person who takes knowledge from its people, he has his *shaykhs*. If we understand this, we know the solution to this expression and the error by which some people try to apply it to one who has no Sufi *shaykh*, and therefore they use it to call to their *shaykhs*, who may themselves be ignorant and in need of *shaykhs*.

One of the common conceptions among some Sufis is that it is impossible to reach Allah except through a Sufi *shaykh*; and this is a great delusion. We have seen Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh's words, "Your arrival at Allah is your arrival at knowledge of Him." Knowledge of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, is open to whoever travels the path to it, whether it is experiential knowledge or scholarly knowledge; and tying knowledge of Allah to the existence of a *shaykh* of a special kind, and deeming sinful those who do not travel under the hand of such a *shaykh*, means that millions of Muslims died ignorant of Allah, among them exegetes and hadith scholars. The truth is that the Sufi terminology of *shaykh-hood* appeared late in Islamic history; so were people before that not knowing Allah, and were they not the best generations absolutely? There is no doubt that our adab as Muslims is to enter houses by their doors; and for everything there is a door through which one enters the house; and every person has his conditions and circumstances. For one person it is presumed that he should go to a *faqīh* *shaykh*; for another, to a scholar of *tawḥīd*; for another, to a Sufi *shaykh*. Fatwa is determined by time, place, and person.

Al-Shaykh Aḥmad al-Zarrūq said on the subject of *shaykhs*: "The poor ones of al-Andalus from among the later ones disputed over whether books suffice in place of the *mashāyikh*. They wrote to the lands, and each answered according to his opening; and the bulk of the answers revolve around three points:

1. Looking at the *mashāyikh*: A *shaykh* of learning can be replaced by books for a clever, sharp person who knows the sources of knowledge; a *shaykh* of upbringing can be replaced by companionship for a person of sound religion and sincere counsel. The commentator of *Bidayat al-Sulūk* said: 'And rarely does one escape the domination of ego, if he attains.' A *shaykh* of elevation can be replaced by meeting and seeking blessing. And taking all of that from one source means that taking it from the *shaykh* in the three respects is most successful and closest to the goal.
2. Looking at the student's condition: the dullard must have a *shaykh* to educate him; the clever person can make do with books for his elevation, but he will not be safe from the rashness of his soul if he reaches, for the servant is tested by seeing himself.
3. Looking at the spiritual exercises: the struggle of *taqwā* does not need a *shaykh*, because its basis is clear and its generality is known; uprightness needs a *shaykh* to show what is most beneficial, and the clever person may make do with books; and the struggle of disclosure and elevation must have a *shaykh* to whom one returns regarding its openings, like the Prophet's return—peace be upon him—to Waraqah ibn Nawfal to consult him on the beginnings of prophecy and its first appearances when the Truth surprised him. This method is close to the first, and the Sunnah is with it, and Allah knows best." (End quote.)

Note his words: "For *taqwā* does not need a *shaykh*." And *taqwā*—as we have detailed in *Jund Allāh Thaqāfatan wa Akhlāqan*—is what Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, demands from His servants; for it contains what comes before it and places the person's foot in what is subtler than it, such as the station of gratitude. And there is no *taqwā* at all without knowledge of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.

After what we have mentioned in this chapter, it is time to clarify the necessity of the *shaykh* in knowledge and education; for our explanation and discussion of errors may have conveyed something that could be understood as meaning that the *shaykh* has no place at all. Therefore we wish to clarify this point:

1. The insightful *shaykh* in matters shortens the path for you. So instead of toiling on the path—any path, whether the path of acquiring knowledge, the path of proving the soundness of the heart, or the path of ridding oneself of a disease—he shortens it for you.
2. The complete *shaykh* protects you from error in understanding, error in conduct, and error in conceptions that may arise from a person's own travel.
3. The *shaykh*, through keeping his company, gives you a state, and you take from him the scent of the knowers, their adab, the light of knowledge, and the illumination of the heart.
4. Merely a person's acceptance to take knowledge or education from its people protects him from many diseases, such as the pleasure of self-conceit, arrogance, or pride.
5. In every situation in which a person is obliged to acquire something and cannot acquire it except through some channel, taking from that channel is considered a duty incumbent upon him from the principle that whatever is necessary for the fulfillment of an obligation is itself an obligation.
6. If the *shaykh* is righteous and a caller to guidance, the texts indicate that attaching oneself to him is beneficial in this world and the Hereafter.
7. Gathering around a *shaykh*, participating in circles of knowledge and remembrance, and forming special brotherhood in these atmospheres result in many benefits in this world and the Hereafter. All of that is a drop in the ocean of the *shaykh's* place and station.

And as much as we emphasize removing errors from conceptions and behaviors concerning *shaykh-hood*, we equally emphasize that the correct starting point is the existence of the *walī al-murshid*.

## The Pledge

The Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, used to take the *bayʿah* (بيعة) (pledge) for entering Islam, and for levels higher than Islam. In one of its aspects, the *bayʿah* was a pledge to the person of the Messenger of Allah, peace be upon him, based on obedience to the Messenger of Allah. A single formula of *bayʿah* was found: the political *bayʿah* given to the Amir al-Muʾminīn (Commander of the Believers). Although the trends in the Muslim *ummah* differed, the *bayʿah* continued to be given on the basis of personal allegiance to an authority within a political framework connected to rule and sovereignty; and so it remained until the fifth century AH, when the *bayʿah* to the *shaykh* appeared in some environments on the basis of commitment to levels higher than Islam. This type of *bayʿah* to *shaykhs* was separated from the political framework, so some people came to have two *bayʿahs*: a *bayʿah* to the sultan for obedience in public affairs, and a *bayʿah* to the *shaykh* for commitment to *taqwā*. Some *shaykhs* began to take *bayʿah* from their *murīdīn* in this framework, and the matter continued thus until the fall of the Islamic state and the end of Islamic rule in many regions. Ignorance prevailed over people, so the question of the caliphate faded from them, and the necessity of working for it; and many people lost the necessity of working to establish Islamic rule in their lands. In the midst of all that, the idea of the political *bayʿah* was lost, while the idea of the Sufi *bayʿah* remained in some circles. So some Sufis confused the *bayʿah* to the Imam with the *bayʿah* to the *shaykh*, considering that the *bayʿah* to the *shaykh* has the same conditions as that political *bayʿah*, and that it carries the same rulings, and that it replaces it. Therefore the jurists clarified this matter: in *Tahqīq al-Fatāwā al-Ḥāmidiyya* from al-Suyūṭī it is said: "A man gave a pledge to a *shaykh*, then gave it to another; which of the two pledges binds him? They said: Neither, and there is no basis for that."

It is clear that the Sufi *bayʿah* has a non-binding character; this is indicated by the hadith narrated by Muslim, which in our terminology today is the political *bayʿah*:

> If allegiance is sworn to two caliphs, kill the latter of them.

[Muslim, Book of Government, Hadith 1853]

Were these *bayʿahs* given to *shaykhs* subject to the known ruling of *bayʿah*, it would be permissible for us to kill all the *shaykhs* except one *shaykh*, as long as they all take *bayʿahs*—and no one says this. Moreover, many Islamic groups have begun to take oaths and *bayʿahs* from those who join them; all of these, if they are for a specific deed, have the ruling of vows; and if they are a *bayʿah* of personal allegiance, they are a non-binding *bayʿah* that it is obligatory to dissolve if there is deviation, departure, corruption, cardiac or behavioral diseases, or tribulations. In general, we say:

1. Our *shaykhs* used to consider the *bayʿah* given to the *shaykh* among the Sufis to be a *bayʿah* for *taqwā*. Therefore they would suffice in it with placing the hand and reciting His saying:

> Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you, [O Muhammad]—they are actually pledging allegiance to Allah. The hand of Allah is over their hands. So whoever breaks his word breaks it to the detriment of himself; and whoever fulfills the covenant he has made with Allah, He will grant him a great reward.

[Quran, Al-Fath 48:10]

without adding anything else. The *bayʿah* in this framework does not have the rulings of the general *bayʿah*, nor does it prevent commitment to the Muslim community and their rightly guided *khalīfa* if one exists; nor does it prevent a person from giving another *bayʿah* for the same good or for another good. For this reason the early Sufis used to enumerate [their] *shaykhs*, while some of the later ones restricted that; the matter is broad if uprightness exists. In this framework there is no objection; but because of the confusion that has occurred, we prefer to call it by another name, such as *ʿaqd* (عقد) (contract) or *waʿd* (وعد) (promise), or to explain to the one giving the *bayʿah* that this *bayʿah* is a *bayʿah* for *taqwā*, an affirmation of what the Lawgiver has obliged us to, not the creation of new rulings—so that the *shaykh's* command in it regarding the permissible does not become a binding obligation, let alone that he declare lawful forbidden or forbidden lawful. Obedience in this case is a loving, friendly obedience in what is permissible.

As for decisions that emanate from the *shūrā* of the Muslims, they are governed by agreed conditions. If the one who gave the *bayʿah* or pledge sees what necessitates its dissolution, or if he sees a legitimate dream with an insightful fatwa free from caprice, let him do what is better and refrain from evil; for the pledge, according to the Hanafi jurists, has the ruling of a vow.

2. The principle is that the only binding *bayʿah* is that given to the community of the Muslims and their rightly guided *khalīfa*. There is no caliphate, according to the Hanafi jurists, for one whose command is not executed; and that requires that power be in his hand. In this case alone the person of the Imam is considered. Thus if the Imam becomes corrupt, the Muslim is not religiously bound to obey his command, but he may not revolt against him except under conditions.

3. An authorized body may take a *bayʿah* for specific Islamic deeds; in this case the commitment is a commitment to action. If the person is unable to perform that action, it is examined whether expiation is due or not.

In general, I call every Muslim to be cautious in matters of vows, faith, pledges, and *bayʿahs*, unless a religious obligation requires him to do something of the sort.

At this point I would like to record a note: many Muslims fall into despair when they see the calamities that accompanied the chain of caliphates until their fall; they fall into despair when they see how deviation from Islamic rule began very early in the *ummah's* history; they fall into despair when they see the condition and reality of the Muslims at their worst; they fall into despair when they see the reality of the global powers; they wonder that our hopes rest on the correct foundations of departure, and they imagine that this is more like dreams. We say to all of them: Are we charged or not? If we are charged by Allah with an action, we must do it, and it is not upon us thereafter if others neglect their charge. We are seekers of a Paradise whose width is the heavens and the earth; what harms us if we gain it and others lose it? The people of every age are charged with establishing all of Islam; they are not asked about the shortcomings of the predecessors or the negligence of the successors. This is the sound thinking in which we are.

Yet despite this, we say: the deviations that occurred gave us lessons, and our duty is to work so that deviation is not repeated. The present reality of the Muslims is not difficult to change if we travel the correct path. And the global powers are nothing compared to Allah's promise to us:

> Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [to authority] upon the earth just as He granted it to those before them and that He will surely establish for them [therein] their religion which He has preferred for them and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security, [for] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. But whoever disbelieves after that—then those are the defiantly disobedient.

[Quran, An-Nur 24:55]

And for them there is much [good]. Our Lord said after these two verses:

> And do not say about those who are killed in the way of Allah, "They are dead." Rather, they are alive, but you perceive [it] not.

[Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:154]

> Our Lord, give us in this world [that which is] good and in the Hereafter [that which is] good and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.

[Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:201]
