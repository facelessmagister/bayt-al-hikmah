# Chapter 8: The Soul, Its Demands, and Its Illnesses

We sometimes find in the texts an overlap in the discussion of the heart and the *nafs* (نفس) such that a person might think, from some texts, that they are one thing. And sometimes it is observed, through reading some texts and through the speech of the Sufis (*ahl al-taṣawwuf* (أهل التصوف)), that they are two separate things. We spoke at the beginning of this epistle about the intellect (*ʿaql* (عقل)), the heart (*qalb* (قلب)), the spirit (*rūḥ* (روح)), and the *nafs*; here we add what deepens understanding.

In the noble *ḥadīth* (حديث), “He will not enter Paradise in whose heart is an atom’s weight of pride.” [Reported by Muslim] From this *ḥadīth* we know that the heart itself is afflicted with the illness of pride. And we find the Qur’anic text saying:

> He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who covers it [in corruption]. [Qur’an, Al-Shams 91:9–10]

There is no doubt that part of the *tazkiyah* (تزكية) of the *nafs* is for a person to purify it of pride. Indeed, among the first meanings of *tazkiyah* is that a person purify his *nafs* from *shirk* (شرك), which is the most despicable manifestation of pride. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> I will turn away from My signs those who are arrogant upon the earth without right; and if they should see every sign, they will not believe in it. And if they see the way of consciousness, they will not adopt it as a way; but if they see the way of error, they will adopt it as a way. That is because they have denied Our signs and they were heedless of them. [Qur’an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:146]

And indeed, this turning away is from this heart. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> So have they not traveled through the earth and have hearts by which to reason and ears by which to hear? For indeed, it is not eyes that are blinded, but blinded are the hearts which are within the breasts. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:46]

Indeed, you find here a convergence between the *nafs* and the heart. Consider the saying of Allah, the Exalted:

> Indeed the *nafs* is a persistent enjoiner of evil, except those upon which my Lord has mercy. [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:53]

And [He says]:

> And I swear by the self-reproaching soul. [Qur’an, Al-Qiyāmah 75:2]

These expressions have a connection to the commands of the *nafs* to the heart. Consider how Allah, the Exalted, says, on the one hand, in glorification and praise of the heart:

> Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured. [Qur’an, Al-Raʿd 13:28]

And on the other hand:

> O reassured soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him]. [Qur’an, Al-Fajr 89:27–28]

So here is a heart that finds tranquility in remembrance, and a *nafs* that has reached tranquility. Here the heart is the very *nafs*. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And a faction worried about themselves, thinking of Allah other than the truth—the thought of ignorance. [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:154]

Suspicion is an ailment of the heart, for it has a connection to creed. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And seek help through patience and prayer, and indeed, it is difficult except for the humbly submissive [to Allah], who are certain that they will meet their Lord and that they will return to Him. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:45]

From these meanings that have preceded, we realize that speaking about the *nafs* sometimes means speaking about the heart, and sometimes it does not mean that. And this is what we transmitted from al-Ghazālī at the beginning of this epistle: that the *nafs*, the heart, the intellect, and the spirit sometimes come as one meaning, and sometimes each has its own indication. To clarify this station, let us give some examples.

If a person is wounded in battle, or experiences heavy bleeding, he feels intense thirst, and feels from his *nafs* an insistence in seeking water. However much he wants to resist his *nafs* and prevent it from asking, he finds himself overcome. So here is a physical impulse that overcomes the heart. Here is a *nafs* that demands and a heart that is overcome. Without conscious awareness, a child begins to eat dirt when his body is in need of calcium. And if the body needs a kind of nourishment, his *nafs* demands kinds of food that contain it, so the person finds himself driven by strong impulses toward a particular kind of food. Here is a *nafs* that demands.

And it is known in the world of animals and humans that the sexual secretions discharged into the blood produce in humans and animals impulses, drives, fantasies, and demands that are sometimes severe, and many people surrender to them. And there is no objection in a heart surrendering to an impulse of permissible desire if it fulfills it in a lawful (*ḥalāl* (حلال)) manner; but the catastrophe is when a person surrenders to it in the forbidden (*ḥarām* (حرام)). Here is a *nafs* that demands, and a heart that responds or does not respond. And there is a type of medication that, if a person uses it, increases the intensity of his temperament, and another type that helps him sleep, and another type that may create in him a desire for seclusion. From this we realize the effect of the nature of food on a person’s conduct. And by that we realize the wisdom in the prohibition of [eating] some animals and foods in Islam. What is discharged into the blood of nourishment or secretions affects the nervous condition, so the human heart receives demands. These demands, which may be part of what the Sufis call *hawājis* (passing impulses), are of categories: among them is the forbidden demand, among them is the permissible demand, among them is the demand that is unavoidable, and ensuring it is from among the obligatory duties (*farāʾiḍ* (فرائض)).

Thus in Islamic law, if a person’s *nafs* longs for sexual intercourse, marriage becomes a religious obligation incumbent upon him. If longing increases to the degree that he fears being overcome with regard to himself, then marriage has become *farḍ* (فرض) upon him, and he must restrain his *nafs* until he marries. Food and drink, both of which are indispensable for the continuation of human life and for putting a person in a state in which he can carry out his duties, are an obligation from among the obligations upon man. Securing such demands for the *nafs* through the permissible is an obligation. If the *nafs* demands reaching that or other things through the forbidden, that is from the category of enjoining evil:

> Indeed the *nafs* is a persistent enjoiner of evil, except those upon which my Lord has mercy. [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:53]

If we understand this issue, we know why some have adopted the convention of distinguishing between the *nafs* and the heart. These intend by *nafs* here the demands, needs, and desires of the body that it casts upon the heart; thus the heart here is one thing and the *nafs* another thing. And if others express the heart by [the word] *nafs*, that is because the heart is the essence of man, and the *nafs* of man is his essence. So these do not distinguish, in this station, between *nafs* and heart. According to this convention, the intended meaning of the illness of the heart and the illness of the *nafs* is one and the same, and the intended meaning of the *tazkiyah* of the heart and the *tazkiyah* of the *nafs* is one thing and the same. The heart here is the very *nafs*, and the *nafs* here is the very heart. And on this basis these texts are to be understood:

> Just as We have sent among you a messenger from yourselves reciting to you Our verses and purifying you and teaching you the Book and wisdom and teaching you that which you did not know. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:151]

And:

> He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who covers it [in corruption]. [Qur’an, Al-Shams 91:9–10]

And [the Prophet], peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Indeed there is in the body a morsel of flesh which, if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Indeed it is the heart.” [Reported by al-Bukhārī]

And man is charged with treating the demands of his *nafs* negatively or positively, and charged with purifying his heart—by the *tazkiyah* of this heart through being freed from its illnesses (*amrāḍ* (أمراض)), such as envy, pride, self-admiration, and love of this world; and through realizing this heart with its lofty moral traits, such as sincerity, reliance upon Allah, fear of Allah, and the like. (And in these two matters is serious negligence and grave error.) Some people neglect the issue of the *nafs*’s demands and their treatment, and neglect the issue of illnesses and moral traits; and some people do not distinguish between the necessary demands of the *nafs* which should be secured through the lawful, and the demands that are actually forbidden; and some people do not know at all what the scales of health and the aspects of illness are, so they do not know by what they attain [health] nor what they should be freed from. And here comes the importance of the complete guide, or the complete prophetic heir, or the learned doer, or the saintly guide.

Islam came with detailed exposition of everything; and among that is the horizons of the heart and the *nafs*, and the treatment of the illnesses of the *nafs* and the heart, and the paths of treatment, and the scales of health and illness. That is something for which there can be no correct answer in this world except in Islam, and no complete explanation of it except in Islam. And indeed those who wrote in these matters, such as Ḥujjat al-Islām al-Ghazālī, wrote, in truth, about the most refined and loftiest matters absolutely. And it is indeed a loss for all of humanity not to read what people like these wrote.

And returning to the beginning of the subject of this chapter, and for further clarification by giving examples, we say: Sexual desire begins to awaken in a person gradually, and that is a normal matter. Some people try to consider that a pathological phenomenon, indeed they think of eliminating it, and that is an error in understanding things fundamentally. In Islam, you are required to marry in order to realize the wisdom behind the existence of this desire in the first place. After marriage you must regulate this desire within the permissible limits, and before marriage you must treat this desire by restraint until you marry. The kinds of treatment may be through fasting, through choosing the type of food, through a person’s immersion in work and remembrance (*dhikr* (ذكر)), through types of physical exercise, and it may be in all of this. Here is the person’s task in this stage. If his *nafs* demands of him fornication, sodomy, or other than that which is forbidden, he must cut off the path to it. If the heart obeyed the *nafs* here—that is, obeyed the demands of the body—then it is sick, since the forbidden desire has overcome it.

From this we understand the Muslim’s stance toward the demands of the *nafs*—and what is meant by *nafs* here is the demands of the body—and we understand what is meant by the illness of the *nafs*—and the *nafs* here is the heart—and we understand why some scholars express the heart by [the word] *nafs*, and why they sometimes do not express the *nafs* as being other than the heart.

Some people proceed on a path of combating every demand of the *nafs*, whatever it may be, and this is an error; for in the *ḥadīth*: “Indeed your *nafs* has a right over you.” [Reported by al-Bukhārī] And some people give their *nafs* everything it desires, and this too is an error. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination, then indeed, Paradise will be [his] refuge. [Qur’an, Al-Nāziʿāt 79:40–41]

And Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And those who strive for Us—We will surely guide them to Our ways. [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:69]

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “And the *mujāhid* (مجاهد) is one who struggles against his own *nafs* for the sake of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.” [Reported by Aḥmad; *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*] And as for the Muslim, he acts on the basis of knowledge: he restrains the *nafs* from its forbidden desires and prevents it from expanding into the permissible, fearing that it will demand the forbidden. This is in the matter of the demands of the body. Then he *tazkiyah*s his *nafs*—that is, his heart—here from every illness, so that the illnesses of the heart do not affect his conduct (*sulūk* (سلوك)). He tries to purify the heart from the root of illness; he tries to realize the heart with healthy moral traits, and to give these traits their full scope in his conduct.

In these matters some people confuse [things] in discussion, considering all the demands of the *nafs* as illnesses like the illnesses of the heart. But the matter is not so. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Have you not considered how Allah presents an example—a good word like a good tree, whose root is firmly fixed and its branches [high] in the sky? It produces its fruit all the time, by permission of its Lord. And Allah presents examples for the people that perhaps they will be reminded. And the example of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, not having any stability. [Qur’an, Ibrāhīm 14:24–26]

When the word “*Lā ilāha illa Allāh*” takes its course in the heart, it burns up all the illnesses and creates in the heart lofty moral traits that become manifest in conduct, such as love for Allah, sincerity to Him, fear of Him, and reliance upon Him. And the person’s body and mind are set right upon the methodology of Islam—that is, upon the methodology of *Lā ilāha illa Allāh*. But if the heart contains disbelief, hypocrisy, or wickedness, then the outcome of the heart necessitates effects in a person’s conduct that must appear. So upon disbelief, hypocrisy, or wickedness, for example, comes envy. In the authentic *ḥadīth*: “Faith and envy do not gather together in the heart of a believing servant.” [Reported by al-Nasāʾī; *ṣaḥīḥ*] And envy has its vile stations in human life.

Thus what ensues from neglecting the health of the heart and its illness—that is, from neglecting the *tazkiyah* of the *nafs* and its *mujāhadah* (مجاهدة)—ensues, until sometimes the demands of the *nafs* and the illnesses of the heart become confused with states of the brain, so that there is sometimes contradiction between the self, thought, and conduct. Islam treated all of this with a wise treatment, and thereby found the true human being. Without that there is no human being and no humanity. And so we say: whenever Islam exists, the human being exists; otherwise, no. And the callers to Allah who do not comprehend these meanings are negligent in the most important of matters absolutely.

Sometimes the demands of the body are violent, making control over them difficult, and sometimes they are mild, making control over them easy, and the Muslim is charged in every condition to exert effort to remain upright upon Allah’s command. If he is overcome and falls into sin, he must repent to Allah immediately. The illnesses of the *nafs* are sometimes complex, and sometimes they are simple. Some hearts resist treatment, while some are very responsive to it, quick in absorbing the manifestations of health. The natures of hearts are originally different: a soft heart and a hard heart; and these are multiple topics we shall see. And for a reason the acts of worship have been multiplied and the deeds have been multiplied, and the types of drawing near [to Allah], and in all of that is wisdom. Human life is not set right except by that. And for every diseased condition there is its cure, and for every healthy condition there is its path leading to it and its causes pointing to it.

If we know the issue of the heart and the *nafs*, and when the *nafs* is considered to be the heart and the heart is the *nafs*, and when the heart is other than the *nafs* in terminology; and if we know how we classify the demands of the *nafs* and situate that within the health of the heart and its illness; and if we know the reality of the heart illness and the *nafs* illness; and if we have grasped, in principle, the issue of treatment and the issue of health, and that all of that has its path; and if we have grasped, in principle, the effects of all of that on conduct—if we have grasped that, it becomes possible to build upon this foundation and move to another chapter.
