# Chapter 4: The Nature of the Heart's Journey to Allah

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in the *ḥadīth* previously cited: “The hearts are four: a clear heart in which is a lamp that shines; a sealed heart with its covering tied shut; an inverted heart; and a heart covered over. As for the clear heart, it is the heart of the believer. As for the inverted heart, it is the heart of the hypocrite who knows then denies. As for the heart covered over, it is the heart in which is faith and hypocrisy; the example of faith in it is like the example of a wholesome plant that sweet water nourishes, and the example of hypocrisy in it is like the example of an ulcer that oozes pus and blood. Whichever of the two overwhelms the other overcomes it.”

In this *ḥadīth* is an exposition of the types of human hearts with respect to the matter of faith. It is clear from the *ḥadīth* that the heart of the disbeliever, whose covering is tied shut, and the inverted heart are of no benefit in the matter of faith. The heart in which is a lamp that shines is the desired heart, the sound heart, and it is the ultimate goal of the journey of the wayfarers. It is the objective in the process of rectifying the heart. The heart that is the subject of treatment is the heart in which remains some light of primordial nature (*fiṭrah*), or the heart in which remains some faith. This heart is the subject of treatment, and it is incumbent upon its possessors as an individual obligation (*farḍ ʿayn*) that they travel the path to its rectification and reform.

The first duty incumbent upon these people is the journey toward reforming their hearts until they reach the level of the believing, knowing heart. There is no doubt that the first duty incumbent upon the possessors of the disbelieving heart and the hypocritical heart is Islam and faith, but this is not something for which we entertain hope, for there is no way for these people to listen in the first place, let alone respond. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Indeed, those upon whom the word [of punishment] has come into effect will not believe, even if every sign should come to them, until they see the painful punishment. [Qur’an, Yūnus 10:96–97]

And:

> And you do not guide whom you like, but Allah guides whom He wills. And He is most knowing of the [rightly] guided. [Qur’an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:56]

And:

> You cannot make hear those who are in graves. [Qur’an, Fāṭir 35:22]

So if that is the case, then the first duty incumbent upon those with sick hearts is the reform of their hearts; then preserving them in a certain state by providing them the daily provision and nourishment they require. This is a matter that varies from person to person. Then observing them from time to time by carrying out the renewal of faith within them, and so it continues until death.

No one will be able to preserve the soundness and health of his heart while being negligent in one of the obligatory duties, or while persisting in an evil deed. Observe that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Indeed, my heart is clouded over, and I seek Allah’s forgiveness a hundred times a day.” [Reported by Muslim and Abū Dāwūd] Thus you see that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, would do something in order to keep his heart in a certain state. Then he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Indeed, faith wears out in your hearts as a garment wears out; so ask Allah to renew faith in your hearts.” [Reported by Al-Ṭabarānī in *al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr* and others; it is a *ḥasan ḥadīth*]

And he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Renew your faith.” It was said, “O Messenger of Allah, how do we renew our faith?” He said, “Increase in saying: *Lā ilāha illa Allāh*.” [Reported by Ibn Mājah with a *ḥasan isnād*]

It is through these texts that we realize the truth of what we have said. The first stage is moving the heart from sickness to health. The second stage is giving this heart its daily provision and necessary sustenance at all times, so that the heart remains preserving its lofty state of faith. This is the state of affairs for every human being throughout his entire life until he meets Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty [of death]. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥijr 15:99]

That is, death, for with it matters are unveiled.

## The Path to Rectifying the Heart

The path to rectifying the heart is knowledge, then acting upon Islam. The place of knowledge in action is first. Thus these are three matters: knowledge, acting upon Islam, and the place of knowledge being first.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in the authentic *ḥadīth*: “The similitude of the guidance and knowledge with which Allah sent me is like abundant rain that fell upon the earth. Some of it was good land that absorbed the water and brought forth grass and vegetation in abundance. And some of it was hard ground that retained the water, so Allah benefited the people thereby: they drank from it, gave drink to others, and irrigated crops. And some of it fell upon a portion of it that was low-lying, neither retaining water nor producing vegetation. That is the similitude of the one who understands the religion of Allah and benefits from what Allah sent me with, learning and teaching; and the similitude of the one who does not raise his head to it and does not accept the guidance of Allah which I have been sent with.” [Reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim]

From this *ḥadīth* we realize that the nature of the heart is determined and clarified by its stance toward the knowledge and guidance sent with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. Whether hearts respond to revelation or fail to respond—all of that is known through knowledge. Knowledge is the first means of rectification. Yet hearts differ in their stances. In any case, if the hearts are of the kind that retains water and produces vegetation, or of the kind that neither retains nor produces vegetation but still has faith in them, then there is no escape from a therapeutic rectifying process. Here comes the necessary role of the educator, the saintly guide, the mentor, or the perfected shaykh.

In general, we understand from this *ḥadīth* that knowledge is indispensable; and with knowledge, acting upon Islam is indispensable as a path for the lights of faith to seep gradually into the heart until it is fully illumined. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> The Bedouins say, “We have believed.” Say, “You have not [yet] believed; but say [instead], ‘We have submitted,’ for faith has not yet entered your hearts.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:14]

So faith has not yet entered, but it is on the verge of entering due to Islam and the practices of Islam. Every Islamic deed a person performs, if the intention in it is sound, has its light that seeps into the heart.

If we now imagine the heart of a person in which there is faith and hypocrisy, and imagine that this person has cut off the extension of hypocrisy from his heart by abandoning wickedness and the gatherings of disbelievers, and by abandoning sins; and imagine that this person has turned eagerly and actively to the works of Islam—prayer, *zakāh*, fasting, *jihād*, remembrance (*dhikr*), recitation of the Qur’an, and the like—such a person will not tarry long before his heart becomes illumined and quickly reaches the believing heart in which is a lamp that shines. All the obligatory duties are indispensable as a path in this rectifying process, and among the obligatory duties is prayer, which is remembrance, but the door of remembrance is broader.

Remembrance has a lofty station in the matter of hearts. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured. [Qur’an, Al-Raʿd 13:28]

But reaching the state in which remembrance gives the heart tranquility is considered an advanced station in the journey of faith. Hence His saying, the Exalted:

> Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured. [Qur’an, Al-Raʿd 13:28]

Much philosophy without much action is of no avail. By the measure of a person’s eagerness for knowledge and action—especially remembrance—he is able to traverse great stages. For this reason we observe that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions were charged with the first verses of *Sūrat al-Muzzammil* for an entire year:

> O you who wraps himself [in clothing], arise [to pray] the night, except a little—half of it—or subtract from it a little, or add to it, and recite the Qur’an with measured recitation. Indeed, We will cast upon you a heavy word. Indeed, the hours of the night are more effective for concurrence [of heart and tongue] and more suitable for words. Indeed, for you by day is prolonged occupation. And remember the name of your Lord and devote yourself to Him with [complete] devotion. [Qur’an, Al-Muzzammil 73:1–8]

For the one who approaches his prayers, obligatory and supererogatory, with many regular devotional recitations (*awrād*), remembrance, and Qur’an recitation, along with knowledge and the fulfillment of all timely obligatory duties—something of this kind greatly shortens the Muslim’s journey to the rectification of his heart, according to the effort and energy he expends. “The tiller tills neither a tract nor a back [of land].”

When he reaches the tranquility, life, and illumination of the heart, he must preserve that state and increase the luminosity of his heart by maintaining a minimum of multiple devotional practices sufficient for his heart’s needs. These needs vary according to people. A person compelled to mix with corrupt or disbelieving environments has different needs of the heart than one who lives night and day in the environment of the mosque and in the company of the righteous. For this reason we observe that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, did not prescribe many types of remembrances and devotional practices for people; rather, after the obligatory duties and obligations, he left the choice to the individual regarding recommended practices, which are numerous. But every Muslim must observe the state of his heart at intervals and find his faith by turning to the complete creed of monotheism (*kalimat al-tawḥīd*). For this reason we observe that Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has prescribed for us annual obligatory duties such as fasting and *zakāh*, and some lifelong ones such as *ḥajj*. All of this has wisdom in the matter of preserving, renewing, vitalizing, and rectifying faith.

## Common Errors in This Domain

Within the spheres we have mentioned, many errors occur that many people fall into. Let us try to identify some of these errors.

### First

Whenever Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, commands a person with something or forbids him something, that is by wisdom and benefit for the person. Everything Allah has prescribed for man and legislated for him is that in which lies his cure and healing. If it happens that a person neglects one of the commands, corruption must ensue in himself and in those around him. This is one aspect. The second aspect is that there is nothing Allah has legislated except that it is by wisdom; if a person fails to realize the wisdom from carrying out the command, corruption will ensue in himself and in those around him.

Let us give examples that clarify the intended meaning: Allah has prescribed prayer, *zakāh*, fasting, *ḥajj*, *jihād*, lawful earning, maintaining ties of kinship, honoring parents, and other obligatory duties. Every obligatory duty addresses man; if he performs it, a benefit follows that is not realized except through it, and if he abandons it, a corruption follows that is not removed except by establishing it. Thus this fighting in the cause of Allah, when it becomes an obligatory duty, what follows from abandoning it? Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, says:

> What is [the matter] with you? Then, when you are told, “Go forth in the cause of Allah,” you adhere heavily to the earth? Are you satisfied with the life of this world rather than the Hereafter? But what is the enjoyment of worldly life compared to the Hereafter except a [very] little. [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:38]

And:

> If you do not go forth, He will punish you with a painful punishment and will replace you with another people, and you will not harm Him at all. [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:39]

So where there is no fighting in the cause of Allah, there is corruption and severing of kinship. So it is for every obligatory duty neglected, and for every prohibited act committed: corruption must follow. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Fight them until there is no [more] fitnah and [until] worship is [acknowledged to be] for Allah. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:193]

Then every duty Allah has legislated is by wisdom. This prayer—Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, said about it:

> Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing. [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45]

And He, the Exalted, said:

> And establish prayer for My remembrance. [Qur’an, Ṭā Hā 20:14]

So when a person performs prayer while heedless of the remembrance of Allah, and his prayer does not prevent him from immorality and wrongdoing, he has not fulfilled the wisdom of prayer. And likewise for every obligatory duty. This fasting—Allah has legislated it as a path leading to piety and restraint of the self. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:183]

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever does not abandon false speech and acting by it, Allah has no need of him abandoning his food and drink.” [Reported by al-Bukhārī] If a person fasts but does not realize the wisdom of Allah for which He legislated fasting, he has not established the duty in its full measure.

From this we realize that educators who do not cultivate the realization of the wisdom for which the command or prohibition was given are deficient; and neither the individual himself, nor the lives of people, can be set right with their deficiency. In our subject at hand, rectification of the human heart cannot take place at all through such negligence.

In neglecting this matter lies one of the errors of some who claim to undertake direction and education among the Sufis and others; consequently they do not reform hearts by their hands, even if they make broad claims, and by that they deceive themselves, their disciples, and listeners.

For the Muslim to have a stance toward everything—positive or negative—is a duty of his time: he is against disbelief, its people, and its system; and with Islam, its people, and its system. He gives his loyalty to Muslims and withholds it from disbelievers. He strives so that the word of Allah may be the highest, and that cannot be except if Islam is dominant and Muslims are ruling. All these are obligatory duties. So when you find an educator who educates to neglect them, indeed to make war on their people, how can a person’s heart be set right with such neglect? These people do not reform hearts; rather, they corrupt minds, hearts, souls, bodies, and individuals.

How far are these from any divine or prophetic upbringing! Were the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, neutral in the conflict between disbelief and Islam? Were the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, permitted to see manifest disbelief and not work to repel it? What did Abū Bakr do regarding apostasy? And now this apostasy is widespread everywhere, as if the world for some Muslims is at the height of Islam! If only these people contented themselves with the stance of the helpless and acknowledged the gravity of the matter; but along with their helplessness they educate to helplessness, philosophize for it, and make war on those who bear the burden of struggle with disbelief and its people—and what a harsh struggle it is!

In this they are no more than examples to whom these verses apply:

> Among you are those who lag behind, and if a misfortune befalls you, they say, “Allah has favored me in that I was not present with them.” But if bounty comes to you from Allah, he will surely say, as if there had never been between you and him affection, “Oh, I wish I had been with them so I could have attained a great attainment.” [Qur’an, Al-Nisāʾ 4:72–73]

And:

> Those [hypocrites] who remained behind rejoiced at their staying [at home] after [the departure of] the Messenger of Allah and hated to strive with their wealth and their lives in the cause of Allah and said, “Do not go forth in the heat.” Say, “The fire of Hell is more intense in heat”—if they would but understand. So let them laugh a little and weep much as recompense for what they used to earn. [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:81–82]

And:

> If it comes to you, they say, “Did we not seek to prevent you?” And they consider themselves pure and say, “Indeed, you are disbelievers.” [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:86]

And:

> They will say, “Did we not take you as a laughingstock and mock you?” [Qur’an, Al-Muʾminūn 23:110]

Indeed, the heart can only be sound within the framework we have mentioned: the complete application of the obligatory duties along with realizing the wisdoms for which Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, legislated them. Whoever does not understand the matter thus is in grave error in understanding the issue of the sound heart.

### Second

Among the major errors committed by some who undertake the work of rectifying hearts is that many of them are unaware that one of the conditions for the soundness or rectification of the heart is abandoning specific meanings, and that one of its conditions is realizing specific meanings. Remembrance in its various forms, and the works of Islam in their various forms, are all matters related to the rectification of the heart; and avoiding negligence in carrying out commands and prohibitions is a condition for the soundness and rectification of the heart.

At this point, some people are heedless of obvious matters. To clarify this point, let us review some meanings.

#### A

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> O Messenger, let them not grieve you who hasten into disbelief—[such as] those who say with their mouths, “We believe,” but their hearts do not believe, and those [Jews] who are Judaizers. They are [vain] listeners to falsehood, devourers of [what is] unlawful. If they come to you, [O Muhammad], judge between them or turn away from them. And if you turn away from them—never will they harm you at all. And if you judge, judge between them with justice. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly. But how is it that they come to you for judgement while they have the Torah, in which is the judgement of Allah? Then they turn away, [even] after that; but those are not [in fact] believers. Indeed, We sent down the Torah, in which was guidance and light. The prophets who submitted [to Allah] judged by it for the Jews, as did the rabbis and scholars by that with which they were entrusted of the Scripture of Allah, and they were witnesses thereto. So do not fear the people but fear Me, and do not exchange My verses for a small price. And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed—then it is those who are the disbelievers. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:41–44]

And Allah says:

> They are listeners to falsehood, devourers of [what is] forbidden. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:42]

Here is a sickness with which healing of the heart is impossible. Here is a person disposed to accept lies, disposed to spy on Muslims on behalf of the disbelievers, a helper to falsehood. How many simple people follow false rumors and believe them against Muslims! “Listeners to a people other”—and how many volunteer to carry news of the believers to the disbelievers! These are the ones of whom Allah said: “Those are the ones Allah has not intended to purify their hearts.”

From this example we realize that the issue of the soundness of the heart has its negative conditions and its positive conditions, but few are those who realize this.

#### B

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And Allah had already taken a covenant from the Children of Israel, and We sent among them messengers. Whenever there came to them a messenger with what their souls did not desire, a party [of messengers] they denied, and another party they killed. And they thought there would be no [resulting] punishment, so they became blind and deaf. Then Allah turned to them in forgiveness; then [again] many of them became blind and deaf. And Allah is Seeing of what they do. They have certainly disbelieved who say, “Allah is the Messiah, the son of Mary.” Say, “Then who could prevent Allah at all if He had intended to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, or his mother or everyone on the earth?” And to Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and whatever is between them. He creates what He wills, and Allah is over all things competent. O People of the Scripture, do not commit excess in your religion or say about Allah except the truth. The Messiah, Jesus, the son of Mary, was but a messenger of Allah and His word which He directed to Mary and a spirit [created at a command] from Him. So believe in Allah and His messengers. And do not say, “Three”; desist—it is better for you. Indeed, Allah is but one God. Exalted is He above having a son. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. And sufficient is Allah as Disposer of affairs. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:70–73]

Then Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And He cast into their hearts [rid of] creatures—because they had broken the covenant of Allah—and disbelieved in His signs—and disobeyed the messenger—and took [his orders] in defiance. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:13]

Note that the hardness of their hearts here was a punishment for deficiency in the covenant with respect to specific meanings. What are these meanings? Establishing prayer, giving *zakāh*, faith in the messengers, supporting them, and lending Allah a goodly loan. And observe that Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, made their saying, “We hear and obey,” a covenant and pact. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And remember the favor of Allah upon you and His covenant with which He bound you when you said, “We hear and we obey.” [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:7]

So let us ask ourselves: which thing was pledged to the Children of Israel in this verse that is not pledged to us? Prayer, *zakāh*, faith in the messengers, supporting them, or lending to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic?

#### C

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> O Prophet, indeed We have sent you as a witness and a bringer of good tidings and a warner, and one who invites to Allah, by His permission, and an illuminating lamp. [Qur’an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:45–46]

And He says:

> And one who invites to Allah, by His permission, and an illuminating lamp. [Qur’an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:46]

And He, the Exalted, says:

> So [Allah] has sent you as a bringer of good tidings and a warner. [Qur’an, Al-Fatḥ 48:8]

And:

> O Messenger, announce that which has been revealed to you from your Lord, and if you do not, then you have not conveyed His message. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:67]

So if those occupied with rectifying hearts had not observed something as clear as supporting the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him—by supporting his sharīʿah, supporting his sunnah, supporting his religion, and supporting the bearers of his sharīʿah—how would the rectification of the heart be accomplished with such a state? From this example we also realize that the issue of the soundness of the heart has its negative and positive conditions.

Perhaps from this example and the one before it we understand that among the first conditions for the soundness of the heart is belonging to the Islamic rank, and being with it and from it—and by the Islamic rank we mean everyone who carries Islam and works for it—so that we are in the rank that fights the enemies of Allah instead of being a help to them and spies upon the Muslims. Belonging to the Islamic rank is the correct path to supporting the messengers, peace and blessings be upon them. For this reason there is no soundness of the heart if belonging is absent. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “It is better for you that you cling to the congregation of Muslims and their imām.” [Reported by al-Bukhārī and Muslim] Ibn Masʿūd, may Allah be pleased with him, explained this as meaning “truth, even if you are alone.”

So when we find people fighting gathering upon the truth and supporting it, that is an error—and how can hearts be sound with that? Some considered Ḥasan al-Bannā, may Allah have mercy on him, mistaken because he intervened in politics, as if Muslims had a choice, while all disbelieving currents gather to reach power in order to realize their disbelieving aims that include the elimination of Islam. As if Muslims had the choice—and the matter is such—not to gather upon Islam in order to support it and prevent its elimination. As if these people have not understood the most obvious basics of Islam, which say that the word of Allah must be the highest, and that the Muslim must walk the path of that; and how can the word of Allah be the highest if Muslims do not work for that by that path? Every disbelieving current works to reach power in an age when power has come to interfere in the small and the great. To whom do we entrust the survival and preservation of Islam when Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, says:

> And if Allah had willed, He could have made you [of one religion], but He causes to stray whom He wills and guides whom He wills. [Qur’an, Al-Naḥl 16:93]

And:

> And We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you [for the cause of Allah] and the patient, and We will test your affairs. [Qur’an, Muḥammad 47:31]

Or do we want to say as the Children of Israel said to Moses:

> Go, you and your Lord, and fight. Indeed, we are remaining right here. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:24]

Indeed, rectifying the heart is one of the essential missions of the messengers. So if those who want to assume the station of the prophets do so without paying the price for it, how catastrophic the disaster! Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Allah has already given you victory in many regions; and on the Day of Ḥunayn, when your great number pleased you, but it did not avail you at all. [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:25]

So many messengers fought—and I have seen some who claim to be walking the path of rectifying hearts considering killing a sign of deficiency. Do these people have any sense? This one was killed, and that one was killed, and ʿAlī was killed, and Ṭalḥah was killed, and al-Zubayr was killed—were these people not perfect, and are those who sit back from *jihād* the complete ones? Is this education of hearts or corruption of both? We seek refuge in Allah from going astray or leading others astray.

#### D

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in the *ḥadīth* reported by Imam Aḥmad: “Were it not that hearts would become hard, and you would have been bored by the *ḥadīth*, you would have heard what I hear.” And he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “For the hard heart is far from Allah.” And Allah, the Exalted, said about those whose hearts He did not intend to purify, and those previously mentioned: “Listeners to falsehood, devourers of [what is] forbidden,” that is, of the unlawful and bribery.

These texts and their likes indicate the numerous negative and positive conditions for the soundness of the heart: distance from unlawful earnings, distance from excess in speech, and other matters. Many of those who busy themselves with educating people are not aware of all this.

### Third

The heart does not reach the state of being a believer with pure faith, in which is a lamp that shines, except by reaching a pure, experiential, heartfelt knowledge of Allah. By the measure of a person’s knowledge of Allah, he increases in submission to His rulings, application of them, commitment to them, and taking them with strength—whatever results from that in terms of breaking habits, pressure of circumstances, or deviation of authority. Full reception from Allah, devotion to His sharīʿah, and taking His Book with strength: that is what the rectification of the heart requires. For that reason the messengers and the believers were addressed with it. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Follow, [O mankind], what has been revealed to you from your Lord and do not follow other than Him any protectors. Little do you remember. [Qur’an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:3]

And:

> Say, “O People of the Scripture, you are [standing] on nothing until you uphold [the law of] the Torah, the Gospel, and what has been revealed to you from your Lord.” [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:68]

And:

> Then is it other than Allah’s religion that they desire, while to Him have submitted [all] those in the heavens and earth, willingly or by compulsion, and to Him they will be returned? [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:83]

And Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, said to His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him:

> Thus We have made you a median [i.e., just] community that you will be witnesses over the people and the Messenger will be a witness over you. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:143]

And:

> We have certainly created man into hardship. [Qur’an, Al-Balad 90:4]

And:

> [And mention] when your Lord took from the children of Adam—from their loins—their descendants and made them testify of themselves, “Am I not your Lord?” They said, “Yes, we have testified.” [Qur’an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:172]

And:

> And He found you lost and guided [you], and He found you poor and made [you] self-sufficient. [Qur’an, Al-Ḍuḥā 93:7–8]

And:

> And We have enjoined upon man, to his parents, good treatment. [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:8]

All this points to the fact that reaching the knowing heart is preceded by full reception from Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. From this we understand the error of those who imagine that the journey to the knowledge of Allah does not require acting upon the rulings, and then imagine that if a person reaches knowledge, it does not matter if he is negligent in the rulings. That is the great error. The Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, were the most knowledgeable of Allah’s creation about Allah, yet despite that they were the most intense of people in *jihād* in His cause and the most committed to the rulings of His sharīʿah. Things must be put in their proper places in all these matters.

The author of *al-Risālah al-Qushayriyyah* mentioned: Two of the great Sufis were in battle against the people of disbelief. One of them turned to the other and said, “Do you now feel the pleasure of your heart that you felt on your wedding night?” He said, “Yes.” Then he said, “As for me, it is likewise.” Look, by Allah, at the state of this class of Sufis, whose hearts were renewed! Recall the state of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, when one of them saw the sweetest days of his life as the day of *jihād*. Khālid, may Allah be pleased with him, said: “No night in which I take my wife as a bride is dearer to me than a day of severe cold on which I stand at the head of a squadron of cavalry that charges or is charged against in the way of Allah, or a day of the most severe heat and hardship upon Islam and the Muslims.”

In brief, we say: The heart’s journey means reaching pure faith and complete knowledge of Allah. It has its negative and positive path. All of that is a prelude to the complete and powerful taking of the sharīʿah of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, establishing His rulings, and making the word of Allah the highest. Between the beginning and the end there is deficiency, negligence, error, and inclination, and we ask Allah to inspire us with guidance and make us among the workers.
