# Chapter 13: Cardiac and Psychological Health

## Its Place among the Circles of Religious Obligation

Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says concerning reaching Allah, the Mighty and Majestic: “Your reaching Allah is your reaching the knowledge of Him.” This is what reaching means: that you know Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, with true knowledge—a knowledge in which the intellect takes its share, the heart (*qalb*, قلب) takes its share, and the spirit (*rūḥ*, روح) takes its share, without that being accompanied by *tajassum* (corporeality), *tashbīh* (likening), *mumāsah* (contact), *ittiṣāl* (connection), *ḥulūl* (indwelling), or *ittiḥād* (union). It is a knowledge in which a human being witnesses his nearness to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and Allah’s nearness to him. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And when My servants ask you, [O Muhammad], concerning Me—indeed I am near. I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls upon Me. So let them respond to Me [by obedience] and believe in Me that they may be [rightly] guided. [Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:186]

So when you know Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, with true knowledge—a knowledge in which rational submission and spiritual taste (*dhawq*, ذوق) are joined together—then you have reached Him; and that is not completed without traveling the path to that.

When reaching Allah is realized, it has many fruits, for every good is but an outflow of this knowledge. Have you not seen how Allah has set forth a good word like a good tree, whose root is firm and whose branches are in the sky, giving its fruit at all times by permission of its Lord? And Allah sets forth parables for mankind so that they may remember. The fruits of true knowledge of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, are so many that they cannot be counted; and the first of them is realization of the station of servitude to Him, which is the highest of stations absolutely. Servitude (*ʿubūdiyyah*, عبودية), as its name indicates, requires outward and inward obedience to Allah in everything.

Reaching Allah means knowing Him, Exalted be His Majesty: knowing that He exists; knowing His attributes—the attributes of majesty and beauty, and the attributes of existence such as knowledge, power, will, life, hearing, sight, and speech—and the negative attributes by which He is exalted above what does not befit His Essence; knowing His names and His acts; and that the heart receives all of this and feels it as taste. This is a meaning beyond mere rational knowledge, although rational knowledge is the ordinary prelude to it. Included in knowledge of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, is knowing the meanings of the ambiguous texts and bearing them upon their correct interpretations; and the wayfarers have their own tastes of these texts while declaring Him transcendent.

In these stations many go astray and many are misguided; the expressions of many fall short, and the expressions of many are understood contrary to their intent. But the one whom Allah opens in these matters a door of understanding and taste in accordance with knowledge is able to distinguish error from correctness, and able to distinguish between what must be rejected of these expressions and the correct interpretations of them. Often we find a man construing a word as disbelief while it has a correct interpretation, and often we find a man defending a word that has no sound aspect but is rather heresy, disbelief, or misguidance. Those whom Allah has especially favored are the ones who put things in their proper places and reach a state in which their knowledge of Him is complete, so that when they speak of that they speak with truth and knowledge. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Exalted is Allah above what they describe—except the chosen slaves of Allah [who do not share in that sin]. [Quran, As-Saffat 37:159–160]

The taste of the wayfarers for the meaning of Allah’s name the First, His name the Manifest, His name the Hidden, His name the Self-Sufficient (*al-Ṣamad*, الصمد), His name the Near, and His other names is far deeper than any rational taste. Those who are steeped in these meanings perceive things that others do not perceive and express them in a way that others cannot. By this I mean the realized ones among them, the subtle ones, whose expressions are bound by knowledge and the texts. As for those who distort and change, they are not the ones intended in this station. Perhaps the first fruit of knowledge is mastery of lofty, pure expression. In this connection Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says:

> “The lights of the wise precede their words; where illumination occurs, expression reaches. Every speech that emerges carries the garb of the heart from which it emerged. If one is permitted to express, his expression is understood by the ears of creation and his allusion is made clear to them. Sometimes realities appear veiled of lights if you are not permitted to disclose them. Their expressions are either an overflow of ecstatic finding or a purposeful guidance of a disciple. The first is the state of wayfarers, the second the state of masters of stability and realization; and expression is effective according to the aptitude of the listener.” [Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh al-Iskandarī, *al-Ḥikam*]

Thus one of the first fruits of complete reaching of Allah is the ability to express correctly the Divine Essence and to indicate it correctly. Look at such expressions to see clearly the reality of this fruit. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said:

> “You are with the cosmos as long as you do not witness the Creator; when you witness Him, the cosmos is with you. The establishment of particularity does not negate being described with humanity; rather, particularity is like the radiance of the sun of day appearing in the horizon though it is not of it. At times the suns of His attributes rise upon the night of your existence, and at times He withholds that from you and returns you to your limits; the day is not from you to you, but it comes upon you. Infer from the existence of His effects the existence of His names, from the existence of His names the establishment of His attributes, and from the existence of His attributes the existence of His Essence, for it is impossible for an attribute to subsist by itself.” [Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh al-Iskandarī, *al-Ḥikam*]

This reaching of Allah, some of whose fruits we have seen, is the highest manifestation of cardiac and psychological health in Islam, and it is the chief sign of this health; but this reaching gives rise to many matters that are all signs of the health of the heart and *nafs*. How, then, do we reach Allah? And once we reach Him, what flows from that reaching?

We have already spoken of litanies (*awrād*, أوراد), spiritual struggle (*mujāhadah*, مجاهدة), the allotment of time, and avoiding the forbidden things; all of these are factors that assist in reaching Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. Here we shall speak of four stages, each stage leading to what follows it; when we know them, we shall know the nature of religious obligation:

1. A stage of carrying out commands and avoiding prohibitions.
2. A stage of realizing the wisdom behind the command and the prohibition.
3. A stage of illumining the heart.
4. A stage of the outpouring of a certain conduct as an effect of that.

Reaching Allah is a bridge between what precedes it and what follows it; meanings lead up to it and meanings flow from it. Therefore we have given precedence to speaking of it and some of its fruits, and here we set forth the matter in fuller detail.

There are divine commands, and every command has its wisdom. We have previously mentioned that every act of worship has its wisdom and its lights, and that the Muslim must act as commanded and realize the wisdom for the sake of which the command was given.

Carrying out the command and realizing its wisdom result in effects in the heart and in the *nafs*. The function of these effects is the perfection of the self and ascent through realization of the highest perfections. As a Muslim, just as I must act, I must observe the wisdom of the action, and I must pursue the perfection of my self as the goal of action. Many people remain in the first circle with weakness in it, without passing on to the last two circles. Some may perceive the second circle, but do not pass to the third, let alone a fourth circle.

The obscurity of these matters is the cause of lack of aspiration toward them; therefore they need complete clarity. Let us pause a while over some meanings so that these stations may become clear. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Indeed, mankind was created anxious: When evil touches him, impatient, and when good touches him, withholding [of it], except the observers of prayer—those who are constant in their prayer, and those within whose wealth is a known right for the petitioner and the deprived, and those who believe in the Day of Recompense, and those who are fearful of the punishment of their Lord—indeed, the punishment of their Lord is not that from which one is safe—and those who guard their private parts except from their wives or those their right hands possess, for indeed, they are not to be blamed. But whoever seeks beyond that, then they are the transgressors, and those who are to their trusts and promises attentive, and those who are in their testimonies upright, and those who [carefully] maintain their prayer: They will be in gardens, honored. [Quran, Al-Maʿārij 70:19–35]

Notice that the trait of anxiousness (*hulūʿ*), whose outward signs are panic at misfortune and withholding at prosperity, is not removed from a person unless a group of matters come together in him: prayer, spending, belief in the Hereafter, fear of Allah’s punishment, guarding the private parts, and bearing witness truthfully and justly. When these matters come together in a person, he is delivered from disease and realizes health. So whenever a person realizes the totality of these meanings, the trait of anxiousness automatically vanishes from him and he acquires the moral qualities of patience and generosity. Realization of patience and generosity is the sign that all these meanings have been established. We are charged with the whole of this: charged with these works, charged with patience, and charged with generosity. Just as it is incumbent upon me as a Muslim to exert effort in action in order to establish prayer, so it is incumbent upon me to realize myself in patience and generosity through struggle of the *nafs* and knowledge of the limits of patience and generosity. My realization of patience and generosity is a manifestation of the health of the heart and *nafs*, and a sign of the health of my path; but patience and generosity require a special exertion, for Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, says:

> And if a woman fears from her husband contempt or evasion, there is no sin upon them if they make terms of settlement between them—and settlement is best. And present in [human] souls is stinginess. But if you do good and fear Allah—then indeed Allah is ever, with what you do, Acquainted. [Quran, An-Nisāʾ 4:128]

There is no state in which stinginess is not present, and its possessor must overcome his stinginess by struggling his *nafs* and traveling the path that leads to that. But how many people begin that beginning and reach that end?

Notice now how many evil effects follow from failure to reach the stations of patience and generosity: there is nothing after patience except disbelief; for without patience there is no faith, and without faith there is no Islam at all. And when stinginess exists, it is no longer possible for there to be cooperation among people on any matter; rather, the very possibility of collective work is nullified. Therefore the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> “When you see stinginess obeyed, caprice followed, worldly life preferred, and every holder of an opinion pleased with his own opinion, then tend to your own self and leave the common people alone; for there are days ahead in which patience will be like grasping live coals. The one who acts rightly during that period will have the reward of fifty men who act as he does.” It was said, “O Messenger of Allah, fifty of us or fifty of them?” He said, “Rather, fifty of you.” [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Trials, Hadith 4341; Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī, Chapters on Tafsir, Hadith 3058; classified *ḥasan gharīb*]

Notice that obeyed stinginess is the first link of diseases; when it exists, a person may well withdraw from people, for there is no benefit in collective work at all.

From the example mentioned we understand how there is a command, a wisdom behind that command, psychological effects that result from it, and how we are charged with all of this. The third of these circles is what we call psychological and cardiac health. Let us give another example. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Indeed, Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties [in exchange] for that they will have Paradise. They fight in the cause of Allah, so they kill and are killed. [It is] a true promise [binding] upon Him in the Torah and the Gospel and the Qur’an. And who is truer to his covenant than Allah? So rejoice in your transaction which you have contracted. And it is that which is the great attainment. [Such believers are] the repentant, the worshippers, the praisers [of Allah], the travelers [for His cause], those who bow and prostrate [in prayer], those who enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong, and those who observe the limits [set by] Allah. And give good tidings to the believers. [Quran, At-Tawbah 9:111–112]

Notice from the two verses that the coming together of the qualities of faith, repentance, worship, praise, traveling (*siyāḥah*, which is fasting or journeying for Allah’s sake), bowing, prostration, enjoining good, and forbidding evil is what results in selling the self and wealth to Allah; so there is no complete *jihād* unless all these meanings are present. I, as a Muslim, am required to realize these qualities and required to sell my self to Allah. If a person acts according to these and then does not sell his self and wealth to Allah, he has fallen short in the duty.

There are works from which a psychological state flows, and from this psychological state works and behaviors flow. This is the fourth circle of religious obligation, and it is what flows from cardiac health. From the two examples we understand that there are works that require the existence of a psychological and cardiac state; this state is what we are charged with, just as we are charged with the works that flow from it. This psychological and cardiac state that we are charged with is the healthy condition of the *nafs* and the heart. Its existence is the sign of health and the sign of uprightness of journeying; and many Muslims are heedless of the matter of psychological and cardiac health in all its dimensions, just as they are heedless of many of the works that lead to it or flow from it—and that is a dangerous point. Until now the thing we intend is not clear, so let us give further examples.

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Recite, [O Muhammad], what has been revealed to you of the Book and establish prayer. Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater. And Allah knows that which you do. [Quran, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45]

And He says:

> Indeed, I am Allah. There is no deity except Me, so worship Me and establish prayer for My remembrance. [Quran, Ṭā-Hā 20:14]

Among the effects of prayer is leaving immorality and wrongdoing, and the goal of establishing prayer is the remembrance of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, in the way Allah has chosen for us. Remembrance, in turn, has the effect of giving the heart tranquility. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured. [Quran, Ar-Raʿd 13:28]

Tranquility of the heart is its healthy state, and we are charged with reaching it. The path to that is remembrance, and among remembrance is prayer; we are charged with that. And among the practical effects of prayer is desisting from immorality and wrongdoing, and we are charged with that. The four circles—among which is cardiac and psychological health—we are charged with all of them, and upon us is to attain them in knowledge and action.

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous. [Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:183]

Fasting is an obligation, and the wisdom of this obligation is reaching *taqwā* (تقوى). *Taqwā* is a moral quality in the heart from which a certain conduct flows, and we are charged with the whole. One part of this whole is cardiac, psychological, spiritual, and mental health, from which a certain conduct flows and which results from a certain possession. In one or another of these circles a kind of deficiency or shortcoming sometimes occurs.

If these matters are clear, let us now try to discuss meanings through which we may understand what is intended by cardiac, psychological, and spiritual health after we have known its place among the circles of religious obligation.

Observe that the Quran has spoken of the *nafs* in one place:

> And I do not acquit myself. Indeed, the soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil, except those upon which my Lord has mercy. Indeed, my Lord is Forgiving and Merciful. [Quran, Yūsuf 12:53]

This is a diseased state of the *nafs*. And in another place:

> And I swear by the reproaching soul [to the certainty of resurrection]. [Quran, Al-Qiyāmah 75:2]

This is a higher state of the *nafs*, for it reproaches its possessor for evil when he commits it. And He said:

> [To the righteous it will be said,] “O reassured soul.” [Quran, Al-Fajr 89:27]

Here is a yet higher state of the *nafs*, for it has taken its share of tranquility and certainty. Notice that the reassured *nafs* is the one to whom it is said:

> Return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him]. [Quran, Al-Fajr 89:28]

This indicates that the reassured *nafs* is the one with whom Allah is pleased and whom He will please. Therefore the reassured *nafs* is the highest healthy state of the *nafs*. The path to this reassured *nafs* is what Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, said:

> And those who disbelieved say, “Why has a sign not been sent down to him from his Lord?” Say, [O Muhammad], “Indeed, Allah leaves astray whom He wills and guides to Himself whoever turns back [to Him]—those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured.” Those who have believed and done righteous deeds—a good state is theirs and a good return. [Quran, Ar-Raʿd 13:27–29]

The path to the reassured *nafs* is turning in penitence to Allah, faith, and much remembrance; and we are charged with all of that. This is a model of psychological and cardiac health and of the path that leads to it. Let us now make a brief excursion and then return to the original subject.

The *ṣūfiyyah* speak of something called a *ḥāl* (حال, transient spiritual state) and of something called a *maqām* (مقام, spiritual station). They consider the *ḥāl* to be the prelude to the *maqām*. For example, when a person first begins to occupy himself with remembrance, he reaches a temporary tranquility of the heart that soon passes away; this is a *ḥāl*. If the person persists in remembrance, he reaches a lasting tranquility of the heart; this is a *maqām*. We are charged, in every manifestation of cardiac and psychological health, to reach the *maqām* so that we may abide in it; but many are unaware of the nature of the stations of health, just as they are unaware of the work required for them.

We are required to be forbearing except when the sanctities of Allah are violated; then we are required that nothing stand before our anger until we establish Allah’s command. Such was the character of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: he did not become angry for himself, but he became angry when the sanctities of Allah were violated. When the sanctities of Allah were violated, nothing could stand before his anger. Thus there are two stations: the station of forbearance and the station of anger for Allah’s sake. Forbearance does not come all at once; rather, forbearance comes through forbearance. When a person begins to struggle against his anger, he fails at one time and succeeds at another. Here forbearance is a *ḥāl* until the person comes to be of the station of forbearance, so that he becomes angry only where it is religiously obligatory for him to be angry. Then he abides in the station of forbearance and is in a healthy cardiac and psychological state.

How numerous are the cardiac and psychological moral qualities with which we are charged! When the totality of these moral qualities become stations in us and we abide in them, then we have possessed cardiac and psychological health, which is one of the four circles of religious obligation with which we are charged.

We have said previously that in the religion of Allah there are stations: Islam, faith (*īmān*, إيمان), excellence (*iḥsān*, إحسان), *taqwā*, and gratitude (*shukr*, شكر). Gratitude has a cardiac side and another practical side; likewise Islam and faith. When a person attains the cardiac side of these stations, that is a sign of the health of the heart, mind, and *nafs*; and this is one of the circles of religious obligation.

Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And [mention] when your Lord took from the children of Adam—from their loins—their descendants and made them testify of themselves, [saying to them], “Am I not your Lord?” They said, “Yes, we have testified.” [This]—lest you should say on the Day of Resurrection, “Indeed, we were of this unaware.” [Quran, Al-Aʿrāf 7:172]

The spirit acknowledges servitude to Allah; therefore by the measure of a person’s realization of servitude to Allah outwardly and inwardly is the health of his heart. Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, created Adam in His image, that is, in His attribute, as the majority of scholars have said. Therefore by the measure that a person takes his share of Allah’s names while realizing servitude and not contending with Allah, Exalted be His Majesty, in what belongs to His sole province, is the sign of health.

Compassion in its place, mercy in its place, generosity in its place, pardon in its place, humbling whom deserves to be humbled, and honoring whom deserves to be honored—all of this is required of us and is realization of Allah’s names together with servitude. But majesty and greatness belong to Allah alone, for they are among the characteristics of Lordship; so if a person contends with the Lord of Majesty in the characteristics of Lordship, that is disease. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in the *ḥadīth qudsī*:

> “Might is My cloak and greatness is My garment; whoever contends with Me in any of them I shall punish.” [Al-Bayhaqī in his *Musʿtakhraj*; also narrated by others; the *ḥadīth* is *ṣaḥīḥ*]

Realization of what should be realized, and abandoning what should not be a manifestation of cardiac, psychological, and spiritual health in a person: Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has made it obligatory upon you that your heart realize certain meanings, and forbidden to you that it contain other meanings. That your heart be thus—negatively and positively—is the sign of health. He has made it obligatory upon you that there be no affection in your heart for the disbelievers, and obligatory upon you that you love Him, love His Messenger, and love the people of faith. He has made it obligatory upon you that you fear none but Him, and obligatory upon you that you fear and revere Him alone. He has made it obligatory upon you that you hope in Him, and obligatory upon you that you do not despair of His mercy. He has made it obligatory upon you that you do not feel secure from His devising, and obligatory upon you that you do not be arrogant or exultant. Every inward act that He has made obligatory must be realized by you, and every inward act that He has forbidden must be abandoned; that is the sign of health. He has made patience, surrender, contentment, and trust (*tawakkul*, توكل) obligatory; if you realize all of this, that is a sign of health. He has made it obligatory upon you to polish the mirror of your heart and to clear the eye of your inner sight; He requires you to contemplate the signs of your heart, to behold His acts, and to feel His attributes. Whenever you realize all of this, it is among the signs of health; and none of it will be completed except through much remembrance, abundant knowledge, comprehensive spiritual struggle, and constant review with the people of this path.

And the root of roots from which everything flows is deepening *tawḥīd* (توحيد) in the heart. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> He sends down the angels, with the inspiration of His command, upon whom He wills of His servants, [telling them], “Warn that there is no deity except Me; so fear Me.” [Quran, An-Naḥl 16:2]

Notice that revelation descends only to warn of Allah’s oneness, so that commitment to His *taqwā* may follow. Therefore the deeper *tawḥīd* becomes in the heart, the more every good follows from it; and *tawḥīd* is not deepened except by remembrance. Indeed, all the remembrances are nothing but deepening the matter of *tawḥīd*. “Glory be to Allah” declares His transcendence; “praise be to Allah” acknowledges that He alone is the Bestower; “Allah is the Greatest” negates the magnifying of anything else in the heart; and “there is no power nor strength except with Allah” negates that there is any doer besides Him.

So has the meaning of cardiac and psychological health for the Muslim, and its place among the circles of religious obligation, become clear after all this? I still do not feel confident that I have succeeded in expressing what I intend, so let me make one final attempt: In Islam there are commands and prohibitions; every command has its wisdom, and every prohibition has its wisdom. Carrying out commands and avoiding prohibitions, together with realizing the wisdom of the command and the wisdom of avoiding the prohibition, result in a cardiac and psychological state. This state is the manifestation of cardiac and psychological health. When the heart and *nafs* are healthy, there flows from it—as an effect—clear water and sweet fruit, the spring of *fiṭrah* (فطرة) and the fruits of *īmān*. This appears in one’s dealings with the Real and with creation. Thus there are four circles: the circle of carrying out the command and avoiding the prohibition; the circle of realizing the wisdom in that; the circle of what results from that of health of heart and *nafs*; and the circle of what flows from this health of effects. We are charged with all these circles, with variation in the degrees of obligation at each stage. Many people err or fall short in understanding these circles and realizing them. Complete health is realization of all these circles; and cardiac and psychological health is the axis of this health. Cardiac and psychological health has as its axis knowledge of Allah and realization of His names, together with complete servitude to Him, Exalted be His Majesty. Let this be the conclusion of this chapter, and perhaps the intended meaning has become clear.
