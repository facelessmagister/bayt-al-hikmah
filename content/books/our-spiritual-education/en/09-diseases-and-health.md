# Chapter 9: The Soundness of Diseases and the Soundness of Health

Man is born upon the *fiṭrah* (فطرة). It is related in the *ḥadīth* reported by the two Shaykhs [al-Bukhārī and Muslim]: “There is no newborn but that he is born upon the *fiṭrah*, the *fiṭrah* of Allah upon which He has created mankind; then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian.” And it is related in the *ḥadīth* reported by Aḥmad: “Every newborn is born upon the *fiṭrah* until his tongue expresses it; when his tongue expresses it, he is either grateful or ungrateful.” In this *fiṭrah*, the *qalb* (قلب) is in its most complete state and the spirit in its most excellent state: the heart is free of *amrāḍ* (أمراض), aflame with the light of *tawḥīd* (توحيد); and the spirit knows Allah and acknowledges servitude to Him. Then, after that, occurs what occurs of heedlessness or deviation. This heedlessness begins with seeing the world of causes and attaching to them from the very moment the infant takes his mother’s breast. Then the child begins to suck from the environment its morals, manners, beliefs, and whatever follows from that of deviation, heedlessness, or forgetting…

Islam came to return man to this *fiṭrah*. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> So set your face to the religion, inclining to truth—[to] the *fiṭrah* of Allah upon which He has created mankind. There is no alteration in the creation of Allah. That is the correct religion, but most of the people do not know—[being] those who turn to Him, and fear Him, and establish prayer, and do not be of those who associate others with Allah—[such as] those who split their religion and became sects, each party rejoicing in what it has. [Qur’an, Ar-Rūm 30:30–32]

From these verses we know that the *fiṭrah* is man’s turning his face to the religion of Allah without turning away from that to anything else; and that turning in penitence to Allah, *taqwā* (تقوى), establishing prayer, and negating *shirk* (شرك). By the measure that these meanings gather in a person, he is upon the *fiṭrah*; and by the measure he falls short in any one of them, he falls short in the matter of the *fiṭrah*. Turning the face to the religion of Allah and negating *shirk* encompass many meanings; *taqwā* encompasses many meanings; and establishing prayer as a true standing is connected with many matters, some related to the issues of the heart and its humility, and others to the works of the body and the orientation of the heart. Whoever grasps all these meanings grasps the reality of the *fiṭrah*, regardless of philosophies, complications, and details. We write here only for believing wayfarers; once this is clear, let us look at the matter in its simplest aspect.

When the heart is illumined by the light of pure *tawḥīd* and sees all things as the act of Allah, it meets every affliction with patience, surrender, and contentment. When the heart is illumined by the light of *tawḥīd*, it finds trust in Allah, sincerity to Allah, humility, and submissiveness. When the heart is illumined by the light of *tawḥīd* and sees all blessings as issuing from Allah, it finds love of Allah and desire to thank Him. All of that is an effect of pure *tawḥīd*, which itself is an effect of knowing Allah, His attributes, His names, His acts, and feeling that. And when the heart is illumined by the light of knowing Allah and His *tawḥīd*, the whole heart turns to the religion of Allah and does not look away from it to right or left; then all *shirk*, great and small, is negated. From such a heart, prayer is performed completely for Allah as the most refined manifestation of servitude to Allah, and the due thanks to Him is offered. Automatically, fear of Allah in this heart becomes great, so receiving from Allah becomes complete:

> Allah has sent down the best statement: a consistent Book, [with promises of reward]. The skins shiver from it of those who fear their Lord; then their skins and hearts relax at the remembrance of Allah. [Qur’an, Az-Zumar 39:23]

From such a heart flows behavior in harmony with the religion of Allah—and that is *taqwā*. The sum of all these matters is the complete *fiṭrah*…

And by the measure of the defect in *tawḥīd*, in belief or feeling, there is major *shirk* or minor *shirk*. From *tawḥīd* soundness branches out, and from *shirk* branch out cardiac and behavioral diseases: arrogance, self-admiration, pride, haughtiness, miserliness, cheating, hatred, greed, over-ambition, rancor, envy, restlessness, agitation, panic, covetousness, hoarding, withholding, cowardice, ignorance, laziness, obscenity, harshness, following caprice, contempt, mockery, despair, haughtiness, withdrawal, foolishness, recklessness, excess, defiance, wrongdoing, enmity, rivalry, obstinacy, contention, jesting, backbiting, slander, lying, tale-bearing, alarmism, evil suspicion, estrangement, baseness, impudence, treachery, betrayal, wickedness, malicious joy, and so on. All of these branch out from *shirk* in origin, but they may afflict the heart of the monotheist, and as a consequence the light of *īmān* (إيمان) and *tawḥīd* is prevented from seeping into the heart:

> The Bedouins say, “We have believed.” Say, “You have not [yet] believed; but say [instead], ‘We have submitted,’ for faith has not yet entered your hearts.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:14]

So faith enters, but it is at the threshold of entry; that is the meaning of the word “not yet” (*lamma*) in the verse. Yet there is a state in which works exist but obstacles prevent the lights from reaching the hearts. Among the manifestations of that is the state of those of whom the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, told us in authentic *aḥādīth* that their faith does not pass beyond their throats—although we belittle our prayer compared to theirs, and our fasting compared to theirs. [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith] This indicates that there are states which, when present, prevent the lights of faith from reaching the heart. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh al-Sakandarī mentioned some expressions in his wisdom sayings that clarify this station. He said:

> “How can the heart be illumined when the forms of created things are imprinted in its mirror? Or how can it travel to Allah while fettered by its desires? Or how can it hope to enter the presence of Allah without being purified from the major ritual impurity of its heedlessness? Or how can it hope to understand the subtleties of secrets while it has not repented from its slips?” [Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh al-Sakandarī, *al-Ḥikam*]

And he said:

> “Lights for which permission has been given to arrive, and lights for which permission has been given to enter—perhaps the lights come to you, but if the heart is filled with images of traces, they depart from where they alighted. Empty your heart of others, and He will meet you with gnosis and secrets.” [Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh al-Sakandarī, *al-Ḥikam*]

Keep what we have presented in mind, and journey with us on another leg. There is a demand of the *nafs* (نفس), a disease of the *nafs*, a yielding to the *nafs* and its demands, and behavioral impulses that are effects of its diseases. The Muslim is charged in all these spheres: he is charged to give the *nafs* its lawful demands, and to strive against its sinful, oppressive demands; he is charged to remove disease by traveling the path of healing, and at the same time charged not to obey the commands of the disease. That is difficult and subtle, and the One Whose help is sought is Allah, the Majestic.

If we wish to perceive some of these forms through a close path, it is enough to contemplate some of the supplications for refuge that Allah, the Majestic, or His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, taught us. These are examples:

**a.** Say, “I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak from the evil of that which He created, and from the evil of darkness when it settles, and from the evil of the blowers in knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies.” [Qur’an, Al-Falaq 113:1–5]

Do you not see, in seeking refuge with Allah from the evil of an envier when he envies, that envy has its evil effects in behavior and upon the envier?

**b.** Al-Tirmidhi and Abū Dāwūd reported from Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, that Abū Bakr said: “O Messenger of Allah, command me with words I should say in the evening and the morning.” He said: “Say: ‘O Allah, Originator of the heavens and the earth, Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, Lord and Owner of all things, I bear witness that there is no deity worthy of worship except You. I seek refuge in You from the evil of my *nafs*, from the evil of Satan, and from his *shirk*.’” He said: “Say it in the morning, in the evening, and when you lie down to sleep.” [Sunan al-Tirmidhī, Book of Supplications; Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Manners]

Do you not see, in his words, peace and blessings be upon him, “I seek refuge in You from the evil of my *nafs*,” that the *nafs* has evil demands—far be it from him that he had any demand of his *nafs* except in Allah; rather, it was for teaching.

**c.** The two Shaykhs [al-Bukhārī and Muslim] reported from Anas from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: “O Allah, I seek refuge in You from helplessness and laziness, cowardice and senility and miserliness, and I seek refuge in You from the punishment of the grave, and I seek refuge in You from the trial of life and death.” [Sahih al-Bukhari; Sahih Muslim]

Do you not see in his seeking refuge, peace and blessings be upon him, with Allah from helplessness, laziness, cowardice, miserliness, and senility an indication of diseases—some of them bodily, and some purely of the *nafs*, which have evil effects in life?

**d.** Abū Dāwūd reported from Abū Hurayrah from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: “O Allah, I seek refuge in You from division, hypocrisy, and bad character.” [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Manners]

Do you not see in this *ḥadīth* an indication of multitudes of diseases?

**e.** The authors of the Sunan reported from Shakl ibn Humayd that he said: “O Messenger of Allah, teach me a supplication by which I may seek refuge.” So he took him by the hand and said: “Say: ‘O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the evil of my hearing, the evil of my sight, the evil of my tongue, the evil of my heart, and the evil of my semen.’” [Sunan Abī Dāwūd; Sunan al-Tirmidhī, and he said: *ḥadīth ḥasan*]

This is a close path by which we took up the matter of psychological and cardiac diseases.

If the matter is thus, then there must be a process of inquiry into the paths of healing from the diseases of the heart and *nafs* so that our journey to Allah may be completed. Every disease of the heart produces, if obeyed by man, a behavior. From envy branch out attempts to harm the one envied; from rancor branch out acts of vengeance; from miserliness branch out acts of withholding; and so say for every cardiac or psychological disease… And the afflictions of the tongue and kinds of speech that follow—sarcasm, mockery, backbiting, tale-bearing, and so on—are but effects of cardiac and psychological diseases. And man’s intimate situations and his yielding to the urges of lusts are but effects of the diseases of the heart and *nafs*, and so on.

Keep this likewise in mind, and journey with us on another leg. We realize here that two things are indispensable: knowledge of the diseases, and *mujāhadah* (مجاهدة) of the *nafs* so that it does not yield to them, and *mujāhadah* to be rid of these diseases. For remembrances (*adhkār*), devotional practices (*awrād*), and works—especially in states in which the heart and *nafs* are complicated by kinds of diseases—are not sufficient by themselves to remove these diseases. Rather, knowledge is indispensable, and along with knowledge *mujāhadah* is indispensable; and remembrance is the provision and necessary companion of the journey. Because of this we find among the *ṣūfiyyah* the technical terms of *mujāhadah*, *takhlīyah* (emptying), *tahliyah* (adorning), and *tazkiyah* (purification). In this station the need of many for a guide, an educator of true insight and discernment, aware of the diseases of souls and the ways of treating them, becomes apparent. Knowledge of the diseases of souls assists in healing souls, and knowledge of the manifestations of health assists in traveling its path. We have previously mentioned that knowledge is part of the journey to Allah; so let it be observed that part of this knowledge is connected to this subject, and al-Ghazālī detailed in his *Iḥyāʾ* what pertains to it. We have previously mentioned the importance of remembrance, worship, and devotional practices in the journey to Allah; let that remain in our memory.

And here something becomes clear to us: the necessity of *mujāhadah* of the *nafs* to prevent it from its caprice, to deliver it from its diseases, and to adorn it with the aspects of its health. That is something complementary to the matter of devotional practices in the journey to Allah, and this is the second practical side in our journey to Allah. And so it is in our journey within this epistle.

The following chapter will be a discussion of *mujāhadah* and its pillars as a starting point toward the health of the *nafs* and heart, on the path of deliverance from the diseases of the heart for the return of the self to the *fiṭrah*. None of this will be completed for anyone except by Allah’s enabling. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> And if it were not for the favor of Allah upon you and His mercy, not one of you would have been pure, but Allah purifies whom He wills. [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:159]

Therefore, the One Whose help is sought in this is Allah alone. Among the supplications of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was: “O Allah, grant my *nafs* its *taqwā*, and purify it; You are the best to purify it, You are its Guardian and Master.” [Sahih Muslim]

If the matter is thus, then the One Whose help is sought is Allah; yet Allah, the Exalted, has linked matters to means, and He has made the purification of people one of the tasks of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> Just as We have sent among you a Messenger from yourselves who recites to you Our verses and purifies you and teaches you the Book and wisdom and teaches you that which you did not know. So remember Me; I will remember you. And be grateful to Me and do not deny Me. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:151–152]

Thus we are charged to take the means to reach a purified *nafs* while seeking help from Allah, the Majestic.

The conclusion, then, is that the starting point in health is the word of *tawḥīd* and the heart’s illumination by it; and the starting point in disease or death is the word of *shirk*, or the heart’s not being fully illumined by the word of *tawḥīd*. From the former branch out all manifest and hidden manifestations of health; from the latter branch out all manifest and hidden diseases. Hence, all the rightly guided have no equal in humbling a person’s heart to *tawḥīd*. When the heart is illumined by the light of *tawḥīd* and man’s behavior is in harmony with that through comprehensive knowledge, constant remembrance, and correct commitment, then an unparalleled clarity occurs in the *nafs* and produces a tremendous change in it. As a consequence, in human souls—or in the souls of a people among peoples, if these souls interact with the word of *tawḥīd*—states occur that do not cross the mind, and fruits appear that bewilder and astonish minds.

These Arabs before Islam had no ancient culture, no deep-rooted civilized habits, no experience in statehood and administration, and no ability to restrain their emotions; and whatever you wish to mention of their deficiencies in many matters, you could mention it—apart from their ignorance of Allah, the Exalted, and the absence of a comprehensive view among them in the affairs of life. When they accepted the word of *tawḥīd* as it should be accepted and realized it as it should be realized—as Allah testified:

> Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. [Qur’an, An-Nūr 24:35]

—and as the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, who were with him at al-Ḥudaybiyah, were made to adhere to the word of *taqwā*—that is, the word of *tawḥīd*—“and they were most deserving of it and worthy of it”:

> When those who disbelieved had put into their hearts the fierceness [i.e., zealotry] of ignorance, Allah sent down His tranquility upon His Messenger and upon the believers and imposed upon them the word of *taqwā*; and they were most deserving and worthy of it. And Allah is Knowing of all things. [Qur’an, Al-Fatḥ 48:26]

—they became people of the word of *tawḥīd*, and their behavior harmonized with the Book of *tawḥīd* (the Qur’an) in what accords with this word. Everything was summarized for them; and behold, this ignorant people became an advanced people, a model of good, and possessed such capabilities that they could bring down mighty states, establish a new order in the world, and the peoples of the world took for themselves the religion of this people as their own religion.

And now—while the Muslims are civilly backward, as we see—at a time when we find peoples in the world who have reached the peak of power and civilization, who possess customs and traditions in governance, politics, and administration, who possess tremendous political awareness, enormous administrative capabilities, and extensive studies in everything—while the Muslims cannot catch up with them in conditions of ordinary progress, let alone have a role of precedence, let alone a role of giving, let alone a role of eminence—one thing alone shortens the path for them: the word of *tawḥīd* and behavioral harmony with it in light of the Book and the Sunnah, through knowledge, teaching, interaction, and commitment. This alone is what shortens the path; thus is produced the completely sound human being in heart, mind, and body; in awareness, morals, and behavior; experienced in the *nafs*, able to elevate and refine it, aware of all its requirements. How quickly would peoples leap from state to state: from political oppression and political servitude, from civil backwardness and behavioral backwardness, and so on. Work would be strengthened, production expanded, and the circles of just interaction diversified; and so in every matter. And from here we recognize the gravity of the audacity of those who wish to come between the Islamic movement and its fulfilling its role completely in shaping the peoples of the Islamic *ummah* in light of the word of *tawḥīd* and the Book of *tawḥīd*, so that an exemplary, advanced, leading *ummah* may exist as an alternative to this *ummah* corrupted by corrupt cultures, corrupt and corrupting governments, and a long, protracted colonialism that sought, during its actual or newly-formed colonial period, not to leave a village except that it destroyed it.

From the word of *tawḥīd*, once it settles in the heart and illumines it, branch out trust in Allah, sincerity, patience, gratitude, *iḥsān*, *taqwā*, and acting by Islam—in *ṣalāh*, *zakāh*, consultation, redress from wrong, maintaining ties of kinship, good character, good neighborliness, a good word, ability for *jihād* (جهاد), noble moral character, and other virtues of character. While from the word of *shirk* branch out contentment with the *nafs* and what follows from that of heedlessness, desire, and sin, and what branches out from that of diseases such as arrogance, self-admiration, envy, and the other forms we have passed over.

Many of the diseases of *shirk* may be covered by an artificial stance of an individual, or by a trial culture in an *ummah*; but that is only a covering for a disease, not its elimination.

In conclusion, let us recall two matters. First: there are diseases in the heart which, when present, prevent the lights from reaching the heart; this requires a process of discovering these diseases and traveling the path of being rid of them—and how manifold are the *nafs*’s wicked ways! Second: the causes of these diseases—some of them are intellectual, and some are psychological; the intellect is cured by knowledge and reflection, but the *nafs* is cured by *mujāhadah*. This requires from us a discussion of *mujāhadah*, which is in truth the direct effect that should flow from correct knowledge and constant remembrance. If we have previously said that the two pillars of the journey to Allah are knowledge and remembrance, then correct knowledge must produce *mujāhadah* of the *nafs* directly, and the provision that assists this *mujāhadah* is remembrance. If knowledge does not give rise to *mujāhadah*, it is not correct knowledge. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said: “For keeping company with an ignorant man who is not pleased with himself is better than keeping company with a scholar who is pleased with himself. And what use is knowledge to a scholar who is pleased with himself?”

This is a sound meaning; for contentment with the *nafs* gives rise to what we have previously seen of arrogance, self-admiration, conceit, and the like. Wherever contentment with the *nafs* is found, there is no knowledge; and wherever correct knowledge is found, there is discontent with the *nafs*; hence *mujāhadah* is found. *Mujāhadah* is the first outgrowth of the two pillars of the journey to Allah—knowledge and remembrance—and without it there is no complete journey to Allah. So let the tenth chapter be on it.
