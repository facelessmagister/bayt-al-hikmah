# Chapter 14: Visions, Unveiling, and Karamah

## The True Objective of the Wayfarer

The fourteenth chapter deals with *ru'ya* (رؤى), *kashf* (كشف), *ilhām* (إلهام), and *karāmah* (كرامة), their place in the religion of Allah, and the common errors concerning them—and within them—in some circles.

The essential thing in traveling to Allah is realization and feeling, tasting the realities of Islam, faith, godfearingness, *ihsān* (إحسان), gratitude, harmony of conduct with all of this, purification of the soul, illumination of the heart, knowledge of Allah in the spirit, surrender to Him, and the intellect bound by the Sharīʿah. In a single word: pure servitude to Allah. That is the ultimate goal sought by the *siddīqūn*; it is the noblest station absolutely, and it is the highest description that can be ascribed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

> Exalted is He who took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Harām to al-Masjid al-Aqṣā. [Quran, Al-Isrā 17:1]
>
> [All] praise is [due] to Allah, who has sent down upon His Servant the Book and has not made therein any deviance. [Quran, Al-Kahf 18:1]

For the wayfarer to Allah, Mighty and Majestic, these are his concerns—or this is his concern—and anything else is only a joy to him if it is a sign of Allah’s favor. He rejoices in it because it is a sign of that favor and a glad tiding of acceptance:

> Say, "By the favor of Allah and His mercy, let them rejoice; it is better than what they accumulate." [Quran, Yūnus 10:58]

The wayfarer may encounter a righteous *ru'ya*, or *kashf*, or he may sense *ilhām*, or a *karāmah* may appear at his hands. None of these is his goal; rather, he rejoices in them only as a sign of acceptance or a glad tiding concerning some matter. Once this is clear, we have known the goal of the wayfarer and, at the same time, the error into which some Sufis fall: they make the objective arrival at *kashf* or at something other than the Face of Allah, Mighty and Majestic.

> And keep yourself patient [by being] with those who call upon their Lord in the morning and the evening, seeking His Face. [Quran, Al-Kahf 18:28]

Yet if some Sufis err by making what is not a goal into a goal, historical observation shows that these meanings—*kashf*, *ilhām*, righteous *ru'ya*, and *karāmah*—are matters found abundantly in the texts and in the life of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

Indeed, these meanings are rarely found outside Sufi circles, and one rarely finds discussion of them resembling the discussion found in the texts except among Sufis. This is proof that sound *tasawwuf* (تصوف) is sound travel on the path of the righteous model; the fruits of following that model appear complete. Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, mentions that the *karāmāt* of Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī are transmitted with *tawātur*, and the praise that Shaykh Ibn Taymiyyah bestowed upon Shaykh al-Jīlānī is such as few attain. All of this is evidence that travel to Allah upon the path of the true Sufis has its virtue and its wholesome fruits. But the fact remains that some Sufis exaggerate in some of these matters or err in them, and this too is a point of criticism. So let us begin presenting the topics of this chapter.

## 1. Kashf (Unveiling)

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, described our lady Maryam, peace be upon her, as a *siddīqah*:

> And his mother was a siddīqah. [Quran, Al-Māʾidah 5:75]

It is well known in the science of creed that Allah, Mighty and Majestic, never sent a messenger except a man:

> And We sent not before you, [O Muhammad], except men to whom We revealed. [Quran, Yūsuf 12:109]

Maryam, then, was a *siddīqah*, not a prophetess or a messenger. Nevertheless, the Qur’an tells us that the angels addressed her:

> And [mention] when the angels said, "O Maryam, indeed Allah has chosen you and purified you and chosen you above the women of the worlds." [Quran, Āl ʿImrān 3:42]
>
> Then she took, besides other than Him, a screen. Then We sent to her Our Spirit, who represented himself to her as a well-proportioned human. She said, "Indeed, I seek refuge in the Most Merciful from you, if you should be fearing of Allah." He said, "I am only the messenger of your Lord to give you the tidings of a pure boy." [Quran, Maryam 19:17–19]

Thus it is legally possible for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, to disclose to someone who is not a prophet or messenger the presence of the angels, such that he hears or sees an angel. The Sufis call this state *kashf*. The texts of the Sunnah mention the possibility of this *kashf*, we find examples of it in the lives of the Companions, and the history of true Islamic *tasawwuf* is replete with accounts of it. Whoever reads the biography of al-Ghazālī and what he wrote—he is a trustworthy man—will see much of this, whether in what befell al-Ghazālī himself or in what he transmitted from others. That is sufficient proof for the fair-minded, for al-Ghazālī is a man considered truthful by the masses of this Ummah.

Let us look at what indicates the possibility of *kashf*, its occurrence in the generation of the Companions, and the paths to it from the texts.

### The First Narration

In a narration of *at-Targhīb wa-t-Tarhīb*, on the authority of Abū Umāmah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ passed by on a fiercely hot day heading toward Baqīʿ al-Gharqad while the people were walking behind him. When he heard the sound of their sandals, he was concerned and sat down so that they came up in front of him. Then, passing by Baqīʿ al-Gharqad, he came upon two graves in which two men had been buried that day. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ stopped and asked, "Whom did you bury here today?" They said, "So-and-so and so-and-so." They said, "O Prophet of Allah, what is the matter?" He said, "As for one of them, he was not careful to cleanse himself of urine, and as for the other, he used to backbite." Then he took a fresh palm branch, split it, and placed the pieces on the two graves. They said, "O Prophet of Allah, why did you do this?" He said, "So that their punishment may be lightened." They said, "O Messenger of Allah, until when will they be punished?" He said, "A matter hidden from none but Allah. Were it not that your hearts would be overwhelmed and that you would multiply talk with me, you would have heard what I hear." [at-Targhīb wa-t-Tarhīb]

Observe his words, peace and blessings be upon him: "Were it not that your hearts would be overwhelmed and that you would multiply talk with me, you would have heard what I hear." This indicates the nature of the obstacle to *kashf*, and it indicates its possibility and the path to it: refraining from excessive speech together with purification of the heart. The ways of purifying the heart are found in the texts, as we shall see.

### The Second Narration

In a narration of *Jāmiʿ al-Fawāʾid*, Ḥanẓalah ibn ar-Rabīʿ al-Asadī, one of the scribes of the Prophet ﷺ, said: Abū Bakr met me and said, "How are you, Ḥanẓalah?" I said, "Ḥanẓalah has become a hypocrite." He said, "Subḥānallāh! What are you saying?" I said, "When we are with the Prophet ﷺ, he reminds us of the Fire and Paradise as though we see them with our eyes; and when we leave him, we are occupied with wives, children, and estates, and we forget much." Abū Bakr said, "By Allah, we also experience something like that." So I and Abū Bakr went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and I said, "Ḥanẓalah has become a hypocrite, O Messenger of Allah." He said, "Why is that?" I said, "When we are with you, you remind us of the Fire and Paradise as though we see them with our eyes; and when we leave you, we are occupied with wives, children, and estates, and we forget much." He said to me, "By the One in whose hand is my soul, if you remained as you are in my presence and in remembrance, the angels would greet you on your beds and in your paths. But, O Ḥanẓalah, an hour and an hour," three times. [Ahmad; at-Tirmidhī]

Observe his words, peace and blessings be upon him: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, if you remained as you are in my presence and in remembrance, the angels would greet you on your beds and in your paths..." This narration shows that every Companion, if he preserved the state he experienced while sitting with the Messenger of Allah and persevered in remembrance, could reach a state in which the angels would greet him.

From these two narrations we understand that silence except in what should not be passed over in silence, and remembrance, are among the means by which a person reaches *kashf*.

### The Third Narration

In a narration of *Jāmiʿ al-Fawāʾid*, al-Bukhārī narrated on the authority of ʿĀṣid ibn Ḥuḍayr: While he was reciting Sūrat al-Baqarah at night with his horse tied beside him, the horse suddenly stirred. He stopped, and it stilled; he recited and it stirred, so he stopped and it stilled; then he recited again and it stirred and moved away. His son was near it, and he feared the horse would trample him, so he turned to the horse. Then he raised his eyes to the sky and saw something like a canopy with lamps in it. He went out so that he would not see it. In the morning he told the Prophet ﷺ, who said, "Recite, O Ibn Ḥuḍayr! Recite, O Ibn Ḥuḍayr!" He said, "I feared, O Messenger of Allah, that it would trample my son, who was near it, so I turned to him and raised my head to the sky, and behold, something like a canopy with lamps. I went out so as not to see it." He said, "Do you know what that was?" He said, "No, by Allah." He said, "Those were angels who drew near because of your voice; had you continued reciting until morning, people would have looked at them and they would not have hidden from them." [al-Bukhārī]

Observe that ʿĀṣid saw them, and observe the Prophet’s words, peace and blessings be upon him: "Those were angels who drew near because of your voice; had you continued reciting until morning, people would have looked at them and they would not have hidden from them." From this text we see the possibility of *kashf*, its occurrence for the Companions, and how recitation of the Qur’an is one of the paths to *kashf*.

There are many texts in the lives of the Companions about some of them seeing the jinn, although the jinn belong to the realm of the unseen. In the *al-Asās* series there are many proofs, texts, and examples of this in the lives of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. From these texts we recognize the possibility of *kashf* and its occurrence for the Companions. So if we find people who have traveled to the furthest reaches of true *tasawwuf* and tell us—being upright narrators—of such things, we should not be astonished at the occurrence itself; rather, we take it as evidence of the soundness of the path.

### Errors Concerning Kashf

Here there are several errors into which some Sufis fall.

First, some of them regard *kashf* as an additional source beyond the Book and the Sunnah, by which unseen realities not mentioned in the Book and the Sunnah can be established. Some regard everything a Sufi says in this field as an argument obliging assent, as if it were a new prophethood, or as if a non-prophet could be infallible. In that there is great excess.

Second, some Sufis link acceptance of someone’s *kashf* to total submission to him in every matter, without verifying its conformity to the Sharīʿah. Consequently, many followers follow their shaykhs as though their shaykhs were infallible—this although *kashf* may be given to a person as an enticement, after which Allah seals his fate with evil, refuge being sought with Allah. The story of Balaam, mentioned in the verses of Sūrat al-Aʿrāf, and what commentators say about it, and what the Isrāʾīliyyāt narrations indicate, all point to this.

Third, some Sufis connect *kashf* with abandoning religious obligation. They hold that once a person has had something of the unseen unveiled to him, he is secure from it, and therefore the obligations fall away from him: no prayer, no fasting, and so on. They cite as evidence:

> And worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty. [Quran, Al-Ḥijr 15:99]

These people are disbelievers who mislead the Ummah, for "the certainty" in the verse is death. The Messenger of Allah worshipped his Lord until death. Did those who do not worship reach a greater certainty than he, peace and blessings be upon him? May the curse of Allah be upon them! Concerning such people, al-Junayd said: "They prayed, but into Hell."

Finally, we say: *Kashf* is possible; it is what the wayfarer to Allah may encounter. It is among the manifestations of Allah’s grace and testing. But we are all bound by the texts. *Kashf* does not establish a new creed, add to the texts, impose a religious duty upon the community, or oblige the Ummah to believe its recipients. Yet there is no blame on one who believes what is indicated by it, provided that belief is in accordance with the texts of the Book and the Sunnah. We say only that the Ummah is not charged to believe its recipients even if they are truthful, for their hearts are not infallible in matters of the unseen, the possibility of interpretation remains, and *kashf* may be a trial for a person or for people, in which the recipient or another may stumble.

With all these restrictions we recognize the place of *kashf* in the Sharīʿah of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, and by their light we can read the books of the Sufis. Whenever we encounter speech about *kashf*, we know the limits of acceptance and rejection. And let us remember what we said at the beginning: the wayfarer’s goal is not *kashf* or anything else he may encounter during his endless journey; rather, his concern is the Hereafter, and his objective is the Face of Allah.

At-Tirmidhī narrated on the authority of Anas, raised to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, who said: "Whoever makes the Hereafter his concern, Allah places richness in his heart, gathers his affair for him, and the world comes to him unwillingly. And whoever makes the world his concern, Allah places poverty between his eyes, scatters his affair for him, and he receives from the world only what was decreed for him." In one narration it adds: "No one grows wealthy except a poor man, and no one wakes except a poor man." [at-Tirmidhī]

Also: "There is no servant who turns to Allah with his heart except that Allah beautifies the hearts of the believers, inclining them to him with affection and mercy, and Allah hastens to him with every good." [at-Tirmidhī]

### The Etiquette of the Seeker

In connection with the topic of *kashf*, we say: it is the etiquette of the wayfarer to Allah not to long for it. In this Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said: "Your looking at the faults hidden within you is better than your looking at the unseen veiled from you."

Among the etiquettes of the wayfarers to Allah, and among the etiquettes of the shaykhs and knowers, is that if something of people’s faults is unveiled to someone, he should conceal it, not boast of it, and let his character be mercy, while attempting to heal and treat the matter and taking precautions. The one to whom something is unveiled does not establish by his *kashf* a legal proof against another; and even his *kashf* concerning himself remains suspect, for he fears it may be a trial from Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said: "He unveiled to you the unseen of His dominion and veiled from you prying into the secrets of His servants. Whoever is made privy to the secrets of the servants without assuming the character of divine mercy will find that privily a trial for him and a cause of drawing punishment upon himself."

Let us turn to something else that the wayfarer may encounter: *ilhām*.

## 2. Ilhām (Inspiration)

Let us examine some of what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said about ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, and what some people said concerning ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, so that we may see a phenomenon that can exist in a Muslim.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said in the hadith narrated by the Two Shaykhs: "There were among those before you people who were spoken to, though they were not prophets. If there is anyone among my Ummah, it is ʿUmar." Al-Suyūṭī explained *muḥaddathūn* as "inspired ones." Aḥmad and al-Bazzār narrated on the authority of Abū Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has placed truth on the tongue of ʿUmar and in his heart." Ibn ʿAsākir narrated on the authority of Ṭāriq ibn Shihāb that a man would tell ʿUmar a report and ʿUmar would say, "Stop, this is a lie." Then he would tell him another report and ʿUmar would say, "Stop." The man would say to him, "Every report I give you is true except what you told me to stop at."

From these transmissions we understand that something unseen can occur in the heart of a Muslim, guiding, directing, and reminding him. This thing is *ilhām*. The phenomenon of *ilhām* in Muslim society, and in the heart of a Muslim, is legally possible; it has happened for many of this Ummah. Indeed, every Muslim frequently encounters it in himself or in those around him, if he has any inward journey to Allah, Mighty and Majestic.

When this principle is clear in outline, we say: The believing heart resembles, in one respect, a receiving apparatus for various kinds of impressions; it receives communications from the realm of the unseen, some of whose proofs are in the texts and some in human experience. They are mixed up for most people, and only a few grasp their secrets. You find even disbelievers discussing psychology: they discuss the conscious and the unconscious, how issues float from the unconscious into consciousness, the association of ideas, intuition, conjecture, inspiration, conscience, remorse of conscience, and all of this they treat as an effect of inward reflection exploring the world of the soul.

They have not gone beyond recording certain sensations in themselves or in others. We Muslims accept the observation and share with people in recording it, but our explanations differ vastly from those of others. Our explanations are pure knowledge, while the explanation of others is pure conjecture. Moreover, non-Muslims always stop at limits they cannot cross: for example, the disbeliever cannot record anything about the phenomenon of the faith-filled heart and the spiritual sensations that a Muslim feels and can record. Thus the horizons of the believer’s unseen perception are peculiar to the Muslim. He has decisive texts by which he can be reassured that his sensations are sound, for the revealed texts explain to him the realities of the world of the soul, the heart, and the mind, and what can occur in them. So when he senses something and finds the text speaking of it, he recognizes the correspondence between the two great realities: the reality of truthfulness in the text, and the reality of the state he is in.

Generally, the heart receives four kinds of impressions.

### Satanic Suggestion

> Satans among mankind and jinn inspire each other with embellished speech by way of delusion. [Quran, Al-Anʿām 6:112]
>
> Have you not seen that We have sent the devils upon the disbelievers, inciting them to evil with constant incitement? [Quran, Maryam 19:83]
>
> Satan promises you poverty. [Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:268]

### Psychical Impulse

> Indeed, the soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil. [Quran, Yūsuf 12:53]
>
> And I swear by the reproaching soul. [Quran, Al-Qiyāmah 75:2]

### Angelic Cast

The Prophet ﷺ said: "In the heart are two impulses: an impulse from the angel calling to good and confirming truth, so whoever finds that should know it is from Allah, Exalted be He, and let him praise Allah; and an impulse from the enemy calling to evil and denial of truth..." [at-Tirmidhī; an-Nasāʾī, al-Kubrā]

### Divine Inspiration

> And those who strive for Us—We will surely guide them to Our ways. [Quran, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:69]
>
> And those who are guided—He increases them in guidance and gives them their righteousness. [Quran, Muḥammad 47:17]

The common people call the satanic impression *waswasah*, the psychical impression *hājis*, the angelic cast into the heart *khāṭir*, and the divine cast *ilhām* or *wārid*.

This is a vast door of great importance, but not every heart senses it. The one who has a living heart senses it, while the heart that is dead does not. This is what the Qur’an speaks of:

> Indeed in that is a reminder for whoever has a heart. [Quran, Qāf 50:37]

Allah defined the place of this heart in the chest so that a person does not confuse it with his mind:

> Nay, it is a glorious Qur’an, in a guarded tablet. [Quran, Al-Burūj 85:21–22]

Whoever has a heart that senses the varied impressions, recognizes them, and distinguishes between them—and some have mentioned signs for each kind of impression so that a person may know the cause and taste, and thus distinguish among these impressions—should consult the detailed treatment in Shaykh Aḥmad al-Zarrūq’s book *Qawāʿid at-Taṣawwuf*.

When all this is clear, we understand how the living Muslim feels with his heart, sensing the currents that blow upon this heart. Whereas the disbeliever only senses the matters of the soul and its impulses and follows the casts of the devils, the Muslim senses many other things because his receiving apparatus is not out of order; it is alive and has its peculiar properties. Thus we recognize that many unseen matters are, for the true Muslim, matters of sensation and taste, but a sensation by an instrument other than the outward senses and a taste by an instrument other than the outward organs. The true Muslim receives direct guidance from the realm of the unseen by means of *ilhām* and angelic *khawāṭir*, and he receives guidance through the prophesied revelation sealed by the Book and the Sunnah.

The Muslim who acts by the Book and the Sunnah moves in any matter by their light, and at the same time he is aided by unseen casts into his heart. But the casts cast into the heart of the believing servant are not only divine and angelic; there are also psychical and satanic casts. Hearts other than those of the prophets are not infallible, and they cannot always distinguish. Therefore the Muslim is charged with the infallible text; he must weigh everything that enters his heart against the scale of the infallible text. Abū Sulaymān ad-Dārānī said: "If a subtle point from the people’s words falls into my heart, I do not accept it except with an upright witness from the Book and the Sunnah, for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has guaranteed me infallibility in the Book and the Sunnah, and has not guaranteed it to me in anything else."

Suppose a Muslim reaches a state in which his heart is able to distinguish among the impressions. Even then, the trial remains so that the believer may stay bound to the text and move by the light of knowledge. Thus the Book and the Sunnah inform us of the testing of the heart: just as the body is tested, so the heart is tested.

> They are the ones whose hearts Allah has tested for righteousness. [Quran, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:3]

The Prophet ﷺ said: "Trials are presented to hearts one after another; any heart that rejects them..." [Aḥmad]

From all this we know that there must be a heart of a particular kind, a heart that rejects trials, and there must be a scale. The scale is the Book and the Sunnah. The required heart is the sound heart that rejects trials and does not accept them, and which, after reaching its destination, is promised protection from trials; but that does not mean it will not be tried—it is tried, yet the trial does not harm it.

### Errors Concerning Ilhām

After all this, we can locate the errors of some people.

First, some imagined that they could dispense with the study of the Book and the Sunnah, and with knowledge of creed, jurisprudence, and the clear-eyed journey to Allah and its principles, by means of *khāṭir*, *kashf*, and *ilhām*. In doing so they have stripped themselves of the scale; where there is no scale, judgment is wrong.

> We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain justice. [Quran, Al-Ḥadīd 57:25]

The Prophet ﷺ said: "I am leaving among you two things: you will not go astray as long as you hold fast to them: the Book of Allah and my Sunnah." [al-Ḥākim; Aḥmad]

Second, some imagined that certain hearts could reach infallibility, so they regarded everything cast into them as continuing revelation. Thus they made the hearts of the awliyāʾ like the hearts of the prophets. This is disbelief and misguidance. Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has charged the creation with a defined message; how can we place on a par what is cast into some hearts with what was cast into the heart of Muḥammad ﷺ?

> And truly, this is a revelation from the Lord of the worlds, which the Trustworthy Spirit has brought down upon your heart. [Quran, Ash-Shuʿarā 26:192–194]

Where is that heart and that revelation in comparison with other hearts and other impressions? However much the claimants claim that a heart ascends to where it perceives what is cast into it, no one other than a prophet may claim infallibility for his heart; otherwise he commits disbelief.

Third, many people set out without a scale, imagining that the hearts of shaykhs are infallible, and so they went astray and led others astray. One of them said to me on behalf of a great Sufi: "With my Qur’an and my verses, if my shaykh ordered me to prostrate to al-Lāt, I would prostrate." Woe to such a person! Is it permissible for a Muslim to believe that what his shaykh orders him is permissible for him to perform even if it is disbelief? Is this not exactly what the Christians did: *They have taken their scholars and monks as lords besides Allah*? The Prophet ﷺ explained that by their making lawful what Allah had made unlawful and making unlawful what Allah had made lawful, and the people obeyed them.

Some people defend such persons by saying that they meant such-and-such, and that the shaykh would never order anything but good. We say: Is there any doubt that prostrating to al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā is shirk? How can one announce readiness to obey even in such a matter? Mere declaration of readiness to obey in such a matter is disbelief. Let not the interpretations of the ignorant mislead you, my brother, from the clear-sighted path. Our shaykh Muḥammad al-Ḥāmid, may Allah have mercy on him, used to recite this line:

> Cast away fancies, O mother of Ḥārūt, and leave us free from the impetuosity of your heedlessness and this...

In short, we have seen what the wayfarer may encounter of *ilhāmāt* and *khawāṭir*, the limits of that, and the aspects of error into which some Sufis have fallen in this station.

In connection with the topic of *khawāṭir* and *ilhāmāt*, we say: nothing helps the wayfarer distinguish among *khawāṭir*, impulses, and the rest as much as eating lawful food and scrupulousness in it. They said: "Whoever knows what enters his stomach knows what stirs in his soul." The matter of eating lawful food and scrupulousness in earning is among the obvious fundamentals of Islam for every Muslim, let alone one traveling the path of great *wilāyah* (ولاية). We therefore do not speak of it at length in this book, since detailed treatment and the path of precision in it belong to the books of fiqh. Yet al-Ghazālī in the second volume of *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm ad-Dīn* devoted to it a discussion that is among the most beautiful, delightful, and excellent to be read in its subject.

Let us turn to another issue that confronts the wayfarers: the issue of dreams and visions.

## 3. Ruʿya and Aḥlām (Dreams and Visions)

Dreams and visions have a great role in human life. This role was great in every age, and in our own age the interpretation of visions has become many-sided, its proponents belonging to various tendencies. The materialists generally regard dreams and night-visions as arising from the preoccupations of the soul and the association of ideas, but this does not explain every kind of vision seen by different sorts of people. Their discourse therefore revolves around one kind of vision.

The Muslims of former times, by the light of revelation, classified visions into three kinds. First, visions that are an effect of the soul’s preoccupations and the association of ideas; these are called psychical visions. Second, visions in which Satan intervenes by gaining control, during sleep, over the place of the association of ideas in the human being, casting into it what he casts; the resulting visions are Satanic visions. Third come spiritual, divine visions. This kind of vision is extremely important, for it may be a glad tiding, a warning, news, a caution, and other meanings of the highest value for guiding a person, influencing his conduct, or directing his orientations.

The scholars of the Ummah, by what Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has related to us in the Qur’an of visions and their interpretations—the vision of Yūsuf, peace be upon him, the vision of the Azīz, the vision of Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him—and by the visions seen by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and interpreted by him, or seen by his Companions and related to him, and by the rules derived and the broad inductive study, were able to write the most precise books on the subject of visions and to lay down the rules by which one may know whether a vision is Satanic, psychical, or divine, and what the symbols of divine visions mean. For visions are predominantly symbolic, as is clear in Sūrat Yūsuf, whether in Yūsuf’s own vision or in the Azīz’s vision.

The wayfarers to Allah, the travelers to Him, and those who turn to Him have a large share of glad tidings, in view of the hadith graded by Mālik, al-Bukhārī, and Abū Dāwūd: "Nothing remains of prophethood except glad tidings." They asked, "What are glad tidings?" He said, "The righteous vision." For the spirit, while the veil of sleep is drawn over the senses, is imprinted from the realm of the unseen with some of these meanings of great import, which have a great role in guiding a person.

If we reflect on this authentic hadith—"The vision of the believer is one of forty-six parts of prophethood"—we realize the importance of vision for a Muslim. And if we know that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to ask his Companions almost every day whether any of them had seen a vision, we realize the ignorance of those who do not give vision its due importance.

But since vision has such importance, there is no doubt that distinguishing the kinds of visions is important, and that the haste of those unskilled in interpreting visions to interpret them is a grave error, because many evils result from it. Most visions come in symbolic garb: their outward form is one thing and their interpretation another. Sometimes the outward form is frightening while the interpretation is a glad tiding. Mistaken interpretation is extremely dangerous. All of this demands knowledge of dream-interpretation and caution in interpretation, for interpreting a vision is in many respects like giving a fatwā: the question may be connected with several doors, and every vision has its keys; its key may lie in a name or a hidden allusion.

Among the main rules is that a vision in the case of prophets is revelation; therefore they base rulings upon it. Thus our master Ibrāhīm acted upon his vision and resolved to sacrifice Ismāʿīl, peace be upon him. But in the case of non-prophets it is not revelation. Visions for non-prophets may be psychical, Satanic, or divine; they are mixed, and even the divine vision often comes in the form of symbols, and the interpreter may err. For this reason the Qur’an used the word *ẓann* in connection with interpreting visions. Yūsuf, peace be upon him, said:

> And he said to the one whom he knew would be saved of the two: "Mention me before your master." [Quran, Yūsuf 12:42]

This shows that Yūsuf interpreted by divine *ilhām*, yet the verse makes us feel that interpretation still retains an element of *ẓann*—while noting that *ẓann* in Arabic sometimes means certainty, and the verse may bear that meaning too. Consequently, the scholars of the Ummah agree that a vision in the case of non-prophets cannot be a source of legislation. They said: If a person saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in a dream—and he is the one whose likeness Satan cannot assume—and he ordered him to do something contrary to the Sharīʿah, we would say to him: "You are dreaming; it is forbidden for you to act upon your vision." How much more so for other visions!

### Errors Concerning Dreams

Some people have made mistakes regarding vision:

- They base positions on visions that contradict the Sharīʿah and the rulings of Allah. Often a Sufi or another builds upon a vision and adopts a stance, such as giving his allegiance to a disbeliever because of a vision. Where then are the texts?
- Sometimes a shaykh directs a disciple’s vision in a direction that does not even serve the disciple’s afterlife, nor agree with the principles of dream-interpretation.
- It has often happened that some shaykhs, on the basis of visions, established matters that are innovations in the view of the jurists.
- Visions have often been used to magnify matters or attribute qualities that the Lawgiver did not, such as a shaykh considering one deed greater in Allah’s sight than another while the texts say the contrary.

Thus the visions encountered by the wayfarers to Allah—which others do not encounter—have often become a cause of legal error, exchanging the blessing for its opposite, so that through ignorance they have become either a path to disbelief, or a justification for legal error, or misguidance. These are examples of what the wayfarer to Allah may encounter, and how it may lead to deviations through ignorance, error, or otherwise. We therefore wanted to clarify the limits of these matters.

Let us turn to an issue that confronts the wayfarers and about which many errors and doubts arise: the issue of *karāmāt*.

## 4. Karāmāt (Miraculous Gifts of the Awliyāʾ)

Shaykh al-Nawawī, may Allah have mercy on him, dedicated a chapter in *Riyāḍ as-Ṣāliḥīn* to some *karāmāt*. Here is what he mentioned:

**Chapter on the Karāmāt of the Awliyāʾ and Their Excellence**

Allah, Exalted be He, says:

> Unquestionably, [for] the awliyāʾ of Allah there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve—those who believed and used to fear Allah. For them are glad tidings in the worldly life and in the Hereafter. No change is there in the words of Allah. That is what is the great attainment. [Quran, Yūnus 10:62–64]

Allah, Exalted be He, says:

> Every time Zakariyyā entered upon her in the prayer chamber, he found with her provision. He said, "O Maryam, from where is this [coming] to you?" She said, "It is from Allah. Indeed, Allah provides for whom He wills without account." [Quran, Āl ʿImrān 3:37]

Allah, Exalted be He, says:

> And when you have withdrawn from them and that which they worship other than Allah, retreat to the cave. Your Lord will spread out for you of His mercy and prepare for you from your affair facility. And you would see the sun when it rose inclining away from their cave to the right, and when it set passing away from them to the left. [Quran, Al-Kahf 18:16–17]

ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Abī Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq, may Allah be pleased with both, narrated that a group of the people of the Suffah came to the Prophet ﷺ when he had prayed Fajr, and he said to them: "None of you should depart until I return." He went in to one of his wives, and they waited until the sun had risen high. Then he came out, and they asked him about the matter. He said: "I had no food last night." Then a man among them went to another man and said, "I have a guest." Another said, "I have a guest," until seven or eight had gathered, each with a guest. They went to the Prophet ﷺ and he said: "Do not depart until I return," and went in to another wife. Then he returned and found them all sitting. He said, "Do not depart; it is time for food." Then he said, "O Anṭarah"—a nickname—"bring us food." She went and brought a bowl larger than it had been before they ate, from which they all ate their fill. Then she brought another bowl, and they ate from it too, and it was larger than it had been before. Then Abū Bakr’s wife swore by Allah that she would not feed them until Abū Bakr had eaten, and Abū Bakr swore that he would not eat. She showed him the bowl, and it was now even larger than before they had eaten. Then Abū Bakr ate and took a morsel to the Prophet ﷺ, who ate from it. Then the guests ate, and it became even larger. The story continues with ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān bringing more food until they were all fed, and Abū Bakr recognizing it as a *karāmah* from Allah despite Satan’s attempt to make him doubt.

Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "There were among the nations before you people who were spoken to, though they were not prophets. If there is anyone among my Ummah, it is ʿUmar." [al-Bukhārī; Muslim]

Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: The people of Kūfah complained about Ibn Abī Laylā [until they feared he could not lead prayer properly]. They sent to Ibrāhīm ibn Yazīd an-Nakhaʿī and said, "O Abū Isḥāq, these people claim you do not know how to pray." He said, "I seek refuge in Allah. I used to pray with them the prayer of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, but I added a bowing." Then a man—ʿUmayr ibn Futaydah—stood and said, "I testify that the first three rows are like the rows of the angels." He ordered the amputation of his hand, then exposed his poverty and need.

ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿUbayd ibn al-Ḥārith narrated on the authority of Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh: The old age and physical weakness that befell him would be removed in the roads when he met young women.

ʿUrwa ibn az-Zubayr narrated that Saʿīd ibn Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl, may Allah be pleased with him, disputed with Arwā bint ʿAws over some land she had taken from his land. Saʿīd said, "I only take from it a whip’s length after what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ allotted." She said, "What did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ allot?" He said, "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: ‘Whoever takes a span of land unjustly will be made to wear a collar of seven earths around his neck on the Day of Resurrection.’" She said, "Take it, for it is yours." Saʿīd said, "O Allah, if she is a liar, blind her in her grave and in the place where she slanders me." It is said that she was blinded in her grave.

Mujāhid narrated that ʿAbdillāh ibn ʿUmar used to pass by a wall and say, "I was told you were the first of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ to be killed." He had been instructed in a vision, and he said to his sisters, "I think I am the first of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ to be killed; settle your affairs well among yourselves." He was indeed the first to be killed. A woman was buried with him in his grave, but the narrator made him a separate grave.

It is narrated from al-Bukhārī through various paths. Budayl ibn Bishr, may Allah be pleased with him, was sent on an expedition under ʿĀmir ibn Thābit al-Anṣārī, may Allah be pleased with him. They came to a tribe called Dhū Ṭalāb and fought them. They took Ḥudhayfah and Zayd ibn ad-Dathnah captive. Ḥudhayfah remained a prisoner with them until they agreed to kill him. A woman of Banū al-Ḥārith bought him, and a funeral bier was prepared. He recognized her fear and said, "Do you fear I will kill him? By Allah, I would not do that." She said, "Woe to me, what a loss!" He said, "By Allah, if he had ever eaten a piece of bread in my house, I would not have handed him over." They took him out of the Sacred Sanctuary to kill him. He performed two units of prayer and said, "O Allah, count them one by one and kill them one by one, and leave none of them." He said, "On any side I fall for Allah, it is in the cause of Allah; if He wills, He will bless my limbs." Ḥudhayfah recited, "And be patient; indeed, Allah does not allow to be lost the reward of the doers of good." The Prophet ﷺ informed his Companions of what had befallen them on the day they were struck, and sent ʿAmr ibn Umayyah ad-Ḍamrī to inform them.

In the chapter there are many other authentic hadiths previously mentioned in their proper places in this book, such as the hadith of the well, the magician, the hadith of Jurayj, the hadith of the People of the Trench, and the hadith of the man who heard a voice at the well saying, "I am the garden of so-and-so," after which his death was decreed.

ʿĀʾisha and ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, never said anything about a matter but it was as he thought. [al-Bukhārī]

This is what Shaykh al-Nawawī mentioned in his book *Riyāḍ as-Ṣāliḥīn* concerning the *karāmāt* of the awliyāʾ and their excellence. By it we know that *karāmah* exists and that legal belief in it is obligatory.

In the books of *tawḥīd* the question of *karāmāt* and breaches of customary causation is usually discussed for the materialists in general. There they mention the miracle (*muʿjizah*), the prophetic sign (*irhāṣ*), the *karāmah*, the dishonoring sign (*ihānah*), and the enticement (*istidrāj*). It is well known that magic does not enter the category of breaches of custom, because it is part of the realm of causes. *Karāmah* is of two kinds: one that is a breach of customary causation, and another that is in accordance with customary causation but is among the manifestations of divine success. The scholars call the latter *muʿāwanah* (معونة). Distinguishing the kinds of breaches of custom and knowing each of them is a subject of the science of *tawḥīd*; refer to it there.

What we wish to emphasize here is that *karāmah* is legally established and is almost of the category of what is necessarily known of the religion. But distinguishing it from other kinds of breaches of custom is extremely delicate, and distinguishing breaches of custom from magic itself requires great precision. All of that is not our subject here. Our subject here is two points.

### The First Point

*Karāmah* has occurred and does occur in Sufi circles. The enemies of *tasawwuf* generally have tried to deny that any *karāmah* occurs for those affiliated with *tasawwuf*, attempting to give these *karāmāt* other names. This is an error and excess. We have already mentioned that Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, said that the *karāmāt* of Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī are narrated with *tawātur*, and that Shaykh Ibn Taymiyyah never mentioned Shaykh al-Jīlānī without adding "qaddasa Allāhu sirruhu." Denying the principle of *karāmah* to the Sufi ranks is not a sound denial and is out of place. In my view, we should not stand toward *karāmāt* in the posture of a denier, nor deal with them sentimentally, but give investigation its full scope. This is the principle: when a *karāmah* is authentically transmitted to us and no legal objection can be made against its recipient, there is no impediment to considering it a *karāmah* from Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Some of our shaykhs had *karāmāt* that were clear and evident. I repeat my wish that the subject of *karāmāt* be pursued to its farthest reaches, for I consider service to this subject among the greatest services that can be rendered to the religion of Allah in this age, since *karāmāt* are an extension of miracles and are among the proofs of Allah over His creation. This is one point.

### The Second Point

Ibn ʿAṭāʾ said in his aphorisms: "Not everyone whom Allah singles out is thereby saved." And he said: "Sometimes Allah bestows a *karāmah* on one whose uprightness is not complete." We introduced these two sayings to prove from the words of the Sufis themselves that some Sufis regard *karāmah* as proof of *wilāyah*, and regard *wilāyah* as a probable sign of infallibility. So when a *karāmah* appears at the hands of a shaykh, they take it as a sign of infallibility—calling infallibility here "preservation"—and then build upon it the obligation of attachment to the shaykh, consultation with him in everything, and adherence to everything he says, taking fatwā and spiritual conduct from him in every matter. This is a matter that sometimes leads to corruption. Imām Mālik said: "Among my shaykhs is one by whose prayer I seek rain, but I do not accept his hadith." Reflect on this mighty saying to understand what we intend.

The awliyāʾ of this Ummah are many, and by Allah’s favor they continue to multiply. If every group of Muslims gives its shaykh the attribute of absolute imamate under the cover of *wilāyah*, what divisions, scattering, and errors will result! One whose *karāmah* has appeared and who is upright is a probable sign of his *wilāyah*, and he is worthy that one ask him for supplication. But if he is not a jurist, fatwā is not taken from him; if he is not expert in the terminology of the sciences, the sciences are not taken from him; and if he is not aware of what goes on around us, we do not give him leadership in political affairs. *Karāmah* is one thing, and the role of imamate for a person is something else.

Consider Mūsā and al-Khaḍir, peace be upon them both. Al-Khaḍir was given some special qualities, but who was better, he or Mūsā? It was Mūsā, peace be upon him, for Allah gave him the office of imamate and modelhood. Deep understanding of matters, putting everything in its place, knowing what we take from each person, and the place in which we put each person in the body of this noble Muslim Ummah—this is among the most important features of wise, aware harmony.

## Al-Bannā’s Words on the Matter

If you have absorbed everything that has passed in this chapter concerning *kashf*, visions, *ilhām*, and *karāmāt*, then you are ready to understand precisely what the Master Ḥasan al-Bannā, may Allah have mercy on him, said in *Risālat at-Taʿālīm* under the pillar of understanding:

> Sound faith, correct worship, and striving have a light and sweetness that Allah casts into the heart of whichever of His servants He wills. But *ilhām*, impulses, *kashf*, and visions are not among the proofs of legal rulings; they are not taken into account except on the condition that they do not conflict with the rulings of the religion and its texts.
>
> Divination, charms, incantations, drawing lines on the ground, soothsaying, claiming knowledge of the unseen, and everything of this sort that is reprehensible must be fought, except what is an āyah from the Qur’an or a transmitted *ruqyah*.
>
> Everyone’s words are to be accepted and rejected except those of the infallible Prophet ﷺ. Whatever comes from the righteous predecessors, may Allah be pleased with them, in agreement with the Book and the Sunnah we accept; otherwise the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger are more worthy of being followed. But we do not slander people personally in matters of disagreement by insult or disparagement; we leave them to their intentions, and they have gone to what they sent forth.
>
> Love of the righteous, respect for them, and praise of them in proportion to the good of their lives known to us is a means of nearness to Allah, Blessed and Exalted be He. The awliyāʾ are those mentioned in His saying: *Those who believed and used to fear Allah*. And *karāmah* is established for them by their legal conditions, while believing that they—may Allah be pleased with them—do not possess for themselves benefit or harm in their lives or after their deaths, let alone for anyone else.

We shall return to the last part of his final paragraph, but now to another chapter on the shaykh and the pledge of allegiance, because of its importance in the question of *tasawwuf* and the many errors surrounding it.
