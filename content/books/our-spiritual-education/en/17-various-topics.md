# Chapter 17: Various Topics

Chapter 17 treats a number of miscellaneous subjects. Although the subjects of this chapter are varied, they share the fact that they must be touched upon in a treatise introducing the science of Sufism and guiding a person to take his share of this knowledge and spiritual journeying.

## That the Journey to Allah Does Not Mean Cutting Off the Soul's Needs or Paralyzing Its Energies

It often happens that wayfarers—and others even more so—fall into a serious error: they imagine that *sulūk* (سلوك) means cutting off the needs of the human soul and disabling its capacities. The truth is that *sulūk* means arriving at a state in which all things return to their proper measure and every action issues from a sound position. For example, the conjugal relationship may, in one of its stages, issue from sheer carnal desire, but after arrival it issues from luminous meanings. Pleasures and delights are not diminished after arrival; rather, the intention is purified and the understanding of the meaning of the sexual marital relationship increases after a radical reversal has taken place in the general structure of the human soul and the human heart. What is said here applies to other domains as well. After the complete journey to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic—that is, when the general structure of the human being becomes a journeying structure—all of a person's actions issue in light of knowledge, and lo, every action becomes a sound, guided action in its proper place: courage in its place, deliberation in its place, careful thought in its place, sacrificing the self in its place, and spending wealth in its place. The journey to Allah leads to the bursting forth of every human energy within its sound framework: the energy of work, the energy of the spirit, the energy of the body, the energy of the heart, and the energy of the soul in social life, political life, economic life, and in the circle of family, neighborhood, country, nation, and humanity. Whoever does not understand the journey to Allah in this way is mistaken; and whoever knows the life of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions—they are the model in everything—will realize the soundness of what we say.

## Intention and Its Rectification

We have seen that the starting point of the journey to Allah is the stirring of resolve, or the turning of the will toward the journey to Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. Hence the will must be rectified and the intention freed. The will must be sincerely for the Face of Allah and liberated from any worldly concern. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And be patient with those who call upon their Lord in the morning and the evening, seeking His face. [Qur'an, Al-Anʿām 6:52]

To seek the Face of Allah along with worship of Him is the station we must be eager for, never abandon, and constantly correct. Distractions are many and obstacles are great: the world tries to turn you away from seeking Allah's Face, Satan tries to turn you away, and the soul with its aspirations makes you forget the resolve to turn to Allah. You are charged with rectifying the will and defining the direction of your turning. Say:

> Indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds. He has no partner. And of this I have been commanded, and I am the first [among you] of the Muslims. [Qur'an, Al-Anʿām 6:162–163]

> Whoever desires the harvest of the Hereafter—We increase for him in his harvest. And whoever desires the harvest of this world, We give him thereof, but in the Hereafter he will have no share. [Qur'an, Al-Shūrā 42:20]

It is the established way of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, that when a person is sincere in turning to Allah and asks for what will draw him near, Allah grants it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "If faith were at the Pleiades, men of Persian descent would reach it." Wayfarers under shaykhs are of different kinds: some travel in order to become guides of people to the truth; some aim to reach Allah's good pleasure in themselves, without other aspirations; and some are attracted to the gatherings of the journey to Allah without clarity, neither in purpose nor in deed. Each has his path. It is the duty of shaykhs to keep ascending, constantly, from a lower stage to a higher one, and it is for disciples to observe the question of sincerity to Allah in beginnings and endings. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ has much to say about the will and its rectification. Among his words: "Whenever a wayfarer's resolve pauses at a disclosure, the calls of truth cry out to him: what you seek lies before you. And whenever the outward forms of created beings appear to turn him from Allah to something else, their realities call out to him: *Indeed, they are but trial, so do not disbelieve.*"

## Service and Its Place in the Journey to Allah

In the life of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions there are many manifestations of service for Allah's sake: the young serving the old, the old serving the young, and companions serving one another. Indeed, when the Messenger of Allah entered his house he would work at the tasks of his household; he would serve some delegations himself, as an honor and a fulfillment of duty; and he would join his Companions in labor. This is a great principle in Islamic life. It is a manifestation of the humility of Muslims toward one another, their mercy for one another, and their lowliness before one another, so that none disdains serving another. The affection of the elder for the young moves him to care for him, and the respect of the young for the elder moves him to serve him. Thus the service of brothers to one another, and their lowliness before Allah, removes arrogance and pride from among them. This is the pure Islamic atmosphere. The people of the journey to Allah have recognized the importance of service in disciplining the soul. They observed that the person who does not disdain serving elders and children is a person freed from many diseases: self-admiration, haughtiness, pride, and the like; and at the same time he realizes a host of virtues: humility, mercy, respect, honoring Muslims, lowliness before the believers, and so on. Therefore they considered the service of brothers and shaykhs for Allah's sake among the nearest paths to Allah, because of the pure feelings and lowliness for Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, that the one who volunteers for service realizes. Since service was a general etiquette among them, which the elder did not disdain and the young eagerly practiced, their atmosphere in this station remained sweet and pure, free of false ornament and deceptive display, far removed from the atmosphere of souls' conceit and pride. Some of our shaykhs, even in their twenties, would bring us our sandals when we first began seeking knowledge, and this had a profound effect in accustoming us to service and humility before the people. The nature of service for Allah's sake cannot be performed by a soul unless faith in Allah and the Last Day is gathered within it, confidence that the One Who honors and humbles is Allah, that whoever humbles himself for Allah is raised by Allah, and faith that a person is rewarded by Allah for serving his brothers. Thus we find that service for Allah's sake is a remedy for the soul and nourishment for the heart from many angles.

## Solitude

A disciple may wish to make a great leap in illuminating his heart, and a shaykh may see that a disciple needs a large spiritual meal as nourishment for his heart or as a remedy for him. Therefore he becomes accustomed to the principle of *khalwah* (خلوة), as a concentrated retreat in which the disciple achieves the greatest possible benefit. Shaykhs differ as to the kind of works preferred in solitude and the preferred length of time. In general, the substance of solitude is *dhikr* (ذكر) and meditation (*mudhākara*) after performing the obligatory prayers of the time. As for the duration, the principle is that it depends on the disciple's state, his free time, the needs of his heart, and the purpose for which the solitude was undertaken. We distinguish between a solitude a person undertakes on his own and a solitude under the supervision of a discerning, legally knowledgeable shaykh. In a solitude supervised by a shaykh, the shaykh determines what it should contain of litanies, meditations, and duration. If a person chooses to undertake a solitude on his own, we prefer its program to be: tens of thousands of seeking forgiveness, tens of thousands of blessings upon the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and tens of thousands of *lā ilāha illā Allāh*; then after that he should devote himself either to the formula of monotheism or to blessings upon the Messenger of Allah until he ends his retreat. Many people debate the question of solitude, but the matter does not require such disagreement. If a person wished to seclude himself in a room to perform permissible works without neglecting a duty, there would be no place for censure; how much more so when a person withdraws to offer himself a remedy or nourishment? The life of the Companions, outside the times of struggle, work, and fulfilling rights, was filled with retreats of Qur'an recitation or remembrance, without extremism; in the *iʿtikāf* of Ramaḍān; and in the solitude of the Messenger of Allah in the Cave of Ḥirāʾ before prophethood, all of which support this topic. Many thinkers in the world have realized the great effect of prolonged solitude on the clarity of thought and soul and the quality of decisions, and have adopted it. We advise every Muslim to adopt the principle of retreats, reviving the Sunnah of *iʿtikāf*. Accustoming oneself to spiritual retreats and intensive solitudes is the correct beginning of Islamic jihād upbringing. Solitude is nothing but an intensive spiritual course in an age in which man is overwhelmed before the mills of time, heart, thought, and nerves.

## Remedies Suited to Particular States

Imām Aḥmad narrated with a sound chain in *al-Targhīb wa al-Tarhīb* from Abū Hurayrah that a man complained to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, of hardness of his heart, and he said: "Stroke the head of the orphan and feed the poor." In this hadith you see how the Messenger of Allah gave the complaining man a remedy suited to his state. In a sound hadith narrated by Muslim, ʿUmar said: "O Messenger of Allah, you are dearer to me than everything except myself." He said: "No, by the One in Whose hand is my soul, until I am dearer to you than yourself." ʿUmar then said to the Messenger of Allah: "Now you are dearer to me than myself." ʿUmar had complained of a state incompatible with the content of the hadith: "None of you believes until I am dearer to him than his family, his wealth, and all the people." So the Messenger of Allah made him understand that this state was not perfection, and merely by reminding him, ʿUmar moved to perfection. Here was a simple state that required a quick remedy: a clear statement, and the remedy met a high readiness, so the state ended immediately. We must not forget that the state of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and the readiness of ʿUmar played the greatest role after the clarification. There were people who kept company with the Messenger of Allah and yet died as hypocrites. From these examples we understand that the diseases of hearts and souls may be complex or simple; sometimes the remedy is a statement and clarification, and sometimes clarification alone is not enough without the patient's own special effort. We may find a person who has lived in an environment where he grew accustomed to arrogance, pride, self-admiration, extravagance, and haughtiness over people. If such a person comes to a shaykh seeking guidance, the shaykh may command him with something that serves as a remedy for all these conditions at once—if the shaykh is knowledgeable about the diseases of souls and their sharīʿah remedies. In this treatise there are examples in which travel, seclusion, asking, or other things are a remedy for some states. Hearts themselves differ and their readinesses differ, so the shaykh must observe the kinds of hearts and the kinds of their readinesses and conduct each person in what suits his state. A person may be a candidate for success in a given matter, and he should be directed to it. We therefore observe that some collective obligations become individual obligations for some people because they are the only ones available to perform them. Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has made Muslims rely upon one another; so nothing is more ignorant than to restrict all Muslims to some narrow meanings in the face of His saying:

> Had it not been for a decree from Allah which preceded, you would have been touched, for what you took, by a great punishment. [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:68]

Ibn Kathīr mentioned the hadith narrated by Imām Aḥmad and others in which the Messenger of Allah said: "Indeed, Allah softens the hearts of some men until they are softer than milk, and indeed Allah hardens the hearts of some men until they are harder than stone." From this text we understand that hearts themselves differ; although all of them are in need of perfection, the shaykh must observe the readinesses and kinds of hearts and direct each heart toward what suits it. A heart dominated by mercy is directed toward devoting himself to calling people to Allah; a heart dominated by love of chastising the disbelievers is directed toward devoting himself to the cause of jihād.

In connection with treating hearts I say: Many workers for Islam do not, in their general taste, accept some of the shaykhs' actions in treating certain diseases, for some of them are displeased to see a person behave in a way that does not conform to what is customary in treating himself. To these people I transmit two reports. Al-Tirmidhī narrated with a chain that he graded *ḥasan gharīb* from Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim: "They say it is pride—meaning self-conceit, swagger, and arrogance—while he has mounted his horse, wrapped himself in his cloak, and milked his sheep. Yet the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: Whoever does this, there is nothing of pride in it." And al-Bukhārī, Muslim, and Mālik narrated that Abū Hurayrah, when left in charge of Medina, came carrying a bundle of firewood on his back and passed through the market saying, "Make way for the amīr!" so that people would look at him. I say: If Abū Hurayrah did that as a way of treating and disciplining his soul, this is something whose examples are abundant in the life of the Companions. Even ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, would sometimes behave in a way his son would reproach him for, and he would then explain to him that he had done it as a remedy. There must be a complete return to a complete Islamic life in which the morality of the generation of the Companions appears in everything.

## Clothing

Some Sufis have tried to tie Sufism to a special garment. What should be said on this matter is: If the issue has a root in the Sunnah, then the Sunnah is the criterion; and if it is a permissible custom that does not lead a person to commit something disliked or forbidden, then that is its direction. We do not restrict ourselves beyond the rulings related to clothing. Accordingly we say:

There are kinds of clothing forbidden to men, such as silk or what is a garment particular to women; and there are garments forbidden to women, namely what is a garment particular to men, except for the need of fighting. The details of such matters are found in books of jurisprudence.

In general, the clothing of a Muslim woman should be covering and ample, neither describing the body nor transparent; the clothing of a man should not reveal the nakedness. These details too are found in books of jurisprudence.

Extravagance in clothing is not appropriate for men or women; extravagance is a relative matter that varies according to people's circumstances.

The Arab dress has a special virtue in several respects, because by it a group of meanings are realized that are not realized by other dress: it covers the nakedness, and by it a person fulfills many Sunnahs, such as eating seated and urinating seated, and so on.

A person may have work clothes suited to his work, like those of a pilot or a soldier; thus clothes of rest differ from clothes of work. The shirt—which people today call a *jallābiyyah* in some countries—was the most beloved garment to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. So let our garment of rest be the *jallābiyyah*, and let there be a head covering such as the *qalansuwah* (cap), the ʿimāmah (turban), or a wrap over the ʿimāmah; that is more complete.

A person should accustom himself not to be enslaved by clothing; that is among the morals of Muslims. Hence the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Wretched is the slave of the dinar and the dirham." And he said: "Shabbiness is part of faith." Among the signs of shabbiness is using a garment even when it has become worn, and not discarding it merely because something has happened to it. It is reported from some Companions that they would patch their clothes. This has importance in economic and social life: that a person not throw away his old garment as soon as a new one appears, for that is wastefulness and extravagance. The matter is qualified by whether the person gives the old garment in charity, or whether the old garment is not wasted but used in some way.

The topic of clothing is a complex matter connected to many things; every nation has clothing linked to its culture and customs. Often a person's wearing the clothing of another nation is a sign of admiration for that nation and its civilization and a kind of contempt for his own nation. This matter should be handled with utmost wisdom in our age: we should not be so strict as to inflate what is disliked into what is forbidden, nor so lax in education that we forget that we have a particular dress that is preferable and best. There is no clothing in which a person's body and limbs are more at ease than our inherited Arab dress, which we received from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. ʿUmar, may Allah be pleased with him, used to send to the Islamic armies instructing them to abandon the dress of the Persians—who were disbelievers at that time—and to adopt the dress of the Arabs. We have touched on the subject of dress and national identity more than once.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever imitates a people is one of them." The jurists apply this hadith to imitation in matters that are part of those people's religious particularities. As for what is shared among the children of Adam, or ordinary imitation that does not undermine an Islamic rite or conflict with a Sunnah, the matter is broad.

There is a situation we shall speak of later: when a shaykh sees that a particular garment is necessary for a person, either as a station or as a remedy; or when an imām, amīr, or one in authority sees that a person should wear a certain garment as a disciplinary measure to achieve a benefit. These two are special situations; a fatwā is determined by time, place, and person, and the fatwā here is what defines the ruling for the individual.

## Abstaining from Asking People

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, trained his Companions not to ask people for anything. In the sound hadith narrated by Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, and al-Nasāʾī from ʿAwf ibn Mālik al-Ashjaʿī, he said: "We were nine, eight, or seven with the Messenger of Allah when he said: 'Will you not pledge allegiance to the Messenger of Allah?'—we had recently pledged allegiance—so we stretched out our hands and said: 'We have already pledged allegiance to you, O Messenger of Allah; for what should we pledge allegiance to you?' He said: That you worship Allah and associate nothing with Him; that you pray the prayers; that you listen and obey; and that you keep a secret.' Then he said: 'And do not ask people for anything.' Indeed, I saw one of those men whose whip fell from his hand, and he would not ask anyone to hand it to him." This is the highest state in Islamic upbringing. A person is permitted in some cases to ask people for his needs, either in a special situation or in a state of necessity, and only to the extent of the need. Imām Muslim narrated from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: "Whoever asks people in order to accumulate wealth is asking only for embers; so let him take little or much." In all cases, work is made the sound state for a person, while asking is only a remedy for an exceptional state. "The upper hand is better than the lower hand." This is the general principle of this topic; the details are found in books of exegesis, hadith, and jurisprudence. We have touched on this topic here because of a mistaken understanding of some shaykhs' actions. It has happened, for example, that a complex case of some heart diseases was treated by a shaykh ordering its bearer to go to the market and ask people to give him, intending to deliver their charity to those who deserve it. If he does that as a remedy, some people have exaggerated in this matter. This subject should be laid to rest in our age, and asking should be returned to the correct original principle we have mentioned.

## Travel

In the past, travel had a special status. It was a general etiquette for seeking knowledge, and a Sufi etiquette for seeking knowledge and training under those qualified for it. A person begins by taking from whoever has knowledge or a spiritual state in his surroundings; then he travels to complete the matter. Sometimes travel is a remedy for certain psychological and heart conditions. For example, a person may fall into infatuation or love because of his environment, and the shaykh may treat such cases by ordering the disciple to travel in order to change his environment or forget. In the hadith narrated by the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, about the man who killed a hundred souls, the scholar ordered the killer to leave his land for another land. From this hadith this topic may draw support. Because of the connection of travel to these and similar matters, educators have regularly discussed travel in their books. Let us quote some of their words with a little commentary.

The author of *Qaṣīdat al-Mabāḥith al-Aṣliyyah* says: "Their doctrine regarding roaming lands: (visiting shaykhs and brothers)," meaning that among the purposes of travel is visiting brothers and shaykhs who know Allah, in order to attain what the sound hadith indicates: "Allah has made mutual love, mutual visits, and mutual giving incumbent among you." "(Then, acquiring knowledge and traces)," meaning that this too is a purpose of travel: seeking knowledge in general and seeking the traces of the hadith in particular, which is intended here by "acquiring traces." "(Or returning a wrong, or for reflection)," meaning that among the purposes of travel is returning wrongs if one of them owes a wrong to someone; that is an obligation. If he owes a poor person a debt, retaliation, or some right, he travels to him to return it or be released from it. Shaykh Zarūq considered that returning the wrongs of people to one another falls under forbidding wrong, and said: "This is upon one who knows that to be the case without deficiency in his religion." This is a noble insight of Shaykh Zarūq, and how good it would be for Muslims to become accustomed to going out for such purposes; the field of daʿwah and propagation in our age has a wide scope in this. May Allah reward him with good. Zarūq also included in this chapter travel to flee a wrong done to a person, or to flee a land in which there is wrong; this is a matter connected to emigration. Among the purposes of travel is travel for reflection and taking lesson. Ibn ʿAjībah explained this: "Reflection is what one sees in one's journey of mountains, springs, seas, trees, lands, kinds of creatures, and varieties of beings." "(Or from inactivity or to drive away repute)," meaning that among the purposes of travel is to travel to flee fame or to flee self-exaltation. This the disciple does at the beginning of his affair so that he may attain perfection, because fame and self-exaltation at the beginning of a disciple's affair may deprive him of perfection in knowledge and spiritual conduct. Thus travel in his case is a remedy and taking the means to reach perfection, so that he may benefit people for Allah in a more complete way and so that sincerity in his heart may be more profound. Ibn ʿAjībah said: "What is meant in this station is: the one whom harm afflicts, or who follows a crooked course, or whom one fears will cause loss or distraction, or to whom the soul inclines and leans." "(Or to the Messenger or to the House of Allah)," meaning that among the purposes of travel is visiting the mosque of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, then his grave; likewise among the purposes is the major and minor pilgrimage and visiting the Sacred House of Allah. These are the reasons for which, or for one of which, the wayfarer to Allah travels. Ibn ʿAjībah said: "Among the remaining benefits of travel is health of body and heart, for he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Travel, and you will be healthy and forgiven.'"

Let us return to the words of the author of *al-Mabāḥith*: "My journeys were not for diversion, but in them was the light of turning to Allah." That is because the Sufi tries not to act, even in a permissible matter, without a sound intention, for intentions turn habits into acts of worship. "Nor were they without asking permission of the shaykh, the fathers, and the brothers," in order to receive their supplications, take their counsel, benefit from their observations, and in case there was a need they could fulfill; and sometimes harm might result from his travel, so they would warn him. "And they were not for gains," meaning, in their terminology, what is given to a person of gifts and charity; this is something a Sufi should not think of at all. Ibn ʿAjībah said: "And they were not for the sake of this world, for that is a base aspiration." "(Or for a person to be praised and eulogized)," meaning that the Sufi does not travel in order to be praised by people, as poets did in the past; this does not cross the mind of a wayfarer to Allah.

After that, the author of *al-Mabāḥith* mentioned some etiquettes of the wayfarer to Allah when he arrives in a land: "Wherever they alight in a land, let them first seek the shaykh, then the poor." This means that among their etiquettes when they alight in a land is to seek its shaykhs, its righteous people, and the poor to Allah in it—by which are meant the wayfarers to Allah in it. Ibn ʿAjībah said: "His words 'then the poor' mean by priority and precedence: first the shaykh, then the poor. He said: This order we have mentioned is when there is choice; if meeting the shaykhs first is difficult, then the poor are put first." The Sufis call them "the poor" after His saying:

> O mankind, you are those in need of Allah. [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:15]

Then the author of *al-Mabāḥith* mentioned etiquettes of meeting shaykhs and sitting with them: "And their custom when they sit with him is that their speech is an answer," meaning that their original practice is silence unless asked, in which case they answer. "If the shaykh engages them in speech, they speak; otherwise silence is better," meaning that if the shaykh asks them to speak, they speak; otherwise their etiquette is silence. Among their etiquettes is waiting for the shaykh to come out without calling to him, sending a messenger to him, or behaving with good manners in sittings and assemblies. Among their etiquettes is participating in general meditations with good manners and perfection, and choosing words carefully in the shaykh's presence, especially in cases of disagreement of opinion, or when hearing, or when seeing a sharīʿah error.

Then the author of *al-Mabāḥith* mentioned the etiquette of the people of the town toward the visitor: "It is obligatory upon the people of residence"—that is, those to whom the traveler has come—"to tend to the visitor with honor." Ibn ʿAjībah explained tending with honor as going to meet him, showing joy in one's face, rejoicing at him, and putting him at ease from his concerns and needs, and settling him in a place. "And he visits the people in the ḥaram," meaning in the sacred land, that is Mecca; that is, the visitor is more entitled to be visited in his state, unless he is in Mecca, for then he must visit the neighbors of the Sacred House, out of reverence for the House of Allah. "And that is out of respect," meaning he begins by visiting the people of the ḥaram out of respect for them as residents of the House of Allah. The matter has more than one aspect. The principle is that knowledge comes first.

Then the shaykh mentioned some etiquettes of the host: "And the host begins with greeting the visitor, with food and honoring, and only afterward with speech, following the example of Ibrāhīm," peace be upon him. That is, they begin with greeting, then food and honoring, then speech, following the example of Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him, with his guests: greeting, then feeding, then speech. They offer food that involves no burden, and if honoring is possible there is no objection to it without extravagance, for extravagance cuts off the path of generosity, wearies the family and people, and may make the guest become a burden. This is a great reason for the interruption of much good. Therefore the Sufi etiquette in this station is to avoid extravagance; that is true Islamic generosity, for it alone is spacious enough for people and by it the character of generosity is preserved in this Ummah. Once extravagance begins, hardship appears in wealth, difficulty for the family and weariness for them. Extravagance differs from person to person: for a rich man what he offers is not considered wasteful even if it is abundant and expensive, unlike a poor man.

"They disliked asking this visitor except about the shaykh or the disciples," meaning that they would not ask him about worldly conditions and their tales, for that is of no concern and veils the heart; rather they would ask him about the shaykh, the disciples, and the wayfarers to Allah, and about the state of the people, in order to be reassured about the soundness of Islam and the Muslims. This is a wide door when a sound intention is present, for even asking about worldly matters earns a person reward if accompanied by a sound intention.

"They disliked his neglecting his litanies, since he had come to increase." A person's litanies are what his shaykh has assigned him, or what he has assigned himself. What is meant here is what he was engaged in during his residence. When he travels he persists in what he was doing unless it becomes difficult. From Allah's mercy to a person is that if he has a deed and is prevented from it by illness or travel, it is written for him as the reward of the complete deed. If his litanies are not difficult, he persists in them, or in some of them; therefore they disapproved of his abandoning them completely. The poet says: "How can he abandon his litanies entirely when he is traveling to seek increase"—whether in his heart-state or otherwise.

"Whoever travels in the caprice of the soul is commanded to sit." Whoever does not maintain a sound intention for his travel so that his travel achieves a sharīʿah purpose, the people of Sufism do not see travel as right for him. Among their etiquettes, as we mentioned, is that they love not to act except with a sound intention, even in permissible matters, so that all their actions become acts of worship. This is the sum of what the author of *al-Mabāḥith* said in the section on travel. Some have mentioned other aspects, so let us mention some of them:

It is preferable for the traveler to lodge with people of his own *mashrab* (مشرب) and not to burden them by staying too long unless he has settled in a place prepared for that and they insist. If his goal is residence, he should hasten to his place of settlement.

Whoever intends to travel should learn the rulings required for it, such as the rulings of shortening prayers, dry ablution, the direction of prayer, and so on.

If they are a group, one of them should be appointed imām, and among his good manners is to consult them.

Ibn ʿAjībah transmitted: "Among their etiquettes is that they do not argue among themselves in their assemblies, saying: This is mine and this is yours—'If only it had been so, it would not have been so,' 'perhaps,' 'maybe,' 'why did I do this?' and 'why did I not do that?' and the like. That is the character of common people. Among them there should be no exclusiveness, disputation, mockery, contempt, quarreling, wrangling, domineering, or belittlement; rather each one of them should be to the elder like a son, to the younger like a father, and to the equal like a brother." This is not particular to travel; it is their etiquette in companionship always, but in travel it is more important to them and they observe it more broadly, for travel reveals every flaw, and only the true friend remains as he was while traveling.

Among their etiquettes is to supplicate with the supplications of travel going and returning, and the supplications of mounting, and to increase in *takbīr*, *tahlīl*, *tasbīḥ*, and the like of remembrances.

If it is easy for him, bringing a gift home for his family, relatives, and neighbors is wholesome.

If he is able to enter his town by day, that is the Sunnah; and the etiquette is not to knock on his people's door at night, so as not to cause them the disturbance of fear and asking who is knocking.

## The Station of Iḥsān

The summit of the journey to Allah is that the wayfarer reaches, in his journey, the station of *iḥsān* (إحسان), expressed in the noble hadith: "That you worship Allah as if you see Him; for if you do not see Him, He sees you." These are two stations, each of which is called *iḥsān*. The Sufis differ as to which of them is higher; the apparent meaning of the hadith is that the first station—"that you worship Allah as if you see Him"—is the higher. Every path has adopted some meanings to reach this station. Knowledge and remembrance are the two pillars of arrival. There is a kind of action connected to this station, and there are meanings that the wayfarer to Allah must observe during his remembrance in order to reach this station. In general, the wayfarer to Allah passes, on his way to the station of *iḥsān*, through what the Sufis call the *fanāʾāt* (فناءات): *fanāʾ* in actions, in which a person senses that everything is done by Allah; *fanāʾ* in attributes, in which a person senses the attributes of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic; and *fanāʾ* in the Essence, in which a person senses the unity and absoluteness of the Divine Essence. Once he is firmly settled in this station, he realizes the station of *iḥsān*, and they then try to move him to the station of witnessing along with seeing creation; this they call the station of *baqāʾ* (بقاء). The wayfarer may reach *fanāʾ* in the Essence directly, then begin to sense what is other than that. As we said, every path has the observations it believes should accompany remembrance or journeying in order to bring the disciple to this result. These observations are either experiential observations indicated by experience, or a kind of application of certain Qur'anic verses. The Sufis hold that remembrance of Allah's name "Allah" is the strongest kind of remembrance for reaching the station of *iḥsān*. Ibn ʿAbidīn said: "There is no remembrance among common people for the possessor of a station above the remembrance of the unique Name." And I say: The common people likewise do not require the unique Name for reaching Allah; whoever imagines otherwise is mistaken and contradicts consensus. We shall return to the unique Name of Allah in a separate section. But here we wish to mention two programs for journeying to the station of *iḥsān* among shaykhs.

First, among the things that Shaykh al-Ghazālī mentions as leading to *murāqabah* (مراقبة) is that a person practice constant reckoning of his soul along with seeking forgiveness; for that is a complete path to reaching *iḥsān*. Al-Ghazālī also says that a person should persist in one remembrance—such as "Glory be to Allah" or "Allah"—and persevere in the remembrance until the Name is settled in his heart, and then the awareness of its meaning is settled.

Second, some Sufis enter the disciple into a stage of solitude and require him to remember the unique Name of Allah, "Allah," directing his attention in the first stage to read the whole manifest universe as Allah, in realization of His saying:

> Read in the name of your Lord who created. [Qur'an, Al-ʿAlaq 96:1]

Then at a later stage they require him to read the unseen universe likewise with this Name. Then at a later stage, while he is remembering the Name "Allah," they require him to observe Allah's unity and absoluteness through certain meanings; and by that they have given him the seeds of the station of *iḥsān*. They then require him to persevere in remembrance and litanies until these seeds sprout and later bear their fruit. They say: "Allah has ways to Him according to the number of created beings." A person may reach the station of *iḥsān* by one formula or another, as long as the obligatory prayers are performed, turning to Allah is present, knowledge is the leader, and the perfect shaykh shortens the path.

## Remembrance of the Unique Name

The comprehensive Name for the Divine Essence is the majestic formula "Allah"; therefore it is called the unique Name, for it is the only Name that indicates Allah in Essence, attributes, names, and acts, whereas other names indicate an essence and an attribute. Moreover, it is not used to name anyone other than Allah, so it is unique among all the names. Therefore whoever says "Allah" has remembered Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, and realized the Qur'anic command:

> And remember the Name of your Lord. [Qur'an, Al-Muzzammil 73:8]

The meaning intended is Allah; whoever remembers Him has remembered Allah without doubt or uncertainty, and whoever contests that is mistaken. When we say "Glory be to Allah," we have glorified Allah and declared Him transcendent, and thereby remembered Him. When we say "Praise be to Allah," we have praised Allah and thanked Him, and thereby remembered Him. When we say "Allah," we have remembered Him. Since glorification is required in itself, and gratitude is required in itself, then remembering Allah is likewise required; and whoever remembers any Name of Allah has remembered Allah. Some people quibble at this point and say: If you began to mention a human being's name over and over—"So-and-so, so-and-so, so-and-so" and "O so-and-so, O so-and-so"—he would be annoyed, and your action would have no meaning. This analogy is mistaken, for remembering Allah is required, and its benefit for us is great and abundant, since remembrance of Allah is what awakens and revives our hearts. To say "Allah, Allah, Allah" is remembrance of Allah and beneficial remembrance for our hearts, so that they may remain mindful of their Lord. Remembrance of Allah is realized through remembering all His Names, and a person is rewarded for it. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them. And leave [the company of] those who practice deviation concerning His names. [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:180]

We see in the Qur'an how Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, reminds us of His Names again and again, so that His Names remain upon our remembrance. If remembrance is accompanied by supplication or a required sharīʿah meaning such as seeking forgiveness, glorifying, monotheism, praise, magnification, and exaltation, then it is remembrance and more; whoever opposes the permissibility of this or that is mistaken. Knowledge of Allah deepens in our hearts through every litany, through every transmitted supplication, and through remembering all the Names of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic. If someone were to say "Allah is Merciful" and repeat it in order to deepen in his heart the sense of Allah's mercy, or to say "Allah is Seeing" and repeat it in order to deepen in his heart the sense that Allah sees him, and so on with every Name of Allah in order to deepen in his heart the sense of all the Names, would he be rewarded or punished? It is better not to enter into argument with someone who opposes the permissibility of such a thing. Once this is established, the unique Name of Allah is the Name in which all names are comprised; if a person repeats it so that the sense of the Divine Essence, its attributes and names, is settled in his heart, where then is the harm? The reward is undoubtedly attained, by Allah's permission, and the effect in the heart is present, by Allah's permission.

Someone may say: We do not find in the Sunnah a concentration on remembering the unique Name of Allah. We say: The Book and the Sunnah contain a general encouragement to remembrance. The Companions often mentioned formulas they had not received directly from the Messenger of Allah, and he approved them and even thanked them. Any remembrance of Allah—whether it is remembering a Name, glorifying, supplicating, sending blessings upon the Messenger of Allah, or anything else—falls under the general comprehensiveness, and its doer is rewarded and thanked. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And remember the Name of your Lord and devote yourself to Him with [complete] devotion. [Qur'an, Al-Muzzammil 73:8]

But why do the people of the journey to Allah consider remembrance of the unique Name of Allah the nearest path to the station of *iḥsān*? They say: When you glorify Allah, the question of declaring Him transcendent deepens in your heart; when you praise Allah, gratitude deepens in your heart; and when you say "There is no deity but Allah," monotheism deepens in your heart. These are all matters that branch from the settling of knowledge of Allah in the heart. If you say "Allah" and repeat it until knowledge of Allah is settled in your heart, then your glorification, praise, and monotheism will be more complete than much glorification and praise in which the heart is not awake to the Names of Allah. We are required to deepen in our hearts knowledge of Allah, declaring Him transcendent, thanking Him, and professing His oneness; and this is complete if we remember the majestic formula "Allah" along with the rest of the transmitted litanies.

Some have argued that remembrance of the unique Name of Allah is a stage in reaching the tasted knowledge that enables us to perform worship, litanies, and supplications at their highest. Let us now consider the wisdom of the formulas of remembrance. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, urged us to persevere in seeking forgiveness, to persevere in sending blessings upon him, and to increase in certain formulas. If you reflect on the wisdom of repeating one of these formulas, you find that one of its aspects is that a particular meaning becomes settled in the heart. This heart needs repetition for meanings to deepen in it. A heart in which knowledge of Allah has not settled needs to remember the Names of Allah until that knowledge deepens. The people of the journey to Allah say: Sitting with the Messenger of Allah gives a person of his illumination what he cannot take from anyone else; therefore we seek means to bring the heart close to this illumination, and so we call for this kind of remembrance. If a heart does not feel at ease with this kind of journey, then any kind of remembrance—whether Qur'an recitation or litanies of any formula—will in the end bring him to tasted knowledge of Allah and to the station of *iḥsān*. What I see is that a person should not be compelled in the particular work assigned to him. In presenting this brief treatment of the question of the unique Name, I do not intend to bind people to it; I only intend to make clear the points of view concerning it. If a heart feels at ease only with what has a specific recommendation from our Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, in the deed, then I defer to that and respect it; indeed I encourage it along this path. But I do not insist upon it, nor do I feel entitled to censure what is not a wrong. In this state, it is like a concentrated remedy and nourishment for the heart, no more and no less; yet apart from remembrance by this Name there is also nourishment and remedy.

Once the point of view on the principle of remembering the unique Name is clear, it remains to mention that some go so far as to say that remembering the Name is recommended (*mandūb*) and hold that shortening its pronunciation by dropping the lengthening is permissible, so that one does not say "Allāh" without prolongation. Some hold that prolonging it more than six beats in a pause is not permissible. We say: Pronouncing the majestic formula in the short form, especially in the *takbīrat al-iḥrām*, invalidates the prayer according to most scholars; they do not consider that merely a lapse in this formula but an invalidating error. However, in the commentary of Shihāb al-Bayḍāwī it is said: "Al-Asnawī said: It is a dialect reported by Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ from al-Zajjāj; there is no lapse in it in that case. In *al-Taysīr* it is a permissible dialect in pause but not in connection; and the more eloquent is to establish it. The verifiers use it frequently in their poems…" As for prolonging the majestic formula, the jurists have been broad: some Shāfiʿī jurists permitted prolonging it in the *takbīrat al-iḥrām* up to even fourteen beats, and some permitted more than that.

Finally we say: The repetition of the unique Name of Allah occurs in more than one hadith. Among these is his saying, peace and blessings be upon him: "The Hour will not be established upon anyone who says: There is no deity but Allah." And among what the Messenger of Allah commanded us to say in distress is: "Allah, Allah is my Lord; I do not associate anything with Him."

## Remembrance

Allah, the Exalted, said concerning prayer:

> And establish prayer for My remembrance. [Qur'an, Ṭā Hā 20:14]

And He said in the context of fasting:

> …and that you may magnify Allah for having guided you… [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:185]

And He said in the context of Hajj:

> And remember Allah during [specific] numbered days. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:203]

And He said in the context of the stoning of the jamarāt:

> And remember Allah during [the] numbered days. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:203]

Thus we see that acts of worship, in their outward performance or in the depth they provide, help us reach Allah. The two pillars of the journey to Allah are remembrance and action. If we wish to elaborate, we say: The highest demand upon a human being is *taqwā* (تقوى), and *taqwā* is not attained except through knowledge and worship. They said: Whoever acts without knowledge, his actions are rejected and not accepted. Worship is the path to *taqwā*:

> O you who have believed, fear your Lord, who created you and those before you, that you may become righteous. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:21]

It is through *taqwā* that we obtain Allah's good pleasure. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Their meat will not reach Allah, nor will their blood, but what reaches Him is *taqwā* from you. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:37]

Worship is remembrance, or a meaning realized by remembrance. From this we understand the importance of remembrance in the religion of Allah. Then, emulating the Messenger of Allah is a path of remembrance, for:

> There has certainly been for you in the Messenger of Allah an excellent example for anyone whose hope is in Allah and the Last Day and who remembers Allah often. [Qur'an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:21]

The Messenger of Allah is the master of the knowers and the arrived ones, although his journey and arrival differ from the journey and arrival of wayfarers; still, the wayfarers have a share of his journey and arrival. Since part of the journey is acquiring the Names of Allah, and since the stages of the journey are completed by passing from one *fanāʾ* to another, remembrance is the means of all that. Therefore the wisdom of prayer is remembrance:

> And establish prayer for My remembrance. [Qur'an, Ṭā Hā 20:14]
## Various Topics (continued)

### Prayer and Remembrance

Allah — Exalted is He — clarified the wisdom of fasting when He said:

> "…and to glorify Allah for that [to] which He has guided you; and perhaps you will be grateful." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:185]

Among the wisdoms realized by fasting is that man magnifies Allah — Exalted is He — for having guided him, and that is *dhikr* (ذكر). And when Allah — Exalted is He — mentioned Hajj, He said:

> "And proclaim to the people the Hajj [pilgrimage]; they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel; they will come from every distant pass, that they may witness benefits for themselves and mention the name of Allah on known days over what He has provided for them of [sacrificial] animals." [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:27–28]

Thus *dhikr* is intended by the obligation of Hajj. And Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater." [Qur'an, Al-Ankabut 29:45]

And describing the hypocrites, He said:

> "And when they stand for prayer, they stand lazily, showing [themselves to] the people and not remembering Allah except a little." [Qur'an, An-Nisa' 4:142]

And the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — said in the authentic hadith: "The likeness of the one who remembers his Lord and the one who does not remember his Lord is like the living and the dead." [Sahih al-Bukhari]

If this is the state of *dhikr*, and if this is the state of prayer in relation to it, then let us speak at length about prayer, following it with a general discussion of *dhikr*.

A preliminary observation: every command of Allah — Exalted is He — that is a kind of *dhikr* is encompassed by prayer. Therefore prayer is the greatest manifestation of carrying out the Qur'an's commands by *dhikr*; it is the highest and greatest form of remembering Allah — Exalted is He — apart from being the highest manifestation of practical worship, since it contains bowing, prostration, and supplication. Hence, to speak of prayer in the context of *dhikr* is the correct beginning for every discussion.

Allah — Exalted is He — commanded the Muslim to glorify, magnify, recite the Qur'an in prayer, send prayers upon the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and peace upon him, seek forgiveness, and supplicate — and all of that is *dhikr*. Each of these has an effect on the human soul, its purification, and its recognition of Allah — Exalted is He. All of that is in prayer, or in the remembrances surrounding it. Therefore prayer is the complete performance of *dhikr*. For this reason Allah — Exalted is He — made the five prayers obligatory, and the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — made abundantly clear to us the supererogatory prayers for whoever seeks more good.

Among the Qur'anic commands of *dhikr* is His saying:

> "…and magnify Him with magnification." [Qur'an, Al-Isra' 17:111]

Allah made the takbirat al-ihram in prayer obligatory, the takbirs of transition from standing to bowing, from standing to prostration, from prostration to sitting, and the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — prescribed for us that we magnify Allah — Exalted is He — thirty-three times after every obligatory prayer. In all of this is learning and confirmation that Allah is greater than everything.

Among the Qur'anic commands is His saying:

> "Exalt the name of your Lord, the Most High." [Qur'an, Al-A'la 87:1]

And His saying:

> "So exalt the name of your Lord, the Most Great." [Qur'an, Al-Waqi'ah 56:96]

Among the Qur'anic litanies is:

> "So exalted is Allah when you reach the evening and when you reach the morning. And to Him is [due all] praise throughout the heavens and the earth. And [exalted is He] at night and when you are at noon. He brings the living out of the dead and brings the dead out of the living and brings to life the earth after its lifelessness. And thus will you be brought out." [Qur'an, Ar-Rum 30:17–19]

The prayer begins with the supplication of praise: "Glory be to You, O Allah, and by Your praise, and blessed is Your name." In our bowing we say, "Glory be to my Lord, the Most Great," and in our prostration we say, "Glory be to my Lord, the Most High," and we glorify after every prayer thirty-three times. If the obligatory prayers and the unrestricted supererogatory prayers occupy many hours of the night and day, you will find that prayer is the practical realization of these commands, and through what is ingrained in the human soul it makes it realize Allah's glory, transcendence, greatness, and worthiness of praise, for He is the Bestower.

Among the Qur'anic commands is His saying:

> "Recite, [O Muhammad], what has been revealed to you of the Book…" [Qur'an, Al-Muzzammil 73:20]

that is, of the Qur'an; and of the sciences, the Qur'an is *dhikr*. Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur'an and indeed, We will be its guardian." [Qur'an, Al-Hijr 15:9]

Recitation of the Qur'an is a pillar of the pillars of prayer. And Allah — Exalted is He — commanded us to praise Him when He said:

> "And say, 'Praise to Allah…'" [Qur'an, Az-Zumar 39:74, etc.]

Among the remembrances of prayer is: "Allah hears the one who praises Him. Our Lord, to You be all praise." And Allah — Exalted is He — commanded us to pray and send peace upon the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and in prayer we say: "Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings," and "O Allah, pray upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad." And Allah — Exalted is He — commanded us to seek forgiveness when He said:

> "And seek forgiveness of your Lord and repent to Him." [Qur'an, Hud 11:3]

And the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — prescribed for us that we say after every obligatory prayer: "I seek forgiveness from Allah," three times.

Thus we find that prayer and its remembrances encompass the mothers of remembrances; it is an obligation in which the greatest commands of *dhikr* are realized, along with other commands such as bowing, prostration, and supplication. Therefore prayer became the pivot of this religion, which cannot stand except by it, as the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — said: "The head of the matter is Islam, its pillar is prayer, and the apex of its hump is *jihad* (جهاد)." Therefore man does not become a rememberer except through prayer, and through prayer man is written among "those men and women who remember Allah much." Prayer is magnification of Allah — Exalted is He — gratitude to Him, servitude to Him, humility before Him, and abasement to Him.

Prayer is the first manifestation of fulfilling obligation, and as soon as the human soul performs it, it immediately moves from one state to another: from pride, self-admiration, arrogance, and conceit to their opposites of good traits. It is a transfer of the human soul from one frame to another, from one condition to another. If this is the place of prayer in Islam, and its connection to the command of *dhikr*, then we must take a picture of it as an essential pillar in the issue of *dhikr*.

Prayer includes obligatory prayers and supererogatory prayers; some are repeated daily, some weekly, some yearly, and some are tied to an occasion. Prayer has remembrances that are part of it, remembrances that precede it, and remembrances that come after it, and all of this flows into the subject of knowing Allah — Exalted is He — and purifying the human soul, deepening the subject of fulfilling all divine obligations. Prayer indeed prohibits immorality and wrongdoing. In the book *Al-Usus fi al-Sunnah* there is a comprehensive presentation of prayer and its remembrances.

If we know the place of prayer in the issue of *dhikr*, then we should know that *dhikr* outside prayer completes prayer and the one who intends it, and at the same time it is a factor whose effects are reflected in the establishment of prayer. Through the heart's state in prayer man knows his true condition with Allah — Exalted is He —, and to the extent that his heart ascends and his spirit recognizes Allah, his prayer is performed properly.

Prayer was, in the life of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — the delight of his eye: "And He made the delight of my eye in prayer." Thus there is complementarity between prayer and remembrances: there is no *dhikr* without prayer, and prayer without remembrances does not bring the heart to life or elevate the spirit to humility. If remembrances are not part of a sound *sayr* (سير) to Allah — Exalted is He — they do not attain their complete wisdom. And it is because of the scarcity of sound *sayr* to Allah — Exalted is He — that the *khushu'* (خشوع) which the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — mentioned would be the first thing taken from the earth has disappeared.

Therefore we recognize the importance of *tasawwuf* (تصوف) in Islamic life in general before we speak of *dhikr*.

After we have known that prayer is *dhikr*, and that prayer has its remembrances that are part of it or follow it — such as the *adhan* (الأذان), the *iqamah* (الإقامة), and the supplication between the *adhan* and the *iqamah* — it is fitting that we know that the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — used to remember Allah in all of his states. Hence he — peace and blessings be upon him — left us remembrances for every state of life: remembrances tied to time, to place, to action, to events; daily remembrances, yearly remembrances, monthly remembrances, lifelong remembrances, and remembrances unrestricted by number, time, or place, as well as remembrances limited in number. It is the adab (أدب) of the Muslim to know all of this, preserve it, and take his share of it. Books have been composed specifically on this, and in the book *Al-Usus fi al-Sunnah* there is a comprehensive presentation of all of it.

It is to be observed that *dhikr* and supplication merge in some situations: every *dhikr* is a supplication inasmuch as it combines recognition, knowledge, and neediness to Allah — Exalted is He —; and every supplication is *dhikr* of Allah. Therefore "supplication is worship." What is first required of the *salik* (سالك) to Allah — Exalted is He — is perseverance in *dhikr*. Since it is not easy for every person to memorize many remembrances and supplications at the beginning, the people of *sayr* to Allah — Exalted is He — have habituated certain remembrances which they enjoin upon the beginner as his daily *wird* (ورد) and constant practice. Thus the paths differed: one path prescribed certain remembrances, another prescribed others, and each path claims its remembrances are distinguished in the subject of *suluk* (سلوك). What I say is: the complete *murshid* (مرشد) is the heir of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —, and this inheritance requires that he revive the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — in the chapter of *dhikr* and in other matters.

Focusing on a particular *dhikr* is not objectionable in itself. But what has spread in some circles — that turning to a *dhikr* other than the one designated in the path is almost a sin — is excess in Allah's religion, and the task of the heir is to remove people from it. We should say: our criticism is not aimed at special cases in which adherence to a single *dhikr* is a remedy or a means of reaching a particular meaning, for these are few in relation to the breadth of time. But to consider that as the original principle that can scarcely be accompanied by anything else — that is what we mean by "excess." What we wish to emphasize is that the heir's mission is revival, and his path must be the path of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —, for the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — gave every person what suited him, presented the kinds of remembrances suitable for people, and left us a legacy in everything. Therefore the heir must observe that.

The gathering of obligatory and recommended acts of worship, and the gathering of supplications and remembrances, deepens knowledge of Allah — Exalted is He — in the heart and fulfills the duties of gratitude to Him — Glorious and Exalted is He. The Qur'an is the reminder of Allah — Exalted is He —, the one that makes Him known, and the teacher for us in everything; therefore it is pure *dhikr*. We must give our spirits their due of all this so that we may be true rememberers of Allah, true knowers of Him, and true servants to Him.

### On *Tawassul* (التوسل)

Al-Mundhiri arranged in his book *Al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib* a chapter entitled "Encouragement toward Salat al-Hajah and its supplication," and the first hadith he mentioned in this chapter is this hadith from Uthman ibn Hunayf — may Allah be pleased with him:

A blind man came to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and said: "O Messenger of Allah, ask Allah to restore my sight." He said: "Shall I supplicate for you?" The man said: "O Messenger of Allah, it has become very hard for me to lose my sight." He said: "Go, perform ablution, then pray two rak'ahs, then say: 'O Allah, I ask You and turn to You by Your Prophet Muhammad — the Prophet of mercy. O Muhammad, I turn to my Lord by you that He restore my sight. O Allah, make him an intercessor for me and make him intercede for me.'" He returned and Allah restored his sight. [Reported by al-Tirmidhi, who said it is a *hasan sahih gharib* hadith; also reported by al-Nasa'i — and the wording is his — Ibn Majah, and Ibn Khuzaymah in his *Sahih*; and al-Hakim said it is *sahih* on the conditions of al-Bukhari and Muslim. The phrase "then pray two rak'ahs" is not in al-Tirmidhi's narration. It was also reported by al-Tabarani, who mentioned at its beginning a story: a man used to come to Uthman ibn Affan — may Allah be pleased with him — for a need he had, but Uthman would not pay attention to him or look into his need. The man met Uthman ibn Hunayf and complained to him. Uthman ibn Hunayf said to him: "Go to the place of ablution, perform ablution, then go to the mosque and pray two rak'ahs in it, then say: 'O Allah, I ask You and turn to You by our Prophet Muhammad — the Prophet of mercy. O Muhammad, I turn to You by my Lord, that He fulfill my need and remember your need, and come back to me so that I may return with you.'" The man went and did what he told him, then came to Uthman's door. The doorkeeper came and took him by the hand, brought him in to Uthman ibn Affan, seated him with him on a rug, and said: "What is your need?" He mentioned his need and Uthman fulfilled it for him. Then Uthman said: "I had not remembered your need until this hour," and said: "If you have any need, come to us." Then the man went out and met Uthman ibn Hunayf and said: "May Allah reward you well. He would not look into my need or pay attention to me until I did what you said." Uthman ibn Hunayf said: "By Allah, I did not do it, but I witnessed the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — when a blind man complained to him of losing his sight. The Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — said to him: 'Can you bear it?' The man said: 'O Messenger of Allah, I have no guide and it has become hard for me.' The Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — said to him: 'Go to the place of ablution, perform ablution, then pray two rak'ahs, then supplicate with these supplications.'" Uthman ibn Hunayf said: "By Allah, we had not parted or spoken long before the man came to us with no ailment at all." Al-Tabarani said, after mentioning his routes: "The hadith is *sahih*." A *tunfisah* is a rug; the word is also used for a mat of palm leaves about a cubit wide.

It is observed from these quotations that Uthman ibn Hunayf, in the time of Uthman's caliphate, taught a man to turn to Allah by the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — after the death of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —. This indicates that the Companions regarded *tawassul* to Allah by the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — after his death as permissible. We have seen al-Tabarani's statement that the hadith is *sahih*, and it is proof for the permissibility of *tawassul* to Allah by His Messenger after his death.

Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "And to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them." [Qur'an, Al-A'raf 7:180]

That is: name Him by them and call upon Him with them. Some tried to understand from this verse that Allah — Exalted is He — is not to be approached by *tawassul* except by His names, and they forbade *tawassul* to Allah — Exalted is He — by any created being, whoever he may be, except when the one used as means is righteous and alive. They understood *tawassul* here to mean supplication itself, and on this basis they forbade *tawassul* by prophets, messengers, and righteous people as long as they are dead. A dispute arose over this, and some tried to give this dispute a doctrinal content, declaring *tawassul* by the dead to be shirk, and some considered denial of *tawassul* by the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — and by prophets and righteous people, dead or alive, to be deviation and misguidance.

The authentic narration that has passed with us indicates that the idea of *tawassul* to Allah by His Messenger — peace and blessings be upon him — existed in the generation of the Companions after the death of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —. It is one of several formulas in how supplication is made. That one of the Companions used one formula does not indicate the prohibition of others. Therefore the permissibility of these formulas is legally valid. If a person feels comfortable with one formula, he may adhere to it; if he sees that the evidence does not permit it, he may discuss it as he would discuss any juristic issue. For this reason al-Ustadh al-Banna — may Allah have mercy on him — considered the disagreement over this matter a juristic disagreement, not a doctrinal one. It is, in his view, a juristic question in which perspectives are broad, and a person should follow what his soul is at peace with. If he is one of the people of evidence, he follows it; if he is not among the people of evidence, he can follow any *mujtahid* in it.

Al-Ustadh al-Banna — may Allah have mercy on him — said in *Risalat al-Ta'alim*, paragraph 90 of the section on understanding: "As for supplication when it is coupled with *tawassul* to Allah by one of His creatures, it is a subsidiary disagreement concerning the manner of supplication, not a matter of creed." Parties contended fiercely with al-Ustadh al-Banna over this stance, and it was an unjust stance from most of them. Had they been fair, they would have considered al-Ustadh al-Banna's word final on this matter, for it is not one of the things known necessarily from the religion; the evidence remains of the probabilistic kind — probabilistic in indication or probabilistic in transmission. Therefore there is room for *ijtihad* in this matter, and every *mujtahid* has his reward. Whatever a person's soul is at peace with in this matter, he may follow, and he may discuss it with others; but *takfir* and accusation of misguidance in this matter are error and excess.

In this matter I repeat what I have said more than once: it was from Allah's favor to al-Ustadh al-Banna — may Allah have mercy on him — that he was able to propose formulas for contemporary Islamic work that can constitute the common ground on which the fair-minded of this *ummah* meet, and that can be the correct launchpad for shared Islamic work toward one Islamic *ummah*, one Islamic state, and one shared vision.

### On the Sufi Calls for Help (*Istighathat*)

It has become customary in some Sufi circles — and others — for some people to call upon righteous men, alive or dead, asking them to relieve a distress, remove a harm, bring a benefit, or repel an injury. We see manifestations of this in ordinary life, in times of crisis, and constantly in some *dhikr* circles. They use in *dhikr* circles the word *madad* (مدد); you find this word repeated many times in the *dhikr* circle during the chanting, during the *dhikr* and the chanting, and between segments of chanting: "Madad, O my master So-and-so," "Madad, O my master So-and-so." Among the manifestations of this tendency is what we find among the common people in some circles, where they call upon al-Khadir — peace be upon him — saying: "O Khadir," "O Khadir the living, may He protect you," as a woman calls to her child or to someone else. Some of the Shi'a go far in this matter, for some of their words to certain Imams almost appear as pure supplication. Perhaps what is found in Shi'a circles is what seeped into circles of other people after they gave it another content and other interpretations.

I distinguish in this matter between an address that has the character of *tawassul* to Allah — which is connected to the previous question we presented in the previous chapter — for the hadith says: "O Muhammad, I turn to my Lord by you for my need." This is a fixed text whose generality is for the blind man; in it the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — addressed the blind man while at a distance after he performed ablution and prayed. Uthman ibn Hunayf then applied it to the one in need before Uthman. What is of this kind, the disagreement over it is the disagreement over the previous issue.

Therefore I distinguish between one who says: "O Muhammad, intercede for me with your Lord that He forgive me," and one who says: "O Muhammad, forgive me." The first form is part of the subject of *tawassul*; the second form enters into the subject of this chapter. Part of this subject is what we find among some who visit the graves of the righteous, asking them directly: "O So-and-so, [help] my husband," "O So-and-so, intercede for me," "O So-and-so, fulfill my need," and the like, whose forms are many and questions numerous.

Al-Ustadh al-Banna placed the matter in its correct framework in this subject, saying in paragraphs 90 and 91 of the section on understanding: "The awliya' are those mentioned in His saying:

> 'Unquestionably, [for] the allies of Allah there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve — those who believed and were fearing Allah.' [Qur'an, Yunus 10:62–63]

And *karamah* is established for them according to its legal conditions, with the belief that they — may Allah be pleased with them — have no power to bring benefit or harm to themselves in their lives or after their deaths, let alone that they could do anything of that for others. Visiting graves — whatever they may be — is a legislated Sunnah in the transmitted manner. But seeking help from those buried, whoever they may be, calling upon them for that, asking them to fulfill needs from near or far, vowing to them, building up graves… and swearing by them, and swearing by other than Allah, and the innovations connected to that are major sins that must be combated. We do not interpret these acts as blocking the means, for whoever studies the life of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — sees in it that protecting the side of *tawhid* is the foremost issue absolutely. There is no doubt that even if there exist some interpretations for these calls, they are at least a door of shirk for some people.

Allah — Exalted is He — commanded us to supplicate *for* those who came before, not to supplicate *to* them. He described the believers by His saying:

> "And [there is a share for] those who came after them, saying, 'Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith…'" [Qur'an, Al-Hashr 59:10]

And the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — taught us in our prayer to say: "Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah." So when the matter is reversed, and instead of supplicating *for* them we supplicate *to* them, that is the error.

Two things made this error spread in some circles. The first: some countries were ruled by the Ottoman state, and some people were influenced by the Batinite call in general. Among them there is a general conception of the Imam as one who knows the unseen, hears the calls of the circles, and you find much of this in their speech. Unfortunately, many students of the Sufi shaykhs consider their shaykhs likewise. We do not deny *kashf* (كشف), but that the shaykh should be considered knower of everything and always foreseeing people's affairs — if a person claims this, he has claimed a station above that of prophethood and messengership. Whoever studies the life of the Messenger — peace and blessings be upon him —, all his sayings, and all that the Qur'an said about our Messenger — peace and blessings be upon him — realizes that what we have said is of the basics. We do not consider anything too great for Allah's power, but from the legally required duties it is necessary not to give a creature more than Allah — Exalted is He — gave to the prophets and messengers. That a person from the [Sufi] stations should claim what was not given to the prophets and messengers is misguidance itself.

My general conception is that the matter of calls to awliya' and shaykhs seeped into Sufi circles from some Shi'a circles, evidenced by the word *madad* used by the Sufis, which is originally a Shi'i term. It is strange that you find some of them declaring a Shi'i an unbeliever when he says, "Madad, O Ali," while he says the same thing with complete ease, claiming that his conception differs from that. True, conceptions may differ, but the side of *tawhid* is scratched in both cases. Shaykh Abu al-Hasan al-Nadwi recorded in his book *Mudhakkirat Sa'ih fi al-Sharq al-Arabi* that he saw at the door of one of the shaykhs of the paths in Sudan a *dhikr* circle whose people were saying: "Madad, O my master Hasan, you are the sultan of the age." He marveled how the scholars of Sudan could remain silent. In my view, influence by some Shi'a circles is the first reason for the spread of this matter in Sufi circles. The alternative to all of that is: "Madad, O Lord," "Maddad, O Allah," "O Allah, grant *madad*," and so on.

As for the second reason for the existence of these matters in Sufi circles, it is the presence of strange reports that are not sound. Among them:

1. Al-Tabarani reported in *Al-Kabir* with a chain that the author of *Majma' al-Zawa'id* said: "Its men are trustworthy, though there is weakness in some of them, except that Yazid ibn Ali did not meet Utbah." — from Utbah ibn Ghazwan, raised to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —: "If one of you loses something, or one of you needs help while in a land where he has no companion, let him say: 'O servants of Allah, help me! O servants of Allah, help me! O servants of Allah, stop it!' For Allah has servants whom you do not see, and it has been tried."

2. Al-Tabarani and al-Bazzar reported with a chain that the author of *Majma' al-Zawa'id* said its men are trustworthy — from Ibn Abbas, raised to the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —: "Indeed Allah has angels on earth besides the recording angels; they write what falls from the leaves of trees. So if one of you suffers a sprain in a deserted land, let him call: 'Help me, O servants of Allah.'"

3. Abu Ya'la and al-Tabarani reported in *Al-Kabir* with a chain that the author of *Majma' al-Zawa'id* said: "In it is Ma'ruf ibn Hassan, and he is weak" — from Ibn Mas'ud, from the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —: "If the mount of one of you bolts in a land, let him call: 'O servants of Allah, stop it!' For Allah has a presence on earth that will stop it."

These are the reports on which the Sufis built their expansions in the matter of calls to shaykhs and awliya' and asking from them. If you verify them, you find them unsuitable as proof for anything. The first hadith is *munqati'* and cannot be used as evidence, especially in a matter connected to creed. The third hadith is weak and cannot support an argument in juristic matters, let alone one connected to creed. As for the second hadith, which reaches the rank of *hasan*, it speaks of angels; the text is about them, so to apply it to others is an error.

Moreover, matters of the unseen require texts. Where are the texts saying that So-and-so is such, or that So-and-so is such? Matters of the unseen do not enter into juristic analogies in the first place. This matter must be uprooted from Sufi circles and others, root and branch, because of what follows from it of scratching the side of *tawhid*. Yet with the existence of interpretation and what we have seen from some apologists for these people, we must not hasten to *takfir* and throwing the charge of shirk except where the charge is in its proper place, with clear proof and evident argument.

### On What Is Called the Ecstatic Utterances of the Sufis (*Shatahat*)

Among the greatest calamities and most terrible deviations in the history of Islam and Muslims is what people have introduced under the title "the ecstatic utterances of the Sufis" (*shatahat al-sufiyyah*). It is of the greatest enormities, the foulest impurity, and the greatest trial. We seek Allah's refuge from whoever does not seek refuge from that.

Aisha — may Allah be pleased with her — was asked in a hadith: "Did Muhammad — peace and blessings be upon him — see his Lord — Exalted is He?" She said: "Glory be to Allah! My hair stood on end because of it." Although this issue is disputed, the skin of our mother — may Allah be pleased with her — shivered at its mention. By Allah, what would her stance be if one of the Companions were with a man saying about himself, "I am Allah"? By Allah, the only stance toward him would be the sword cutting off his neck. The stance of the Muslims in this matter has been such in every age witnessed by good: the age of the Companions, the Successors, and the Successors of the Successors, and even after that, until they killed al-Hallaj.

Al-Suyuti mentioned in *Tarikh al-Khulafa'* that in the year 309 AH al-Husayn al-Hallaj was brought into Baghdad mounted on a camel as an exhibit, then crucified alive, and it was cried out against him: "This is one of the callers of the Qarmatians, so know him." Then he was imprisoned until he was killed in the year 309. Al-Suyuti also says in the same book: "And in the year 309, that is after three hundred, al-Hallaj was killed by the fatwa of the judge Abu Umar and the jurists and scholars that his blood was lawful. In his circumstances there are reports that people have composed separate works." It is noteworthy that between his imprisonment and killing were about nine years, which indicates that [the authorities] did not hasten to kill him. If this is how it was until the killing of al-Hallaj, and the *ummah* agreed on the obligation of killing him for it, is this not proof that the early generations of this *ummah* were agreed on cursing whoever dares to say such to Allah? And the great tragedy is that what al-Hallaj said, for which the *ummah* agreed on killing him, became a philosophy that is taught and studied, until some mention when it is permissible for a person to say "I am Allah" and when it is not. May the curse of Allah be upon those who do not seek refuge from those who do not seek refuge from such a thing.

That man should witness that everything is done by Allah, including the actions of man himself — that is one thing; and that man should say about himself, "I am Allah" — that is another thing. That he should witness that everything subsists by Allah — that is one thing; and that he should say about himself, "I am Allah" — that is another thing. It is of the blindness of heart, sight, and insight that such enormities should be hidden in the *ummah* under any justifications and interpretations. Are these people not ashamed before Allah and before the servants of Allah when they spout such speech? Our Lord said:

> "They have certainly disbelieved who say that Allah is Christ, the son of Mary." [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:17]

And these people want us to call a [divine] state by a man who says, "I am Allah." What ignorance is this, what disbelief is this, what filth and fraud? And how can a heart find rest in hearing such filthy uncleanness and consider it a state? By Allah, it is nothing but the devil's deceptions and whisperings. By Allah, I see no treatment for these people except killing if they persist in these spoutings and claims. Some of what these misguided people use as evidence: they say that the authentic divine hadith says: "Whoever shows enmity to a *wali* of Mine, I declare war upon him," and "My servant does not draw near to Me with anything more beloved to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him, and My servant continues to draw near to Me with supererogatory acts until I love him. When I love him, I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes, and his foot with which he walks. If he asks Me, I will surely give him, and if he seeks refuge with Me, I will surely protect him." I say: Is this what you use as evidence that it is permissible for a person to say about himself that he is Allah? The hadith itself says: "My servant continues to draw near to Me…" Do they overlook the word "servant" and resort to a metaphorical issue to say a word that is disbelief itself? They say: the divine hadith says: "O son of Adam, I fell sick and you did not visit Me. He said: O Lord, how can I visit You when You are the Lord of the worlds? He said: Did you not know that My servant So-and-so fell sick and you did not visit him? Did you not know that if you had visited him you would have found Me with him? O son of Adam, I asked you for food and you did not feed Me. He said: O Lord, how can I feed You when You are the Lord of the worlds? He said: Did you not know that My servant So-and-so asked you for food and you did not feed him? Did you not know that if you had fed him you would have found that with Me…" I say: Is this what you use as evidence for such a thing, when the hadith itself says, "My servant So-and-so fell sick"? Do they ignore the word "servant" and dare such audacity against Allah?

Allah — Exalted is He — made clear that the pledge of allegiance to His Messenger — peace and blessings be upon him — was a pledge to Allah in the complete sense:

> "Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you, [O Muhammad] — they are actually pledging allegiance to Allah. The hand of Allah is over their hands." [Qur'an, Al-Fath 48:10]

And He — Glorious is His majesty — said:

> "Whoever obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah." [Qur'an, An-Nisa' 4:80]

So would anyone say that Muhammad is Allah, or that one of the Companions said that about himself? Woe to him, woe to him! How can he permit what Allah — Exalted is He — did not permit to His Messenger, for He commanded him:

> "Say, 'I am only a man like you, to whom has been revealed that your god is one God.'" [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:110]

And these people say, "I am Allah." How then does the pure creed of truth that the first generations were upon surge in the hearts of Muslims, so that they kill whoever dares such speech, cutting off the root of this accursed disbelief? The *ummah* was agreed until the killing of al-Hallaj that the one who says such a thing is obligatory to kill. So how has such speech become like ordinary language in many circles? It is a grievous thing, and it is something that this *ummah* must be purified of, by establishing Sufi circles free from deviation and fraud.

Hujjat al-Islam al-Ghazali said in his *Ihya'*:

> "As for *shath* (شطح), we mean by it two kinds of speech. The first: the long and broad claims of passionate love for Allah — Exalted is He — and of union that dispenses with outward deeds, until some reach the claim of *ittihad* (اتحاد), lifting of the veil, vision by sight, and direct address, saying: 'It was said to us such-and-such, and we said such-and-such,' and likening themselves in that to Husayn ibn Mansur al-Hallaj, who was crucified for uttering words of this kind, and citing as proof his saying, 'I am the Truth,' and what is narrated from Abu Yazid al-Bistami that he said, 'Glory be to me, how great is my majesty!' This is a kind of speech whose harm to the common people is great, so much so that a group of farmers left their farming and put forth such claims. For this speech is alluring to the nature, since in it is idleness from deeds along with the purification of the soul by reaching stations and states. So the foolish are not incapable of claiming it for themselves, nor of picking up ornate words. And whenever they are rebuked for that, they are unable to say anything but: this rebuke comes from knowledge and disputation, and disputation is a veil, and disputation is an act of the soul; whereas this speech appears only from inner disclosure of the light of truth. Thus and the like of it its evil has spread in the land, and its harm among the common people is great, so that one who utters something of it, killing him is preferable in Allah's religion to reviving ten."

> "As for Abu Yazid al-Bistami — may Allah have mercy on him — what is narrated from him is not authentic; and even if something is from him, perhaps he was narrating it from Allah — Exalted is He — as words he repeated to himself, as if he had heard while saying: 'I am not I; there is no god but I; so worship me.' It should not be understood from him except as a narrative.

> "The second kind of *shath*: words whose apparent expressions are splendid and whose phrases are tremendous but behind which there is no substance. These are either not understood by their speaker, but issue from a confusion in his mind and a disturbance in his imagination, due to his lack of grasp of the meaning of what he has heard — and this is the most of it; or they are understood by him but he is unable to express meanings in refined words, and there is no benefit in this kind of speech except that it confuses hearts, stuns minds, bewilders thoughts, or leads one to understand from it meanings not intended, so that each person's understanding follows his whim and nature. After this speech the Shaykh al-Ghazali says: As for the enormities, what we mentioned under *shath* enters into them, and there is another matter particular to them: diverting the words of the Shari'ah from their understood outward meanings to inward matters from which no benefit reaches the minds, like the practice of the Batinis in interpretations. This too is forbidden, and its harm is great. For if words are diverted from the import of their outward meanings without support in a transmitted report from the Legislator, and without a necessity calling to it from rational evidence, the result is nullification of trust in words and loss of the benefit of the speech of Allah — Exalted is He — and the speech of the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him —. For what is understood from it will not be trusted, and the inward has no control; rather, motives conflict in it and it can be interpreted in various ways. This too is of the widespread great innovations, and perhaps their authors intend to amaze people, for souls incline toward the strange and delight in it. By this path the Batinis reached the destruction of the whole Shari'ah by interpreting its outward meanings and applying them to their whim, as we have mentioned of their doctrines in the book *Al-Mustadhihri*, a kind of refutation of the Batinis. Among the interpretations of the people of enormities is the interpretation of some of them of His saying:

> 'Go to Pharaoh; indeed, he has transgressed.' [Qur'an, Taha 20:24]

They say: it is an allusion to his heart, and they say: he is the intended Pharaoh, and he is the tyrant over every human being. And in His saying:

> 'And cast down what is in your right hand.' [Qur'an, Taha 20:69]

They say: it means everything you rely upon and depend upon besides Allah — Exalted is He — should be cast away. And in His saying:

> 'And eat and drink until the white thread of dawn becomes distinct to you from the black thread [of night].' [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:187]

They intend by it seeking forgiveness in the depths of the night. And so on until they distort the Qur'an from its beginning to its end away from its outward meaning and from the interpretation transmitted from Ibn Abbas and the generality of scholars. Some of these interpretations are known to be false with certainty — such as interpreting Pharaoh as the heart, for Pharaoh is a bewitched person about whose existence recurrent reports have reached us, and the call of Moses to him, like Abu Jahl, Abu Lahab, and other disbelievers. He is not of the genus of devils and angels, which are not perceived by the senses, so that interpretation could extend to his words. Likewise interpreting the dawn meal as seeking forgiveness, for it concerns food, and the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him — said: 'Take the pre-dawn meal, for in it is blessing,' agreed upon, and 'Hasten to the blessed food.' These are matters known by recurrent transmission to be false as reports. Some are known by predominant probability, and that is in matters not connected to the senses. All of that is forbidden, misguidance, and corruption of religion for people. Nothing of it has been transmitted from the Companions, the Successors, or al-Hasan al-Basri, despite his zeal in calling and admonishing people.

His saying — peace and blessings be upon him — 'Whoever interprets the Qur'an according to his own opinion, let him prepare his seat in Hell' — the version of this hadith [reads]: 'Whoever says something about it without knowledge' — and in another version: 'according to his own opinion' — its meaning is not general; it means that his aim and opinion are to establish a matter and confirm it, so he seeks the testimony of the Qur'an for it and applies it to it without there being a linguistic or transmitted indication supporting its application to it. It is not to be understood from it that interpreting the Qur'an by inference and reflection is rejected, for there are verses about which six, seven, or more meanings have been transmitted from the Companions and the exegetes. Not all of them can be heard from the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him —, for they may be mutually incompatible and not accept combination; then that is inference by sound understanding and lengthy reflection. It is to this that the supplication for Ibn Abbas — may Allah be pleased with him — points: 'O Allah, grant him understanding in religion and teach him interpretation.'

Whoever permits among the people of enormities such interpretations, despite their knowledge that they are not meant by the words, and despite their intending by them to call creatures to the Creator, is like one who permits invention and fabrication against the Messenger of Allah — peace and blessings be upon him — of what is in himself as a truth but which the Shari'ah does not speak; that is wrongdoing and misguidance and entering into the threat understood from his saying — peace and blessings be upon him —: 'Whoever lies about me intentionally, let him prepare his seat in Hell.' Rather, the evil in interpreting these words is greater and more serious, because it nullifies trust in words and cuts off the path of benefit and understanding from the Qur'an entirely. You have already known how the devil diverted the impulses of truth from praiseworthy to blameworthy. All of this is not except in names: if you follow these people out of habituation to the famous name without attention to what was known in the first age, you will be like one seeking the honor of wisdom by following one called wise, for the name 'wise' has come to be applied to the physician, the poet, and the astrologer in this age — through heedlessness of the change of words."

This concludes al-Ghazali's words.

### Some States Encountered by Those Journeying to Allah

1. One state encountered by those journeying to Allah — Exalted is He — is boredom and fatigue. This is a state that afflicts workers when they do not give themselves rest in work. The noble authentic hadith alluded to this: "Take from deeds what you can bear, for Allah does not grow weary until you grow weary, and the deeds most beloved to Allah are those done constantly, even if little." Therefore there is a state of boredom that affects the heart. Imam Ali — may Allah be pleased with him — said: "Give the hearts rest hour after hour, for when hearts grow weary they become blind." This indicates that the state of boredom should be guarded against by the *salik* to Allah: by not burdening his soul beyond its capacity, by giving his soul rest through some of its lawful enjoyments, and by the wise person intending a righteous intention in that, so that his rest becomes relaxation and worship. When the wise person grows weary of one deed, he shifts to another: if he is satiated with recitation, he occupies himself with *dhikr*; if he is satiated with *dhikr*, he occupies himself with action; if he grows weary of one science, he occupies himself with another; if he is satiated with the religious sciences, he occupies himself with general reading; if he is satiated with all of that, he occupies himself with reflection and contemplation; and after giving his family their rights of the duties of time. This is a matter that draws attention and is difficult to encompass. Ibn Ata' said: "There is no blame on you for the existence of boredom, provided you do not abandon obedience, and for the existence of gluttony in you, provided you restrict it for yourself at certain times, so that your concern is prayer, not the existence of prayer; for not every praying person is constant."

2. Another state encountered by those journeying to Allah is contraction (*qabd*) and expansion (*bast*). These are two states that alternate upon the heart like night and day. The people of *suluk* distinguish between the psychological contraction caused by grief over missing something, and the spiritual contraction caused by [spiritual] poverty; between the psychological expansion caused by the soul's attachment to a worldly matter, and the heart-expansion caused by [spiritual] richness. The *salik* to Allah must be very alert to these two states and know how to receive and treat them, for contraction may lead to bad adab with the Real or with people, and expansion may lead to bad adab with the Real or with people. Controlling oneself in expansion is harder; therefore they said: "Only a few preserve the limits of adab in expansion." Concerning the wisdom of contraction and expansion, Ibn Ata' said: "Your expansion is so that He does not keep you with contraction, and your contraction is so that He does not leave you with expansion, and your taking you out of them is so that you do not belong to anything beneath Him. The knowers are more fearsome when they are expanded than when they are contracted, and they do not stand at the limit." Psychological contraction is caused by ignorance of Allah, and it is a punishment. Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "Then after distress, He sent down upon you security [in the form of] drowsiness, overcoming a faction of you, while another faction worried about themselves, thinking of Allah other than the truth — the thought of ignorance, saying, 'Is there anything for us [to have done] in this matter?' Say, 'Indeed, the matter belongs completely to Allah.'" [Qur'an, Al-Imran 3:154]

For this they said: "No grief or sorrow comes to us except from our ignorance of the Living, the Sustainer." As for spiritual contraction, it may be an introduction to [knowing] Allah, or an effect of the heart's sensing awe of Allah. Psychological expansion is an effect of ignorance of Allah, or an effect of the soul's delight in a lawful or unlawful pleasure; one must be very wary of this kind of expansion, for it may sometimes be a cause of Allah's displeasure. In the story of Qarun there is a lesson:

> "When his people said to him, 'Do not exult. Indeed, Allah does not like the exultant.'" [Qur'an, Al-Qasas 28:76]

As for spiritual expansion, it is an effect of obedience, or a feeling of intimacy, or other heart-meanings. Allah — Exalted is He — said:

> "Say, 'In the bounty of Allah and in His mercy — in that let them rejoice; it is better than what they accumulate.'" [Qur'an, Yunus 10:58]

In any case, man must observe his two states of contraction and expansion, recognize their causes, and exercise control over them; for contraction may be an effect of neglecting the rights of time, and therefore they said: "Whoever does not observe time, all his time is displeasure."

3. Another state encountered by those journeying to Allah is dispersion (*tafriq*) and gathering (*jam'*). By *jam'* is meant that the heart senses a kind of general disturbance, or lack of tranquility, or scatteredness. It is of types: that a person feels people and forgets the Real; or that a person senses anxiety, agitation, disturbance, or something of the sort. Sometimes the cause is known, and sometimes it is not. These two states visit the *salik* frequently. As for the non-*salik*, he is in a state of constant dispersion, because the original condition for him is heedlessness. When the heart awakens and begins to sense states of annihilation in deeds, annihilation in attributes, and annihilation in essence, then it may sense this state — the state of dispersion or gathering. Sometimes dispersion reaches a degree of intensity in which man finds himself almost unable to do anything; and sometimes a person passes from a state of gathering considered the highest or most exalted station to a state of dispersion that is almost pure waswasa. In such a station Ibn Ata' said: "O light, when darkness comes upon you, it is so that you may know the measure of what you have of it."

One of the texts by which we understand the issue of dispersion and gathering and their alternation upon the heart is this text from Ubayy: "I was in the mosque when a man entered to pray and recited a recitation that I disapproved of. Then another entered and recited a recitation different from that of his companion. When we finished the prayer we all entered upon the Prophet — peace and blessings be upon him —, and I said: 'This one recited a recitation and I disapproved of it, and another entered and recited differently from his companion.' Then he — peace and blessings be upon him — struck me on the chest, and I broke into a sweat as if I were looking at Allah — Exalted is He — in fear. He said to me: 'O Ubayy, I was commanded to recite the Qur'an upon one *harf*…'" In this text we find great dispersion followed by great gathering.

Dispersion has its causes and gathering has its causes, and among these causes is what Allah — Exalted is He — mentioned:

> "And know that Allah intervenes between a man and his heart." [Qur'an, Al-Anfal 8:24]

When the *salik* to Allah falls into dispersion, let him strive to know its causes, avoid them, and try — as much as he is able — to remain in a state of gathering with Allah.
