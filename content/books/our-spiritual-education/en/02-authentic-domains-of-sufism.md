# Chapter 2: Authentic Domains of Sufism

You will find in the books of this science tens of thousands of issues: some in the course of laying down its topics, some in mentioning historical matters, and some in discussing its subject and its most eminent practitioners. Yet the authentic domains of this science go back to a group of matters, all of which are interrelated and some of which overlap with others. In its principal inquiries, it studies the spirit (*rūḥ*), the heart (*qalb*), the intellect (*ʿaql*), and the soul (*nafs*). It studies the experiential side of the science of creed, the inward side of jurisprudential issues, and the practical, experiential side of the Book and the Sunnah. Then it seeks complete realization of the state of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions, and progress through the stations of *Islām* (إسلام), *īmān* (إيمان), *iḥsān* (إحسان), *taqwā* (تقوى), gratitude, and so on. These inquiries have two aspects: theoretical and practical. We may say that these are the principal domains of this science. But as with every science, its principal domains necessarily give rise to other derivative domains. This requires linguistic usages, technical terms, and special expressions; it requires the existence of schools and imams, experiences and events; and it requires the existence of a methodology that must be verified, clarified, and refined. All of this requires controls and rules that curb excess, ward off deviation, and keep matters within their proper bounds. All of this has become attached to this science and part of it. This chapter introduces the principal domains of this science as we have defined them, so let us survey them briefly so that we may grasp the nature of this science through our knowledge of its principal domains.

## 1. The Spirit in the Science of Sufism

There is no inquiry into the essence of the spirit in the foundations of this science, for that is a matter governed by the texts, and the texts do not speak of that essence:

> And they ask you about the spirit. Say, "The spirit is of the command of my Lord. And you have not been given of knowledge except a little." [Qur'an, Al-Isrā' 17:85]

So investigating the essence of the spirit is overburdening oneself, and the people of this science are far from overburdening. Therefore their speech about the spirit revolved around two issues: returning the spirit to its original knowledge, and returning it to its original servitude. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And [mention] when your Lord took from the children of Adam—from their loins—their descendants and made them testify against themselves, [saying to them], "Am I not your Lord?" They said, "Yes, we have testified." [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:172]

Abū al-Dardā' narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Allah took the covenant from the loins of Ādam and brought forth from his loins every seed that He would create. Then He spoke to them face to face: 'Am I not your Lord?' They said: 'Yes indeed, we testify.'" [Ahmad] Thus the spirit, in the origin of creation, knows Allah, acknowledges servitude to Him, and recognizes that He is its Lord. But through its mixture with the body, accidents befall it, and it loses part of its knowledge and servitude as a result of that, and as a result of what it hears, receives, and absorbs from its environment. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Every child is born upon the *fiṭrah* (فطرة), then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian." [Sahih al-Bukhari and others] So the spirit begins to be affected by a host of factors surrounding it, and this results in a greater or lesser distance from its pure knowledge of Allah and its servitude to Him. This demands returning it to its origin and to its original state. People often fall into excess that distances them from the *fiṭrah*, or into negligence that distances them from servitude. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> O People of the Scripture, do not commit excess in your religion or say about Allah except the truth. [Qur'an, Al-Mā'idah 5:77]

And He said about the People of the Scripture:

> And many of them are transgressors. [Qur'an, Al-Mā'idah 5:62]

Returning the spirit to its complete original condition is not an easy matter that every human being masters; this science investigates what concerns this affair. The spirit must return to its complete knowledge of Allah, and this requires that a person realize Allah's Names along with complete servitude to Him. Its path is sound knowledge, keeping company with the people of that state, and remembrance of Allah, the Exalted. He said:

> And rely upon the Ever-Living who does not die, and exalt [Allah] with His praise. And sufficient is He to be, with the sins of His servants, Acquainted—He who created the heavens and earth and what is between them in six days and then established Himself above the Throne, the Most Merciful; so ask about Him one well informed. [Qur'an, Al-Furqān 25:58–59]

Note His saying, "so ask about Him one well informed." This text carries more than one meaning, one of which is: ask those who know Allah about Allah. In Luqmān's counsel to his son, He said:

> And follow the way of those who turn back to Me. [Qur'an, Luqmān 31:15]

Those who return to Allah have a well-trodden path. Knowledge of Allah and His attributes, and knowledge of pure servitude to Him, have a path and a method, and taking from the people of that state and following their example, along with much remembrance and meditation upon the Hereafter—that is the path of the spirit's return. We emphasize the matter of remembrance because through remembrance complete realization of Allah's Names and knowledge of Him is achieved. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, narrating from his Lord: "I am with him when he remembers Me." [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim] So Allah, the Exalted, is with the servant when the servant remembers Him, and Allah's companionship of the servant has many effects. Among the most prominent is Allah's care for the servant, so that he neither errs nor stumbles; and among them is that Allah causes him to realize His Names. Allah's companionship of the human spirit makes that spirit receive from Allah's Names and attributes—such as knowledge, wisdom, and mercy—while realizing servitude to Him, the Glorious and Exalted. This is the first domain of the science of Sufism.

## 2. The Heart in the Science of Sufism

There is much speech about the heart in the Book of Allah. He, the Exalted, said:

> It is not the eyes that are blind, but the hearts in the breasts that are blind. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:46]

So the heart may become blind. He said:

> [That] He may make what Satan has thrown a trial for those within whose hearts is disease and those whose hearts are hard. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:53]

So hearts may harden. He said:

> In their hearts is disease, so Allah has increased their disease. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:10]

So hearts may become diseased. He said:

> No! Rather, the stain has been upon their hearts for what they were earning. [Qur'an, Al-Muṭaffifīn 83:14]

And He said:

> Allah has set a seal upon their hearts and upon their hearing, and over their sight is a veil. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:7]

So the heart may be sealed and may have the stain upon it. He said:

> And [it is] so the hearts of those who disbelieve in the Hereafter would incline toward it and that they would be satisfied with it and that they would commit that which they are committing. [Qur'an, Al-Anʿām 6:113]

So the disbelieving heart is susceptible to the whispers of the devils among mankind and the jinn. He said:

> The Day when neither wealth nor children will avail, except one who comes to Allah with a sound heart. [Qur'an, Al-Shuʿarā' 26:88–89]

So the heart has a healthy state in which it is sound. He said:

> Those are the ones Allah has tested their hearts for purity. [Qur'an, Al-Tawbah 9:49]

So the heart is tested, just as the body is tested, and accordingly it either fails or succeeds. He said:

> They have hearts with which they do not understand. [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:179]

So there are hearts that do not comprehend. He said:

> And know that Allah intervenes between a man and his heart. [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:24]

So man may will something, but the heart does not obey. He said:

> And whoever believes in Allah—He guides his heart. [Qur'an, Al-Taghābun 64:11]

So there is no guidance for a heart except through faith in Allah. And He said:

> And among them are those who listen to you until when they depart from you, they say to those who were given knowledge, "What has he just said?" Those are the ones upon whose hearts Allah has set a seal, and they have followed their desires. [Qur'an, Muḥammad 47:16]

So this is a state in which Allah seals the heart of its possessor. Likewise, you find much speech about the heart in the hadith of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. He said: "Beware, there is a piece of flesh in the body: if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Beware, it is the heart." [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim] And he said: "Temptations will be presented to the hearts in succession, like a straw mat's straw being woven stick by stick. So any heart that accepts them will have a black dot put in it, and any heart that rejects them will have a white dot put in it, until the hearts become of two kinds: a white heart like a smooth stone, which temptation cannot harm as long as the heavens and the earth endure; and another black and corrugated like a worn-out water-skin, which neither knows good nor rejects evil except what suits its whim." [Sahih Muslim] Abū Khālid asked Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ: "What is black and corrugated?" He said: "Intense whiteness in blackness." I said: "What is 'corrugated'?" He said: "Turned inside out."

Ḥudhayfah said: "The Messenger of Allah narrated to us two hadiths, one of which I have witnessed, and I am waiting for the other. He told us that trustworthiness was inspired in the depths of men's hearts; then the Qur'an was revealed, and they learned from the Book and from the Sunnah. Then he told us about the removal of trustworthiness, saying: A man will sleep, and trustworthiness will be taken from his heart, leaving only a trace like a blister; then he will sleep again, and it will be taken from his heart, leaving only a trace like an ember rolled over one's foot, producing a blister. Then people will begin to trade with one another, and almost no one will be trustworthy, until it is said: So-and-so's family has a trustworthy man, and a man is praised for being sharp, clever, and wise, while in his heart there is not even the weight of a mustard seed of faith. There came a time when I made no pact with a man without later wishing, if he were a Christian or a Jew, that he had been faithful to his pact with me. Now, however, I would not make a pact with anyone except so-and-so and so-and-so." [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim]

And he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The hearts are of four kinds: a polished heart like a shining lamp; a sealed heart, which is the heart of the disbeliever; an inverted heart, which is the heart of the pure hypocrite who knew and then denied; and a heart with two inclinations, in which faith and hypocrisy are mixed like a plant watered with sweet water, and like a sore into which pus and blood run—whichever of the two overcomes the other prevails in it." [Ahmad]

Thus we find much speech about the heart in the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him. What are the signs of this heart's health and sickness? What are the scales of its uprightness and deviation? What are the controls of its completeness and deficiency? How do we restore sound sight and hearing to it? How does it become enlightened, and how does it become dark? What is the path of spiritual progress toward illuminating it? All of this is part of the science of Sufism, and all of it has its specialists, its researchers, and its scholars. It is not permissible for the Muslim community to be devoid of them; whenever the community is devoid of them, this means that kinds of knowledge have begun to disappear from the earth.

Abū al-Dardā' narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with a chain graded *ḥasan gharīb* by al-Tirmidhī: "We were with the Prophet when he raised his gaze to the sky and said: 'This is the time when knowledge will be taken from the people until they are unable to benefit from any of it.' Ziyād ibn Labīd al-Anṣārī said: 'How can it be taken from us when we recite the Qur'an? By Allah, we shall recite it and teach it to our wives and children.' The Prophet said: 'May your mother be bereaved of you, O Ziyād! I used to count you among the jurists of the people of Medina. Do not the Torah and Gospel exist with the Jews and Christians, yet they do not benefit from them?'" Jubayr said: "I met ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit and said: 'Will you not hear what your brother Abū al-Dardā' said?' I informed him of what Abū al-Dardā' had said. He said: 'If you wish, I shall tell you of the first knowledge that will be lifted: it is *khushūʿ* (خشوع). Soon you will enter the congregational mosque and not find in it a single man in a state of humility.'"

Consider these texts:

> But as for those in whose hearts is disease, then it increases them in evil upon their evil; and they die while they are disbelievers. [Qur'an, Al-Tawbah 9:125]

> Say, "It is, for those who believe, a guidance and a cure. And those who do not believe—there is deafness in their ears and blindness over them." [Qur'an, Fussilat 41:44]

> And when Our verses are recited to them as clear evidences, those who disbelieve say of the truth when it has come to them, "This is obvious magic." [Qur'an, Al-Anfāl 8:2]

> There has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book, by which Allah guides those who pursue His pleasure to the ways of peace and brings them out from darknesses into the light, by His permission, and guides them to a straight path. [Qur'an, Al-Mā'idah 5:15–16]

> And We have certainly made the Qur'an easy for remembrance, so is there any who will remember? [Qur'an, Al-Qamar 54:17]

> Allah has sent down the best statement: a consistent Book wherein is reiteration. The skins shiver therefrom of those who fear their Lord; then their skins and their hearts relax at the remembrance of Allah. [Qur'an, Al-Zumar 39:23]

> Then do they not reflect upon the Qur'an, or are there locks upon [their] hearts? [Qur'an, Muḥammad 47:24]

From observing these texts, you see scales by which to know the health and sickness of the heart through its states with the Qur'an. Through them you realize that some people have a heart, and therefore some of them have no heart. In all of this, the heart is other than the physical heart that regulates the circulation of blood, which humans and animals share. It is another heart connected to that heart in some way, and its location is the breast. Allah said:

> It is not the eyes that are blind, but the hearts in the breasts that are blind. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:46]

And He said:

> And the hearts reached the throats. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥijr 15:14]

This subject—the heart, its health and sickness—is a principal topic of the science of Sufism. The practicing Sufis, by way of approximation, are the most prominent people who have spoken about this subject throughout the ages, until they became its specialists. But when ignorance prevailed among those who speak about this science, matters became confused until the path of the heart's health became a mark of error. Consequently, diseases of the hearts spread, and that became part of the maladies of this age. It is natural, therefore, that reviving this aspect should be part of contemporary renewal.

From the texts we have mentioned and the observations we have made, we may put down broad guidelines for the issue of the heart as signposts on the straightest path to this subject:

1. The matter of the heart is a vast matter; its sickness and health are two delicate issues on which the corruption or righteousness of a person's worldly life and Hereafter depend. If the heart is sick, this is accompanied in this world by contradictory, mistaken stances in which a person remains anxious and bewildered, and the outcome of his affair is ruin and loss: "Whomever Allah sends astray—there will be no guide for him." [Qur'an, Al-Aʿrāf 7:186]

2. Rectifying the heart requires knowledge, action, and companionship: knowledge so that one may know the nature of health from sickness; action so as to eliminate and expel the sickness; and companionship so that one's resolve in travel and spiritual conversation may remain firm. All of this is part of the science of Sufism.

## 3. The Intellect in the Science of Sufism

In Islamic terminology one observes the legally responsible intellect (*al-ʿaql al-taklīfī*) and the divinely legislated intellect (*al-ʿaql al-sharʿī*). The legally responsible intellect is possessed by every human being unless he is insane, and by it a person is held legally responsible. This is the minimum level of intellect by which a person is considered responsible, accountable, and answerable before Allah for his conduct. After that, people are of two kinds: a group that has understood on behalf of Allah and comprehended His address, believed in Him, and committed themselves to Him—these are the truly intelligent; and a group that has not understood on behalf of Allah and has not committed itself to Him—these have not attained the divinely legislated intellect. Allah, the Exalted, said about the people of the Fire:

> And they will say, "If only we had listened or reasoned, we would not have been among the companions of the Blaze." [Qur'an, Al-Mulk 67:10]

This type of intellect has its seat in the heart and has degrees. There is the complete divinely legislated intellect, whose sign is a person's control of his desires according to Allah's command, along with understanding from Allah and surrender to Him. This type of intellect, and the way to reach it, is one of the inquiries of Sufism. How do we make our hearts understand on behalf of Allah? How do we discipline ourselves according to Allah's command? What is the path to that? All of this is part of the science of Sufism. There is no doubt that this is connected to the issue of the hesitant will and strengthening it, and to opposing the soul that commands evil and training it. Thus the subject of the intellect is connected to the world of the heart on one side and the world of the soul on the other. When the heart is weak before the power of the soul that commands evil, it surrenders to its desires and whims that oppose the law of Allah. And as the heart grows strong, it resists these desires; yet it remains weak before some of them, and sometimes yields to the whim of its own lower soul. In everything, this side is studied and perfected by the science of Sufism.

Discipline according to Allah's command does not mean that a person leaves behind all the desires of his soul; man is tested by these desires, and the Law-Giver has given him the right path to fulfilling permissible desires and opened for him outlets to escape from forbidden desires. All of this is part of the path, for true travel toward Allah is travel in harmony with the *fiṭrah*, not opposing or transgressing it. We may find a person wanting to repent from fornication, but when he finds himself in a tempting situation, he feels overcome and dragged toward disobedience by his soul and his devil, despite hating what he is in. What should this Muslim do to strengthen his heart to repel the sin and keep away from it? There is a group of measures: increasing the light of his heart; purifying his soul; traveling the lawful path to satisfy his desire within permitted bounds; or lessening the drives of desire through certain spiritual exercises, such as reducing food, fatiguing the body, eating less, and avoiding incitements to desire. All of this is part of the treatment by which the Muslim overcomes sin; overcoming sin is, in his regard, intellect. The matter is very broad: there are sensory desires, and there are spiritual desires such as love of leadership, stubbornness, greed for the world, and so on. There is also the discipline of the limbs, including the tongue, according to Allah's command; the discipline of the soul and heart according to Allah's command; and progress toward realizing all the commands. All of these are effects of the existence of the divinely legislated intellect in a person. In order to reach the divinely legislated intellect, one must travel the path to it. This is one of the domains and principal inquiries of this science. Indeed, sound practical progress in this science is, in truth, progress toward reaching the complete divinely legislated intellect. Therefore those who desire this science should desire such as this, and those who object to it should not object to all of it.

## 4. The Soul in the Science of Sufism

Some Sufis consider the soul to be the spirit after it has mixed with the body. The soul's mixture with the body gave the body effects upon it; the cause of these effects is the body's needs, which the soul embraces. When the body comes to have diseased demands and there is no discipline of the soul or rectification of the heart, the demands of the soul grow and renew themselves, and the body moves in service of them toward ruin. When the spirit mixed with the body, the two of them acquired aspirations, among which is the desire for sensory or spiritual immortality. This is the matter that Satan exploited in the downfall of Ādam:

> "Should I guide you to a tree whose possession will give you immortality?" [Qur'an, Tāhā 20:120]

Thus meanings arise in the soul that often lead to diseases. Diseases beget one another, increasing or decreasing, but they remain diseases. Therefore Allah, the Exalted, sent His laws to struggle against this soul until it becomes upright. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "There is no *jihād* (جهاد) greater than one who strives against his own soul in the cause of Allah." [Sunan al-Tirmidhī] And He said:

> But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [his] desire, indeed, Paradise will be [his] refuge. [Qur'an, Al-Naziʿāt 79:40–41]

Therefore the starting point of psychic health or sickness is non-contentment with the soul. Ibn ʿAṭā' Allah said in the *Ḥikam* (أحكام): "The origin of every sin, desire, and heedlessness is contentment with the soul; and the origin of every obedience, vigilance, and chastity is discontentment with it. Therefore keeping company with an ignorant man who is discontented with his soul is better for you than keeping company with a scholar who is content with his soul. For what knowledge is there in a scholar content with his soul, and what ignorance is there in an ignorant man discontented with his soul?" And Shaykh Zarūq said: "The foundations of blameworthy character traits are three: contentment with the soul, fear of creation, and despair of provision. From the first arise desire, heedlessness, and sin; from the second arise anger, envy, and rancor; from the third arise sickness, greed, and miserliness. Yet committing to a single foundation removes all of them: discontentment with the soul in all circumstances and caution against it at all times." And al-Sāmī said: "As for the character traits of the soul, they are: arrogance, self-admiration, boastfulness, haughtiness, deceit, aversion, greed, hope, malice, envy, irritation, panic, malingering, covetousness, appetite, withholding, cowardice, ignorance, laziness, vulgarity, harshness, following caprice, contempt, mockery, weakness, haughtiness, sharpness, foolishness, recklessness, disputation, domineering, wronging, enmity, rivalry, obstinacy, opposition, contention, competition, backbiting, slander, lying, trickery, commotion, bad opinion, estrangement, baseness, impudence, treachery, betrayal, iniquity, and Schadenfreude… and so on, of which the enumeration is lengthy. Therefore the seeker must know them, shun them, and strive to replace them with what is better; for one who does not know them only grows more backward with the passage of days. Replace arrogance with humility, sharpness with deliberation, lying with truthfulness—and with Allah is success."

By way of digression we say: The foundations of treatment as seen by the people of spiritual travel to Allah lie in opposing the soul when it demands sin or excess in the permissible, and in bearing harm from creation in obedience to Allah, and in adhering to the bounds of dress between the legal and recommended limits. Returning to the original subject: Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]. [Qur'an, Al-Shams 91:7–10]

And He said:

> Indeed, the soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil. [Qur'an, Yūsuf 12:53]

And He said:

> And I do not acquit myself. Indeed, the soul is a persistent enjoiner of evil, except those upon which my Lord has mercy. [Qur'an, Hūd 11:53]

And He said:

> O soul at peace, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him], and enter among My [righteous] servants, and enter My Paradise. [Qur'an, Al-Fajr 89:27–30]

These verses mention states of the soul: there is a purified soul, a seduced soul, a soul that commands evil, a self-reproaching soul, and a soul at peace that deserves Allah's pleasure and is itself pleased with Allah. From all of this, and from His saying:

> But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [his] desire… [Qur'an, Al-Naziʿāt 79:40]

one understands that the soul needs to be struggled against. Allah said:

> And strive for Allah with the striving due to Him. He has chosen you and has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty. [Qur'an, Al-Ḥajj 22:78]

What is this struggle? What are its limits? What are its lawful means? What are the states of the purified soul that must be realized through it? All of this is one of the principal inquiries of the science of Sufism and one of its domains. Indeed, the purification of the soul (*tazkiyat al-nafs*) is one of the most important matters of Sufism—indeed, it almost constitutes a science in itself. It is a matter that has almost disappeared in this community except among this group, although the purification of souls is one of the principal purposes of the sending of the Messengers, peace be upon them. Allah said:

> Just as We have sent among you a Messenger from yourselves who recites to you Our verses and purifies you and teaches you the Book and wisdom. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:151]

You rarely find anyone who speaks about the matter of purifying the soul and knows the nature of this purification and its path from outside this group. Try to compare the works of Muslim scholars throughout the ages and count how many of them spoke about this subject: you will find only a few from outside this group who gave this subject its due or were adequate to it. Even Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, one of the luminaries who flourished in this subject, had a Sufi upbringing and education before he studied under Ibn Taymiyyah, and he gave Sufism a Salafī orientation. Without that early upbringing, Ibn al-Qayyim would not have been able to elaborate as he did. Indeed, when he spoke in his book *Madārij al-Sālikīn* about the meanings of travel to Allah, he built upon the book *Manāzil al-Sā'irīn* by the Sufi al-Harawī. Were it not for Ibn al-Qayyim, one would not find in Ibn Taymiyyah's school anyone who spoke about this science and devoted a book to it.

From what has passed, we realize that purifying the soul requires one who purifies it and requires struggle on the part of its possessor. This requires knowledge: knowledge of the states of the soul and its deficiencies, and knowledge of the path of realization in the states and ways of ridding oneself of shortcomings. All of this is among the principal domains of the science of Sufism.

## 5. Sufism and the Experiential Side of the Science of Creed

In the science of creed, doctrinal questions are usually presented, the proofs are set forth, and the major issues over which there is disagreement between Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah and others are mentioned. The gustatory, emotional, experiential, and practical side, and the path to it, are barely touched upon. For example, in the science of creed it is presented that Allah, the Exalted, is described by hearing, sight, speech, will, power, life, and knowledge; but for the servant to feel that Allah hears him, that Allah sees him, for the heart to taste, as it reads the Qur'an, that the Qur'an is the speech of Allah, and for a person to feel that everything around him is an effect of Allah's power—these meanings and their likes are not usually studied in books of the science of creed. They are usually studied in books of Sufism, for these are the books that investigate tasting the meanings of creed. Note that this realization is not merely recommended; sometimes it is obligatory. We observe that the Sunnah gave the matter of tasting the meanings of creed very great emphasis. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Taste the sweetness of faith: whoever is pleased with Allah as Lord, with Islam as religion, and with Muhammad as Messenger." [Sahih Muslim] And he said: "There are three qualities, whoever has them will taste the sweetness of faith: that Allah and His Messenger are more beloved to him than anything else; that he loves a person only for the sake of Allah; and that he hates to return to disbelief after Allah has saved him from it as he would hate to be thrown into the Fire." [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim]

In books of creed you may read words about faith and its conditions, about disbelief and its manifestations, and about hypocrisy and its definition; but books of Sufism are the ones that speak about the practical path to realizing the meanings of faith, the practical path to realizing certainty and tranquility, and they mention therein ways of ridding oneself of hypocrisy. All of these are matters in which it is not enough for a person merely to know their definition. A person may know their definition and still remain far from their realities if he does not see a path to them. The Bedouins said: "We have believed." Say: "You have not [yet] believed; but say, 'We have submitted,' for faith has not yet entered your hearts." [Qur'an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:14]

Al-Ṭabarānī narrated in *al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr* with a chain whose men are the men of *Ṣaḥīḥ* from Umm Tamīm, may Allah be pleased with her, who said: "I was with the Prophet when Ḥarmlah ibn Zayd came and sat before him. He said: 'O Messenger of Allah, faith is here,' and he pointed to his tongue, 'and hypocrisy is here,' and he pointed to his chest, 'and we do not remember Allah except little.' The Prophet remained silent, then Ḥarmlah repeated it and the Prophet took hold of the edge of his garment and said: 'O Allah, make his tongue truthful, his heart grateful, grant him Your love and the love of those who love You, and turn his affair to good.' Ḥarmlah said: 'O Messenger of Allah, I have brothers who are hypocrites among whom I was a leader; shall I point them out to you?' He said: 'No. Whoever comes to us in a manner we approve, we ask forgiveness for him; and whoever persists in his religion, Allah is more entitled to him. Do not tear away anyone's veil.'" Thus we find that the tongue's profession is one thing, and what is in the heart is another. What, then, is the path to realizing the meanings of creed?

You find a person who has memorized much about the traits of the Messenger of Allah but is far from following his example; and you find a person who knows little but is eager to follow his example. You find a person who has taken his share of the inheritance of prophethood in necessary traits such as trustworthiness, conveyance, truthfulness, and perspicacity; and you find a person who speaks about such things while being the farthest of people from them. Mere knowledge is one thing, and traveling to realize it is another. What, then, is the science that points to the path and completes the aspects usually discussed in books of creed? That science is Sufism among the Islamic sciences. Even though much has been mixed with this science, that does not negate it; nor should it make us overly sensitive toward it. Rather, we must purify it and give it its proper limits and rights. The science of creed is what restricts Sufism, and Sufism is what completes the science of creed insofar as it is its experiential side. If it goes beyond that by contradicting it or producing new creeds that oppose the Book and the Sunnah, or that oppose the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah inherited from the righteous predecessors, then there is deviation, misguidance, and reprehensible innovation. When you read in a Sufi book, or hear from a Sufi, words that do not occur in the Book or the Sunnah, or are not normally spoken by the righteous predecessors, and are neither a technical term nor a correct understanding of the texts nor a realization of a meaning mentioned in the Book and the Sunnah—then do not hesitate to reject it, confident that what you have done is precisely true Sufism and nothing else. These are the people of spiritual conduct whom the community must accept alongside you.

Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī said: "When a subtle point from the speech of the Sufis occurs in my heart, I do not accept it except with the testimony of a reliable witness from the Book and the Sunnah, for Allah has granted me infallibility in the Book and the Sunnah, and He has not granted it to me in anything else."

Among the famous counsels of the imams of spiritual conduct is the saying of one of them: "O my son, be a *muḥaddith* (حَدِيث) who is a Sufi, and do not be a Sufi who is a *muḥaddith*." The meaning is that the Sufi who is a *muḥaddith* keeps the text in mind while the subtle point is behind him, whereas the *muḥaddith* who is a Sufi keeps the subtle point in mind while the text is behind him. When you find in a book, or hear from a person, a subtle point about a text that contradicts the understanding of the community of creed, the community of legal reasoning, the community of exegesis, or the principles of jurisprudence, cast it aside without hesitation. Sufism is realization; if its people wish to produce new creeds, new legal reasonings, mistaken interpretations, or corrupt constructions in matters of creed based on fabricated or weak hadiths, then one should not hesitate to refute them. Indeed, such matters are the first thing intended by the hadith: "Whoever introduces into this matter of ours what is not from it, it is rejected." [Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim]

What innovation is greater than producing new matters in questions of creed that did not occur in the heart of a Companion or on his tongue? Indeed, if anyone had uttered such a thing before that generation, they would have killed him or punished him without hesitation. O Allah, we are at peace with those with whom You are at peace, and at war with those against whom You have waged war; we are innocent of everything that contradicts the guidance of Your Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions.

Among the later Sufis, it has become a mark of spiritual arrival for a person to say: "I am Allah," and a mark of spiritual openings for someone to say: "The universe is Allah." By Allah, these people deserve nothing but the sword cutting their necks, no matter what garments of asceticism they wear or what robes of righteousness they adorn themselves with. The Qur'an came to say:

> Cursed were those who said, "Indeed, Allah is the Messiah, son of Mary." [Qur'an, Al-Mā'idah 5:72]

Yet these people say of everything that it is Allah. Do you see any Muslim hesitating to use the sword against them? I say these words knowing full well what interpretations they give to them; but by Allah, I would rather kill one who says this, even if he had the best interpretation, than be a coward or remain silent while they have whatever interpretation they have. What interpretation can make a Muslim heart accept a person saying "I am Allah" or the like of that odious, wicked disbelief? True Sufism is tasting the true creed; when anything is added to that, it becomes heresy and is no longer Sufism. However, we say that we must not rush to declare disbelief except after verifying our understanding and the attribution of the statement to its speaker. The phrase "This text is disbelief, and Allah knows best about its author" is a judicious phrase when it truly fits the case.

After this digression we return to say: among the principal domains of the science of Sufism is this side that we have called the experiential side of Islamic creed—the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah. As for anything beyond that, let its people fear Allah.

Do you think any of the righteous predecessors of this community understood the torment in a verse such as His saying:

> They will be given to drink [from] a boiling spring; for them will be no food except from a thorny plant which neither nourishes nor avails from hunger. [Qur'an, Al-Shuʿarā' 26:54–55]

as a pleasure from the torment? And do you think any of the predecessors understood a verse such as His saying:

> Indeed, those who disbelieve—there will be for them the fire of Hell; they will not be finished off by death, nor will its torment be lightened for them. Thus do We recompense every ungrateful one. And they will cry out therein, "Our Lord, remove us; we will do righteousness—other than what we were doing!" [Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:36–37]

to mean that the disbelievers take pleasure in the torment, so that even if they were offered to come out of the Fire they would not come out? Is not linking such meanings to Sufism an affirmation of creeds contradictory to what the righteous predecessors were upon, never mentioned by Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah in their books? Is this not misguidance and disbelief itself? Strange indeed are such tendencies; stranger still is that those who say such things consider themselves knowers of Allah and people of the Real. By Allah, they are the most ignorant of Allah's creation concerning Allah, and they are people of the reality of disbelief. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And they have attributed to Allah partners from among His servants; indeed, man is clearly ungrateful. [Qur'an, Al-Zukhruf 43:15]

To make anyone a partner of Allah from among His servants is manifest disbelief. Do you see those who say that the universe is a part of the divine essence condensed—are these knowers of Allah? Woe to them, woe to them! O Allah, we seek refuge in You from the interpretations of the ignorant, the excesses of the extremists, and the pretenses of the falsifiers.

This type of Sufism that distorts texts from their places and establishes creeds contradictory or opposed to the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah is not Islamic Sufism; it is misguidance from the truth. The Sufism we know and call to is the Sufism by which a person realizes the meanings of creed. Its possessor knows Allah with the knowledge of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah and has a gustatory, experiential knowledge that agrees with the criteria of the Book and the Sunnah. Its possessor is realized in following the Messenger of Allah in outward and inward matters; he feels the affairs of the Hereafter as if he sees them with his own eyes, and the same is true of his sensing all the matters of creed. As for the Sufis having special creeds of their own, this is misguidance from Sufism itself. What is meant is that those who are complete in it and have made it a science derive it from the Book and the Sunnah, so that it produces among its people correct understanding and correct tasting of the texts. But distorting texts from their places is the path of the Jews with their scriptures, not the path of the believers. By Allah, some of these people have gone further astray than the Christians, for the Christians made the Messiah a part of Allah, while these people have made everything a part of Allah.

True Sufism is realization of the matters of creed, and nothing beyond that.

## 6. Sufism as a Complement to the Science of Jurisprudence

Books of jurisprudence usually begin with the topic of purification, but they do not speak much about the inward meanings that should accompany the act of purification. Then they discuss prayer: its conditions, pillars, obligatory elements, recommended practices, etiquette, disliked matters, and nullifiers, but they do not discuss the inward meanings that should accompany it—such as *khushūʿ*, the path to it, and the factors leading to it—although it is a science among the sciences, indeed, by the testimony of the texts, the first science to be lifted from the earth, as the hadith states. What, then, is the science that completes the science of jurisprudence in these matters? There is no doubt that it is Sufism; for it is the science that usually studies such matters. It is the science that completes jurisprudence in the inward aspects, such as the science of sincerity and the path to it. Indeed, it is what prepares a person to comply with jurisprudential rulings, for a person does not complete his compliance except when his spiritual travel is complete. Therefore the people of spiritual conduct spoke about annihilation in the acts of Allah, annihilation in His attributes, and annihilation in His essence—topics generally above the ordinary—then they speak about annihilation in the rulings. The practical result of the gustatory knowledge of Allah, the Exalted, is complete adherence to His rulings. From here we understand the misguidance of some who look askance at Sufism, considering travel toward Allah equivalent to escape from His rulings. How can that be when Allah, the Exalted, said to His Messenger:

> And indeed you are of a great moral character, so follow not what they say. [Qur'an, Al-Qalam 68:4–5]

Therefore al-Junayd said, concerning a group that made arrival at Allah equivalent to escape from the rulings of the Sharīʿah: "Yes, they arrived—but to Hellfire." The jurists used to say: "Whoever studies jurisprudence but does not study Sufism becomes corrupt; whoever studies Sufism but does not study jurisprudence becomes a heretic; and whoever combines them has truly realized." Sufism is a necessary complement to jurisprudence; and jurisprudence is necessary as a judge over Sufism, as a regulator and guide for action. Whoever misses any of that has missed half the matter. Sufism and jurisprudence are two complementary sciences; if they conflict, that is error, misguidance, or deviation. By conflict is meant that a Sufi proceeds far from jurisprudence, although jurisprudence is the judge; or that a jurist keeps away from realization, which is a mark of corruption of the heart. The jurist must study Sufism, and the Sufi must study jurisprudence. By that is meant that the jurist's knowledge should include what relates to rulings and what relates to the path of action and realization, and that the Sufi's knowledge should include the rulings he needs. All of this should proceed upon sound health and in light of correct knowledge. Therefore the great imams of spiritual conduct, such as Shaykh al-Rifāʿī, said: "The end of the jurists and the Sufis is one." This is said here because some ignorant Sufis hurl at everyone the saying: "Whoever has no shaykh, his shaykh is Satan." An ignorant Sufi says it while inviting people to his ignorant shaykh; another ignorant Sufi says it while inviting people to his learned shaykh; and a mistaken Sufi says it without knowing how to place it in its proper contexts. Whoever has no shaykh is an ignorant person who does not learn and refuses to learn—such a person's shaykh is Satan. As for the person who travels in light of knowledge, his imam is knowledge and the Sharīʿah. Among the rules that Shaykh Zarūq mentioned in his book *Qawāʿid al-Taṣawwuf* is the subject of the seeker's need for a shaykh. He said that *taqwā* (تقوى) does not need a shaykh because of its clarity, and he said: "The discerning man is sufficiently served by the Book in his advancement, though he is not safe from the rashness of his own soul." Thus what matters is a person's ability to learn, then to travel in light of this knowledge. This is the minimum that Allah has imposed upon His servants, and this can be available to a person if he has the ability to learn and understand through personal reading of reliable, well-known books, or he may take it from knowledgeable practitioners, whether they are conventionally called Sufis or not. This is a hidden subject, but we have wanted to emphasize it by mentioning it more than once.

Returning to our subject: the science of Sufism and the science of jurisprudence are two complementary sciences, and both are necessary for every person, with the observation that what one person needs of them differs from what another needs. Deeper engagement in them or in one of them falls under communal obligations in relation to the community, and under recommended acts in relation to every Muslim. By this section we have come to know another principal domain of the science of Sufism.

## 7. Sufism and the Practical, Experiential Side of the Book and the Sunnah

The Book and the Sunnah are texts; the Muslim is charged with understanding them and realizing them. If there is understanding of the texts without realization, there is a deficiency. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, "was the Qur'an in character," and the Companions of the Messenger of Allah used to memorize some of the Qur'an, reflect upon it, and act by it before moving on to something else. The knowledgeable practitioners and the realized Sufis are those who combined understanding and realization together. What is *īmān* and what is its reality, and how is it realized? What is *Islām* and what is its reality, and how is it realized? What is *iḥsān* and what is its reality, and how is it realized? What is *taqwā* and what is its reality, and how is it realized? What is gratitude and what is its reality, and how is it realized? The same is true of patience, surrender, contentment, reliance upon Allah, love of Allah, sincerity; and the same is true of modesty, generosity, chastity, humility, not looking longingly at what others possess, asceticism, piety, and *khushūʿ*; and the same is true of the etiquette of outward and inward in prayer, Zakah, fasting, pilgrimage, travel, *jihād*, mutual exhortation and remembrance, commanding good and forbidding evil, the etiquette of companionship and neighborliness, kindness to parents and maintaining ties of kinship, and so on through all the matters addressed by the texts. Sound understanding of the texts and sound realization of them together make for complete adherence to the Book and the Sunnah. The godly scholars exerted their utmost to reach the understanding of the Book and the Sunnah, and the realized Sufis exerted their utmost to realize the Book and the Sunnah, so that their meanings remain alive and embodied by people who are models for others throughout the ages. Through all of this Islam remained and remains alive. No deficiency occurs except from a mistaken or deficient understanding, or from a deficient or incomplete realization. Both of these have occurred, and therefore what has happened has happened. There must be a complete return to both so that the matter may be rectified and Islam may live.

The greatest calamity occurs when a mistaken understanding is joined with a mistaken realization—and the ugliest thing we see of that is among some ignorant Sufis. Then what befell other communities befalls this community: distortion of words from their places and realization in paths of misguidance. Here comes the task of the godly scholars to restore matters to their proper place, repudiating the interpretations of the ignorant, the distortions of the extremists, and the pretenses of the falsifiers.

At His saying:

> And there are some among them who hurt the Prophet and say, "He is an ear." Say: "A good ear for you." He believes in Allah and believes the believers, and is a mercy to those of you who believe. And those who hurt the Messenger of Allah—there is for them a painful punishment. [Qur'an, Al-Tawbah 9:61]

and His saying:

> Then your hearts became hardened after that, being like stones or even harder. For indeed, there are stones from which rivers burst forth, and there are some of them that split open and water comes out, and there are some of them that fall down for fear of Allah. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:74]

some ignorant Sufis stopped and referred the pronoun in "from it" back to Allah, the Exalted. That is distortion of words from their places and an ignorant understanding of the texts that none of this community would approve. Such things are many among people like them. If the scholars remain silent before this contention, little of knowledge—and indeed of Islam—remains that is not torn apart. In this situation the duty of the active scholar is to restore the matter to its proper place for the sake of sound understanding, and to make the Muslim realize what is required by the correct understanding of the text: fleeing from hardness of the heart by knowing its causes, fleeing from what gives rise to it, and realizing what corresponds to it of humility before Allah, Lord of the Worlds, and *khushūʿ* to Him. This is the correct domain of the scholar and the Sufi, or of the scholar-Sufi; anything else is not from knowledge in any respect, nor from Sufism in word or deed.

In this connection we mention this text: Al-Dārimī narrated from Muʿādh that he said: "A time will come upon the people when the Qur'an will be opened to them such that a woman, a child, and a man will read it. The man will say: 'I have read the Qur'an, so why do the people not follow me?' Then he will stand and fight them, but they will not follow him. Then he will build a mosque in his house and pray in it, but they will not follow him. He will say: 'I have read the Qur'an, and I do not follow anyone until I bring them a hadith that is not in the Book of Allah and that they did not hear from the Messenger of Allah, so that they may follow me.' Muʿādh said: 'Beware of what he brings, for it is misguidance.'" And Abū Dāwūd narrated from Muʿādh, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: "There are trials ahead of you in which wealth will be abundant and the Qur'an will be opened, so that it will be taken by the believer, the hypocrite, the free man, the slave, the young, and the old. Then a speaker will almost say: 'Why do the people not follow me when I have read the Qur'an, and they do not follow me until I innovate for them something other than it? So beware of what he innovates, for whatever is innovated is misguidance. I warn you against the slip of a wise man; for Satan may speak a word of misguidance through the tongue of a wise man, and a hypocrite may speak a word of truth. If you see a wise man saying something famous of which people say, "What is this?" do not let that turn you away from him, for perhaps he is preserving a legacy and will meet the truth when he hears it, for the wise man has a light." The true Sufi is one who is not content with mere understanding but tries to combine understanding with realization, where others fall short of that. Anything else is not Sufism; it is deviation and misguidance.

When the Sunnah is defined, it is said: it is what has been transmitted from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, of word, deed, tacit approval, or attribute. Attributes are of types: some are sensory, and some are moral; the moral or inward attribute is what the Sufis call a *ḥāl* (حال). The realized Sufis are among the most eager of Allah's creation to realize the outward and inward attributes of the Messenger of Allah. Just as they are eager to follow his example in dress, food, drink, and demeanor, they are eager to follow him inwardly and to realize his inward state. He, peace and blessings be upon him, used to be heard making a humming sound from within when he prayed, like the sound of a boiling pot, from the intensity of his humility. This is a *ḥāl*. And the garment he loved most was the shirt, which in today's usage is called the *jallābiyyah*; this is an attribute. The Sufis are the quickest of people to realize the practical and inward attributes of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him.

This is another principal domain of true Sufism.

If a person grasps what we have mentioned in these seven sections, he has grasped the nature of the science of Sufism and its true domain. He consequently understands the aspects of excess and deviation from this science, recognizes the error of those who fight it wholesale, and through that understands many of the reasons that lead some people to fight this science because of the deviations of some who claim attachment to it. It is incumbent upon the people of knowledge in every age to put matters in their proper places, without sensitivities, without timidity, and without fear of the blame of those who blame in falsehood; for that is part of realizing His saying:

> They strive in the cause of Allah and do not fear the blame of a critic. [Qur'an, Al-Mā'idah 5:54]

We ask Allah to make us among them.

Another ordinary matter: since these are the domains of Sufism, Sufism should be considered a science, and this science has technical terms like every other science. Therefore we find in this science terms such as *ḥāl*, *maqām*, *baqāʾ*, *fanāʾ*, *qabḍ*, *basṭ*, and many others. All of them express correct meanings in origin; if some extremists who claim this science give them mistaken conceptions, that does not affect the core of the truth. And as this science arose and had technical terms, the people of this science adopted many things for establishing it and realizing its content, whether as a trace from a text or a trace from experience. These things became part of it.
