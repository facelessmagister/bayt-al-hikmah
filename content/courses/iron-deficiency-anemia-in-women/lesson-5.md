## Prevention: Eating for Iron Health

The best way to prevent iron deficiency anemia is to eat a diet rich in bioavailable iron.

### Top Iron-Rich Foods

#### Animal Sources (Heme Iron — Best Absorbed)

| Food | Iron (mg per serving) |
|------|----------------------|
| Beef liver (3 oz) | 5.0 |
| Oysters (3 oz) | 8.0 |
| Beef, lean (3 oz) | 2.1 |
| Chicken, dark meat (3 oz) | 1.0 |
| Turkey (3 oz) | 1.0 |
| Tuna (3 oz) | 1.4 |

#### Plant Sources (Non-Heme Iron)

| Food | Iron (mg per serving) |
|------|----------------------|
| Fortified cereals (1 cup) | 4–18 |
| Lentils, cooked (1/2 cup) | 3.3 |
| Spinach, cooked (1/2 cup) | 3.2 |
| Tofu (1/2 cup) | 3.4 |
| Chickpeas, cooked (1/2 cup) | 2.4 |
| White beans (1/2 cup) | 3.9 |
| Pumpkin seeds (1 oz) | 4.2 |
| Dark chocolate (1 oz) | 3.3 |

## Maximizing Iron Absorption

### Do: Pair Iron with Vitamin C
Vitamin C converts non-heme iron into a form that is easier to absorb.

**Good combinations**:
- Lentil curry with tomatoes
- Spinach salad with lemon juice
- Fortified cereal with orange juice
- Tofu stir-fry with bell peppers

### Do: Cook in Cast-Iron Pans
Acidic foods (like tomato sauce) cooked in cast-iron pans absorb iron from the pan itself. This can add a meaningful amount of iron to your meal.

### Don't: Block Absorption
Certain foods and drinks block iron absorption. Avoid consuming these within 1–2 hours of iron-rich meals:

- **Coffee and tea**: Contain tannins and polyphenols that bind iron
- **Calcium-rich foods**: Milk, cheese, yogurt compete with iron for absorption
- **Whole grains and legumes**: Contain phytates, which inhibit absorption (soaking or fermenting reduces this)
- **Antacids**: Reduce stomach acid, which is needed for iron absorption

### Don't: Take Calcium and Iron Together
If you take both calcium and iron supplements, take them at different times of day.

## When Diet Is Not Enough: Supplements

### Who Needs Supplements?

| Group | Recommendation |
|-------|----------------|
| Women with heavy periods | Often need supplements |
| Pregnant women | Universally recommended |
| Vegetarians/vegans | May need supplements |
| Those diagnosed with IDA | Required until resolved |
| Athletes | Often need extra iron |

### Types of Iron Supplements

| Type | Feature | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Ferrous sulfate | Most common | 65 mg elemental iron per 325 mg tablet |
| Ferrous gluconate | Gentler on stomach | Lower elemental iron |
| Ferrous bisglycinate | Chelated form | Better absorbed, less stomach upset |
| Liquid iron | Alternative for those who cannot swallow pills | Often has a metallic taste |

### How to Take Iron Supplements

- **Best time**: On an empty stomach with water or orange juice
- **Avoid**: Coffee, tea, dairy, or antacids within 2 hours
- **Side effects**: Constipation, nausea, stomach pain, dark stools
- **Tip**: If stomach upset occurs, take with a small amount of food (but not dairy)

### How Long Until You Feel Better?

| Timeline | What Happens |
|----------|-------------|
| 1–2 weeks | Energy may start improving |
| 2–4 weeks | Hemoglobin begins to rise |
| 2–3 months | Hemoglobin returns to normal |
| 6 months | Iron stores (ferritin) replenish |

**Important**: Even if you feel better after a few weeks, continue taking iron for at least 3–6 months to rebuild your iron stores.

## Treatment of Severe Cases

### Intravenous (IV) Iron

For people who:
- Cannot tolerate oral supplements
- Have severe deficiency
- Need rapid replenishment (e.g., before surgery)
- Have conditions that prevent absorption (e.g., celiac disease, bariatric surgery)

IV iron is administered in a clinic or hospital. It works faster than oral supplements but carries a small risk of allergic reaction.

### Blood Transfusion

In rare, life-threatening cases of severe anemia, a blood transfusion may be necessary. This is reserved for emergencies and is not a treatment for routine iron deficiency.

## Special Considerations

### Vegetarians and Vegans

- Need approximately **1.8 times** the standard iron recommendation
- Focus on combining iron with vitamin C at every meal
- Consider cooking in cast-iron pans
- May need supplements, especially for women

### Athletes

- Exercise increases iron needs due to increased red blood cell turnover
- "Foot strike hemolysis" (destruction of red blood cells in the feet during running) contributes to iron loss
- Female athletes are at particularly high risk
- Sports anemia is common and usually requires supplementation

### College Students on a Budget

Affordable iron-rich meal ideas:
- **Rice and beans** with salsa (vitamin C from tomatoes)
- **Peanut butter sandwich** on fortified bread with orange slices
- **Oatmeal** with pumpkin seeds and dried apricots
- **Eggs** with sautéed spinach and bell peppers
- **Lentil soup** with lemon juice

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Prevention is easier than treatment** — eat iron-rich foods daily.
2. **Pair iron with vitamin C** and avoid coffee/tea with iron-rich meals.
3. **Supplements are safe and effective** when needed; take on an empty stomach.
4. **Treatment takes time** — 2–3 months for hemoglobin recovery, 6 months for stores.
5. **See a doctor for diagnosis** — do not self-diagnose or self-treat without confirmation.
