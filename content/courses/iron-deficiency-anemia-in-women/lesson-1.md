# Lesson 1: Introduction to Iron and Its Role in the Body

## What You Will Learn
- What iron is and why your body needs it
- The connection between iron and blood
- How iron supports your daily energy and focus

---

## What Is Iron?

Iron is a mineral — a naturally occurring substance found in soil, water, and food. Your body cannot make iron on its own, so you must get it from what you eat. Though you only need tiny amounts (measured in milligrams), iron is essential for life.

Think of iron as the body's "oxygen delivery system manager." Without it, your body cannot transport oxygen efficiently.

---

## Why Does Your Body Need Iron?

### 1. Oxygen Transport
Your blood contains red blood cells. Inside each red blood cell is a protein called **hemoglobin**. Hemoglobin is what makes blood red, and it contains iron at its center.

When you breathe in, oxygen enters your lungs. Hemoglobin grabs that oxygen and carries it through your bloodstream to every cell in your body — your brain, muscles, skin, and organs. Without enough iron, your body cannot make enough healthy hemoglobin.

### 2. Energy Production
Iron helps your cells produce energy. Every cell in your body has tiny structures called mitochondria, which are like mini power plants. Iron helps these power plants work efficiently.

When iron levels drop, people often feel tired and weak — not because they are lazy, but because their cells are literally running low on fuel.

### 3. Brain Function
Your brain needs a constant supply of oxygen. Iron helps deliver that oxygen. Low iron can affect concentration, memory, and even mood.

### 4. Immune Support
Iron plays a role in immune function — your body's ability to fight infections. However, this is a delicate balance. Too little iron weakens immunity, but too much can also be harmful.

---

## Where Is Iron Stored in the Body?

Your body is efficient at storing iron for future use:

- **Bone marrow**: Where new red blood cells are made
- **Liver**: Stores iron in a protein called ferritin
- **Spleen**: Recycles iron from old red blood cells
- **Muscle**: Stores iron in a protein called myoglobin (for muscle oxygen use)

When your body needs more iron, it releases it from these storage sites.

---

## Types of Dietary Iron

There are two types of iron in food:

### Heme Iron
- Found in animal foods
- More easily absorbed by the body (about 15–35% absorbed)
- Sources: red meat, chicken, fish, liver

### Non-Heme Iron
- Found in plant foods
- Less easily absorbed (about 2–20% absorbed)
- Sources: lentils, beans, spinach, fortified cereals, tofu

Your body absorbs non-heme iron better when you eat it with vitamin C (like citrus fruits, tomatoes, or peppers).

## Daily Iron Needs

| Group | Daily Iron Need |
|-------|----------------|
| Adult men | 8 mg |
| Adult women (pre-menopause) | 18 mg |
| Adult women (post-menopause) | 8 mg |
| Pregnant women | 27 mg |

Notice that women of reproductive age need **more than twice** as much iron as men. We will explore why in a later lesson.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Iron is a mineral your body must get from food.
2. Its main job is to help hemoglobin carry oxygen in your blood.
3. Without enough iron, your body cannot produce energy efficiently.
4. Women need more iron than men, especially during their reproductive years.
5. There are two types of dietary iron: heme (animal) and non-heme (plant).
